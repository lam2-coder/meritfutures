## ADR-355: the provisioning job store owes a drain, the drain is a ONE-SHOT pull that ADR-241 ruled FOR rather than against, the vendor already publishes the three methods it needs and `JobQueue` publishes none of them, and the emitter gap two files record as the drain's first blocker does not touch this shape at all (2026-09-06, status: proposed)

**`enqueueProvisioningOp` puts a `provisioning_queue` row and a `pgboss` job on ONE transaction, and nothing in this repository ever takes that job back out.** [ADR-338](ADR-338.md) wrote the queue adapter and stated the question it could not answer in its own words: *"The row that gives the saga a caller owes a drain, or owes the argument for running without one."* [`schedule.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/schedule.ts)'s provisioning row names this row for it. **This is that row, and it rules that the drain IS owed.**

**THE RULING'S WHOLE CONTENT IS THAT "DRAIN" AND "CONSUMER" ARE NOT THE SAME SHAPE, AND THE CORPUS HAS BEEN TREATING THEM AS ONE.** [ADR-241](ADR-241.md) ruled this deployable a one-shot job because *"a long-lived process has no exit code to fail with"*, and [`src/queue.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/queue.ts) withholds `consume` and `start` on that ruling. Both are correct and neither is weakened here. **But a drain need not stay up.** A bounded pull that claims what is queued, settles it, reports and exits is a discrete run with a discrete completion, which is the shape ADR-241 ruled FOR and the only shape [`CRON_INVENTORY`](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md)'s grammar can watch. **So the drain is owed and ADR-241 is not narrowed by an inch.**

### 1. The question, re-derived rather than carried

[`apps/worker/src/provisioning/saga.ts:104-131`](../../apps/worker/src/provisioning/saga.ts) is the producer, and it is real and committed:

```
tx.insert('provisioningQueue', { accountId, operation, payload, payloadHash })
queue.enqueue(tx.sqlExecutor('job-enqueue'), { queue: PROVISIONING_QUEUE_NAME, ... })
```

One handle, one transaction, which is [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s transactional-enqueue criterion met rather than approximated. **The consumer side is empty.** [`src/queue.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/queue.ts) publishes `declareQueue` and `enqueue` and withholds `consume`, `start` and `stop` by construction, and [`test/queue.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/queue.test.ts) asserts over the tree that no module here calls the first two.

So the job store has a producer and no consumer. **The question is not whether that is untidy. It is whether ADR-241 forbids the fix.**

### 2. THE RULING

**RULED: this deployable OWES a drain for `PROVISIONING_QUEUE_NAME`, and the drain is a ONE-SHOT PULL, invoked per cycle by the external schedule, whose failure signal is the process exit code.**

**RULED: `consume` and `start` stay withheld, permanently, for this purpose and every other.** They are the long-lived shape. `src/queue.ts`'s refusal of them is correct, is not weakened here, and this entry adds no method to `WorkerQueue`.

**RULED: the drain is the consuming half of a job [`CRON_INVENTORY`](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md) ALREADY CARRIES, and it mints no new inventory row and no new dead-man switch.** The register's **Provisioning CSV push** row reads *"every 15 minutes | continuous | two consecutive cycles missed"*. That is a discrete, repeating invocation with a per-cycle completion, and a switch phrased on cycles missed is satisfiable only by a job that finishes. **The cadence is READ OFF THE REGISTER AND IS NOT INVENTED HERE.**

#### 2.1 Why this does not narrow ADR-241, argued from its reasoning rather than around it

ADR-241 section 3 is the crux and it rules a SHAPE. Four of its own sentences decide this:

| ADR-241 says | what it decides here |
|---|---|
| *"A LONG-LIVED PROCESS HAS NO EXIT CODE TO FAIL WITH. That is the whole of it."* | The objection is to a process that **stays up**. A pull that claims, settles and exits leaves an exit code on every cycle |
| section 7: *"the ruling still holds and the shape that satisfies it is a thin supervisor that invokes this process, **not** a timer moved inside it. **The thing that must not happen is the timer.**"* | The prohibition is named, and it is the timer. A one-shot drain has no timer |
| *"A queue is how a job is HANDED OFF, not how it is STARTED on a clock, **and the nightly batch has no producer to hand it off from**."* | The dismissal of `@merit/queue` is **qualified on the absence of a producer**. Provisioning HAS one, on one transaction. The qualifier carves provisioning IN rather than out |
| section 6: *"Five other jobs in `CRON_INVENTORY` are built and unwired ... **Each needs its own entrypoint decision and its own row.**"* | ADR-241 **declined jurisdiction** over this job's entrypoint and said so. This is that row |

**SO THE RULING IS ADR-241 APPLIED AND NOT ADR-241 NARROWED**, and the test of that is that nothing ADR-241 closed reopens: no timer, no supervisor, no process that stays up, no status that reports only its last moment, and no job whose completion a dead-man switch cannot see.

**THE OPPOSITE READING IS AVAILABLE AND IT IS WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST ARGUABLE, so it is stated here rather than in section 8.** One could read ADR-241's ruling sentence, *"the worker's nightly batch is a ONE-SHOT PROCESS"*, as a statement about the whole deployable, and read `src/queue.ts`'s clause 3, *"`consume` IS THE POLLER AND IT IS THE SAME REFUSAL ... a consumer is a process that stays up"*, as already having settled that a queue consumer of any kind is refused here. **That reading is coherent and this entry rejects it on one fact: `consume` is a process that stays up because pg-boss's `work()` makes it one, and it is not the only way to take a job off a queue.** Section 5 measures the alternative at the pinned version rather than asserting it. A refusal that generalizes from one vendor method to every possible consumption is a refusal resting on an accident of which method the adapter happened to wrap.

#### 3.1 [ADR-039](ADR-039.md)'s phrase does NOT apply here, and the half that transfers is the other half

The dispatch asked whether *"a queue nobody drains is a fail-open with extra steps"* describes this. **It does not, and the mismatch is worth stating because the phrase is memorable enough to be misapplied.**

ADR-039's queue is fail-OPEN in the strict sense: the OTP breaker degrades, **registration continues**, and phone verification is deferred. The queue is what lets an unverified identity through. **Provisioning is fail-CLOSED and the corpus says so at the top of [STATE](../STATE.md)'s surviving-items list**: *"fail-closed provisioning brings no account online at all."* An undrained provisioning queue admits nobody. `provisioning_queue.status` defaults to `'queued'` (`0007:281`) and the account sits at `provisioning_pending`. **The failure is a trader who paid and cannot trade, not a risk door left open.**

**WHAT DOES TRANSFER IS THE VISIBILITY HALF, and it is the more general half of ADR-039 rather than the weaker one.** *"A degraded mode nobody is watching becomes the normal mode"*, and the control ADR-039 actually mandates is that `deferred_registrations` is a **reported figure**. **Provisioning has no such reported figure**, and the two indexes that would serve one already ship: `provisioning_queue_status_idx` on `(status, queued_at)` and the partial index on `accounts (created_at) WHERE status = 'provisioning_pending'`, both in `0007`. **The queue depth and the oldest pending account are derivable today and are reported by nothing.** That is recorded as `OQ-355-03`.

### 4. The failure channel, and TWO LIVE CLAIMS THIS ROW FALSIFIES

**This is the finding most likely to change what the next session builds, so it is stated before the sizing.**

Two files record the same blocker in nearly the same words. [`src/queue.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/queue.ts): *"Somebody must eventually run the maintenance and consume these jobs, and that row **meets the emitter gap before it meets anything else**."* [`provisioning/queue-adapter.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/provisioning/queue-adapter.ts): *"it meets the failure channel before it meets anything else: pg-boss reports a supervise failure on an emitter `pgBossQueue` does not expose."*

**BOTH ARE TRUE OF `consume` AND `start`. BOTH ARE FALSE OF THE SHAPE THIS ROW RULES**, and they are kept beside their corrections under `RI-14` rather than deleted.

The emitter gap is real and it is a property of the **supervisor**. `start()` runs a background pass and has no caller to throw to, so its failures go to an `EventEmitter` nobody exposes. **A one-shot pull has a caller.** Measured at the pinned version, `pg-boss@12.28.0`, from the vendor's own `dist`:

| method | how a failure reaches somebody |
|---|---|
| `manager.fetch` | `db.executeSql` directly, and the `catch` **rethrows everything except SQLSTATE 23505**, with the vendor's own comment that swallowing it *"turned every failed fetch into a silent `[]` with no error event"* |
| `manager.complete` | `assertDb(options)` then `db.executeSql`. No worker, no emitter, no `catch` |
| `manager.fail` | the same |

**So the drain's failure channel is the one ADR-241 ALREADY BUILT**: the call rejects, nothing catches, the top-level `await` in [`start.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/start.ts) makes Node exit non-zero, and [`test/entrypoint.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/entrypoint.test.ts) already spawns a process and asserts exactly that. **The drain does not wait on the event sink, on `packages/queue` exposing an emitter, or on the `RI-04` fence [ADR-348](ADR-348.md) and [ADR-349](ADR-349.md) measured.** It needed none of them, and two files said it needed one of them first.

**AND THE CORRECTION CUTS AGAINST THIS ENTRY'S OWN CONVENIENCE, which is why it is worth trusting.** It removes a blocker this row could otherwise have cited to explain why nothing was built.

### 5. Whether the ruled shape is buildable, measured in three places

#### 5.1 The vendor publishes it

`node_modules/.pnpm/pg-boss@12.28.0/node_modules/pg-boss/dist/index.d.ts`, at the version `pnpm-workspace.yaml:175` pins:

```
31:    fetch<T>(name: string, options: types.FetchOptions & { ... 
34:    fetch<T>(name: string, options?: types.FetchOptions): Promise<types.Job<T>[]>;
50:    complete(name: string, id: string | string[], data?, options?): Promise<types.CommandResponse>;
51:    fail(name: string, id: string | string[], data?, options?): Promise<types.CommandResponse>;
```

**AND `fetch` DOES NOT REQUIRE `start()`, WHICH IS THE LOAD-BEARING HALF.** `boss.js` guards `work()` on a `#stopped` flag and `manager.work` throws *"Workers are disabled. pg-boss is stopped"*. **`fetch` is guarded by neither**: `index.js:243` forwards straight to `manager.fetch`, which resolves a db handle from the caller's options and runs one `plans.fetchNextJob` statement. **No supervisor, no sixty-second interval, no timer, and nothing holding the event loop open.** That is the entire basis of `src/queue.ts`'s objection to `start`, and it is absent from this path.

#### 5.2 `JobQueue` does NOT publish it, and that is where the work lives


**Five methods, and not one of them is a pull.** `consume` plus `start` is the only consumption path the interface offers, and it is the long-lived shape. So **the ruled drain is not expressible on today's port**, and the first slice of building it is a sixth method on `JobQueue`.

**THAT SLICE IS `packages/queue`'s ROW AND NOT THIS ONE.** [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 5 and [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 4 clause 4 both rule that an interface change is taken by the row whose OBJECT it is, and `job-queue.ts` makes the change deliberate by construction: `EveryJobQueueMethodIsListed` is a compile error the moment a sixth method is added without listing it. **Taking it here would be taking it as a MEANS, which is the move `src/queue.ts` refused one layer up and which this entry is not entitled to make just because it is the row that noticed.** It is owed and it is not taken.

### 6. Which job the drain belongs to, and the cadence question the corpus states TWICE

**The corpus names two cadences over provisioning and they are two different acts, which is worth separating because [ADR-107](ADR-107.md) section 6 flagged the strain and could not repair it inside its fence.**

| source | act | cadence |
|---|---|---|
| [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.1, `ST-M2-9` | *"Entitlement hygiene, provisioning queue drain, cost projection"*, **a stage of the nightly batch**, *"Alarms; non-blocking for eligibility"* | nightly |
| [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.6.1 and `CRON_INVENTORY` | **Provisioning CSV push** | every 15 minutes |

**RULED: the `pgboss.job` drain belongs to the FIFTEEN-MINUTE PUSH and not to `ST-M2-9`.** The job `enqueueProvisioningOp` writes carries `{account_id, operation, payload_hash}`, which is a unit of delivery work: something must pick it up and push a CSV. `ST-M2-9` is a nightly **hygiene and reporting** stage over `provisioning_queue` rows, explicitly *"non-blocking for eligibility"*, and a queue whose delivery waited for it would put a trader's account online up to a day after they paid. **The two are not competing schedules for one job; they are two jobs, and only one of them is a consumer.**

**THIS SHARPENS A WORD RATHER THAN CHANGING ONE.** [`ports.ts:44`](../../apps/worker/src/provisioning/ports.ts) cites `ST-M2-9` to justify `SystemTx`'s `'nightly-batch'` reason word, and ADR-107 section 6 already recorded that *"a fifteen-minute cron reading a word that says nightly is a reason nobody has to justify joining."* **This entry does not repair that either**, for ADR-107's own stated reason: widening `SystemReason` edits [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), which this fence does not hold. It is named a second time, with the distinction above added, so the next row that holds that fence does not have to re-derive which of the two stages the word was reaching for.

**AND M02 CONSTRAINS THE SHAPE IN A WAY THAT SURVIVES THIS RULING.** `OQ-M2-06` refuses an out-of-cycle push because it would be *"a second way for the queue to be driven, which is a new mechanism on a path whose whole design argument is that there is exactly one."* **The ruled drain is the SAME driver on its stated cycle, not a second one**, which is the property that keeps this ruling consistent with an open founder question rather than pre-empting it.

### 8. Open questions, which are the founder's and are recorded as options rather than values

**No poll interval, retry count, backoff, visibility timeout or concurrency limit is invented here.** The cadence in section 2 is quoted from `CRON_INVENTORY` rather than chosen. What is genuinely missing:

**`OQ-355-01`. How many jobs does one cycle claim?** `fetch` takes `batchSize` and defaults to 1 (`manager.js:1082`, `limit: options.batchSize || 1`). No document states a figure. **Options:** one per cycle, which makes a backlog drain at four accounts an hour and is almost certainly too slow; a fixed batch, which needs a number; or drain-until-empty within a bounded cycle, which needs the bound. **This entry recommends none of the three** because the right answer depends on the vendor's own CSV push limits, which are `V-M2-nn` and unanswered.

**`OQ-355-02`. What is `provisioning_queue.attempts` compared against, and what happens at the ceiling?** `0007:282` declares `attempts integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (attempts >= 0)` and **nothing in this repository ever increments it or reads it.** A drain that retried forever and a drain that gave up after one failure are both consistent with the column as it stands. A ceiling is a number Merit will be held to, because past it an account that paid does not come online and somebody has to be told.

**`OQ-355-03`. What reports the queue depth?** Section 3.1. ADR-039's transferable control is a reported figure and provisioning has none. The two indexes that would serve one already exist.

**These three are why a drain written by this row would have been a drain with three invented numbers in it**, and that is the strongest argument for the deliverable being a ruling.

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**Named rather than left for a reader to find.**

**THE STRONGEST OBJECTION IS THE ONE IN SECTION 2.1 AND IT IS NOT FULLY ANSWERED.** `src/queue.ts` clause 3 refuses `consume` on the ground that *"a consumer is a process that stays up"*. This entry answers that `consume` is such a process because of how `work()` is built, not because consumption requires it. **A reader who thinks the corpus meant to refuse consumption in this deployable ALTOGETHER, rather than refusing the long-lived form of it, will read section 2 as narrowing ADR-241 after all.** That reader is owed a direct answer and the honest one is that ADR-241 never contemplated a pull, because the job it was ruling on had no producer to pull from.

**THE CADENCE RULING IN SECTION 6 RESTS ON A READING OF `ST-M2-9`'s ONE TABLE CELL.** *"Entitlement hygiene, provisioning queue drain, cost projection"* is nine words and this entry leans on *"non-blocking for eligibility"* to conclude the nightly stage is not the delivery path. **AND NOTHING HERE WAS RUN AGAINST A DATABASE.** Every claim about pg-boss is read off the pinned vendor's `dist` and its type declarations. `fetch` not requiring `start()` is read from the absence of a guard in two files, which is a strong reading and is not an execution. **The first session to build the drain should run it before trusting section 5.1**, and if `fetch` turns out to need a started instance, section 2's shape survives and section 5's sizing does not.
