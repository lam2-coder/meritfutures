## ADR-384: the line split three rows queued for `RI-35` was never what `RI-35` needed, the registration lands with the runbook row unsplit, and the split is refused by a gate rather than by taste (2026-09-06, status: proposed)

**[ADR-375](ADR-375.md) SECTION 7, [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) SECTION 9 AND [ADR-380](ADR-380.md) SECTION 10 EACH NAMED THE SAME ONE-STEP QUEUE AND NONE COULD REACH IT: split `docs/ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md`'s replay row, then register the absence claim in `RI-35`.** Each of the three recorded that `docs/ops/runbooks/**` was outside its fence. This row was given that fence.

**RULED: THE SPLIT IS NOT THE REPAIR, AND IT NEVER WAS. The registration lands with line 21 unsplit, because `RI-35`'s leg 1 anchors a VERBATIM SUBSTRING and stores no line number.** The queue was one step long and the step was the registration alone.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `384`, branch `claude/the-line-split-nobody-could-reach`, session **579**, off the wave integration branch rather than off `main`.
- **CONTROL OVER AN OPERATIONS DOCUMENT AND OVER `RI-35`'s SCOPE. Ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line. Merging is not signing. `E2` read owed.**
- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE AND NEITHER IS A THREE-HOUR-OLD ADR.** Every figure inherited from [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) and [ADR-380](ADR-380.md) was re-derived on this tree. Most reproduce. One is imprecise and section 7 states it.

### 1. The obstacle, read at the row that wrote it rather than at the two that restated it

**[ADR-375](ADR-375.md) SECTION 7 IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE THREE THAT GIVES OBSTACLE 4 A REASON, AND THE REASON HAS SINCE EXPIRED.** Its words:

> **FOURTH, AND IT IS WORTH SEPARATING FROM THE OTHERS: BOTH HALVES OF THAT ROW ARE ON ONE LINE.** [...] `RI-35`'s leg 1 anchors a claim to a VERBATIM substring of a single line. **So any registration bound to that line, including the honest caller half that would go green today, breaks the moment the owed repair rewrites the row.**

**THE OBSTACLE WAS A FRAGILITY ARGUMENT ABOUT A PENDING EDIT AND NOT A PROPERTY OF THE CHECK.** The edit it was waiting on is [ADR-376](ADR-376.md)'s, which landed in the same merge that carried [ADR-375](ADR-375.md), and [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) section 9 records it as CLEARED under obstacle 3. **A MEASUREMENT OF THE FACT IS NOT A RE-DERIVATION OF THE OBSTACLE.** Both later rows measured the number that was never the mechanism. This is the same shape [ADR-380](ADR-380.md) section 5 found one wave earlier: a real property recorded with a cause that does not hold, and a remedy implied by the wrong cause that nobody could take.

**ONE ARTIFACT AND ONE CLAIM.** `replay-audit-src-caller` probes for a wiring of `runReplayAudit` under `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src` with comments stripped, and the claim binds the runbook's live caller clause `live` against it.

**AN ARGUMENT POSITION COUNTS, AND THE CASE THAT DEMANDS IT FAILED FIRST.** [ADR-338](ADR-338.md) found the door probe blind to a door HANDED to something rather than called. The same shape was written into this row's cases before it could cost anything, and the first version of this probe **failed that case**: a registry written `[runNightlyBatch, runReplayAudit]` puts the entry point last, where the next character is `]`, and the character class had `,` and `)` alone. The runbook says `src/` because a demo is not a schedule and cannot give a dead-man switch anything to watch, and a probe widened to the swept scope would report `present` off that demo and retire a sentence true of every deployable.

**NO NEEDLE IS REGISTERED, AND THAT IS A MEASUREMENT.** A needle on `runReplayAudit` reaches exactly ONE unregistered line in the swept scope: [`apps/worker/src/schedule.ts:407`](../../apps/worker/src/schedule.ts), whose `NO CALLER` is the absence word with the needle one line below it, inside the job registry row for this same job, stating this same absence in the same words. **That is a claim site OWED, not a needle to tighten**, and `apps/worker/**` is a concurrent row's fence on this wave. ### 7. The first `docs/` site, and the rule that admits it

**[ADR-375](ADR-375.md) SECTION 13 QUESTION 2 ASKED WHETHER A `docs/` FILE MAY BE AN `RI-35` CLAIM SITE AT ALL AND LEFT THREE OPTIONS. THIS ROW TAKES (b): YES FOR LIVE RUNBOOKS, NEVER FOR DATED RECORDS.**

**THE TWO ARE DIFFERENT KINDS OF SENTENCE AND ONLY ONE OF THEM CAN GO STALE.** An ADR, a session log or a review records a measurement made on its own day, so a sentence in it the tree later falsifies is still a true record of what was measured; binding one would demand a repair that rewrites history, which is `CI-06/derivable-counts`' ruling and `RI-16`'s exclusion 1. A runbook asserts what is true NOW, to somebody deciding at 07:00 whether an alarm that did not fire had a subject.

**IT IS ONE ENTRY AND NOT A WIDENING.** Leg 6's sweep still reads no `docs/` file, and a case asserts both halves: a `docs/` file carrying a registered needle AND an absence word produces nothing, while the same sentence moved into the swept scope produces the finding. **Leg 1 was always able to read the site the register names. What nobody had done is decide whether it may**, and a check whose scope depends on which files happen to be registered is a scope nobody chose.

**THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT, AT FULL STRENGTH.** [ADR-375](ADR-375.md) called this "a change to what the check is FOR" and [ADR-371](ADR-371.md) declined a widening one wave earlier. A founder who reads `RI-35` as a check over SOURCE may hold that a runbook's claims belong to prose review, which is option (a) and is what has been happening. **The answer this row gives is that the runbook sentence is load-bearing in exactly the way a comment is**: it is read to decide whether to act, it went half false once already (the ports half, [ADR-346](ADR-346.md)), and nothing caught it.

The benefit is that the sentence which tells an operator the dead-man switch has nothing to watch cannot quietly stop being true, which is the exact thing that already happened once to the other half of that same sentence.

  **THE SECOND IS THAT THREE CONSECUTIVE ROWS QUEUED A REPAIR NOBODY NEEDED, AND EACH ONE VERIFIED IT.** [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) and [ADR-380](ADR-380.md) both re-derived obstacle 4 and both re-derived the character count, which is the fact. Neither re-read the sentence that gave the fact its force, and that sentence had expired one row before the first of them wrote it down. **A re-derivation that measures the fact and not the reason is how a wrong obstacle survives being checked twice.** The remedy is not more checking; it is that an obstacle is recorded with the condition that would clear it, so a later row can test the condition rather than re-measure the fact.

  **THE THIRD IS SECTION 4 AND IT IS THE ONE THAT COULD HAVE GONE THE OTHER WAY.** Had `CI-06v` not existed, the split would have landed in some earlier wave, the table would have ended at line 22, eighteen rows of a runbook would have rendered as prose, and every gate would have been green. **The gate that caught it was written for a different reason entirely**, which is the best argument in this entry for the corpus's habit of writing structural gates nobody has a case for yet.
