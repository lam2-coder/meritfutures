### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE HOME IS `packages/db`, AND THE FORM IS A TYPED CATALOGUE READ.** `catalogRows`, `catalogRowsWhere` and `catalogRowAt` return the row type derived from `TABLES[K]`, which `schema.ts` already declares, instead of `unknown`. Section 5 says exactly what that buys and section 5.1 says what it does NOT.
>
> **2. A NEW WORKSPACE PACKAGE IS REFUSED, AND NOT ON COST.** Its entire content would be knowledge of `packages/db`'s property names, held OUTSIDE `packages/db`, where that package can invalidate it with no compile error anywhere. It is the same coupling with one more manifest edit in each deployable and one more place for it to rot. Section 3.
>
> **3. THE GROUND [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) GAVE FOR CALLING THIS A DELIBERATE DOOR DOES NOT SURVIVE THE SOURCE.** [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) never mentions the return type and [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) foreclosure 3 is about statement shape. **The `unknown` is inherited rather than ruled**, so what is owed here is a decision nobody has taken rather than an amendment to one somebody did. Section 4.
>
> **4. AND THE DOOR HAS NO CALLER, WHICH IS A FENCE FACT AND NOT A JUDGEMENT.** Zero occurrences under `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src` that are not comments. **The first caller is the one this ruling exists to unblock**, which is the only moment at which a door's shape can be chosen with its question already answered rather than before it. That is [ADR-078](ADR-078.md)'s test met rather than dodged. Section 4.
>
> **5. THE DECODER SPLITS AT THE LINE THAT ACTUALLY EXISTS, AND IT IS NOT THE LINE [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) DREW.** The engine may know the DATABASE's own names and already does, in `PlanVersionSizeRow`'s own header and in `decodePlanRules`. It may not know `packages/db`'s property spellings. So the homeless part is a KEY MAPPING and nothing else: the value decode has a home in the engine today, and the row-level read becomes a total rename with both ends declared. Section 6.
>
> **6. WHAT REMAINS STATED PER CALLER IS THE RENAME, AND THAT IS ACCEPTED RATHER THAN HIDDEN.** After ruling 1 the driver-side callers still each write a field mapping. It is `FM-16` by the letter and it is the smallest residue any arrangement leaves, because the only two places that could hold it once are `packages/db`, where it already is, and a package that must not exist. Section 5.1.
>
> **7. THE CAP-SCHEDULE ROW IS THE NEXT DISPATCH, AHEAD OF THE DOOR ROW, AND IT IS SPECIFIED IN SECTION 7.** The codec, the collapse of all three readers, and a fence reaching [`apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts), so the payout ceiling stops being three statements and the one that has diverged is repaired in the same commit set.
>
> **8. [ADR-078](ADR-078.md) IS READ FOR THE EXPORT HERE, WHICH IS THE READING [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) DID NOT TAKE.** That entry rules *"where withholding causes reimplementation, the limit is being applied against its own purpose"*. Withholding a cap-schedule codec has already produced three implementations of one payout ceiling and one of them rounds. Section 7.
>
> **9. `catalog.ts`'s LOCAL `PlanVersionSizeRow` DOES NOT COLLAPSE ONTO THE ENGINE'S. IT IS RENAMED.** The two types answer different questions and neither is usefully the other's subset, and the engine's type refuses the price BY NAME. What is owed is the NAME, which this tree states three times for three different shapes. Section 8.
>
> **10. NO GATE WAS WEAKENED AND NO FENCE WAS WIDENED.** No test skipped, no assertion relaxed, no key admitted, no migration written, no `status` field moved, no source file edited, no export moved, nothing signed.

### 3. The candidate set is TWO, and it is derived rather than asserted

**[ADR-286](ADR-286.md) named two candidates. They are the only two, and the manifests are why.**

| Deployable | Workspace dependencies declared |
| --- | --- |
| `apps/api` | `@merit/affiliate`, `@merit/db`, `@merit/enrichment`, `@merit/kyc`, `@merit/ledger`, `@merit/psp`, `@merit/rules-engine` |
| `apps/worker` | `@merit/db`, `@merit/rules-engine` |

**THE INTERSECTION IS `@merit/db` AND `@merit/rules-engine`.** The engine is foreclosed for this value by section 6. **So `packages/db` is the only place in this tree that both deployables can already reach**, and every other answer requires a package that does not exist.

**A NEW PACKAGE IS REFUSED ON WHAT IT WOULD CONTAIN RATHER THAN ON WHAT IT WOULD COST.** A decoder does not need to IMPORT `@merit/db` to read a driver row: `toSizeRow` reads `row['sizeCents']` off an `unknown` and imports nothing from that package. So the new package's content would be a hard-coded copy of another package's property names, checked by nothing, in a package `packages/db` cannot break loudly. **That is worse than the position it replaces**, because today the copy at least lives beside the runtime guard that reads it.

**`.npmrc` SETS `node-linker=isolated`, SO A DECLARATION IS THE CAPABILITY AND NOT MERELY THE RECORD OF IT** ([`apps/api/package.json`](../../apps/api/package.json)'s own `//dependencies` note). A new package therefore costs a manifest edit in both deployables before it holds a single line, and `apps/api`'s manifest note records that an admission there is *"an authority change on the deployable that serves the whole public surface"* rather than a side effect.

**FIRST: [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) DOES NOT MENTION THE RETURN TYPE.** `grep -c unknown docs/decisions/ADR-233.md` is zero. That entry rules the KEY LIST, the absence of a write verb, and the disclosure argument being absent for a row nobody owns. It rules nothing about what a caller is handed.

### 5. What a typed door buys

1. **THE KEY NAMES BECOME A COMPILER QUESTION, ACROSS EVERY CATALOGUE READER RATHER THAN THIS ONE.** Today `readSize` reaches for `'sizeCents'`, `'drawdownCents'` and nine more as STRINGS against a bag of `unknown`, and the only thing standing between a mistyped key and a wrong money value is a runtime guard that throws on absence. `readPlan`, `readPurchase` and every future catalogue read are in the same position.
2. **IT IS THE ROOT OF BOTH HALVES OF [ADR-286](ADR-286.md), WHICH THAT ENTRY SAYS IN ITS OWN SECTION 7 ITEM 4** and could not take because `packages/db/**` was out of its fence.
3. **THE DECLARED TYPE IS TRUE AT RUN TIME BECAUSE DRIZZLE'S COLUMN MAPPER MAKES IT TRUE**, which `packages/db`'s own suite already records: a numeric STRING never reaches the `effectiveAccountCap` guard, because *"Drizzle's own `integer` column mapper runs `parseInt` on a string before any Merit code sees the value"* ([`account-cap-door.test.ts:284`](../../packages/db/test/account-cap-door.test.ts)). `size_cents` is read `{ mode: 'bigint' }` ([`schema.ts:442`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts)), so a `bigint` is what arrives.
4. **IT NEEDS NO RENAME FROM ANYBODY**, which is the property [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) correctly made the test of every candidate. `apps/site` is untouched: it has no database and reads a wire object.

#### 5.1 And what it does NOT buy, stated as limits rather than discovered later

1. **IT DOES NOT DELIVER A DECODER.** It SHRINKS one. After it lands, a driver-side caller still writes a field mapping from the typed row onto the engine's `PlanVersionSizeRow`, and that mapping is still written once per caller. **What changes is that both of its ends are declared**: a key that does not exist is `TS2339` rather than a runtime throw on a money read. A key that exists and is the WRONG one still compiles, because `drawdownCents` and `bufferCents` are both `bigint`. Section 12.
2. **IT DOES NOT REACH INSIDE THE `jsonb`.** `payoutCapScheduleCents: jsonb('payout_cap_schedule_cents').notNull()` ([`schema.ts:452`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts)) carries no `.$type<>()`, so the blob stays untyped, correctly: a `jsonb` document is not a column value and the type system has nothing to read. **The one divergence that has actually happened on this value is inside that blob**, which is why section 7's row is dispatched first and does not wait on this one.
3. **IT TYPES THE READ `apps/api` WILL MAKE AND LEAVES THE READ `apps/worker` ALREADY MAKES UNTYPED.** The worker reads the size grid through `SystemTx.rowsWhere` on `systemDb('nightly-batch')` ([`batch/adapter.ts:1637`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)), not through the catalogue verbs. **Whether the same treatment is owed to the other read verbs is NAMED here and NOT ruled**: it is a much larger surface, it has real callers, and it is a separate row.
4. **THE ROW TYPE CARRIES MORE THAN THE ENGINE'S DOES.** `price_cents`, `reset_price_cents`, `id` and `created_at` are columns and would be on the typed row. The engine's type refuses the first two by name, and that refusal is preserved because the DECODER's output is still the engine's type; what changes is that the price is visible at the decode site, where `readSize` already sees it.
5. **A TYPE DERIVED FROM `schema.ts` IS A TYPE DERIVED FROM A TRANSCRIPTION.** [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) foreclosure 4 says *"nothing yet makes the transcription complete"* and that the suite catches an INVENTED key rather than requiring exhaustiveness, and no check in this tree compares a `schema.ts` column TYPE against the DDL. **So the runtime guards must not be dropped on the strength of the declared type**, and a row that lands this must say so in the file.

### 6. The line that actually exists, and why the engine is foreclosed for the ROW but not for the BLOB

**`RI-01` IS THE MANIFEST HALF BY ITS OWN WORDS** ([`repo-invariants.mjs:205`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs)): *"It does NOT read the source: an import statement, a wall-clock read and a `node:` builtin are the same defect class and are caught by the `merit/engine-purity` ESLint rule."* That rule bans imports, clocks, locales, floats and ambient globals. **Neither half would see the string `'sizeCents'` inside an engine source file.** So the foreclosure is a principle the corpus holds rather than a check the runner enforces, and a principle has to be stated precisely enough to apply.

**STATED PRECISELY, IT IS THIS: THE ENGINE MAY KNOW THE DATABASE'S OWN NAMES AND MAY NOT KNOW ANOTHER PACKAGE'S PROPERTY NAMES.** Both halves are already visible in the tree.

| The engine already knows | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Every COLUMN name of `plan_version_sizes` | `PlanVersionSizeRow`, *"transcribed column for column from `0004_catalog.sql`"* ([`types.ts:587`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) |
| The stored `jsonb` KEYS of `plan_versions.rules` | `decodePlanRules` ([`rules-codec.ts:472`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/rules-codec.ts)), exported at [`index.ts:206`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/index.ts) |
| The stored `jsonb` KEYS of the cap schedule | `SizeCapScheduleStep`'s `from_ordinal` and `cap_cents` ([`types.ts:567`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) |
| **And may not know** | |
| `packages/db`'s camelCase property spellings | Nothing in the engine reads one, and this ruling keeps it that way |

**THE DIFFERENCE IS NOT A PREFERENCE.** A column name is a fact about the schema, which the engine is entitled to hold because a rule is stated against a column. A property name is another package's private mapping, which that package may change without a migration and without telling anybody.

**SO THE HOMELESS PART OF THIS DECODE IS A KEY MAPPING AND NOTHING ELSE**, and that is why ruling 1 puts it in the package that already holds it. `schema.ts` states both spellings on one line, `sizeCents: bigint('size_cents', { mode: 'bigint' })`, and it is the only file in this repository that does.

**AND IT IS WHY THE CAP SCHEDULE IS NOT BLOCKED BY ANY OF THIS.** `from_ordinal` and `cap_cents` are what Postgres stores, every reader asks for the same two keys whatever it asked for the row, and [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) already landed exactly this move for the neighbouring blob. **`decodePlanRules` is the precedent and it is not an analogy**: same package, same `unknown` parameter, same stored-key reading, ruled and landed one wave ago.

### 7. The next row, ruled and specified: the cap schedule, its collapse, and a fence that reaches the divergence

**RULED: YES, AND IT GOES BEFORE THE DOOR ROW.** [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) section 10 named this row as *"a strictly better row than the one this session could have run"* and left the scheduling open. It is dispatched here, and the ordering is part of the ruling.

**WHY IT GOES FIRST, IN THE ORDER THE REASONS DECIDE IT.**

1. **IT REPAIRS A LIVE DIVERGENCE ON A SERVED MONEY VALUE.** `readCapSchedule` admits a `cap_cents` past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` and hands back the rounded double, and refuses the base-10 string [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) ruling 5 blessed. Its two peers do neither. The door ruling repairs nothing that is wrong today.
2. **IT IS INDEPENDENT OF THIS ENTRY, SO NOTHING HERE CAN INVALIDATE IT.** The codec takes `unknown` and reads stored keys. It lands identically whether the catalogue verbs are typed or not, and it is unaffected by which spelling any caller reads the ROW under.
3. **[ADR-078](ADR-078.md) POINTS AT THE EXPORT RATHER THAN AWAY FROM IT.** That entry rules that the export limit *"exists to stop reimplementation"* and that *"where withholding causes reimplementation, the limit is being applied against its own purpose"*. Three implementations exist and one has diverged. **This is the case ADR-078 wrote the sentence for**, and [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) cited that entry for the opposite conclusion without reaching section 2.
4. **[ADR-286](ADR-286.md) RULING 6's THREE REASONS ARE ANSWERED RATHER THAN IGNORED.** Reason 1, *"it moves no blocker"*, is true and is not a reason to leave a defect live. Reason 2, *"its right home is inside the row decoder"*, is answered by section 6: the row decoder is a rename and the blob decode is a decode, and they do not belong in one function. Reason 3, the `E2` read spent on a behaviour-preserving motion through the nightly fold, **is the one that survives, and the fence below is the answer to it**: with `apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts` in scope the diff carries a behaviour change, so the read is spent on something.

### 8. Three declarations of one name, and the ruling on collapse

**THE NAME `PlanVersionSizeRow` IS DECLARED THREE TIMES IN THIS TREE, FOR THREE DIFFERENT SHAPES.** [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) section 7 item 3 registered one of them. The third is registered here.
