## ADR-201: the payout-velocity window is seven trading days against thirty scaled to seven, and the settled threshold PROVES that reading rather than this entry choosing it  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**A velocity is a count over a period. [Session 374](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-374.md)'s `B2` is that `payout_velocity`'s threshold is fixed in three documents and its window in none, so the one number that pages when the firm's own money leaves abnormally fast cannot be evaluated, and every implementation that meets it invents the missing half differently. THIS ENTRY IS THE WINDOW. Its spine is that the missing half was never a free choice: of the three readings `avg_30d_cents` admits, one makes the alarm PROVABLY unable to fire, one makes it fire every day forever, and exactly one makes 2.5x mean what three documents say it means. The reading is derived from the settled threshold rather than picked, and the trading-day question is answered explicitly and against the corpus's own strongest counter-source.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `201`, session **381**, dispatched as `claude/payout-velocity-window`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER WAS RESERVED AND NONE IS NEEDED**, which section 8 establishes rather than assumes: a window is not a fact about a row.

- **MONEY PATH (a payout control). [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) READ IS OWED** on this ruling. The entry ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **THIS SLICE RULES AND DOES NOT BUILD.** `apps/**` and `packages/**` are **not opened for edit at all**: no evaluator, no `PolicyNumber`, no constant, no test. Two files under `apps/` were READ, and both reads are reported as findings in section 10 rather than acted on. **A half-shipped evaluator would be `B2` one level down**, which is the defect this entry exists to close.

- **WHAT IS PROVED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED**, separated before the argument starts. That two of the three readings of `avg_30d_cents` cannot be the intended one is **PROVED**, arithmetically, in section 3; one of the two proofs needs no data at all. That four documents state the threshold identically and none states the averaging basis is **DERIVED**, by exhaustive search, in section 2. What is **JUDGED** is the trading day over the calendar day (section 4), the attribution of an off-calendar settlement (section 5), the empty-denominator answer (section 7), and where the number lives (section 8).

### 1. The premise, re-derived, and it is TRUE IN SUBSTANCE AND FALSE AS SPELLED

The dispatch and the [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row both read that the threshold is fixed in three documents **and the window in NONE**. Session 374's own summary row says the same: *"Three documents fix the 2.5x threshold and none states the 30-day window"* ([session 374:46](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-374.md)).

**Checked at source, that sentence is false as written.** [M06:95](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) states both windows, in words, on the row that defines the panel:

> *"Trailing 7 day settled cents against the 30 day average, alarming above 2.5x (constitution M6)"*

Seven days and thirty days are both there. So is "average". **What is missing is not the window. It is what the average averages**, and session 374's longer text says so precisely where its own summary row does not: *"no document says whether `avg_30d_cents` is the 30-day total, its daily mean, or that mean scaled to seven days"*.

**The correction matters because it changes what this entry has to produce.** A missing window would be filled by naming two numbers. A missing averaging basis is filled by naming the arithmetic that makes the two numbers commensurable, and that arithmetic is what section 3 proves. Stated per document rather than as a group:

| Document | numerator window | denominator period | what the average averages | day unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT:166](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) | **not stated** | "30-day avg" | **not stated** | **not stated** |
| [M06:95](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) | "Trailing 7 day settled cents" | "the 30 day average" | **not stated** | **not stated** |
| [INFRA:160](../architecture/INFRA.md) | **not stated** | "the 30 day average" | **not stated** | **not stated** |

**So the honest statement of `B2` is three sentences rather than one.** The numerator window is stated in **one** of the three. The denominator's period is stated in **all three**. The averaging basis and the day unit are stated in **none**, and those two together are the half without which the ratio is not a number.

### 3. The reading is PROVED and not chosen

`avg_30d_cents` is a **cents** figure on the wire, sitting beside `last_7d_cents`, also cents ([API_CONTRACT:905](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). Session 374 named the three readings it can bear. Take each against the threshold clause 1 just settled, and let `S(n)` be settled cents over the trailing `n` days on whatever day unit:

| Reading | `avg_30d_cents` | Steady-state `ratio_bp` | What the 2.5x alarm becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.** the 30-day total | `S(30)` | `10000 * S(7) / S(30)` | **PROVABLY DEAD** |
| **B.** the mean daily figure | `S(30) / 30` | ~`70000` | **FIRES EVERY DAY, FOREVER** |
| **C.** the mean scaled to seven days | `S(30) * 7 / 30` | ~`10000` | **a control** |

**Reading A is refuted with no data at all.** The trailing 7 days are a **subset** of the trailing 30 days, so `S(7) <= S(30)` for every possible history, so `ratio_bp <= 10000` always. `10000` is never greater than `25000`. **The alarm can therefore never fire, on any input, ever.** That is not a calibration concern; it is a proof that reading A makes [INFRA:160](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s pager a decoration.

**Reading B is refuted by one division.** In any steady state, a seven-day total is about seven times a daily mean, so `ratio_bp` sits near `70000`, which is `2.8x` the threshold. The pager fires on the first day and every day after. Silencing it would take payouts falling by roughly 64 percent against their own trailing mean, meaning **the alarm is quiet exactly when something is badly wrong and loud the rest of the time.** [FM-M6-07](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) names this outcome for the CUSUM in words that transfer without a change: *"Either constant alarms, which become noise, or none, which is the same as no chart."*

**Reading C is the only survivor.** A trailing week against the week a normal thirty days would have produced sits near `10000 bp` when nothing is happening, and reaches `25000 bp` when a week's payouts run two and a half times normal. **That is the sentence [MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT:166](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) already says out loud**, and it is the only reading under which it says anything.

**RULING 2. `avg_30d_cents` is the trailing thirty-day settled total scaled to seven days.** The threshold forces it. This entry did not choose between three defensible options; it eliminated two and reported which one was left.

### 4. The unit is the TRADING DAY, and the reason is not that the corpus has a calendar

**The dispatch is right that this must be answered here rather than left to whoever writes the evaluator**, because a velocity over calendar days and one over trading days differ by every weekend and every holiday, and both look correct in review.

**The weak argument first, so it is not mistaken for the ruling.** [GLOSSARY:44](../GLOSSARY.md) admits exactly two units for an obligation Merit binds itself to, trading days over `TradingCalendar` and wall-clock hours over `now()`, and no calendar-day unit exists anywhere in the corpus. That is real, and it settles the **spelling**: a window in "calendar days" would be a third unit this corpus has never used. But that table binds **obligations**, and a measurement window is not an obligation, so it cannot decide the question on its own.

**The ruling rests on stationarity, which is the whole job of a ratio.** A ratio exists to be invariant to everything that is not the signal. The denominator normalizes away volume. The day unit has to normalize away the calendar, and it does that only if the process filling the numerator advances on the same clock as the window that bounds it. Two facts, both at source, say which clock that is:

1. **Payout supply is generated by gates counted in trading days.** [Minimum trading days](../GLOSSARY.md), [win days](../GLOSSARY.md) (*"A trading day whose realized P&L is greater than or equal to the configured win-day floor"*) and the [cadence gap](../GLOSSARY.md) (*"The minimum number of trading days that must elapse between payouts"*) are every one of them counted in trading days. **No account becomes newly eligible over a weekend**, because nothing that makes an account eligible advances over a weekend.
2. **The snapshot's own clock is already a trading day.** [GLOSSARY:52](../GLOSSARY.md) defines the [last closed day](../GLOSSARY.md) as *"The most recent **trading day** for which the nightly batch has completed and marks are final"*, and `INV-M6-12` forbids any alarm from reading anything else: *"An indicative figure is never presented as an as-of-last-closed figure, and no breaker, alarm, or task threshold reads one"* ([M06:56](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)).

**So the supply process and the right-hand edge of the window both already advance on the exchange calendar, and a calendar-day window would impose a third clock on a quantity that has two.**

**The failure that produces is not a rounding difference, and the corpus has already measured its size once.** [M01:941](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), on the cadence gap: *"Five trading days is 7 calendar days in June and can be 9 to 10 across the year-end cluster."* Apply that to a ratio of two windows:

- **Inside the year-end cluster**, seven calendar days can hold two or three trading days. Almost no new eligibility clears, the numerator collapses, and the thirty-calendar-day denominator still spans mostly-normal weeks. The ratio **dips**, so a genuine extraction surge occurring inside the cluster is **MISSED** by the one control that watches for it.
- **In the first full week back**, the eligibility that accumulated through the cluster clears into seven calendar days, against a denominator whose thirty calendar days include the dead stretch. The ratio **spikes** and **the pager fires on a calendar artifact.**

**That is `FM-M6-07`'s "either constant alarms or none", arrived at from the other direction, on a control [INFRA:160](../architecture/INFRA.md) attaches a PAGE to.** Under trading days both terms count the same kind of day, the ratio is invariant to weekends and holidays by construction, and steady state stays near `10000 bp` in December as in June.

**RULING 3. Both windows are counted in TRADING DAYS, over [`TradingCalendar`](../GLOSSARY.md) and the `trading_calendar` rows, and never by wall-clock date arithmetic.** The numerator is the **7 trading days** ending at the trading day of the snapshot's `as_of`, inclusive of that day. The denominator is the **30 trading days** ending at the same day, inclusive. `trading_calendar` became readable through the accessor when [session 377](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-377.md) registered it, so this ruling names a table the tree can already read rather than one it cannot.

### 5. The attribution rule, which is what keeps ruling 3 clear of the corpus's own warning against it

**The strongest source against ruling 3 is [GLOSSARY:47](../GLOSSARY.md), and it is quoted in full rather than paraphrased because paraphrasing it would soften it:**

> *"A trading day is not a business day in either direction: the exchange trades on days banks are shut and shuts on days banks trade. Whoever implements an expiry sweep and reaches for "the only calendar in the database" reaches for a different set of days, and that substitution is wrong on roughly 104 days a year while looking exactly right in review."*

**The objection is real and it is specific.** A payout **settles** on the rail's clock, not the exchange's; [GLOSSARY:38](../GLOSSARY.md) says of the business day that *"Merit quotes it and never computes it"* and that *"settlement follows the rail on the rail's own schedule"*. Payouts therefore settle on Saturdays, on Thanksgiving, and on days CME is shut. **A rule that counted only settlements landing on trading days would silently drop them, and dropping settlements from a liability alarm understates money that has actually left.** That would be exactly the substitution `GLOSSARY:47` warns about.

**It is answered by separating two questions the objection folds together.** The trading calendar is **not** asked whether a settlement happened, or when the rail moved the money, or what Merit owes anybody. It is asked only **which snapshot first sees it**, and that is `last closed day` semantics already in force on every other figure of this response.

**RULING 4. Every settled payout is attributed to exactly one trading day: the first trading day on or after the UTC date of its settlement.** A settlement dated on a Saturday, a Sunday or an exchange holiday is attributed to the next trading day, which is the first `as_of` whose batch could have seen it. **No settlement is ever dropped and none is ever counted twice**, so the sum over 30 trading days is the sum over every settlement in the interval those days span. The window is bounded by trading days; the money counted inside it is all of the money.

**Ruling 4 is also what makes ruling 2's arithmetic exact.** Without attribution, a "7 trading day" window spans 9 calendar dates and a "30 trading day" window spans about 42, and `9 / 42` is not `7 / 30`, so scaling by `7/30` would carry a standing bias of roughly 8 percent even in steady state. With every settlement attributed to exactly one trading day, the two windows are exactly 7 and exactly 30 of the same unit and the scaling is exact.

### 6. The arithmetic, stated in integers because money is integer cents

Let `A(d)` be settled cents attributed to trading day `d` under ruling 4, and let `D` be the trading day of the snapshot's `as_of`. Write `T(1) = D`, `T(2)` the previous trading day, and so on.

```
last_7d_cents  = sum over k in 1..7  of A(T(k))
total_30_cents = sum over k in 1..30 of A(T(k))

avg_30d_cents  = floor(total_30_cents * 7 / 30)
ratio_bp       = floor(last_7d_cents * 10000 / avg_30d_cents)      // see section 7 for avg = 0
alarm          = ratio_bp > 25000
```

**Every term is an integer and no float appears at any point**, which is the constitution's rule and applies to this entry's examples as much as to the code. `total_30_cents * 7` is computed before the division so the scaling loses at most 29 cents rather than compounding a per-day rounding thirty times.

**The two `floor`s round in opposite directions and that is stated rather than hidden.** Flooring the **denominator** makes it smaller, which makes `ratio_bp` **larger**, which errs toward alarming: the conservative direction for a control watching the firm's own money leave, and the same posture as the three named places [GLOSSARY:246](../GLOSSARY.md) says Merit's conservatism deliberately lives. Flooring `ratio_bp` errs the other way, by **at most 1 bp against a threshold of 25000**. The asymmetry is reported because a later reader who finds it should find it here rather than discover it, and neither `floor` is worth a rounding mode of its own at those magnitudes.

**RULING 5. The arithmetic above is the definition of the three `payout_velocity` numbers, and any implementation that produces a different number for the same rows is wrong rather than differently calibrated.**

### 7. The empty denominator, which nothing in the corpus has answered for this panel

`ratio_bp` is a non-nullable `number` on the wire and `alarm` a non-nullable `boolean` ([API_CONTRACT:905](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). **If no payout settled in the trailing 30 trading days, `avg_30d_cents` is `0` and the division is undefined.** That happens at launch, and again after any stretch with payouts frozen. No document answers it, and a definition that leaves it open is not finished.

**The corpus has a mechanism for this, and it does not reach here.** `INV-M6-07` says *"A circuit breaker firing on a sample below its minimum is recorded as **insufficient data**, never as a breach of threshold... A ratio computed on three transactions is not a ratio"* ([M06:51](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)), and `SD-M6-02` builds it: `plan_breaker_state` carries `sample_size`, `min_sample` and a state that includes `insufficient_data` ([M06:70](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)). **But `INV-M6-07` binds circuit BREAKERS, and payout velocity is not one.** [GLOSSARY:257](../GLOSSARY.md) defines the circuit breaker as the control that *"pauses **new sales** (never payouts)"* and names exactly two: RCR below 1.0, and a plan's loss ratio. Payout velocity pauses nothing; it **pages**. So the invariant does not reach it, and the row that carries the mechanism does not exist for it.

**RULING 6. When `avg_30d_cents` is `0`, `ratio_bp` is `0` and `alarm` is `false`.** A velocity alarm is a **relative** control and a firm with no payout history has nothing to be relative to; firing on an empty denominator is `AS-M6-02`'s self-inflicted outage with a pager instead of a sales pause, and `FM-M6-02`'s *"the breaker loses credibility permanently"* applies to an alarm exactly as it does to a breaker. **The absolute exposure is not left unwatched by this**: `P-M6-01` and `P-M6-02` report it in cents on the same page and read no ratio at all.

**And the gap this leaves is named rather than closed.** A `0 / false` is indistinguishable on the wire from a quiet week, so an operator cannot tell "nothing unusual" from "nothing to compare against". `plan_breaker_state` solves precisely that with a state and a sample size; **this panel has no row, no state and no `min_sample`, and adding one is a wire-shape change that is neither this fence's nor this entry's** (section 10, finding 3).

### 8. Where the number lives, priced rather than assumed

**The dispatch is right that this is not a detail: a window that is a literal in one file is `B2` one level down, because the second implementation invents a different literal and nothing catches it.** Four homes, priced against what the corpus already does with numbers of each kind.

| Home | Priced | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A column** | No number is reserved and none is needed. A window is not a fact about a row; it is the definition under which every row was computed. [`0049`](../../packages/db/migrations/0049_reserve_coverage_snapshots.sql) refuses to store `breaker_armed` for the neighbouring reason, that *"storing it would recreate in one column exactly the drift item 1 removes from another"* ([ADR-188](ADR-188.md)), and a stored window beside a stored ratio is that same drift with an extra column | **REFUSED**, and the conditional number is not taken |
| **A config value an operator sets** | This is the option that looks most responsible and is the one that must be refused loudest. **The window is the parameter that decides whether the pager fires when the firm's own money leaves abnormally fast.** An operator who can widen it to 90 trading days silences the alarm with no code change, no migration and no record. `INV-M6-13` already refuses the console the exact analogue: *"No admin action extends a hold, a freeze or a restriction past its own clock, and no console affordance offers to... an 'extend' button is the one control-shaped affordance that would delete the control"* ([M06:57](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)). **A settable velocity window is that button** | **REFUSED** |
| **A launch candidate** | The [parameter-status ruling](gates/parameter-status-launch-candidates-versus-structural-rulings-founder-ruling-2026-08-14.md) scopes launch candidates to **plan** parameters, and gives the reason in its own words: they are tunable up to launch *"because each is a row in `plan_version_sizes` rather than a constant in code"*. This window is firm-scoped, belongs to no plan, and has no `plan_version` to be pinned to. **There is no launch-candidate home for it, so this is not a choice declined but an option that does not exist** | **NOT AVAILABLE** |
| **A structural ruling, carried as one named constant in one module** | The RCR breaker's `1.0` is exactly this shape: fixed in the GLOSSARY, deliberately stored nowhere, and named as *"one of the three places Merit's conservatism deliberately lives"* ([GLOSSARY:246](../GLOSSARY.md)) | **RULED** |

**RULING 7. The window is a STRUCTURAL RULING, fixed by this entry and movable only by a superseding ADR.** It is not a column, not a config value, not a launch candidate, and not a plan parameter.

**RULING 8. It is carried in the implementation as ONE named, exported constant in ONE module, cited to this entry, and every reader imports it.** Two literals at two sites is `B2` again, so the implementing session must not spell `7`, `30` or `25000` at a second site. This is the same posture [GLOSSARY:35](../GLOSSARY.md) already fixes for the calendar itself: *"One module (`TradingCalendar`) reads it and everything else calls that module."*

**The corpus has already built the shape ruling 8 asks for, and finding it is what makes the ruling concrete rather than aspirational.** `apps/worker/src/breaker/ports.ts` declares a `PolicyNumber` that is either `{ state: 'stated', value, cite, quote }` or `{ state: 'unstated', value: null, cite, quote }`, and `LOSS_RATIO_POLICY` carries the sibling breaker's five numbers in it, with a test that *"asserts both `unstated` members are still `unstated`, so filling one in is a red suite rather than a quiet commit."* **Before this entry, every number in a `PAYOUT_VELOCITY_POLICY` would have had to be `unstated`, because there was no document to cite.** What this ruling delivers, stated in the tree's own vocabulary, is the `cite` and the `quote` that let them be `stated`. **No such policy is written here** and the file is not opened for edit; section 10 hands it on.
