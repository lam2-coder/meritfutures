## ADR-170: the verification page enters the contract, and the field that addressed it for two weeks now resolves (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-153](ADR-153.md) repaired a contract row naming an endpoint the contract defines nowhere. [ADR-168](ADR-168.md) found the same defect a second time, inside the section it was editing, and deliberately left it: `API_CONTRACT` section 6 ships `verify_url: string; // public verification page` and `GET /verify/:code` is defined in no section of that document.** This entry is the session `ADR-168` named. It admits the verification page and the revocation write that produces the state the page publishes, and it refuses the two leaderboard rows because an open founder question and an absent table each say no on their own.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `170`, session **301**, dispatched as `claude/contract-verify-endpoint`. **Not money path by content.** It writes no code, registers no route, opens no transaction and claims no migration number. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is owed on the handlers these rows produce and is not discharged here.
- **This is a SECURITY ruling and not a routing one.** A public, unauthenticated endpoint keyed on a code is an **oracle over Merit's own payout book**, however it is dressed, and `AS-M11-04` is the scenario written about this exact endpoint. What a held code discloses is section 3; the constant-time clause `INV-M11-05` names this endpoint by name is section 4.
- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every figure below is a `grep`, a `sed` over a migration, or a composed server, quoted at the file it came from. The dispatch's two central claims were reproduced and both held; where its framing did not survive contact with the sources it is said so in place.

### 2. THE RULING, and it is two rows admitted and two refused

> **Clause 1. `GET /verify/:code` is a MISSING ROW and it lands, in `API_CONTRACT` section 6.3, with its disclosure set and its timing mechanism STATED rather than assumed.**
>
> `M11` is `approved` and marks the endpoint `NEW, public` and `Owns`, so under FREEZE it is committed scope. **What forces it now rather than later is finding 1**: the contract has been shipping a field whose value addresses this page, so a client reading `CertificateResponse` today is told a page exists that the specification defines nowhere, and the honest response to a `verify_url` is to follow it. Sections 3, 4 and 5 are the ruling's substance and the row is their transcription.

> **Clause 2. `POST /admin/certificates/:id/revoke` is a MISSING ROW and it lands, in `API_CONTRACT` section 8.**
>
> It is the **write side of the read side clause 1 defines**: the only state clause 1 publishes that is not simply the claim is the revocation, and an entry that specifies how a revocation is published without specifying how one is recorded leaves the published half with no producer. `M11` section 4 gives it completely (*"`revocation_class` required, reason required, writes `admin_actions`. `owner` or `ops`"*), findings 10 and 11 give its request schema from two `CHECK`s, and section 6 below writes it from them rather than from that prose.

> **Clause 3. `PATCH /me/leaderboard` and `GET /public/leaderboard` are REFUSED for now and are NAMED AS OWED. They do not land.**
>
> Not because they are out of scope: `M11` marks both `NEW` and `Owns` and finding 12 does not dispute that. **They are refused because neither can be typed truthfully today.** `GET /public/leaderboard`'s field set **is** `OQ-M11-03`'s answer (finding 12) and that question is open, so a shape written here would be this contract answering a founder question in a schema. `PATCH /me/leaderboard` is worse rather than better for having its two columns: finding 13 shows `INV-M11-10`'s reversibility clause requires removing historical entries from a table that does not exist, so the endpoint's own approved semantics cannot be honoured by the schema, and **the half that could be typed is the half that does nothing**. Admitting the opt-in write for a surface that does not exist gives a client a control with nothing behind it, which is [ADR-040](ADR-040.md)'s `PayoutListItem` lesson arriving through a verb instead of through a union.

**So of the four rows `ADR-168` left outstanding, TWO LAND AND TWO ARE NAMED AS OWED.** The count after this entry is that **`M11` section 4's six endpoints are four defined and two undefined**, and the two undefined are blocked on `OQ-M11-03` and on a table, not on a session.

### 3. WHAT A HELD CODE DISCLOSES HERE, AND IT IS NOT THE IMAGE'S SET

`ADR-168` ruled this question for `GET /certificates/:code/image.png` and its answer is the precedent: the claim is bounded by `INV-M11-01` to the account's plan, size, trading day and the kind-specific value, plus `INV-M11-04`'s disclosure, and **it carries no identity, no email, no display name, no cumulative total and no lifetime figure, so a held code names a result and does not name a person.**

**This endpoint discloses a STRICTLY LARGER set, in three named ways, and each one is forced by an invariant rather than chosen.**

| Added over the image | Forced by |
|---|---|
| The revocation half as **structured data**: `revoked.at` and `revoked.class` | `INV-M11-07` requires that *"the public text differs"* between *"the fact is not true"* and *"the account was later enforced"*. The image renders revocation as pixels; a verification result has to be branchable, and `certificate_verifications.result` already distinguishes `revoked` from `valid` in the log |
| The signature half: `signature`, `signing_key_id`, `claims_schema_version` | `INV-M11-02` makes an offline signature check *"a convenience for third parties"*, and this is the only row in the contract that serves it. `INV-M11-06` requires `signing_key_id` to travel with the certificate or the first rotation makes every historical signature unverifiable. `SD-M11-01`'s schema version is what lets a third party know which claim shape was signed |
| `issued_at` and `code` as fields rather than as printed text | The image prints both. A machine-readable surface that omitted them would force the reader back to the image, which is the surface `INV-M11-02` says is **not** the authority |

**And it withholds the same set, plus three this entry adds because they are this row's occasion to be named.**

| Withheld | Why |
|---|---|
| identity, email, display name, cumulative total, lifetime figure | `INV-M11-01`, unchanged from `ADR-168`. `AS-M11-07` and [M12](../plans/M12-transparency-platform.md) own aggregates and a certificate surface must never become an aggregate publisher |
| **`certificates.id`** | Finding 9. `id` is a *different key from `code` on purpose*, so the public token can be rotated after an incident. **Publishing `id` beside `code` would defeat the rotation**: a holder who kept the immutable key can still correlate the certificate after the token it was told to forget has changed. The public surface is addressed by the rotatable token and by nothing else |
| **`payout_request_id`** | It names a row in the payout book. `AS-M11-04` is the scenario about reconstructing that book, and a foreign key into it is the most direct version of the attack the endpoint's own invariants are written against |
| **`revoked_reason`** | `ADR-168` foreclosure 3, re-derived: [`certificates`](../architecture/data-model/certificates.md) types it **internal** free text, and finding 11's `CHECK` writes it beside the class, so a response carrying both would publish the internal half by the one route nobody audits. `INV-M11-07` and `AS-M11-05` |

**One sentence, and it is the whole disclosure posture: a held code names a result, and this row names it more precisely than the image does without naming anything more about a person.**

#### 3.1 THE DEFERRED CODE ANSWERS `unknown`, AND THAT IS WHY THE LOG'S UNION IS FOUR AND THE RESPONSE'S IS THREE

Finding 7: `certificate_verifications.result` admits `valid`, `unknown`, `revoked` and `deferred`. `ADR-168` read that as the corpus already accepting *"that a public lookup on a code can resolve to a deferral"*, and that reading is correct **about the log**. It is not a statement about the response, and this entry rules them apart.

> **`GET /verify/:code`'s response union is THREE members. A code whose row is deferred answers `unknown`, with `INV-M11-03`'s exact wording, indistinguishably from a code that resolves to no row at all.**

**Three reasons, and the third is the one that decides it.**

1. **Nobody legitimately holds a deferred code.** `ADR-168` clause: `code`, `verify_url` and `image_url` are `null` while the state is `deferred`, against a `NOT NULL` column, because *"a deferred certificate is a claim Merit has NOT made yet"*. The trader was never given the token. A lookup that hits a deferred row is therefore somebody holding a token they were not issued.
2. **The trader's own need is already served, authenticated.** `GET /certificates` ships `deferred: { reason, until }` to the caller whose certificate it is, scoped to their identity. **The public surface has no deferred audience to serve**, and an endpoint that discloses to nobody in particular discloses to everybody.
3. **`deferred` is a risk disclosure wearing a state name.** `INV-M11-09` defers issuance exactly when the account has an **open severity 4+ flag**. A public `deferred` answer therefore tells whoever holds the token that the account behind it is under risk review, and [M07](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md) does not publish detector detail. `ADR-168` named this in its own section 4 as the reason the deferral **copy** is unruled; this is the same hazard one layer out, where the leak is not the wording but the existence of the state.

**And the log keeps the fourth value precisely because the response does not have it.** The two incidents behind a deferred lookup and an unknown lookup are completely different, and only `certificate_verifications` can tell them apart:

| Response | `certificate_verifications.result` | What the operator is looking at |
|---|---|---|
| `valid` | `valid` | a verification |
| `revoked` | `revoked` | a verification of a card whose row has moved. `FM-M11-02`'s recovery path working |
| `unknown`, row is deferred | **`deferred`** | **somebody holds a token that was never issued.** An internal leak, or a 128-bit guess that hit. Either is an incident and neither is enumeration |
| `unknown`, no row | `unknown` | a typo, or `FM-M11-04` in progress. `AS-M11-04`'s signature is a high distinct-code rate with a near-total unknown rate |

> **The response's three-member union is an ALLOWLIST decision under section 1's response-shape policy, and it is NOT [ADR-040](ADR-040.md)'s defect.** `ADR-040`'s rule is that a union must not advertise a value the table cannot hold, because that gives a client a branch that never fires. Here the table **can** hold the value and the response deliberately declines to carry it, which is the opposite direction and is what an allowlist is for. Stated because the two are one word apart in a review and an implementer who read only the rule would "fix" the union to match the `CHECK`.

#### 3.2 `withheld` IS NOT TYPED, AND ON THIS SURFACE IT COSTS NOTHING

Finding 8, and `ADR-168`'s foreclosure 2 followed through to this row: a withheld certificate is one **Merit never made**, and `0020` has no column that distinguishes it from a row that does not exist. So it cannot appear in a union here, on `ADR-040`'s rule as `ADR-168` applied it to `CertificateListItem`.

**On the public surface that is the right answer anyway, and it is worth saying so rather than recording it as damage.** `M11` section 3.1: *"Withheld means Merit never made the claim."* The answer to a lookup on a claim Merit never made is `INV-M11-03`'s *"no certificate with this code"*, which is what the missing column produces by accident and what the invariant requires on purpose. **The column's absence costs nothing here and costs everything on the admin surface**, which is section 6.2 below.

### 4. THE CONSTANT-TIME CLAUSE REACHES THIS ENDPOINT, AND THE CACHE THAT WOULD BREAK IT DOES NOT

`ADR-168` recorded that `INV-M11-05`'s no-timing-difference clause does **not** reach the image endpoint, and its reasoning was that a render is orders of magnitude slower than a `404` and that `FM-M11-05`'s remedy separates a cached valid code from an uncached one **before an attacker asks about an invalid one**. Finding 5: the clause names **this** endpoint, in those words, so the same argument is not available and the mechanism has to be stated.

#### 4.1 The cache tension resolves by SCOPE, and the scoping is in `FM-M11-05`'s own text

Finding 6. `FM-M11-05` is a **render** failure mode, its blast radius is *"Cards do not render"*, and its remedy caches **rendered bytes**. Verify renders no bytes. **The remedy has no purchase on this row, and this entry forecloses extending it here.**

> **`GET /verify/:code` carries NO per-code response cache and NO `Cache-Control` measured in minutes. It is `no-store`.**

**And the second reason is stronger than the first.** The image endpoint's minutes-long cache is affordable because `INV-M11-08` re-renders from the live row and a stale card self-corrects within minutes. **The verify page is the recovery path for the card that did not self-correct** (`FM-M11-02`: *"The verify code inside the image is the recovery path"*, `AS-M11-02`). An intermediary holding a cached `valid` for a code that was revoked five minutes ago is the exact failure the whole revocation design routes around, arriving at the one surface that was supposed to be authoritative. **`no-store` is required by the revocation design before it is required by the clock**, and the two requirements happen to agree.

#### 4.2 The mechanism is a FLOOR, and "make the work equal" cannot be the mechanism

**Equalising the work does not honour the clause and it is important to say why, because it is the approach an implementer reaches for first.** Even with no application cache and a single indexed equality on `certificates_code_uq`, two valid codes differ: one row is in shared buffers and the other is not, one index page is warm and the other is cold. **The database's own cache reproduces `FM-M11-05`'s tension one layer down, where no scoping ruling can reach it.**

> **The response is emitted at a fixed time floor that dominates every path: a hit on a warm row, a hit on a cold one, a miss, and a syntactically invalid token alike. The floor is set above the p99 of the slowest of those, measured, and it is configuration rather than a number stated here.**

Four consequences, each stated because each is a place the mechanism is quietly lost:

1. **A malformed code is NOT a validation error.** `GET /verify/:code` **has no `validation_failed`.** A token of the wrong length or alphabet answers `unknown`, identically and at the same floor. A shape check that short-circuits ahead of the lookup is a faster path, and it would additionally hand an attacker the token's alphabet and length for free, which is the non-enumerability half of the same invariant rather than the timing half.
2. **`certificate_verifications` is written on EVERY path**, including `unknown` and including the malformed token, whose `code_hash` is the hash of whatever arrived. `SD-M11-04` requires it and finding 7's reserved-sequence header says the table exists for this endpoint. **The write is inside the floor's budget**, so an insert that is the same work on every path costs nothing observable, and the anomaly detector sees the whole population rather than the resolvable part of it.
3. **A floor set from a p50 is worse than no floor**, because it advertises compliance while leaking on exactly the tail an attacker samples. The floor is a measured p99 ceiling or the clause is not honoured, and a slice that ships this endpoint owes the measurement rather than the number.
4. **It costs a legitimate holder real latency and that is the trade being made.** `AS-M11-04`'s first counter is that the code space is not walkable, and the clock is the second line rather than the first.

#### 4.3 THE SIZE CHANNEL IS OPEN, IT IS NAMED, AND IT IS NOT A DEFECT

**The response body for `unknown` is smaller than the body for `valid`, and no padding of the clock closes that.** Named rather than left for a reviewer to find, and then argued down on evidence rather than closed:

`INV-M11-05` names timing. Closing the size channel would mean padding every response to a fixed length, which no approved document asks for, and **it would buy nothing**: the size only distinguishes a hit from a miss for a caller who has already sent a code, and a caller who has already sent a code learns the same thing from the body itself. The channel is redundant with the answer. **It matters only to an attacker who has already guessed a 128-bit token, at which point the endpoint is answering them honestly by design.** `ADR-168`'s warning applies in this direction too: an implementer who read `INV-M11-05` as covering every observable would chase this one forever.

### 5. WHAT MAKES ENUMERATION ACCEPTABLE, AND EVERY ITEM IS A CONTROL THAT ALREADY EXISTS

`AS-M11-04` is written about this endpoint and its first counter carries the weight: **the attack is infeasible rather than merely rate limited.**

| Control | Where it already is |
|---|---|
| **128 bits of entropy, no sequence, no structure** | `INV-M11-05`. `SD-M11-01`'s `code` is *"the short unguessable token"*, `NOT NULL` under `certificates_code_uq` in `0020`. **Possession of a code is evidence that somebody was given it**, which is the premise sections 3 and 4 both rest on |
| **Rate limited, per IP and per ASN** | `AS-M11-04` counter 3, finding 15. Section 11 gains a row and the limits are **data rather than prose**, on the `POST /auth/otp` sms row's precedent and the image row's. **Per ASN and not per `code`**, which is where this row and the image row differ: the image serves one hot card to many viewers legitimately, and a verification of one code by thousands of distinct sources is `AS-M11-04`'s signature rather than a viral card |
| **Instrumented, and the instrument is a table rather than an edge counter** | `SD-M11-04`, finding 7. Every lookup writes `certificate_verifications` with hashed inputs only and 90 day retention, and section 3.1's four-way `result` mapping is what makes the log say more than the response does. `certificate.verify_anomaly` fires on the distinct-code and unknown-rate signature |
| **Minimal claims, so a successful harvest yields facts about accounts and never about people** | `INV-M11-01`, `AS-M11-07`. Section 3's withheld table is this control written as a schema |
| **A trader who wants it stopped can have it stopped** | `trader_request` is a member of `certificates.revocation_class`'s `CHECK` in `0020`, and clause 2's endpoint is how it is exercised. **A revoked row answers `revoked` on the next lookup with no cache in the way** (section 4.1), so the stop is immediate rather than eventual |

### 6. THE ROWS, WRITTEN FROM THE `CHECK`s

[ADR-158](ADR-158.md)'s method and `ADR-168`'s: every row below is derived from a constraint or a `CHECK` rather than from the prose that describes it, and both entries found real contradictions doing it.

#### 6.1 `GET /verify/:code`

Auth **none**. `result` is the three-member union of section 3.1. `revoked.class` is `certificates.revocation_class`'s `CHECK`, in the `CHECK`'s order. `statement` is the **only** user-visible sentence the endpoint produces and it is rendered server side, because `INV-M11-03` fixes the unknown wording and `INV-M11-07` fixes that the class drives the revoked one: **a client handed only the class would compose its own sentence, and the second client to do it would compose a different one, which is precisely the inconsistency `INV-M11-07` exists to prevent.** The class ships beside it for branching and never for copy. **`statement`'s `account_enforced` text is `OQ-M11-02` and is owed** (finding 14); the field is typed and the copy is not this entry's.

**A revoked certificate still returns its claims, on all four classes.** `AS-M11-05`: for `account_enforced` *"the claim stands and the account was later closed under a named ToS clause ... It does not say the certificate is invalid, because it is not"*. A page that withheld the claim on revocation would be the retroactive denial that scenario exists to make impossible, and it would do it to `fact_untrue` as well, where the holder already has a card printed with the claim and would be left comparing it against a blank page. **One shape on all three results also means the response's SHAPE never discloses more than its `result` field already does.**

#### 6.2 `POST /admin/certificates/:id/revoke`

**`:id` is `certificates.id`, the uuid, and NOT the public code, and the column decides it rather than a convention.** `admin_actions.subject_id` is `uuid NOT NULL` (finding 10), so an audit row keyed on the public token **could not be written at all**; and finding 9 makes `code` rotatable after an incident, so an audit trail keyed on it is an audit trail that loses its subject at exactly the moment the subject matters. Section 1's rule that admin surfaces answer `403` rather than `404` is unchanged: existence is not a secret from an authorized operator.

**The request carries two fields and NOT three, and the third is derived because the schema will not let it be supplied.** `admin_actions.initiative` is `NOT NULL` with no default over `('enforcement','trader_request','operational')`, and `admin_actions_on_behalf_matches_initiative` makes `on_behalf_of_identity_id` non-null **exactly** when the initiative is `trader_request`. `certificates.revocation_class`'s four members map onto that vocabulary totally:

| `revocation_class` | `admin_actions.initiative` | `on_behalf_of_identity_id` | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fact_untrue` | `operational` | null | `FM-M11-01`: Merit's own signed proof asserted something false. **Merit correcting Merit**, and no act against the trader |
| `account_enforced` | `enforcement` | null | `AS-M11-05`, `M07`. The one class that follows an act against the trader |
| `issued_in_error` | `operational` | null | *"a system fault, reversible"* |
| `trader_request` | `trader_request` | **`certificates.identity_id`** | The word is the same word in both vocabularies, and the biconditional then **requires** the identity. It is on the row already |

> **So `initiative` is NOT a request field.** An endpoint that took it would let an operator record an enforcement as something the trader asked for, or a trader's own withdrawal as an enforcement, and that is exactly the misattribution `admin_actions_on_behalf_matches_initiative` was written to prevent, arriving through a request body instead of through a column. **The class the operator states is the only thing they get to state, and everything else the audit row needs is derived from it and from the certificate.**

**One `reason` string writes two columns.** `admin_actions.reason` is `text NOT NULL` (*"no unexplained admin action, ever"*) and `certificates_revocation_is_complete` requires `revoked_reason` beside the class. Both are the internal half; neither reaches a trader surface.

**The response is the PUBLIC shape, and that is the control rather than a convenience.** It returns exactly what `GET /verify/:code` will return for that code, with `result: "revoked"`. **An operator cannot revoke without being shown the sentence they caused**, which is `AS-M11-05`'s whole concern rendered at the moment of the act rather than discovered afterwards on a public page.

**Re-revocation is permitted and is not a `conflict`.** Finding 11's `CHECK` permits overwriting the triple, correcting a misclassified revocation is a real operation, and `admin_actions`' `before` and `after` are what distinguish a correction from a replay. No new error code is minted for a state the table does not refuse.

#### 6.3 A DEFERRED CERTIFICATE CANNOT BE REVOKED, AND THAT LEAVES A REAL HOLE THIS ENTRY NAMES RATHER THAN FILLS

> **`POST /admin/certificates/:id/revoke` refuses a certificate whose `deferred_until` is set, with `validation_failed`.**

Finding 16: `M11` section 3.1 draws `issued --> revoked` and `deferred --> withheld` and **draws no `deferred --> revoked` edge**. The table permits the write, so the refusal is the endpoint's and not the schema's, and it is stated here so it is not discovered as a bug.

**The alternative reading was checked rather than dismissed, and it fails on `M11`'s own text.** *Encode `withheld` as deferred-plus-revoked, with class `account_enforced`* is coherent against every `CHECK` in `0020`. It is refuted by `AS-M11-05`, which fixes `account_enforced` as **"the claim stands** and the account was later closed"; a withheld certificate is one *"Merit never made"*, so publishing "the claim stands" for a claim that was never made is exactly backwards. `M11` section 3.1 says the two states are different *"on purpose"* and that a system with only `revoked` *"would have to publish and then retract, which is strictly worse for both parties"*.

> **The consequence, stated plainly: THERE IS TODAY NO ENDPOINT AND NO COLUMN BY WHICH AN ENFORCEMENT REACHES A DEFERRED CERTIFICATE.** `withheld`'s missing column costs nothing on the public surface (section 3.2) and costs the whole transition here. It is **owed to the slice that gives `withheld` a column**, and it is a migration, which is not this fence.

**And the reverse edge is owed too.** `revoked --> issued: revocation reversed (issued_in_error corrected)` is drawn in section 3.1, is permitted by finding 11's `CHECK`, and `M11` section 4 lists no endpoint for it. **Named as owed. This entry does not mint one**, because an un-revoke is a publication decision and `M11` never asked for the route.
