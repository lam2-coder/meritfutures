## ADR-174: `LT-07` is not a posting with one bad leg, it is a posting whose two legs are on the wrong sides, so no code is minted and `0052` returns to the pool unspent  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**The reservation asked whether `LT-07`'s credit leg needs an eighth account class. It does not, and the reason is that the credit slot is not the row's only defect.** [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1 writes the external leg as two postings against `firm_treasury`, `LT-06` crediting it `amount_cents` and `LT-07` debiting it, and [`posting.ts:235-236`](../../packages/ledger/src/posting.ts) applies `+amountCents` on a debit and `-amountCents` on a credit. **So over the whole external leg the two postings move `firm_treasury` by ZERO**, whatever its kind, on the one account [M05:141](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) names as where a cash movement books, in the leg whose own row calls it *"The external leg's cash movement"*. **A code substituted into the credit slot completes a posting that is wrong in the other slot**, so this entry mints none, and migration `0052` is RETURNED to the pool unspent.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `174`, session **315**, dispatched as `claude/lt07-credit-leg`. The three findings were found by `packages/rail` ([#411](https://github.com/lam2-coder/meritfutures/pull/411)) and are **re-derived here at source and then executed against a running database**, not inherited from that merge or from the reservation. **The reservation held findings (A) and (B) and left the split to the entry: they are TWO rulings and (B) takes [ADR-175](ADR-175.md)**, on the ground argued in section 5. **(C) is recorded as an ABSENCE in section 4 and no direction is inferred from it.**

- **MONEY PATH.** The subject is which accounts a cash movement touches and in which direction. **No migration lands, so no [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is owed on one**; the reservation said `OWED IF A MIGRATION LANDS` and none does. `0009`, `0027` and `0038` are byte for byte unchanged.

- **This entry rules what `LT-07` is NOT and refuses to write what it IS**, which is [ADR-124](ADR-124.md)'s shape one table over: that entry ruled the global halt's shape and refused to design its storage, on the ground that the storage is a migration and a ruling does not write one. **What blocks the repair is named exactly in section 3 rather than left to be rediscovered.**

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. NO CODE IS MINTED AND `0052` IS RETURNED TO THE POOL UNSPENT.** The reservation made the number conditional on the ruling minting a code and it does not. `0009` and `0027` are unchanged, the vocabulary stays closed at seven, and the migration sequence gains no hole: [ADR-167](ADR-167.md) returned `0051` unspent and [ADR-169](ADR-169.md) re-took it the same afternoon, so the pool is the mechanism rather than the exception.
>
> **2. THE ROW IS NOT REPAIRABLE BY NAMING A CODE, and that is this entry's finding rather than its refusal.** By finding 6 the external leg moves `firm_treasury` by zero. Filling the credit slot with any of the seven, or with an eighth minted for the purpose, leaves that measurement exactly where it is, because the measurement reads the DEBIT slot. **`LT-07` as written derecognizes no cash**, and the batch 1 gate ruled that cash derecognizes at settlement.
>
> **3. `trader_wallet` IS REFUSED, ON ARITHMETIC RATHER THAN ON [ADR-124](ADR-124.md).** Under `SD-M5-07`, `LT-06` already debits `trader_wallet` by `amount_cents` at approval. An `LT-07` crediting `trader_wallet` restores it, so the trader's wallet would end an external withdrawal holding exactly what it held before it, and the money would have left the firm. **That refusal needs no `kind` and no scope argument**, which matters because finding 11 shows [ADR-124](ADR-124.md)'s version of this refusal quotes the row it is being used to repair. **`LT-07` stays firm-only and [ADR-124](ADR-124.md) clause 3 is not amended**; what is recorded is that a later session repairing this row may not read that clause as evidence about the row.
>
> **4. THE EXTERNAL LEG NEEDS A THIRD ACCOUNT ROLE AND THE CHART DOES NOT SAY WHICH CODE HOLDS IT.** [STATE_MACHINES section 3.2](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) draws `approved --> transferring --> settled` and [M20:156](../plans/M20-wallet.md) rules that at a halt *"`LT-06` debited the wallet at approval, so the halt is holding a transfer, not a claim"*. **So an obligation exists between the two moments and must be recorded somewhere**: the wallet no longer holds it and the cash has not left. The pooled class that used to hold exactly this was retired by `SD-M5-07` for `LT-01`, which had no such gap because [M05 section 3.1](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) makes the internal leg one transaction. **The repair is therefore a decision about which account carries an in-flight obligation, and it is section 3's, not this entry's.**
>
> **5. NO DOCUMENT IS AMENDED.** [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1's `LT-07` row stands as written, because rewriting half a defective row is how a reader stops being able to see that it was defective. The row is now cited by an entry that says what is wrong with both of its legs, which is what a ruling on an approved document's defect can do without designing the replacement.

### 3. What this entry deliberately does NOT rule, and it is a short list on purpose

> **Which account carries the external leg's in-flight obligation, and therefore what `LT-07`'s two legs actually are.**

Three shapes are available and **each one needs an answer to finding (C)**, which section 4 records as an absence:

| Shape | What it costs | Why this entry cannot take it |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) An eighth firm class for the in-flight obligation** | Supersedes [`0009`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql)'s CHECK and [`0027`](../../packages/db/migrations/0027_triggers_invariants.sql)'s `LEDGER-C2`, and adds a fourth statement to [`accounts.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/accounts.ts). Both refusals were EXECUTED in findings 2 and 3 | **The migration must write a `kind` literal for the new class**, and the new class balances against `firm_treasury`, so its `kind` is not writable while `firm_treasury`'s is unknown |
| **(ii) `LT-06` and `LT-07` collapse to ONE posting at settlement** | Contradicts [M20:156](../plans/M20-wallet.md) (*"`LT-06` already posted"*) and [STATE_MACHINES section 3.2](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)'s three-state rail | It rules on `LT-06`, which is outside this entry's fence, and it would delete a posting an approved plan and an approved drawing both rely on |
| **(iii) `psp_clearing` or `reserve` carries it** | No migration at all, which makes it the cheapest and the easiest to get wrong | **Neither code's meaning nor its `kind` is written in any file in this tree**, which is finding (C) widened to the codes beside `firm_treasury` |

**Naming the interlock is the useful half of this entry.** `(A)` cannot be closed while `(C)` is open, and `(C)` is an absence that no file in this tree can fill, so the next session on this row is one that first rules the chart of accounts and only then rules the posting. **That is the opposite order from the one the reservation anticipated**, and saying so is cheaper than a migration written in the anticipated order.

### 4. Finding (C), recorded as an ABSENCE, with no direction inferred from it

**No file in this tree states a `kind` for `firm_treasury`, and the absence is larger than one code.** Findings 8, 9 and 10 are the measurement: nothing seeds the chart, the schema accepts three different kinds for the same code, and the one table that carries a balance against an `account_code` is deliberately anchored outside the ledger and carries no key into it.

**The only `kind` literals anywhere in this tree are three fixtures inside a probe.** [`probe_ledger_constraints.sql`](../../scripts/db/probe_ledger_constraints.sql) seeds `trader_withdrawable` and `trader_wallet` as `liability` and `fees_revenue` as `revenue`, and its purpose is to watch `LEDGER-C1`, `LEDGER-C2` and zero-sum fire. **A fixture chosen to make a constraint reject something is not a statement of the chart**, and reading it as one is exactly the *"A class appearing first in a migration is a class nobody defined"* failure [`0009:46`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) exists to name. **So four of the seven codes have no `kind` written anywhere at all**: `firm_treasury`, `psp_clearing`, `reserve` and `promotional_credit`.

**Finding 7 is evidence and it is deliberately not treated as a derivation.** Five documents call `firm_treasury` the account where a cash movement books. Cash is an asset in every accounting convention, and this entry still does not write `asset`, for two reasons. **The first is that the corpus's own arithmetic disagrees with it**: `LT-06` credits `firm_treasury` at approval, and if the account is cash then the external leg derecognizes cash at approval, which is the timing [ADR-027](ADR-027.md), [ADR-033](ADR-033.md), [ADR-067](ADR-067.md) and [`0038:48-49`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) each refused on `LT-01`. **The second is that nothing in this session's fence needs the answer**, because clauses 2 and 3 hold for every kind. **An entry that rules a fact it does not need, from evidence that contradicts a row it is not repairing, is guessing with a citation attached.**

**`LT-06`'s row is READ here as evidence and is RULED on nowhere.** Findings 6 and this section both read it, and the reservation's fence is `LT-07`. What is recorded is that the two rows cannot both be right, and that whoever rules the chart owes a reading of `LT-06` in the same entry.

### 5. Why (A) and (B) are TWO rulings

The reservation left the split to be argued. **They are two, and the argument is that they live in two tables and one of them is blocked while the other is not.**

- **(A) is about `ledger_accounts` and `ledger_entries`**: which account a leg names and which side it sits on. It is BLOCKED by section 4's absence and this entry says so.
- **(B) is about `ledger_transactions`**: the value of one `text NOT NULL UNIQUE` column on the header row. **A transaction's key is decided by which event it records and against which reference, and both are columns on the header** (`kind`, `reference_kind`, `reference_id`), so the key is decidable with the entries still undecided. [ADR-175](ADR-175.md) rules it and is usable the day it lands.

**Folding (B) into this entry would file a convention that governs every posting in the system inside an entry whose own ruling is a refusal**, and a reader arriving at `LT-02` or `LT-08` would have to know that `LT-07`'s entry is where their key is spelled. [ADR-124](ADR-124.md) section 5 names that hazard in its own words: a finding recorded and owned by nobody is on its way to being treated as handled. **[ADR-172](ADR-172.md) is the precedent the reservation itself cites**, where a two-finding reservation was split and the split was right.

### 6. Alternatives considered

- **Mint the eighth class now and call it the in-flight payout obligation.** **Rejected on section 3's interlock rather than on cost.** The migration's first line is a `kind` literal for a class that must balance against `firm_treasury`, and finding 9 EXECUTED the fact that the database will accept whichever literal is typed. A guessed `kind` on a merged money-path migration is unfixable by definition: [E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) makes a merged migration superseded and never edited, so the wrong guess survives as a row every later migration has to work around. **`0038` clause 4 is the standing precedent and it refused an eighth class on a smaller question than this one.**
- **Substitute `trader_wallet` and record that [ADR-124](ADR-124.md) clause 3 was written on a stale phrase.** **Rejected by clause 3 on arithmetic**, which is a stronger refusal than the scope argument and does not require reopening [ADR-124](ADR-124.md). The stale-phrase observation is still recorded, as finding 11, because the next session will otherwise read clause 3 as a constraint on the repair.
- **Rule `firm_treasury` an asset from finding 7's five sites, and derive both legs from that.** **Rejected in section 4.** It is the most tempting option in this entry: it would close (A), (C) and the fence in one move. It closes them by writing the one fact every document in the chain declined to write, against arithmetic in `LT-06` that contradicts it, on an entry whose fence is `LT-07`.
- **Amend [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1's `LT-07` row to name the debit and credit correctly and leave the account open.** **Rejected by clause 5.** The row cannot be half-corrected without naming what the credit account is, and a row reading *"debit the in-flight obligation; credit `firm_treasury`"* would state a design this entry has just refused to take.
- **Report all three findings again and rule nothing.** **Rejected, and it is what session 314 already did correctly.** The findings were reported once, with their sources, by a session that had no number. This one has two. Reporting them a second time would make [ADR-124](ADR-124.md) section 5's own hazard concrete: five documents each stating half of one thing, and now six.

### 7. Consequences

- **`0052` is free and is the next migration number.** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `174` is amended in place, on [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3 which `CI-06f` enforces, rather than joined by a second row. `packages/db/test/migrations.integration.test.ts` asserts the on-disk sequence rather than a high-water mark, so a later session may take it.
- **`LT-07` is still unpostable, and the reason recorded against it is now different and smaller.** Before this entry it was three findings pointing at three files. After it, it is one open question: which account carries the external leg's in-flight obligation, and what is `firm_treasury`'s `kind`.
- **[ADR-124](ADR-124.md) clause 3 keeps its conclusion and loses its stated ground**, and finding 11 is where a later session reads that rather than rediscovering it.
- **The chart of accounts has never been seeded and that is now written down.** Finding 8 is the largest thing this session measured and it was not in the dispatch: `ledger_accounts` is empty after every merged migration, so every `kind` in this system is a value some future seed will type for the first time.
- **[`packages/rail`](../../packages/rail/src/settlement.ts)'s `LT_07_FINDINGS` are updated to say they are RULED rather than merely reported**, and `lt-07.test.ts` gains the assertions that make this entry's ruling go red if `0009`'s vocabulary widens or `0052` appears. The three original cases are unchanged and still pass, because no primary source they read is amended.

### 8. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.** **MONEY PATH.** No migration lands and no code posts, so the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is owed on the diff that eventually repairs the row and is not discharged here.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Three of the decisions here are judgements rather than derivations. **That the interlock is real** (section 3) is the load-bearing one: this entry says (A) cannot be closed until (C) is answered, and the coherent opposite is that finding 7's five sites are enough to write `asset`, close (C) by ruling rather than by derivation exactly as the dispatch permits, and close (A) behind it in the same wave. **This entry refuses that on the ground that `LT-06`'s own row contradicts it**, and a founder may read that contradiction as `LT-06` being the defective row rather than as evidence that `firm_treasury` is not cash. **That `trader_wallet` is refused on arithmetic and not on scope** (clause 3) leaves [ADR-124](ADR-124.md) clause 3 standing with a ground this entry has just called a citation of the defect, which is a state a founder may prefer to resolve by amending that entry instead. **That no document is amended** (clause 5) leaves [M05:136](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) reading, in an `approved` plan, a posting that this entry has proved moves no cash; the alternative is to strike the row, and striking it deletes the evidence that the corpus wrote it.
