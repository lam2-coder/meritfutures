## ADR-316: the withdrawal approval sweep's port, and `decideApproval` does not move. The lock the driver needs is already the one `INV-M20-01` names and both authorities can take it, what the driver shares with the route is seven terms of eight, and the event registry names `apps/api` as the producer of an edge that deployable cannot drive  (2026-09-04, status: proposed)

**Row `316` is [ADR-305](ADR-305.md) section 7 slice 5, and it owes two things: the sweep's port, and where `decideApproval` lives or a written refusal to move it with the duplication cost stated.** Both are below. **The move is REFUSED and section 6 states the cost in full.**

**THE REFUSAL IS NOT [ADR-286](ADR-286.md)'s REFUSAL AND THE DIFFERENCE IS MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSERTED.** That entry refused a home for a decoder **both** deployables needed. `decideApproval` ([`:1728`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is needed by **one**, and it is not the one that holds it: `grep -rn decideApproval apps/api/src` returns five lines, of which one is the declaration, one is the single call inside `driveApprovals` ([`:1801`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)), and three are docblock references. `grep -rn driveApprovals apps/api/src` returns three lines and **no fourth line is a caller**. **So the whole approval-decision half of `apps/api` is reachable from its own suite and from nothing else**, and the question row `316` was dispatched to answer is not "where do two deployables share this" but "why is it here at all".

**AND THE SECOND MEASUREMENT IS WHY THE ANSWER IS STILL NOT A MOVE.** `decideApproval`'s last term is dual control, `dualControlRequired` ([`:1708`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) returns `false` for a null hand on its first line, and **a sweep has no hand**. The driver reaches seven of that function's eight terms and can never reach the eighth. The eighth is the one that binds `DUAL_CONTROL_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, the constant `admin-wallet.ts` owns, and it is the one two live gates read this file for.

### 2. The lock is already shared, and that is what makes the port buildable

**[ADR-305](ADR-305.md) section 4's finding is that a clock has no identity, and the open question it leaves is what a sweep takes INSTEAD of `lockScope`.** The answer is that it takes the same lock through a different door, and this was not obvious before it was measured.

`lockScope` ([`:947`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is declared on `WithdrawalTx` under a docblock that says **no method takes an identity** ([`:934`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)). Underneath it, `packages/db` implements it as a row lock on **`identities`**, not as an advisory lock: [`scoped-db.ts:3912`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) renders `scopePredicate('identities', identityId)` under `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, and the block above it at [`scoped-db.ts:1302`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) records the choice in terms, that `pg_advisory_xact_lock(bigint)` is what the invariants' prose names and that there is no way to send it that is not `sqlExecutor`.

**`SystemTx` HAS THE SAME LOCK AND IT IS THERE ON PURPOSE.** `lockAt` ([`:3729`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) is declared on that handle under a docblock whose whole reason is that `INV-M5-07`'s lock is shared with the nightly batch, *"so the authority the batch runs at has to be able to take the same one"*. Its implementation at [`scoped-db.ts:3981`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) is the identical `lockingSelectStatement` over `unscopedAddressPredicate`.

**SO `systemTx.lockAt('identities', { id })` AND `scopedTx.lockScope()` LOCK THE SAME ROW AND CONTEND WITH EACH OTHER.** [`M20:57`](../plans/M20-wallet.md) states `INV-M20-01` as *"Ledger constraint plus a per-identity advisory lock"*, and the estate implements it as a row lock for the reason quoted above. A sweep at `systemDb('nightly-batch')`, which is the only authority `apps/worker` has (`WORKER_REASON` at [`db.ts:112`](../../apps/worker/src/db.ts)), can take `INV-M20-01`'s per-identity lock **without a new accessor door, without a new `SystemReason` member and without a raw-SQL reach-around**.

**THIS IS THE FINDING THAT SIZES THE PORT DOWN.** A session that read `lockScope`'s docblock alone would conclude that the sweep needs a lock primitive the estate does not have, and would either widen `sqlExecutor` or invent one. It needs neither. **The lock is the one line of the port that needed a ruling and it turned out to need nothing.**

### 3. THE RULING, part 1: the sweep's port

**A NEW MODULE AND NOT A GROWTH OF `sweeps/ports.ts`, ON THAT FILE'S OWN ARGUMENT.** `EXPIRY_TABLES` ([`ports.ts:83`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts)) is a two-member union declared narrow for a stated reason, and that file's header rules that *"a sweep is not a batch step and folding it in would give the hourly job the nightly job's dependency graph"*. The identical sentence holds one step down: the hourly halt-release job needs two tables and a clock, the approval driver needs five tables, a ledger and the wallet's own statement, and folding the second into the first would make `EXPIRY_TABLES` carry three tables that job never touches.

**The module is `apps/worker/src/withdrawals/ports.ts` with the job beside it.** The directory name is the one judgement in this section; everything else below is derived. A slice that places it elsewhere states a reason and this row does not bind it.

#### 3.1 The tables, as a closed union

```
export const APPROVAL_TABLES = [
  'identities',
  'walletWithdrawals',
  'walletEntries',
  'payoutDestinations',
  'kycVerifications',
] as const;
```

Every member is a real Drizzle property name: `identities` at [`schema.ts:290`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), `walletWithdrawals` at [`schema.ts:2939`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), `walletEntries` at [`schema.ts:2897`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), `kycVerifications` at [`schema.ts:1767`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), `payoutDestinations` at [`schema.ts:5060`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts). **The suite asserts that, which is the half the port file cannot assert itself**, exactly as `sweeps/ports.ts` says of its own two.

**`ledgerTransactions` and `ledgerEntries` are absent DELIBERATELY**, transcribing `EXPIRY_TABLES`' own exclusion: the posting does not go through this handle, it goes through the ledger port in 3.3, **so nothing here can write a ledger row by naming a key**.

**`adminActions` is absent and its absence is the machine arm.** A sweep is nobody's decision and has no actor to record. That is `ExpiryTx`'s stated reason for having no `insert` at all, and it survives here for the audit table while **failing for `walletEntries`**, which is why this port has an `insert` and that one does not: this job's whole purpose is to move money, and the wallet's own statement is where a movement is recorded.

#### 3.2 The handle

```
export interface ApprovalTx {
  rowsWhere(key: ApprovalTable, where: ApprovalFilter): Promise<unknown[]>;
  rowAt(key: ApprovalTable, at: ApprovalFilter): Promise<unknown>;
  lockAt(key: ApprovalTable, at: ApprovalFilter): Promise<unknown>;
  insert(key: ApprovalTable, values: ApprovalValues): Promise<unknown[]>;
  updateAt(key: ApprovalTable, at: ApprovalFilter, values: ApprovalValues): Promise<unknown[]>;

```

**Structurally declared and importing nothing**, which is `sweeps/ports.ts`'s idiom and now its **requirement** rather than its accident: [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) rules one door and one acquisition point, and `grep -rlE "from '@merit/db'" apps/worker/src` must print `apps/worker/src/db.ts` and nothing else. `SystemTx` is assignable to this shape with no import in either direction.

**THERE IS NO `terms` MEMBER AND NONE IS REACHED FOR.** `ExpiryTerms` ([`ports.ts:125`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts)) exists because the expiry sweep filters on a clock. This job filters on `status`, which is an equality, and it evaluates the cooling clock **in memory** because the decision has to be able to say `destination_cooling` rather than return one row fewer. **A read that filtered the cooling window in SQL would delete a hold reason**, which is the shape of error `wallet.ts` and `wallet-withdrawals.ts` both record as their own: the rows crossing the boundary are the window's rather than the match's.

**THERE IS NO `deleteAt`.** An approval destroys nothing.

#### 3.3 The ledger and the events

```
export interface ApprovalLedgerPort {
  postLt06(tx: ApprovalTx, facts: WalletWithdrawalFacts): Promise<void>;
}
```

**SUPPLIED BY THE WIRING AND NOT BUILT HERE**, which is `ExpiryLedgerPort` ([`ports.ts:229`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts)) and its stated reason, and that reason is stronger here than there: the module refused a second transcription of `LT-01`'s split at [`ports.ts:215-220`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts), and `LT-06` is a **single** transfer, debit `trader_wallet` credit `withdrawals_in_flight` ([`reversal.ts:218`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts)), which is exactly the shape a session would be tempted to inline. **This port names no ledger account and contains no ledger arithmetic.**

**Its argument type is `packages/ledger`'s own.** `WalletWithdrawalFacts` ([`reversal.ts:184`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts)) already carries the four fields, and its `withdrawalIdempotencyKey` docblock already anticipates this caller in terms: `LT-06` posts under the row's own bare key *"because the approval edge is reachable from a sweep and an operator console as well as from a route"*. **The port takes that type structurally rather than restating it**, and the day slice 1 exports the builder the adapter is one line.

```
export interface ApprovalEventPort {
  emit(tx: ApprovalTx, event: ApprovalEvent): Promise<void>;
}
```

**It takes the transaction**, on `ExpiryEventPort`'s reason relied on rather than restated: the event commits with the approval or not at all. **The names are the registry's and none is invented**: `wallet.withdrawal_approved` and `wallet.debited` are both catalogued rows ([`EVENTS.md:301`](../architecture/EVENTS.md), [`EVENTS.md:285`](../architecture/EVENTS.md)). **Both name `apps/api` as the producer and section 8 finding 1 is that they are now wrong.**

#### 3.4 The job's outside world

```

  readonly ledger: ApprovalLedgerPort;

```

`transact` takes the unit of work rather than handing back a handle, which is `ExpirySweepIo` ([`ports.ts:290`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts)) and `AdminPayoutBackend.operator`'s shape and their reason. `now` is injected and is the only clock in the job. **The unwired default refuses every call** and the message says what the value it would have to invent is, which here is whether a trader's wallet claim was extinguished.

#### 3.5 THE ORDERING, WHICH IS THE WHOLE OF THE CONCURRENCY CONTROL

**`transact` IS CALLED ONCE PER IDENTITY, PLUS ONCE FOR THE SCAN. THE SWEEP IS NOT ONE TRANSACTION AND THIS IS THE RULING, NOT A PREFERENCE.**

1. **The scan. One transaction, no lock, read only.** `rowsWhere('walletWithdrawals', { status: 'requested' })` and `rowsWhere('walletWithdrawals', { status: 'cooling' })`, **two reads and not one**, because `APPROVABLE_STATUSES` ([`:1617`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is two values, an `IN` is an `OR`, and an `OR` is one of the two terms [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) still refuses. Both reads are served by `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx`, whose leading column is `status` ([`0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql:191`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql)). **The scan produces identity ids and nothing else is carried out of it.**
2. **Per identity, one transaction.** `lockAt('identities', { id })` FIRST, per section 2. Then **re-read that identity's candidates under the lock**, because the scan's rows were read without one and `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx` is not unique ([ADR-158](ADR-158.md) finding 8, quoted at [`:346`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)), so a row the scan saw as `requested` may have been cancelled by its own trader in between. **A decision made on a scanned row is a decision made outside the lock, which is the whole defect the lock exists to prevent.**
3. Then the identity row, the verification chain, the destination, the position. Then decide. Then write the status. Then post `LT-06`. Then the wallet entry. Then emit. **Then commit, which is when the lock is released**, because a row lock is released at `COMMIT` and at nothing earlier.

**ONE TRANSACTION FOR THE WHOLE SWEEP IS REFUSED AND THE REASON IS NOT TIDINESS.** The lock is held to commit, so a sweep-wide transaction holds **every** scanned identity's `identities` row until the run ends. `lockScope` is the first thing the request handler does, so every trader opening a withdrawal, and every door that takes that lock for any reason, blocks behind the batch. **And one failure would roll back every identity's posting**, turning a single bad row into a night with no approvals at all.

**THE DESTINATION READ IS ADDRESSED BY TWO COLUMNS AND NOT ONE**, which is the one place a system-authority port cannot copy `WithdrawalTx` verbatim. `payout_destinations`' primary key is `(identity_id, destination_ref)` ([`schema.ts:5081`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts)). `WithdrawalTx.destination(destinationRef)` takes one argument because the accessor supplies the identity; **this handle has no scope, so the identity is an explicit conjunct and a port that forgot it would read another identity's cooling window.**

**THE POSITION IS READ UNDER THE LOCK AND THE DECISION CARRIES A TERM FOR IT. `decideApproval` HAS NO SUCH TERM.** [`M20:57`](../plans/M20-wallet.md) requires every debit checked against the live position inside the same transaction, [`M20:156`](../plans/M20-wallet.md) states that *"LT-06 debited the wallet at approval"*, and `APPROVAL_HOLDS` ([`:1602`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) has no member naming an insufficient position. **The function is sound as written, because `driveApprovals` posts nothing and therefore debits nothing**, and it is not sufficient for a driver that does. `insufficient_funds` ([`:1321`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is a request-time HTTP refusal and is not this; `balanceOf` ([`:652`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is the fold, over rows the port must supply.

**THE HOLD NAME AND THE TRADER-FACING OUTCOME ARE NOT MINTED HERE.** The recommendation is that a short position is a **hold** rather than a cancellation, on the ground that money the trader spent between request and approval is not Merit's decision to close their withdrawal over, and that the row stays open and retries. **It is a recommendation and not a ruling**, because a hold that never clears sits inside `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT` forever and the counter-argument is real. The name lands in `src/`, which is outside this row's fence.

### 6. THE RULING, part 2: the move is REFUSED, and the cost

**`decideApproval` STAYS AT [`wallet-withdrawals.ts:1728`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts). The driver states its own machine-arm decision in `apps/worker`. No new package is created and nothing is moved into `packages/`.**

**FOUR REASONS, AND THE FOURTH IS THE ONE THAT DECIDED IT.**

1. **THERE IS NO SHARING TO ARRANGE TODAY.** One deployable needs the predicate and the other has no caller for it. A shared home for a function with one live caller is a package built for a second reader who does not exist, which is the shape [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) refused under a different name.
2. **THE TWO ARMS ARE NOT ONE FUNCTION.** Seven terms of eight are common, the eighth is unreachable from a clock, and the driver needs a ninth the route does not have. A shared function would either carry a parameter that is always `null` on one side and a term that is always satisfied on the other, or split into a core plus two wrappers, which is three declarations where there are two today.
3. **TWO LIVE GATES READ THAT FILE FOR THIS FUNCTION, AND NEITHER IS WEAKENABLE.** [`admin-wallet.test.ts:564`](../../apps/api/test/admin-wallet.test.ts) asserts that `wallet-withdrawals.ts` contains `DUAL_CONTROL_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, inside a case whose subject is that the threshold sits on the approval edge and on no other money-out route. [`adr-140-identity-status-form.test.ts:177`](../../apps/api/test/adr-140-identity-status-form.test.ts) names the pair `wallet-withdrawals.ts` and `decideApproval` in a table its own comment says was corrected **upward** and may only be corrected upward. A move rewrites both, in `apps/api/test/**`, which is outside slice 7's fence and outside this row's.
4. **THE OPERATOR ARM CAN ONLY EVER LIVE IN `apps/api`, AND IT IS THE HALF THAT BINDS THE CONSTANT.** `DUAL_CONTROL_THRESHOLD_CENTS` is declared at [`admin-wallet.ts:420`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) and `decideApproval`'s own docblock refuses a second literal in terms, *"a second literal here would be a second number to move"*. `apps/worker` has no operator, no admin origin and no `admin_actions` door. **Moving the function would take the dual-control comparison out of the deployable that owns the number, and duplicating the number is the thing the docblock already forbade.**

#### The duplication cost, stated in full

**WHAT GETS DUPLICATED.** Seven ordered terms and the hold names they return: `not_approvable`, `identity_not_active`, `payouts_frozen`, `kyc_not_verified`, `halted`, `provenance_missing`, `destination_cooling`. Plus the guard choice between `G-COOLING-ELAPSED` and `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED`. Plus `APPROVABLE_STATUSES`' two members. Plus the ordering **rationale**, which `decideApproval`'s docblock argues at length and which is not arbitrary: identity terms first because they are facts about the person, and the operator term last.

**WHAT DOES NOT GET DUPLICATED**, which is the part that makes the cost bearable: `dualControlRequired`, `DUAL_CONTROL_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, `ApprovalHand` and the three operator columns stay in `apps/api` and appear nowhere in `apps/worker`. **The worker's copy is smaller than the original, not equal to it**, so the two are not two statements of one predicate; they are a subset and a superset of a shared seven.

**WHO PAYS WHEN THEY DRIFT, AND IN WHICH DIRECTION.** The sweep will be the only live writer of the approval edge, so a change made to `apps/api`'s copy alone **changes nothing at all**. That is the dangerous direction: a reader who tightens a term there, correctly, has tightened nothing, and the tree gives no sign. The party who pays first is that reader; the party who pays next is the trader, because the seven terms guard `LT-06`, the posting that extinguishes a wallet claim, and a term dropped on the worker's side approves a withdrawal for a restricted identity, an unverified one, or a halted row. **A term dropped on the `apps/api` side costs nothing until an operator door lands, and then costs everything at once.**

**AND THE COST IS PAID INTO A CENSUS RATHER THAN INTO A MEMORY**, which is [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) section 6's own remedy and this repository's standing preference for a mechanical assertion over a bigger reader. **Slice 7's suite holds the approval predicate at exactly two statements, names both files, asserts that the seven hold names and their ORDER are identical in both, and asserts that the worker's copy carries no dual-control token and the route's carries one.** A third statement turns it red. The day the collapse lands, the same case is what says so.

### 7. What would have to change for the move to become right

**Stated so a later reader can check whether the condition has arrived, rather than re-argue the ruling.**

1. **An operator approval endpoint gains a contract row.** [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) declares none today, and `ApprovalHand`'s existence says one was expected. On the day it lands, `apps/api` has a live caller, the census's answer stops being *one live copy and one specimen*, and a shared home is forced rather than optional. **This is the trigger to watch.**
2. **A new package both deployables declare**, which is [ADR-286](ADR-286.md)'s candidate A and the only surviving candidate here. It is cheap in this workspace: `packages/enrichment` declares nothing and buys no authority. **It is refused now because it would be created for one reader**, and it becomes right the moment condition 1 is met.
3. **A ruling that the api-side copy is deleted rather than moved.** It is the smallest of the three and it is refused for a stated reason: the function is [ADR-232](ADR-232.md)'s record of a ruled edge and is the operator arm's implementation waiting for its door. **Deleting it would make condition 1 more expensive than it needs to be**, and would delete two gates' subject with it.

### 8. Three findings this row did not go looking for, and repairs none

**All three are outside this fence. All three are things slice 7 will meet after starting, which is exactly the class [ADR-305](ADR-305.md) exists to move in front of a session rather than behind it.**

1. **THE EVENT REGISTRY NAMES THE WRONG PRODUCER, IN TWO CELLS.** [`EVENTS.md:301`](../architecture/EVENTS.md) gives `wallet.withdrawal_approved` the producer **API**, and [`EVENTS.md:285`](../architecture/EVENTS.md) gives `wallet.debited` the producers *"API at checkout, API at withdrawal approval"*.
