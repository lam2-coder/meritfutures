## ADR-233: A `firm`-class read on the scoped transaction, because the objection that governs a `pair` row is absent for a row that belongs to nobody (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**Two ports, one architectural gap, and underneath it two blockers neither port had ever named.** `useCheckoutBackend` and `usePayoutBackend` gave the same reason word for word in its first clause: _"a `firm` READ ON A ONE-TRANSACTION PORT"_. Money in and money out were blocked by one thing, and that thing is now built.

**The ruling is a narrow, named capability on `ScopedTx` over a closed list of five keys.** `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` ([`scoped-db.ts:2558`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) is `coupons`, `geoRestrictions`, `midHealth`, `planVersions` and `planVersionSizes`; the handle gains `catalogRows`, `catalogRowsWhere` and `catalogRowAt` and **no write verb**. A port that runs every method on one transaction can now read its catalogue on that transaction, which is the one property [`firmDb()`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) cannot give it: `transaction(firmDb(), ...)` opens a second transaction on a second connection, so the price would be read outside the transaction the purchase commits in.

**The disclosure argument that governs `pair` and `owned` is ABSENT here rather than outweighed, and that is the whole ground.** [ADR-106](ADR-106.md) keeps a `pair` key out of `ScopedTableKey` because _"returning the row to either party discloses the other"_; [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) keeps a scoped read filtered because the row is somebody's. A `firm` row is **nobody's** -- the class is registered for a table that _"declares NO column against `identities(id)`"_ -- so there is no party to disclose and no tenancy to read around. There is nothing here to weigh. This corpus has taken that move twice already: [ADR-230](ADR-230.md) on the absent `RETURNING`, and [ADR-191](ADR-191.md) on `either`.

**THE CROSS-IDENTITY READ IS A DIFFERENT QUESTION AND IT DID NOT SURVIVE THE SAME REASONING.** `affiliates` is `owned` on `identity_id` ([`scope.ts:1092`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) and `affiliate_clicks` is `derived` through it ([`scope.ts:1108`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)), so the row belongs to somebody and the objection is fully present. **Refused.** Section 5.

**AND NEITHER PORT IS WIRED, WHICH IS THIS ENTRY'S HARDEST SENTENCE.** The row's stop condition was at least one of the two, and the ruling that was supposed to unblock them did unblock them **of the thing the row named**. Underneath, each port carries a blocker no reason on it had ever stated, neither of which is a door and neither of which is inside this fence: `PayoutTx.subject` needs a `RuleState` whose `engineGates` has no decoding, and `CheckoutTx.accountCap()` -- the **first** call of both checkout handlers -- needs a cap that appears in no column of any of the 65 migrations. Section 6.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `233`, session **423**, dispatched as `claude/a-firm-read-on-one-transaction`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN AND `0071` IS NOT RESERVED**: the ruling needs none, and reserving a number against a migration nobody wrote is how a registry acquires a hole.

- **MONEY PATH, BOTH DIRECTIONS.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **`E2` IS OWED**: this widens what a request handler holding an identity may read, and the tables it admits carry the price of every product Merit sells.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That the capability reaches five keys and refuses every other `TableKey` on all three verbs **before building a statement**, that two identities render byte-identical SQL, that no write verb exists, and that four seeded defects turn it red is **MEASURED**, in section 7. That `max_accounts_per_entity` is in no migration column, that no decoding for `engine_gates` exists under any `src/`, and that `coupons.code` and `plan_version_sizes_version_size_uq` were absent from `schema.ts` is **DERIVED** by search, at the scope each claim names. What is **JUDGED** is that an explicit list beats a registry derivation here (section 4), that the cross-identity read is not the same question (section 5), and that neither port may be wired today (section 6).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every claim below was read at its own file.

### 2. The ruling

**A `firm`-class READ on `ScopedTx`, over a closed list, with three verbs and no fourth.**

|                        |                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **The list**           | `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` ([`scoped-db.ts:2558`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)), five members, `as const satisfies readonly FirmTableKey[]`                                                                 |
| **The type**           | `CatalogTableKey` ([`:2575`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)). A key outside the list is a compile error; a key that is not `firm` cannot be added to the list at all                                    |
| **The run-time guard** | `refuseUncatalogued` ([`:2591`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)), run FIRST on all three verbs, because the type is castable and `sqlExecutorOn` reads its own closed vocabulary for exactly that reason |
| **The verbs**          | `catalogRows`, `catalogRowsWhere`, `catalogRowAt` ([`:2717`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)). No `insert`, no `updateAt`, no `deleteAt`, no lock                                                        |
| **The predicate**      | **NONE, AND NONE IS POSSIBLE.** `scopePredicate` throws on every one of these keys, which the suite asserts, so the unfiltered read is the class read correctly rather than a filter omitted                 |

**WHAT IT IS NOT.** It is not a sixth door: the five doors in [`apps/api/src/db.ts`](../../apps/api/src/db.ts) are ways to REACH the database, and this is three methods on a handle a caller already holds. Nothing here widens who may open anything. What it changes is **which transaction a catalogue read runs in**.

**IT IS NOT `FirmTx` ARRIVING ON A SCOPED HANDLE EITHER, and the difference is two things, neither of which is tenancy.**

1. **NOT EVERY `firm` TABLE IS CATALOGUE.** `otp_challenges` is `firm` because the row is written _"before anybody is anybody"_ ([`scope.ts:1433`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) and `code_hash` is authentication material; `treasury_balances` ([`:820`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) and `reserve_coverage_snapshots` ([`:1466`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) are the firm's own position, which `AS-M12-04` rules unpublishable. The class is **45 keys of a registry of 112**, derived at the moment of writing, and a checkout needs five. **Handing a request handler the class because it needs a slice of the class is the widening this package exists to refuse.**
2. **A READ IS NOT A WRITE.** `FirmTx` carries `insert`, `updateAt` and `deleteAt` over the same class. A buyer's transaction that could `UPDATE geo_restrictions` would be a checkout that switches off its own geo block.

### 3. Why the objection is absent rather than outweighed, said in those terms

**An absent objection is a stronger ground than an outweighed one**, because an outweighed objection returns the moment the weights move and an absent one does not. Two classes are excluded from `ScopedTableKey` and the exclusions are not interchangeable ([`scope.ts:1495`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) says so):

| Class               | Why a scoped read is refused                                                                                    | Does it apply to `firm`?                                          |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pair`              | Two identities own the row, and a filter is not a projection, so the counterparty's uuid returns with every row | **NO.** No identity owns the row                                  |
| `owned` / `derived` | The row is one person's, and an unfiltered read is [ADR-008](ADR-008.md)'s BOLA failure                         | **NO.** There is no person and no filter that would mean anything |

**SO THE QUESTION "WHY ARE YOU READING ROWS THAT ARE NOT YOURS" DOES NOT ARISE, AND `firmDb()` ALREADY SAYS SO.** Its own docblock is that it _"takes NO reason, because the question a reason answers ... does not arise about a row that is nobody's"_. This ruling changes nothing about that answer; it changes the connection the answer is given on.

**WHAT IS AT STAKE INSTEAD IS THE LIST**, and that is why the list carries the argument and the class does not.

### 4. Why an explicit list and not a registry derivation

[ADR-230](ADR-230.md) derived `PartyWritableTableKey` from `SCOPE_RULES` rather than listing it, on the ground that the ruling **is** per table and already lives in the registry: `PairRule.writer` is required, carries its own `why`, and defaults to nothing. **That reasoning cannot be copied here and following it mechanically would be the error.** `FirmRule` carries `class` and `why` and nothing a derivation could read, so a derivation would yield `FirmTableKey` **entire** -- the class, not a slice of it, which is precisely what section 2 refuses.

**That is `ParentedTableKey`'s situation exactly and this takes `ParentedTableKey`'s answer**: a closed list, joined by a diff on `packages/db` with an argument attached. **The argument a member owes is not _"this row is nobody's"_**, which is already true of all 45; it is _"a request handler holding an identity has a reason to read this INSIDE its own transaction"_, and each of the five names the port method that reason belongs to.

### 5. The cross-identity read, ruled separately and REFUSED

`CheckoutTx.clickByToken` ([`checkout.ts:753`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)) returns a `ClickRef` whose `affiliate` carries `affiliates.identity_id`, and `couponByCode` returns the same shape ([`checkout.ts:457`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)). **This is not the question section 3 answered and it does not inherit its answer.**

|                          | `firm` catalogue                            | The affiliate rows                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who owns the row         | **Nobody**                                  | `affiliates` is `owned` on `identity_id`; `affiliate_clicks` is `derived` through it |
| ADR-008's objection      | **Absent**                                  | **Present.** A buyer-scoped read returns the empty set, which is the class working   |
| What a grant would admit | An unfiltered read of a row with no tenancy | A read ACROSS a tenancy boundary, for a caller who proved a different identity       |

**REFUSED TODAY, and the refusal is not a shrug.** A grant would need three things this entry cannot supply from inside its own fence: a vocabulary saying which columns of another identity's row a buyer may see (every door in `packages/db` returns whole rows, which [ADR-231](ADR-231.md)'s own approval block names as where it is most likely wrong); an argument about what the buyer's HANDLER learning an affiliate's identity uuid amounts to, when the checkout response carries no affiliate field; and a per-table reason on the registry, which `OwnedRule` has no field for.

**AND THE REFUSAL REGISTERS A GAP THAT IS NOT THIS SESSION'S TO CLOSE.** [ADR-230](ADR-230.md) gave the buyer `insertAsParty` on `attributions`, stamping the buyer and **requiring the counterparty from the caller**, and justified not validating that counterparty on the ground that _"the identity it names is one the caller already held (on `attributions` it comes out of a coupon the buyer typed or a click token the buyer presented)"_. **That premise is not satisfied by any door in this tree.** The coupon and the click token are held by the buyer; the **identity** they resolve to is not, and there is no read that resolves it. So `insertAsParty` on `attributions` has no possible caller until this question is ruled, and the write door and the read refusal are two halves of one decision that were taken three days apart. **Registered rather than repaired**, because repairing it is the grant this section just refused.

### 6. THE FINDING: neither port clears, and the firm read was not the binding constraint on either

**Both entries in [`wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts) lose their lead clause and neither is deleted.** That is assertion 2 of that file's own three working, and it is the second time in a week for `useCheckoutBackend`.

#### 6.1 `usePayoutBackend`: a `RuleState` this deployment cannot produce

`PayoutTx.subject` ([`payouts.ts:428`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts)) returns `PayoutSubject` whose `state` ([`payouts.ts:331`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts)) is a `RuleState`. `RuleState.engineGates` ([`types.ts:1006`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) is `EngineGateResults` ([`types.ts:961`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) and `rule_states.engine_gates` is `jsonb`. **There is no decoding for that column anywhere**, and writing one would be inventing a corpus fact: `RuleStateWriterIo.encodeEngineGates` ([`state-writer.ts:337`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/state-writer.ts)) has no implementation under any `src/`, and the stored encoding is a corpus amendment. **`rule_states` also holds no rows and nothing in a deployment writes one.**

**NONE OF THAT IS THIS ENTRY'S DISCOVERY, WHICH IS THE FINDING RATHER THAN AN EXCUSE.** `ELIGIBILITY_BLOCKER` ([`account-reads.ts:851`](../../apps/api/src/routes/account-reads.ts)) has stated all three about `GET /accounts/:id/eligibility` since session 401, and `INV-M5-02` requires **both** payout endpoints to call `evaluatePayout` with identical inputs, so it was always one blocker across two endpoints. **No reason on the payout port had ever named it**, and a session dispatched to remove what that entry named would have removed it and found the route still answering 503.

#### 6.2 `useCheckoutBackend`: a cap with no source, on the first line of both handlers

`accountCap()` ([`checkout.ts:736`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)) is the **first** call in both handlers, before the plan, before the coupon, before the geo check. Its `maxAccounts` ([`checkout.ts:491`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)) is _"the identity's cap, `max_accounts_override` folded over the plan default"_, and **the plan default is in no column of any migration**: `grep -rn max_accounts packages/db/migrations` returns **1** line, and it is `identities.max_accounts_override`, the per-entity **exception**. `databaseAuthBackend` reports the identical finding about `Me.max_accounts` ([`auth-backend.ts:1520`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts)) and refuses `readMe` for it.

**AND THIS RULING BRINGS THE VALUE WITHIN REACH WITHOUT SETTLING IT, WHICH IS WORTH SAYING PRECISELY.** `limits.max_accounts_per_entity` is in the stored `plan_versions.rules` jsonb ([data-model README](../architecture/data-model/README.md)), which `catalogRowAt('planVersions', ...)` now reaches. It is still not an answer: **`accountCap()` takes no plan**, is called before the plan version is resolved on the purchase path and before `resetTarget` on the reset path, and asks a **per-identity** question of a **per-plan-version** parameter that carries no `CV-nn`, is validated by nothing, and which no two published versions are required to agree on. Choosing one published version's number is a ruling about who is refused a purchase.

#### 6.3 Why nothing was wired anyway, stated rather than assumed

**Installing `databasePayoutBackend` with `listPayouts` live and `subject` rejecting is the exact shape `usePayoutBackend`'s own entry refuses**: a live-looking `GET` in front of the arm that approves payouts. **Installing `databaseCheckoutBackend` with `accountCap` rejecting serves nothing at all**, because that call is unconditional: every checkout of every payment method would 503 on the first line, which raises the wired count and serves no request, and `useCheckoutAdapters`'s entry already rules that shape _"not a wiring"_.

**The row's stop condition is therefore NOT MET and it is reported rather than reached for.** A backend built to meet it would have had to invent a cap, a decoding, or both, on the two paths where money enters and leaves.

### 8. The transcription repair the capability found

Two of the five tables were **unaddressable through every accessor in this package**, and the database had bounded both for years. `uniqueKeys()` reads [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts) and not the DDL:

| Key                             | In the DDL                                                                   | In `schema.ts` before this session                                   |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| `(plan_version_id, size_cents)` | `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX plan_version_sizes_version_size_uq`, `0004_catalog.sql` | Absent, and this is the spelling `getTableConfig` cannot see at all  |

**This is [ADR-231](ADR-231.md)'s `certificates_code_uq` repair on two more tables**, and the second one is written as a table-level `unique()` where the DDL writes an INDEX. The difference is stated rather than glossed: `uniqueKeys()` reads `config.uniqueConstraints` and not `config.indexes`, this file generates no DDL, and what is transcribed is the **property** the accessor needs, which is that the column set bounds the row count to one. **A unique index does that exactly as a unique constraint does.**

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS THE LIST, BECAUSE THE LIST IS THE WHOLE CONTROL.** Section 3 argues that no disclosure objection exists for a row nobody owns, and if that is right then the only thing standing between a request handler and 45 `firm` tables is five names in one array. **The failure mode is a future session that reads the shape and not the reason**, adds `otp_challenges` because it is `firm` and it needed one column, and finds every gate green except the one case that names three tables by hand. **If you would rather the argument a member owes lived on the registry as a required field**, the way `PairRule.writer` does, that is a `scope.ts` session and it is strictly better; this entry could not take it, because `scope.ts` is a file three consecutive sessions have deliberately left closed and widening a fence to finish is the move the row forbade.

  **THE SECOND IS SECTION 5, AND IT IS WORTH MORE THAN THE CAPABILITY.** [ADR-230](ADR-230.md) shipped a write door whose stated justification assumes a read that does not exist. **Either the buyer may learn the affiliate's identity, in which case ADR-230's premise is true and a read must be built to make it so, or they may not, in which case `insertAsParty` on `attributions` is a door with no possible caller.** This entry refuses the read and registers the contradiction rather than resolving it, because resolving it in the direction that grants would be a money-path disclosure ruling written at the end of a session about something else.

  **THE THIRD IS WHETHER YOU WANT THIS WAVE RESIZED.** The row was sized for shipped product and section 6 reports that neither port could ship. **Both remaining blockers are cheap to state and neither is a door**: `accountCap()` needs a ruling about which plan version states a per-identity cap, and `rule_states.engine_gates` needs an encoding declared. **Both are single-objective sessions and both are worth more than a third attempt at these two ports**, because each unblocks an endpoint that has been 503 since it was written.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **Section 3's argument proves too much if you let it.** _"There is no party to disclose"_ is true of `treasury_balances` and of `reserve_coverage_snapshots`, and `AS-M12-04` rules the coverage ratio unpublishable anyway -- so **tenancy is not the only reason to withhold a row, and this entry's central argument only ever addressed tenancy**. Section 2's first bullet is what carries the rest, and it carries it as a hand-written list rather than as a principle. **That is the residual risk and it is five names wide.**
