## ADR-242: an ops plane is four different questions, and one of them is a database read whose blocker is a door somebody already ruled (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**Row `242` sent one session at `setInternalOpsSource` on the reading that its obstruction is "an ops plane rather than a database read". The row also asked the right question about that reading: whether an ops probe is a read of state a process WROTE, which `ApiDb` can serve, or a live call to a process, which it cannot, and it said the answer may differ per method. IT DIFFERS. Splitting the four is the whole of this entry.**

**ONE OF THE FOUR IS A DATABASE READ.** `readReconStatus` reads `reconciliations`, a registered table a process writes, and the exact filter it needs is already written in this deployable. What refuses it is not an ops plane. It is the DOOR, and [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) clause 1 ruled that door two hundred numbers ago under a condition [ADR-237](ADR-237.md) measured as unmet.

**AND THE ENTRY'S OWN SENTENCE IS FALSE OF `runBatch`.** *"None of the four is a shape `ApiDb` offers"* fails there: `firm(fn)` yields a `FirmTx`, `FirmTx` carries `sqlExecutor('job-enqueue')`, and that is structurally the transaction `packages/queue`'s `enqueue` requires. `runBatch` is blocked on an AUTHORITY, which is a manifest line, and not on a shape.

**THE PORT STAYS BLOCKED AND NOTHING IS WIRED.** One read out of four, with that one's door refused, is a source with one live arm and three that reject. The row named that outcome in advance and called it a good one; this entry agrees and says why in section 7.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `242`, session **432**, dispatched as `claude/the-ops-plane-is-not-a-database-read`. The row is **CONDITIONAL** on ruling, and it is taken.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH.** No ledger account, no balance, no payout, no migration and no reserved number. No shipped source file under `apps/` or `packages/` changes. `E2` is not owed. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That `renderDeepHealth` and `renderJobs` each refuse a partial answer, that `apps/api/src` reaches the network in exactly two files and neither is one of the three vendors, that `JobQueue` declares five methods and none reads a depth, that `reconciliations` is scope class `derived` and a `FirmTx` cannot name it, that a `FirmTx` satisfies the queue's transaction shape, and that `apps/api` declares no `@merit/queue` is **MEASURED**, by executing it. That `readReconStatus`' fields map onto `0014_marks.sql`'s columns and that ADR-171's condition is unmet is **DERIVED**, at the file and line each claim names. What is **JUDGED** is that one constructible method out of four does not wire this port (section 7), that the door is not takeable here even setting ADR-171 aside (section 5), and that the ops plane two of these methods need is an entry of its own rather than a thing to slip into a wiring slice (section 8).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every citation in row `242` was re-derived and one did not survive: the row quotes *"None of the four is a shape `ApiDb` offers"* as sitting at [`internal.ts:842-845`](../../apps/api/src/routes/internal.ts). Those lines are the message body of `wired()`'s throw and say nothing about `ApiDb`; they support the OTHER clause of the sentence they sit beside. Section 1.

- **The per-method measurement is [`docs/reviews/2026-08-29-internal-ops-plane.md`](../reviews/2026-08-29-internal-ops-plane.md)** and every number in this entry comes from it.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE FOUR METHODS ARE RULED SEPARATELY AND THE PARTITION IS ONE READ AND THREE COMMANDS.** `readReconStatus` is a database read of state a process wrote. `readDependencies`, `readJobs` and `runBatch` are not, and each is not for a different reason. Sections 3 to 6.
>
> **2. `readReconStatus`' BLOCKER IS THE DOOR AND NOT THE PLANE, AND THE DOOR IS ALREADY RULED.** `reconciliations` is scope class `derived` ([`scope.ts:1339`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)), so `ApiDb.firm` refuses the key AT COMPILE TIME and `ApiDb.scoped` has no identity on this surface. The handle that serves it is `SystemTx` at `systemDb('operator-console')`, and [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) clause 1 refuses that door until an `AdminSessionSource` a deployment can install exists. **[ADR-237](ADR-237.md) measured that condition and it is unmet**: nothing in this tree can write an `operator_sessions` row.
>
> **3. THE DOOR IS NOT TAKEN HERE, AND THE SECOND REASON IS INDEPENDENT OF ADR-171.** Its only caller would be `databaseInternalOps`, which cannot be constructed because the other three methods reject. **A door whose whole justification is one caller, and which has none, is a primitive admitted before a caller exists** ([`job-queue.ts`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts)'s rule, cited by [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) clause 3 and applied by [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) clause 2 to this same door).
>
> **4. `runBatch` IS BLOCKED ON AN AUTHORITY AND NOT ON A SHAPE, AND THE ENTRY'S SENTENCE IS CORRECTED.** `ApiDb.firm(fn)` hands its callback a `FirmTx`; `FirmTx extends TxCommon` ([`scoped-db.ts:2803`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) carries `sqlExecutor(reason)` ([`scoped-db.ts:3044`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) at the single reason `'job-enqueue'` ([`scoped-db.ts:2623`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)); and the `SqlExecutor` it returns declares `executeSql` and nothing else, which is the whole of `JobTransaction`. **The transactional enqueue ADR-006 requires is expressible with the doors this deployable holds today.** What it lacks is the manifest line, which is where the capability is acquired ([ADR-117](ADR-117.md) section 5).
>
> **5. THE PORT STAYS BLOCKED AND NO ADAPTER IS INSTALLED.** One live arm beside three that reject is refused on `usePayoutBackend`'s rule, and this port's own docblock refuses it harder: four setters *"would buy the ability to half-wire the operator console, which is not an ability anybody has asked for"*. The triple does not move.
>
> **6. NO RPC CHANNEL IS BUILT AND NONE IS PROPOSED HERE.** Two of the four methods need this process to call other processes. That is a decision about where the operator console's liveness surface lives, it is an entry of its own, and section 8 names it rather than smuggling it into a wiring slice.
>
> **7. THE ENTRY IS NARROWED TO NAME EXACTLY WHAT REFUSES EACH METHOD, AND EVERY ABSENCE BEHIND IT IS EXECUTED.** [`test/internal-ops-constructibility.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/internal-ops-constructibility.test.ts) runs fourteen cases. The day any of the four obstructions lifts, a case goes red and the entry expires rather than waiting to be noticed, which is [ADR-235](ADR-235.md)'s rule and [ADR-240](ADR-240.md)'s application of it.
>
> **8. NOTHING UNDER `apps/worker/**` IS EDITED AND NO ASSERTION READS IT.** That tree is a concurrent session's. What this session would need from it is named in section 8 as a report rather than taken.

### 3. `readDependencies`: a command, and the renderer forbids the partial adapter

[API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 9 names four dependencies and [`internal.ts:200`](../../apps/api/src/routes/internal.ts) transcribes them in the contract's order: `db`, `sftp`, `rise`, `psp`. **One is inside this process. Three are other systems**, and a probe of another system is an outbound call rather than a row anybody wrote.

**MEASURED over `apps/api/src` (52 files) and over every shipped `src` of every workspace member (319 files):**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Files under `apps/api/src` reaching the network outbound | **2**: [`turnstile.ts`](../../apps/api/src/turnstile.ts) and [`otp-delivery.ts`](../../apps/api/src/otp-delivery.ts) |
| Either one an SFTP, Rise or PSP client | **No.** A CAPTCHA verifier and an OTP delivery vendor |

| Rise modules in `apps/api/src` that call outward | **0.** Every one is an inbound webhook receiver |

**THE DECISIVE FACT IS BEHAVIOURAL RATHER THAN ARGUED.** `renderDeepHealth` throws on a missing probe by ruling: *"A missing probe is not a passing one: rendering the response without it would report the estate on the strength of the checks that happened to run."* So there is no adapter that probes `db`, which this process could do, and omits the three it cannot. The test executes that once for a `db`-only answer and once per dependency in a loop over `DEEP_HEALTH_DEPENDENCIES`, so a fifth dependency is covered without an edit.

### 4. `readJobs`: four sub-answers, and only a fragment of one is a read

| Field | Where the answer lives | Read? |
|---|---|---|
| `queues[].depth` and `.failed` | pg-boss's own tables, in its own Postgres schema | **No.** No migration installs that schema |
| `deadManSwitches[].expected_by` | A cell of a markdown table, by the field's own docblock: *"CRON_INVENTORY's 'Expected by' cell, verbatim"* | **No.** It is a document |
| `deadManSwitches[].firing` | The alarm, in the monitoring stack | **No.** [ADR-240](ADR-240.md) met the identical fact one endpoint over |
| `deadManSwitches[].last_completed_at` | For ONE of the register's rows, `events` filtered to `batch.completed` | **Yes, for one row of twenty** |

**MEASURED.** **0** of **67** migration files names pg-boss's schema in any spelling, which [`pg-boss-queue.ts:40`](../../packages/queue/src/pg-boss-queue.ts) states of itself: *"THAT MIGRATION DOES NOT EXIST YET ... `start()` against a database with no pg-boss schema therefore FAILS, loudly, which is the right failure."* **`JobQueue` declares five methods** and none reads a depth. **CRON_INVENTORY's scheduled table carries 20 rows**, and their dead-man predicates are heterogeneous by design: *"two consecutive cycles missed"*, *"canaries not found"*, *"a plan with no `plan_breaker_state` row for the day"*.

**AND THE RENDERER FORBIDS THE PARTIAL ADAPTER HERE TOO.** `renderJobs` throws on an empty switch list, quoting the register: *"a job in this table without a dead-man switch is a job that does not exist"*, because *"an empty list here says nothing is firing where the true statement is that nothing is being watched"*. An adapter answering the queue half alone cannot render; one answering the single `batch.completed` row would publish a page watching one job out of twenty, on the endpoint an operator opens to tell "nothing is firing" from "nothing is watched" apart.

### 5. `readReconStatus`: the one read, and the door that refuses it

**IT IS A READ, AND EVERY FIELD MAPS TO A COLUMN.** `reconciliations` is [`0014_marks.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0014_marks.sql)'s table, whose `:171` is `status text NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('match', 'mismatch', 'resolved'))`. `ReconMismatchRow`'s seven members are seven columns. `ReconSnapshot.asOf` is the port's clock by its own docblock, which is what lets the module compute an age without reaching for one. **A process writes the rows**: [`apps/worker/src/recon/sweep.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/recon/sweep.ts), read here and not edited.

**AND THE READ IS ALREADY WRITTEN IN THIS DEPLOYABLE.** [`liability.ts:1193`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/liability.ts) is `rowsWhere('reconciliations', { status: 'mismatch' })`, over `LiabilityTx`, a handle narrowed AT THE PORT. `readReconStatus` is that read plus a clock. **So the missing thing is not a shape and not a vocabulary.**

**WHAT REFUSES IT IS THE DOOR, AND EVERY ONE OF THE FIVE IS WALKED TO ITS OWN DECLARATION:**

| Door on `ApiDb` | Why it cannot serve this read |
|---|---|
| `scoped(identityId, fn)` | Needs an identity. The operator surface has none, and the response is firm-wide across every account |
| `firm(fn)` | `FirmTx.rowsWhere<K extends FirmTableKey>` and a `derived` table is not a `FirmTableKey`. **A COMPILE ERROR**, asserted with a `@ts-expect-error` `tsc` also checks in the other direction |
| `resolution(fn)` | One table, one address: `users` by `email` |
| `establishment(fn)` | One verb, and it is a write |
| `publicLookup(fn)` | One row of `certificates` by `code` |

**ADR-171 CLAUSE 1 REFUSES THE SIXTH**, and its section 9 states the condition: *"A slice may declare `operator(fn)` on `ApiDb` when an `AdminSessionSource` a deployment can install exists in this tree."* **[ADR-237](ADR-237.md) is the measurement and the condition is unmet**: `OperatorAssertionVerifier` has no implementation here, nothing can write an `operator_sessions` row, and that entry states it as a ruling rather than an omission.

**A NARROW DOOR WAS CONSIDERED AND IT IS REFUSED ON A GROUND ADR-171 DID NOT NEED.** ADR-171 section 9 names two admissible answers to *"what stops `databaseAuthBackend` reaching it"*, and one of them is a door narrowed to a table set at the door. A door reading only `reconciliations` would satisfy that test: `databaseAuthBackend` reaching it gets no `users` row and `B1` stays refused. **It is still not taken here, because its only caller cannot be constructed.** The other three methods reject, the port is one port, and an adapter that could not be composed would leave the door with no call site at all. That is the primitive-before-a-caller `job-queue.ts` names and ADR-120 clause 3 cites for this exact refusal. **The narrow door is therefore not refused on its merits and is not endorsed either**: it is recorded as available to whoever holds a slice that can actually compose the source.

### 6. `runBatch`: a command whose blocker is a manifest line

**IT COMMANDS.** `BATCH_RUN_ACCEPTED` is 202 for that reason in its own words: *"the batch runs on the worker and this route has accepted a request rather than completed a run."* No ruling here moves that.

**AND `ApiDb` OFFERS ITS SHAPE, WHICH IS WHERE THE ENTRY WAS WRONG.** The four steps of the derivation are in ruling 4 above, and the module's own header reached the same conclusion when it was written: *"the wiring slice that adds the manifest line supplies an implementation that opens a transaction, takes `sqlExecutor('job-enqueue')` off it and calls `enqueue` inside it."* **That sentence was always the true blocker and the `BLOCKED` entry did not carry it.**

**THREE THINGS BLOCK IT AND NONE IS A SHAPE:**

| Blocker | Measured |
|---|---|
| **The manifest.** `apps/api` declares no `@merit/queue` | Asserted in the new test file. The manifest is *"THE ONLY PLACE THE CAPABILITY CAN BE ACQUIRED"* ([ADR-117](ADR-117.md) section 5, measured from this very package), so admitting it to the deployable serving the whole public surface is an authority change in the shape [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) gave the database admission, and it is a slice with its own subject rather than a line |
| **The job store.** pg-boss's schema has no migration | **0** of **67** |
| **The consumer.** Nothing runs the batch a row would enqueue | Reported in section 8. **NOT THIS SESSION'S** |

**A WORKSPACE-WIDE COUNT, BECAUSE IT IS THE SHARPEST VERSION OF THE FIRST ROW.** `@merit/queue` is declared as a dependency by **zero** packages in this workspace. The only manifest naming the string is the package's own `name` field. **The queue is a library nothing depends on**, which is a stronger statement than the one the entry made and is the one worth carrying.

### 7. Why one constructible method does not wire the port

**IT IS ONE PORT WITH FOUR METHODS AND ITS OWN DOCBLOCK IS THE ARGUMENT:**

> ONE PORT AND NOT FOUR, because the four rows are one operator surface served by one deployment: a wiring slice that could supply the recon read and not the health probe would be a deployment that has not been finished, and this file answers that the same way whichever method is missing. Four setters would buy the ability to half-wire the operator console, which is not an ability anybody has asked for.

`usePayoutBackend`'s rule says the same thing from outside: a port with one live arm and one that rejects is refused. **Here it would be one live arm and three.** And the one live arm is not live either, because its door is refused.

**SO THE HONEST OUTCOME IS A NARROWED ENTRY AND A SHARPER SET OF QUESTIONS, AND THE ROW SAID SO IN ADVANCE**: *"If two of the four are constructible and two are not, the port stays blocked and its entry narrows to name exactly the two that refuse and why. THAT IS A GOOD OUTCOME."* It is one and three rather than two and two, and the entry names all four.

**THE TRIPLE DOES NOT MOVE.** `{ declared: 24, wired: 8, blocked: 16 }` is unchanged, because no port is added, none is wired and none is retired.

**WHAT THIS SESSION WOULD NEED FROM THE WORKER, STATED PRECISELY RATHER THAN TAKEN:**

- **A CONSUMER FOR THE BATCH QUEUE, AND THE QUEUE'S NAME.** `runBatch` returns `started`, `resumed` or `duplicate` and `renderBatchRun` refuses a `started` with no job id, on the ground that *"a trigger that reported success and enqueued nothing"* is `RB-01` arriving as a green response. **An enqueue into a queue nothing consumes is that same failure moved one layer out**: the response is honest about the row and dishonest about the run. ### 11. Alternatives considered

**WIRE `readReconStatus` AND LET THE OTHER THREE THROW.** Refused. It is `usePayoutBackend`'s rule and this port's own docblock, and the wired count would move on an operator console three quarters of which answers 500. **A raised count that serves nothing is the failure `wiring.test.ts` exists to catch, arriving through its own front door.**

**SPLIT `InternalOpsSource` INTO FOUR PORTS SO THE READ ONE CAN BE WIRED.** Refused, and the port's docblock refused it first, in the sentence quoted in section 7. It would also not help: the read one's door is refused too.

**TAKE THE OPERATOR DOOR, NARROWED TO `reconciliations`.** Refused on the caller and not on the design. Section 5.

**BUILD AN RPC CHANNEL FROM `apps/api` TO `apps/worker` FOR THE PROBES AND THE TRIGGER.** Refused, and the row forbade it in advance. Two deployables that call each other synchronously to serve one operator route is an architecture decision with its own blast radius, and it is section 8's entry rather than this one's.

**RETURN THE NUMBER UNSPENT.** Considered seriously, because seven sessions have done it correctly today and describing four probes is not ruling on them. **It is not this outcome**: the four are ruled separately with evidence, one of the entry's own sentences is refuted, the citation the dispatch relied on is corrected, and fourteen executable cases replace a paragraph that could not fail.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST IS WHETHER THE DEEP-HEALTH ROW BELONGS ON THIS DEPLOYABLE AT ALL.** Section 3 rules that three of its four probes are outbound calls to systems `apps/api` holds no client for. **The honest question underneath is whether an API process should be probing an SFTP server and a PSP on an operator's request**, or whether those probes belong to whatever already watches them and this route should read their verdicts. The corpus states the endpoint and not its topology, and a decision either way is a purchase or a design, not a transcription.

  **THE THIRD IS THE `@merit/queue` ADMISSION, WHICH IS THE ONE REAL DECISION SECTION 6 DEFERS.** It is an authority change on the internet-facing deployable with the largest reachable code path in the estate, in exchange for one operator route. **A trigger that lives on the worker's own surface would need no admission at all**, and the trade is yours: one manifest line on the public API, or an operator endpoint served by a different process.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **The `readJobs` partition assumes pg-boss stays the backend.** ADR-006 chose it and ADR-086 contained it behind `JobQueue`; a move to a Redis backend makes the depth half unambiguously a live call rather than an unbuilt read, and the ruling for that method would be reached by a different argument to the same place. **And the `last_completed_at` fragment may be more than a fragment.** Section 4 counts it as one row of twenty because CRON_INVENTORY's predicates are heterogeneous; a reader who thinks the other nineteen reduce to event lookups would rule `readJobs` differently, and would still meet the missing depth reader and `renderJobs`' refusal.
