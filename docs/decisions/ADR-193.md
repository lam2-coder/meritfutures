## ADR-193: a reversal may not chain onto another reversal, the rule refuses the LINK and not the operation, and it lives in a trigger because the builder is not weak here but incapable  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[`0009:103-104`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) has promised two rules since the ledger was created and its constraint could only ever carry one.** The comment reads *"A reversal may not reverse itself, and may not chain onto another reversal: a reversal of a reversal is an adjustment and should be posted as one"*, and [`ledger_transactions_no_self_reversal`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) at `0009:105-107` is `CHECK (reversal_of IS NULL OR reversal_of <> id)`. **That is the whole of what a row-level `CHECK` can say, because a row-level `CHECK` cannot query another row.** [`0059`](../../packages/db/migrations/0059_reversal_chain.sql) is the second clause, as `LEDGER-C3`.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `193`, session **355**, dispatched as `claude/reversal-chain`. **Migration `0059` was reserved REQUIRED-IF, and the condition is met: it is TAKEN.** Sections 2 and 3 are why.

- **MONEY PATH. [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) READ IS OWED** on [`0059`](../../packages/db/migrations/0059_reversal_chain.sql) and **nothing here discharges it**: not the ruling being one function and one trigger, not the chain having been watched landing and then refused, and not this entry being merged. **Merging is not signing.** The entry ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line.

- **`0009`, `0027`, `0038`, `0052`, `0053`, `0054`, `0055`, `0056` and `0057` are byte for byte unchanged, and `0009`'s constraint is NOT superseded.** That is a deliberate difference from [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql), which DID supersede an object `0009` created. `ledger_transactions_no_self_reversal` says something true and narrow about ONE row and it goes on saying it; `LEDGER-C3` says the other thing about a SECOND row. **Two statements, two objects, and no `DROP`.**

- **WHAT IS DERIVED AND WHAT IS JUDGED, separated before the argument starts.** That the chain lands on the merged schema is DERIVED, in section 1, executed rather than inherited. That a row-local `CHECK` cannot reach it is DERIVED. **What is JUDGED is whether a chain is wrong at all**, which is section 2 and is the question this entry was sent to answer rather than to assume, **and where the rule lives**, which is section 3. Section 8 is what a founder read adds.

### 1. The finding, re-derived at source rather than inherited

**ALL 58 MIGRATIONS WERE APPLIED IN ORDER, FORWARD-ONLY FROM EMPTY, TO A REAL PostgreSQL 16 UNDER `ON_ERROR_STOP`, AND THE 57-FILE BASELINE WAS BUILT FIRST SO THAT EVERY FIGURE BELOW IS A MEASUREMENT.** Counts read from `pg_tables`, `pg_trigger`, `pg_proc`, `pg_constraint` and `pg_indexes`, never a grep.

**THE CHAIN LANDS, AND WHAT IT DOES IS WORSE THAN THE DISPATCH DESCRIBED.** Session 354 reported three rows, B naming A and C naming B, all three INSERTs landing. Reproduced here on the 57-file schema, using `LT-06` and `LT-09`'s own shapes against a real `wallet_withdrawals` row and the per-identity positions [`0054`](../../packages/db/migrations/0054_identity_ledger_accounts.sql)'s trigger opens:

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| `LT-06` posts, the withdrawal transitions to `failed`, `LT-09` reverses it | Commits clean. `withdrawals_in_flight` net **0**, `WD-C1` satisfied |
| A third transaction reverses `LT-09` | **LANDS.** `withdrawals_in_flight` net **-25000**, `trader_wallet` net **+25000**, global sum **0** |

**SO THE CHAIN DOES NOT MERELY VIOLATE A COMMENT. IT RESTORES THE EXACT DEFECT [ADR-189](ADR-189.md) AND [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql) EXIST TO CLOSE**: the trader's wallet claim is extinguished a second time and the obligation stands again against a withdrawal that is already terminal. **`WD-C1` is silent, and not by a defect in `WD-C1`**: it is a constraint trigger on `wallet_withdrawals`, armed `AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE` on that row, and the row does not move again. A posting written after a withdrawal reaches its terminal state is unwatched by construction, and the chain is the shape that does it while wearing a correction's clothes.

**THE TWO THINGS THAT LOOK LIKE THEY CLOSE IT DO NOT, AND EACH WAS CHECKED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** [`0038`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)'s `ADJ-C1` check 1 at `0038:323-332` refuses a chain **one table over**, cites `0009` as its authority, and is a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger on `account_adjustments`; the three rows above were written straight into `ledger_transactions` with **no adjustment row anywhere**. [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql)'s UNIQUE index does not reach it either, **because a chain gives each row a DIFFERENT `reversal_of`**.

**AND `0057:156-157`'s SENTENCE IS A GUARANTEE WITH AN EXPIRY DATE, WHICH THAT FILE COULD NOT HAVE KNOWN AND THIS ONE CAN STATE.** It says `account_adjustments` *"already enforces it where reversals are actually written today"*. The word doing the work is *"today"*: [`reversalPosting`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts) writes `reversal_of` outside `account_adjustments`, refuses only key-equality, and has no caller outside `packages/ledger` yet. **The expiry is the slice that wires `LT-09`**, and after this entry the sentence does not need to expire, because the rule it leans on is no longer one table over.

### 2. WHETHER, before WHERE. A reversal of a reversal is a RE-APPLICATION, the re-application is LEGITIMATE, and what is refused is the LINK

**THE DISPATCH WAS RIGHT TO PUT THIS FIRST AND THE ANSWER IS NOT THE OBVIOUS ONE.** An entry that closes this by hardening `0009`'s comment has hardened a comment. The operation a chain expresses is real: an original was reversed, the reversal was itself wrong, and the original's effect should stand after all. **This ruling does not forbid that outcome and could not.**

**THE CORPUS ALREADY RULES WHAT SHAPE IT TAKES, AND IT DOES SO IN THE SAME SENTENCE AS THE PROHIBITION.** `0009:104` is *"a reversal of a reversal is an adjustment and should be posted as one"*, and [`0038:293-296`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)'s note under `assert_adjustment_reversal_is_sound` finishes the thought: *"reversing a reversal here is therefore not a hard case, it is a new credit with its own reason and its own second key"*. **`ADJ-C1` refuses the chain one table over WITH A REMEDY ATTACHED, and reading that reason is what this section was sent to do.** The reason is not that the money must not move. It is that the movement must be authored, with its own reason, rather than derived by negating a negation.

**SO THE RULE IS ABOUT WHAT A ROW MAY CLAIM AND NOT ABOUT WHAT MONEY MAY MOVE.** A re-application posted as a new transaction with its own `kind`, its own `idempotency_key` and its own reason is **accepted** by `0059`, and section 5 watches it being accepted. The same movement wearing `reversal_of` is refused. **Three arguments carry that, and they are separated because they fail separately.**

| # | Argument | Why it is not aesthetic |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The chained row is the ORIGINAL's entries again.** [`reversalPosting`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts) swaps each transfer's two sides and carries the amount across, so `reverse(reverse(A))` is `A`, leg for leg. **A fresh money movement is therefore posted under a correction's vocabulary**, and a reader reconciling the ledger reads "a correction" where the truth is "a second charge" | Section 1 measured what that costs on the one path the corpus has already ruled: the obligation returns, on a row nothing watches |
| **2** | **`SD-M5-05`'s own reason stops holding at depth 2.** [`0009:94-97`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) adds the column so that *"reconstructing which reversal answered which original"* is instant *"at exactly the moment (a chargeback dispute, an audit) when it must be instant"*. At depth 1 that is a lookup. **At arbitrary depth it is a walk, and whether a given transaction is live depends on the PARITY of a chain nobody bounded** | The column stops answering the question it was added to answer, which is the delta's own stated failure mode arriving by a different door |
| **3** | **The corpus declares nine `LT-nn` shapes and exactly two of them carry `reversal_of`.** [M05 section 2.1](../plans/M05-payout-system.md): `LT-03` reverses `LT-01`, `LT-09` reverses `LT-06`, and **nothing is declared that reverses either of those two** | A chained row is not a transaction this corpus declares. Under FREEZE a behaviour not in the corpus is not in scope, and a chain needs a tenth shape nobody has ruled |

**THE ALTERNATIVE WAS SEARCHED FOR RATHER THAN DISMISSED.** `docs/`, `packages/` and `apps/` were searched for a corpus requirement that a reversal be reversed, under re-apply, reinstate, un-reverse and reversal-of-a-reversal. **Every hit is about re-applying a migration, a patch or a seed, and not one is about a ledger posting.** The re-application has no caller waiting for it and it has a home when one arrives.

**WHERE THIS RULING IS NARROWER THAN IT LOOKS, said plainly:** it binds a row's `reversal_of` and nothing else. A handler that posts the original's entries again under a new kind, a new key and its own reason is accepted, and **should be**, because that is the shape `0009:104` names. If such a posting is ever wrong, it is wrong for a reason this entry does not hold.

### 3. WHERE the rule lives: a trigger, because the builder is not merely insufficient here but INCAPABLE

**[ADR-189](ADR-189.md) SECTION 4 ARGUED THIS PAIR AND ITS ARGUMENT APPLIES.** At [ADR-189:78](ADR-189.md): *"a builder proves what a caller who called it built, and says nothing about a caller who did not"*. **The dispatch cited section 5 for it; section 5 is the execution table and the argument is section 4**, which is section 6's first finding.

**WHAT IS DIFFERENT HERE IS STRONGER THAN ADR-189's CASE AND IT DECIDES THE NUMBER ON ITS OWN.** `reversalPosting(original, header)` receives `reversalOf` as a `string` ([`reversal.ts:122`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts)). **Whether that string names a row that is itself a reversal is a fact about a row.** `packages/ledger` opens no database transaction and cannot, which that file already says in its own `@param` note at [`reversal.ts:259`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts): *"The caller reads it; this library opens no transaction and cannot."* **A guard written there would either refuse nothing or lie**, and [`0001:90`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql)'s rule is that a gate that silently stops gating is worse than an absent one. **This one could never start.**

**AND THERE IS A SECOND DOOR THAT DOES NOT PASS THROUGH `reversalPosting` AT ALL.** [`posting.ts:136`](../../packages/ledger/src/posting.ts)'s `PostingHeader` carries `reversalOf?: string`, so `posting({ ..., reversalOf: x }, transfers)` writes the column directly and [`post.ts:176`](../../packages/ledger/src/post.ts) carries it to the INSERT. **A rule enforced in `reversalPosting` is bypassed by the public constructor one file over, without a cast and without a lint.** [`posting.test.ts:130`](../../packages/ledger/test/posting.test.ts) already builds a posting that way, in the suite, today.

**SO `packages/ledger` GAINS NO GUARD, AND THE ABSENCE IS THE RULING RATHER THAN AN OMISSION.** What it gains is [`reversal.ts:46-62`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts), inside the *"WHAT THIS FILE CANNOT PROVE"* section that runs `reversal.ts:32-62`: it names `LEDGER-C3` as the third thing the database carries that this file does not, for the same reason it already names `WD-C1` and the UNIQUE index. **A later reader who deletes the trigger learns from this file what it was carrying.**

### 4. The shape of the guard, and the three choices in it that differ from their precedents

`LEDGER-C3`, [`0059:171-219`](../../packages/db/migrations/0059_reversal_chain.sql): one function and one trigger. `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE ON ledger_transactions`, `FOR EACH ROW`, `WHEN (NEW.reversal_of IS NOT NULL)`.

**THE ID CONTINUES `0027`'s FAMILY RATHER THAN OPENING ONE.** `LEDGER-C1` refuses a transaction that debits and credits one account, `LEDGER-C2` refuses an entry against an undeclared class; both are about a transaction's shape and so is this. `ADJ-C1` check 1 stays exactly as it is: **that one binds `account_adjustments` and this one binds every reversal in the ledger, including the ones no adjustment row will ever accompany**, which after `LT-09` is wired is most of them.

| Choice | Precedent | Why this file differs |
|---|---|---|
| **`BEFORE` rather than a DEFERRED constraint trigger** | `WD-C1` ([`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql)) is `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED` | **`0057` is deferred because the transition it guards and the posting that answers it are mutually dependent. Nothing here is.** The row being reversed must exist before the reversing row can name it, so no legitimate posting order needs the check postponed, and `BEFORE` puts the error on the offending statement rather than at `COMMIT` |
| **`OR UPDATE`, where `0038`'s two triggers are `AFTER INSERT` only** | `0038` ends with `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON account_adjustments`, so an INSERT trigger there sees every row that will ever exist | [`0026:88-107`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) revokes `UPDATE, DELETE` on `ledger_transactions` from `merit_app` **and `PUBLIC`**, and **not from the owner, which is the role every migration runs as**. [`0026:109-115`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) says single-column UPDATEs to append-only tables are performed by `SECURITY DEFINER` functions owned by `merit_migrator`, so **an UPDATE path to this table is a shape this estate has already designed for.** Measured, not assumed: section 5 row 6 builds the chain by `UPDATE` and watches an INSERT-only trigger miss it |
| **A "cannot see" branch beside the chain check** | `ADJ-C1` check 2 is kept as a second line and its own note says it is UNREACHABLE while a CHECK stands | **This branch is REACHABLE and it was measured in two isolation levels.** The FK guarantees the target exists, but the FK is checked AFTER the row trigger, so this function reads a row the FK has not yet vouched for. It fails closed |

**THE `WHEN` CLAUSE IS THE ARM AND THE EARLY RETURN IS NOT REDUNDANT.** The trigger fires only for rows carrying a `reversal_of`. The `IS NULL` return inside the function is kept for [`0027:97-100`](../../packages/db/migrations/0027_triggers_invariants.sql)'s stated reason, a guarantee that depends on a clause a later migration could drop being a guarantee with a dependency. **Measured rather than asserted**: with both removed, section 5 row 7 watches every ordinary posting in the ledger refused.

**AND WHAT IT DOES NOT DO, so the absences read as decisions.** No table, no column, no enum value, no ledger code, no index, no grant, no revoke, and no edit to a merged migration. **It does NOT bound how many reversals one ORIGINAL may have**; `0057`'s UNIQUE index already does and restating it here would be two objects claiming one rule. **It does NOT require that a reversal exist for anything**: `WD-C1` requires one on the failure path and this file is silent on every other path.

**HOW IT INTERACTS WITH `0057`'s UNIQUE INDEX, STATED HERE AND IN THE FILE HEADER RATHER THAN LEFT TO A READER, BECAUSE THE TWO LOOK LIKE ONE RULE AND ARE NOT.** `ledger_transactions_reversal_of_idx` refuses **two reversals of ONE transaction**: two rows carrying the SAME `reversal_of` value. `LEDGER-C3` refuses **a reversal OF a reversal**: one row whose `reversal_of` names a row that itself carries one. **Different rows, different columns, and neither implies the other.** A chain gives every row a different `reversal_of`, so the index is satisfied by it and always was; two reversals of one original are both un-chained, so `LEDGER-C3` is satisfied by them.

### 6. Findings this session found, which were not in the dispatch

**FINDING 1. THE DISPATCH AND [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) ROW `193` BOTH CITE *"[ADR-189](ADR-189.md) SECTION 5"* FOR THE TRIGGER-VERSUS-BUILDER ARGUMENT, AND IT IS SECTION 4.** Section 5 is *"Executed, not believed"*, an eight-row table of measurements. The argument is at [ADR-189:78](ADR-189.md), inside section 4, which runs from `ADR-189:64` to `ADR-189:93`. **The argument itself is exactly as described and this entry applies it**, so nothing downstream is wrong; the pointer is. **`RI-16` is blind to it** for [session 354](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-354.md)'s landmine 2 reason and because a section number is not a line citation at all.

**FINDING 2. THE CHAIN IS NOT MERELY UNENFORCED, IT DEFEATS `WD-C1` ON THE ONE PATH THE CORPUS HAS RULED, AND NEITHER THE DISPATCH NOR [ADR-189](ADR-189.md) SECTION 6 SAYS SO.** Both describe the chain as a general ledger rule left open. Section 1 measures what it does concretely: the obligation returns to **-25000** and the trader's claim is extinguished a second time, on a withdrawal already terminal at `failed`. **`WD-C1` cannot see it, because it is armed on `wallet_withdrawals` and that row never moves again.** ADR-189's judgement that the two holes were separable was right about their mechanisms and understated their consequence.

**FINDING 3. `WD-C1` IS BLIND TO EVERY LEDGER POSTING WRITTEN AFTER A WITHDRAWAL REACHES A TERMINAL STATE, AND THE CHAIN IS ONLY THE SHAPE THAT DOES IT WHILE LOOKING LIKE A CORRECTION.** `LEDGER-C3` closes the chain and **does not close this**. Any transaction posting against `withdrawals_in_flight` under `('wallet_withdrawal', <a settled or failed withdrawal>)` still lands unwatched. **It is reported and not closed**: the remedy is a guard on `ledger_entries` or on `ledger_transactions` that reads a withdrawal's status, which is a rule about a posting nobody has ruled and a slice this session does not hold. It belongs with the slice that wires `LT-06` and `LT-07`.

**FINDING 4. `0057`'s ARGUMENT FOR ITS OWN SCOPE IS UNCHANGED BY THIS ENTRY AND ITS EXPIRY CLAUSE IS RETIRED.** `0057:156-157` leaned on `account_adjustments` enforcing the chain rule *"where reversals are actually written today"*, and [session 354](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-354.md) landmine 3 registers that the sentence expires when a slice posts `LT-09`. **After `0059` the sentence does not need to expire**: the rule is no longer one table over. `0057` is not edited, because the sentence it carries was true when written and is now merely superseded by a stronger fact, which is what a later migration is for.

**FINDING 5. `assert_reversal_does_not_chain` COULD HAVE BEEN WRITTEN WITH A LOCAL VARIABLE NAMED `found`, AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS SESSION'S PROBE FUNCTION WAS.** PL/pgSQL's `FOUND` is a special variable; declaring `found boolean` shadows it, `IF NOT FOUND` then reads an uninitialised local, and **the branch silently never fires** while the FK quietly covers for it. It cost two mis-read race measurements before it was caught by instrumenting the function rather than reading it. **The shipped function declares one variable and it is not called `found`**, and this is recorded because it is `LEDGER-C1`'s own class of defect: a guard that reconciles perfectly and is wrong.

### 8. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.** **MONEY PATH. A MIGRATION LANDS.** The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is owed on [`0059`](../../packages/db/migrations/0059_reversal_chain.sql) and is **NOT discharged** by this entry, by the chain having been watched refused, or by this entry being merged. **Merging is not signing.**

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

**THE FIRST THING TO READ IS SECTION 2, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY PART OF THIS ENTRY THAT IS A JUDGEMENT RATHER THAN A MEASUREMENT.** That the chain lands, that a row-local `CHECK` cannot reach it, and that the trigger refuses it are all executed. **That a chain SHOULD be refused rests on reading `0009:104` and `0038:293-296` as ruling the re-application into `account_adjustments` rather than merely away from `reversal_of`.** A founder who reads those sentences as a note about one table rather than as a general ledger rule is reading them differently, and the consequence is concrete: this trigger would then be forbidding an operation the ledger has no other way to express. **The counter-argument this entry accepts is that nothing in the corpus asks for the re-application yet**, searched for and not found, so the ruling costs nothing today and could be revisited by a superseding migration if a real caller appears.

**THE SECOND IS THAT THIS GUARD BINDS EVERY REVERSAL IN THE LEDGER AND NOT ONLY `LT-09`.** That is the same widest-thing-in-the-diff `0057` had, one column over. `LT-03` is a reversal of `LT-01` and it may not be chained either, and neither may any reversal a later module posts. **This entry judges that correct because the argument in section 2 is about `reversal_of` and not about withdrawals**, but a founder who wants the rule scoped to one reference kind should say so now, because narrowing a trigger later is a supersession and migrations are never edited.
