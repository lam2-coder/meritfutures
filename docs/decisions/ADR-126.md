## ADR-126: the vocabulary that moves is the TABLE and never the REASON, and mint and resolve are not the same shape  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-120](ADR-120.md) measured that [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) unblocked everything a session can DO and nothing that MAKES one, and left two constructions unruled: minting a session, and resolving a person from the address they typed. It named them together, as B1 and B2, and said in its own words that "THE TWO TOGETHER ARE WHY A DEPLOYMENT STILL CANNOT MINT A SESSION". That is true of the CONSEQUENCE and false of the REMEDY.** This entry rules them apart. It rules that [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 1's FINDING is discharged rather than superseded and its DISPOSITION survives on an argument that is not its own; that RESOLVE is an authority problem and MINT is not; that the closed vocabulary a request handler needs is one of TABLES and never one of REASONS; and that a construction generic over the `derived` class would have handed a trader one leg of a double-entry posting, which is the measurement that turned a preference into a ruling.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `126`, session **238**, dispatched as `claude/auth-authority`. **MONEY PATH, auth, [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) strict.** The number was RESERVED before dispatch and the fence names it REQUIRED. **The design authority for the SHAPE is DELEGATED by the dispatch**, in [ADR-112](ADR-112.md)'s own words and for its own reason: [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2 and [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) clause 5 both said a `packages/db` authority ruling is the founder's, and this session is the founder taking it. **What is delegated is the shape and what is not is the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read**, which is owed on every line this lands and is not discharged by this entry's own approval line.

- **Context:** [session 218](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-218.md) declared `AuthBackend` blocked and every auth route on `main` answers `503` because of it. [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) removed one of the three reasons; [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) wired four of sixteen methods and reported the other two reasons as rulings it would not take in passing. This is those two.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) CLAUSE 1's FINDING IS DISCHARGED AND NOT SUPERSEDED. THE TWO CASES ARE DISTINGUISHABLE AND THE DISTINCTION IS CHECKABLE.** ADR-109's caller HELD a door and lacked a predicate: `idempotency_keys` is `owned`, so `scopedDb` reached it, and clause 1's own table records *"Reaches `idempotency_keys`? Yes"* on that row. [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) supplied the predicate and [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) section 6 wired the identity arm in full. **This session's callers hold the predicate and lack a door.** Nothing in clause 1 was wrong and nothing in it is overturned.
>
> **2. ITS DISPOSITION IS UPHELD ON AN ARGUMENT THAT IS NOT ITS OWN, AND THE OLD ARGUMENT IS RETIRED BY NAME.** Clause 1 refused a third `SystemReason` member because *"admitting `'request-handler'` would buy a door that still writes the whole table"*. **That sentence stopped being true the day ADR-112 merged** and it must not be cited again. `SystemReason` stays at two members and `SqlExecutorReason` at one, for a reason that is arithmetic rather than inherited: **a `SystemReason` member grants all 104 registered tables at six verbs, and the two constructions need one table at one verb each.**
>
> **3. THE VOCABULARY THAT MOVES IS THE TABLE AND NEVER THE REASON.** `SystemReason` and `SqlExecutorReason` are closed vocabularies of WHY and every member of either grants EVERY TABLE. A closed vocabulary of WHICH grants exactly what it names. **The control is identical in kind and strictly smaller in surface**: joining either list is a diff on [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) with an argument attached, which is the whole of what [ADR-084](ADR-084.md) accepted about `systemDb`.
>
> **4. RESOLVE IS AN AUTHORITY PROBLEM AND IT IS A FOURTH DOOR, NOT A THIRD REASON.** `resolutionDb()` is READ ONLY, NON-TRANSACTIONAL, returns AT MOST ONE ROW, has no `transaction()` overload and therefore cannot participate in a write at any authority. It reaches a closed set of tables **by a closed set of columns per table**, because the table alone is not enough: finding 5 measures that `users.id` is also unique, and a table-only list makes every user row readable pre-authentication by naming a uuid. This is [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) clause 3's move exactly, and [`scoped-db.ts:939`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) states it there in its own words: *"SO THE VOCABULARY STAYS AT TWO MEMBERS AND A NARROWER DOOR IS ADDED."*
>
> **5. MINT IS NOT AN AUTHORITY PROBLEM AND TREATING IT AS ONE WOULD HAVE WIDENED A VOCABULARY FOR A CONSTRUCTION THAT DID NOT NEED IT.** By the time a handler mints, it has resolved, so it HOLDS an identity. What it lacks is a way to write a `derived` row whose parent it can prove. `ScopedTx.insertUnder` requires the caller to NAME the parent and PROVES it, by reading the via table through `scopePredicate` **on the same transaction source**, before any INSERT is sent. Zero proving rows is a throw and the row is not written.
>
> **6. `insertUnder`'s KEY TYPE IS A CLOSED LIST AND NOT THE `derived` CLASS, AND THAT IS FINDING 6 MADE INTO A RULE.** `ledger_entries` is `derived` with a `hop`, so the class-generic version, which reads correctly and which this session's first draft had, is money creation behind a handle every authenticated request already holds. **The generalisation was measured before it was refused.**
>
> **7. THE THIRD CONSTRUCTION IS NAMED AND NOT BUILT.** Establishing an identity (`identities`, `class: 'root'`) has no door and no caller. Section 7.

### 3. Clause 1, which is the work the dispatch asked for, done in the direction it did not expect

**The dispatch put the question as a fork:** *"you must either distinguish the two cases or supersede that clause, and saying WHICH is the work."* **The answer is DISTINGUISH, and the reason it is not SUPERSEDE is a fact rather than a preference.**

| | [ADR-109](ADR-109.md)'s case | This entry's cases |
|---|---|---|
| The table | `idempotency_keys`, `class: 'owned'` | `users` (`owned`) and `sessions` (`derived`) |
| Did the caller hold a door? | **YES.** `scopedDb(identityId)` reaches an `owned` table, and clause 1's own table says so | **NO.** `scopedDb` needs the identity the read is trying to FIND, and cannot INSERT a `derived` row at all |
| What was missing | **A PREDICATE.** Every method narrowed by tenancy or by nothing | **A WORD.** Both acts are already expressible at `systemTx` (finding 3) |
| Who discharged it | [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) built it, [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) wired it | This entry |

**SO CLAUSE 1's FINDING IS NOT MET BY A COUNTER-EXAMPLE. IT IS MET BY A DIFFERENT QUESTION**, and the honest record is that ADR-109 answered its own question correctly and this one is not it.

**WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE IS THE REASON UNDER THE DISPOSITION, AND IT IS RETIRED IN PUBLIC RATHER THAN QUIETLY REPLACED.** ADR-109 clause 1's five-row table has four rows failing on one column, *"Can name one row?"*, including the hypothetical `systemDb('request-handler')`. Every one of those four rows now reads YES. **A reader who cites that table today cites a measurement of a tree that no longer exists**, and this is the entry that says so. [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) section 5 reached the same conclusion first and declined to act on it because acting was not its subject; that judgement is upheld and this entry is the subject.

### 4. Clause 2 and 3, and the BOLA surface of every widening this entry considered, priced

**[ADR-008](ADR-008.md) scoped the accessor precisely to bound the BOLA surface, so a widening is priced in that currency and in no other.**

| Widening | Tables it grants | Verbs | Rows per call | Tenancy in the predicate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A third `SystemReason` member** | **104**, the whole registry | **6** (`rows`, `insert`, `rowsWhere`, `rowAt`, `updateAt`, `deleteAt`) | unbounded on `rows` | none |
| **A second `SqlExecutorReason` member** | 104, plus every table with no rule at all | arbitrary SQL | unbounded | none, and no accessor control survives it |

**THE THIRD ROW IS THE ONE THIS SESSION NEARLY TOOK AND IT IS WHY THE TABLE IS HERE.** It looks like the narrow choice beside row one, and on the column that matters it is not: 19 tables includes the one where a single-row insert is a money-creating write. **A number that is small is not the same as a surface that is bounded**, and the only reason this entry knows the difference is that the class was enumerated instead of trusted.

**AND ROW FOUR IS WHY A TABLE LIST WAS NOT ENOUGH EITHER.** `users` carries `id` and `email` as unique keys. With the table admitted and the column free, `resolutionDb().rowAt('users', { id })` reads any user's row, pre-authentication, on a uuid. **That is not a leak somebody would notice**, because it reads exactly like the resolve the door was built for. `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` is the second half of the vocabulary and naming `id` is `TS2353` at the call site with a throw behind it.

**WHAT THE RESOLUTION DOOR DOES NOT BOUND, STATED RATHER THAN LEFT.** It cannot stop a handler DISCLOSING what it learned. `POST /auth/otp` deliberately never confirms whether an address exists, and [`0002_identity.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) puts the reason on `otp_challenges.attempts` in its own comment: *"Lockout WITHOUT enabling user enumeration."* The door hands a handler one row or `undefined`; **whether the response distinguishes those two is the handler's ruling and not the accessor's**, and no shape of this door could make it the accessor's.

### 5. Clause 5, and the inversion at its centre

**ON AN `owned` TABLE THE ACCESSOR STAMPS THE TENANCY AND REFUSES A CALLER WHO NAMES IT. ON A `derived` TABLE IT MUST DO THE OPPOSITE, AND THE REASON IS [ADR-041](ADR-041.md).**

`scopedInsertStatement` writes `identity_id` from the handle because the handle KNOWS it, and `refuseTenancyColumn` refuses a caller who supplies one, because *"silently overwriting a value somebody wrote is how a wrong belief survives a code review"*. **A `derived` row has no such value to write.** `sessions` reaches an identity through `users`, an identity may hold MORE THAN ONE user, and `scope.ts` says why in the `users` rule itself: *"A USER IS A LOGIN AND AN IDENTITY IS THE PERSON."* So `user_id` is a choice among this identity's own children and only the caller knows which.

**THAT IS [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) SECTION 5's SECOND DRAFT FINDING, ONE CLASS ALONG.** That draft refused a scoped caller naming a `derived` rule's local column in an ADDRESS, and the tree refused the draft: `analytics_snapshots` was left with no addressed write at all, because *"one identity owns many accounts, so the caller's half really does have to name which"*. **The same sentence is true of an INSERT and it points the same way.**

**SO THE PARENT IS READ AND NOT GUESSED, AND [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) SECTION 4's OBJECTION IS ANSWERED RATHER THAN IGNORED.** That section named this construction and declined to build it, on the argument that establishing the parent *"inside a transaction is a `SELECT` the caller could have skipped and outside one is a race"*.

- **The race half is why the read is on `source` and not on `client()`.** The proof and the INSERT are one unit of work on one connection, so the parent cannot be re-parented between them.
- **The redundancy half is a cost this entry accepts, on [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) clause 2's own trade.** That clause paid a run-time uniqueness check to make an addressed write name one row *"BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by the caller's care"*. This pays one indexed primary-key lookup to make a session unmintable for anybody else by the same standard. **A caller that already read the parent pays that lookup twice, and the alternative is a guarantee that holds because every caller remembered.**

**THE REFUSAL IS ORDERED AND THE ORDER IS THE CONTROL.** Zero proving rows raises before any INSERT is built, and the suite asserts on the RECORDER rather than only on the throw: a refusal that raises after writing the row would pass a test that only watched the exception.

**AND `hop` ONLY.** A `semi-join` is one-to-many in the direction traversed, so proving the parent proves that SOME row of this identity's is on the other end rather than that this one is. `ledger_transactions` is the registry's only `semi-join` and it is refused by the type before the run-time check is reached; the check is the half that survives the registry moving.

### 6. What a caller writes, and what it cannot

```
// RESOLVE: no identity, one table, one column, at most one row.
const user = await resolutionDb().rowAt('users', { email: typed });

// MINT: the identity the resolve returned, and the parent PROVED.
await transaction(scopedDb(identityId), (tx) =>
  tx.insertUnder('sessions', { userId, refreshTokenHash, expiresAt, authFactor }),
);
```

| Written by hand | Refused by |
|---|---|
| `resolutionDb().rowAt('users', { id })` | `TS2353`. The column is outside `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` |
| `resolutionDb().rowAt('sessions', { id })` | `TS2345`. The table is outside `ResolvableTableKey` |
| `transaction(resolutionDb(), fn)` | `TS2769`, on all three overloads. There is no fourth |
| `tx.insertUnder('ledgerEntries', { ledgerAccountId })` | **`TS2345`. THE MONEY-CREATION REFUSAL, AT COMPILE TIME** |
| `tx.insertUnder('purchases', { id })` | `TS2345`. `purchases` is `owned`, and `insert` stamps it |
| `tx.insert('sessions', { userId })` | `TS2345`, unmoved. `OwnedTableKey` excludes `derived` |
| `tx.insertUnder('sessions', { refreshTokenHash })` | a throw: the parent is not named and the handle cannot stamp it |
| `tx.insertUnder('sessions', { user_id })` | a throw: Drizzle keys values by PROPERTY name, so the SQL spelling would be dropped from the statement and the parent proved against a value the row never carried |
| `tx.insertUnder('sessions', { userId: <another identity's> })` | **zero proving rows, a throw, and NO INSERT SENT** |

### 7. The third construction, which nobody has named and this entry does not build

**`identities` IS `class: 'root'` AND `OwnedTableKey` EXCLUDES IT.** [`scoped-db.ts:283`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) gives the reason at the type: *"inserting one CREATES an identity. A handle already bound to an identity has no correct row to write there."* So an address that resolves to NOBODY cannot be signed up at any authority a request handler holds, and that is a third gap on the same endpoint.

**IT IS REPORTED AND NOT BUILT, AND THE REASON IS THE ONE THAT DISTINGUISHES IT FROM THE OTHER TWO.** [`job-queue.ts`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts)'s rule is that a primitive admitted before a caller exists is a primitive nobody can remove. **The other two constructions have a caller that is blocked ON THEM and named by a signed-pending entry; this one has no caller at all**, because the endpoint that would sign somebody up is `POST /auth/verify`, which is blocked on the OTP digest whatever this file does.

**AND IT IS A HARDER RULING THAN EITHER OF THESE, WHICH IS WHY IT IS WORTH A NUMBER RATHER THAN A LINE.** A sign-up writes `identities` AND `users` in one unit of work, at an authority that by definition has no identity, and it is the one act in this estate where a caller genuinely creates a scope from nothing. **A `resolutionDb` shaped door will not serve it**, because that door is read only, deliberately, and section 4's price for making it otherwise has not been paid by anybody.

### 8. Why building here is correct and `mintSessionToken` was not

**[ADR-120](ADR-120.md) section 4 refused to write `mintSessionToken` on `job-queue.ts`'s rule, and that refusal was right.** The distinction is which end of the stack the gap is at. `mintSessionToken` sat ABOVE the gap: nothing could insert the row it would mint for, so it was a helper built on air. **These two constructions ARE the gap.** [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) is the precedent in the same direction and it is exact: it built `updateAt` for four callers, none of which existed in the tree when it merged, and its section 12 opened by saying so.

**AND THE HONEST CONSEQUENCE IS STATED HERE RATHER THAN BURIED IN SECTION 11.** This entry unblocks **no endpoint on its own**. [`auth-backend.ts`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts) gives `verifyOtp` three independent blockers, `NO_PRE_IDENTITY_READ`, `NO_DERIVED_INSERT` and `NO_OTP_DIGEST`. **This removes the two that are `packages/db`'s.** The third needs a deployment secret or a migration, and [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) finding 9 is right that it is not a wiring slice's and right that it is not timidity.

### 10. The seed that turned NOTHING red, reported rather than smoothed over

**`refuseUnaddressed` inside `resolutionPredicate` was deleted and the whole suite stayed green.** That is [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) section 8's own warning arriving in this file: *"a guard with nothing to find looks exactly like a guard finding nothing wrong."*

**THE GUARD IS UNREACHABLE ON TODAY'S VOCABULARY AND THAT IS THE HONEST DIAGNOSIS.** `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` declares one column, that column is unique, and `refuseUnresolvableAddress` runs first and refuses everything else, so no input exists that reaches the fold and fails it. **It is a guard against a future member and not against a present caller.**

**SO IT IS ASSERTED WHERE IT CAN BE AND SAID TO BE WHAT IT IS.** One case reads the call site out of the source and turns red when the call is removed; another asserts, mechanically over `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS`, that every declared column is a unique key `schema.ts` carries, which is the property the guard exists to check. **Neither is a behavioural assertion and the suite says so at the site rather than letting a later reader assume otherwise.** The guard stays, on the fail-closed argument that the day a second member lands it is the thing standing between a non-unique column and a many-row read at an authority carrying no tenancy at all.

### 12. Alternatives considered

- **A third `SystemReason` member.** Refused on clause 2 and priced in section 4: 104 tables at six verbs, in exchange for one table at one verb. **It is a genuinely different offer from the one [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) declined and it is still the wrong one**, and this entry says which of those two sentences is new.

- **One establishment door serving both acts.** Refused, and it is the shape this entry started with.
