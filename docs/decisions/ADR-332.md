## ADR-332: the pool executor ADR-331 refused to mint. A closed vocabulary is widened by PARTITION, and the door that outlives a transaction may not leave one behind  (2026-09-05, status: proposed)

**[ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 4 clause 3 ruled that the constructor executor and its reason are *"OWED TOGETHER, TO A ROW WHOSE SUBJECT THEY ARE"*, and clause 4 ruled that the one-door row may not take them as a means.** This entry is that row. It reproduces the blocker ADR-331 measured, mints the second `SqlExecutorReason` member and the pool-shaped producer that spends it, and answers the question a pool-shaped raw-SQL door on the money accessor has to answer: **what stops it becoming `client()` under another name.** **The half worth reading is section 4**, because three of its four legs were already true and the fourth had to be built after a measurement said the obvious design was unsafe.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `332`, branch `claude/a-pool-that-outlives-the-transaction`, session **523**. [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 5, on [ADR-084](ADR-084.md) section 2's pool and [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 5's rule about whose row an authority change is.

- **MONEY PATH BY FILE.** [`packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) is the accessor every money read and write goes through, and this diff widens a deliberately closed vocabulary on it and publishes a new door from it. **FOUNDER `E2` READ OWED**, and six reads are inherited ([ADR-331](ADR-331.md), [ADR-327](ADR-327.md), [ADR-328](ADR-328.md), [ADR-329](ADR-329.md), [ADR-330](ADR-330.md) and [ADR-303](ADR-303.md)'s repaired citation), all `proposed` and all UNSIGNED.

- **NOT A MIGRATION AND NO SQL.** `git diff --stat 3cbd7223..HEAD -- packages/db/migrations scripts/db` is EMPTY. `0082` already granted what the measurement needed and [ADR-327](ADR-327.md) measured the set.

### 2. THE REPRODUCTION, WHICH IS ADR-331's AND THEN ONE STEP FURTHER

#### 2.1 How it was taken

`0001`..`0082` applied forward-only from empty under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1` against **PostgreSQL 16.13**, the estate [ADR-327](ADR-327.md) section 3.1 measured its grant on. **118 base tables in `public` and 12 in `pgboss`**, which is [ADR-331](ADR-331.md)'s count reproduced rather than copied. The queue was constructed by [`pgBossQueue`](../../packages/queue/src/pg-boss-queue.ts) and every statement ran as `merit_app`, so nothing reached the database with more authority than the application has.

#### 2.2 One `locked()` plan, and the caller's transaction is gone

Verbatim from the transcript, on **today's `sqlExecutorOn`, before any edit**:

```
--- A. role: {"current_user":"merit_app"}
--- A. before any plan: {"second_statement_of_a_transaction":true}
--- A. declareQueue() returned
--- A. after ONE locked() plan: {"second_statement_of_a_transaction":false}
```

`now()` is the transaction's clock and `statement_timestamp()` is the statement's, so the pair reads `true` inside a transaction that has run a statement and `false` in autocommit. **ONE PLAN AND THE FLAG FLIPS.** `declareQueue` is a `locked()` plan, so it bites at construction, exactly as ADR-331 measured.

#### 2.3 WHAT THE LOST TRANSACTION COSTS, WHICH ADR-331 DID NOT MEASURE

**A FLAG FLIPPING IS EVIDENCE AND NOT YET A PRICE, SO THE PRICE WAS TAKEN.** In the same block, after that plan, an `enqueue` ran on the same handle and then the caller FAILED, so `transaction()` issued its `ROLLBACK`:

```
--- A. enqueue inside the SAME caller block returned: a job id
--- A. the caller threw, so transaction() ROLLED BACK: the caller fails, so `transaction` must ROLL BACK
--- A. jobs on the queue after the caller ROLLED BACK: {"n":1}
--- A. queue rows the rolled-back block created: {"n":1}
```

**THE JOB SURVIVED A ROLLBACK THAT WAS SUPPOSED TO TAKE IT.** That is [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s central consequence stated as its own failure: *"committed the purchase, lost the provisioning job"*, inverted into "rolled back the purchase, kept the provisioning job". The mechanism is not subtle: the plan's `COMMIT` ended the caller's transaction, every later statement on that connection was autocommit, and `transaction()`'s `ROLLBACK` had nothing left to undo. **A supervisor built on the transaction-bound executor does not merely fail; it silently converts the one property the enqueue path exists for into its opposite**, and it does so at construction, before any Merit code has run a line of its own.

### 3. THE RULING

> **1. `SqlExecutorReason` GAINS `'job-supervisor'`, AND THE VOCABULARY IS STILL CLOSED BECAUSE IT IS NOW PARTITIONED.**
>
> The member admits exactly the statements pg-boss runs on the handle it is constructed with: queue metadata, the polling fetch, maintenance and completion. **It does NOT admit the enqueue**, which is `'job-enqueue'` on a caller's transaction, and it does not admit any statement a Merit module writes for itself.
>
> **THE TWO WORDS ARE NOT TWO CALLERS AND NOT TWO SERVICES. THEY ARE TWO CONNECTION LIFETIMES**, and a lifetime is the one thing about this shape no type can carry. [`job-queue.ts:64-66`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts) says so in its own words: *"being inside a transaction is a fact about a connection at a moment and no TypeScript type observes a moment."* [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 3 refuses a member that names a SERVICE, on the ground that `SystemReason` names KINDS OF ACCESS; this member names a kind of access that the type system cannot express and that section 2.3 measures the cost of confusing.
>
> **AND THE WIDENING BOUGHT NO CALLER A ROW IT COULD NOT ALREADY REACH.** [`scoped-db.ts:3557`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) and [`:3560`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) declare `TransactionSqlExecutorReason` and `PoolSqlExecutorReason` as `Extract`s of ONE member each; [`:3587`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) narrows `TxCommon.sqlExecutor` to the first and [`:4319`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) narrows the pool door to the second. **`tx.sqlExecutor('job-supervisor')` and `poolSqlExecutor('job-enqueue')` are both compile errors**, and both are also runtime throws past a cast. A third member is still a diff on this file with an argument attached, and it now also has to say which producer it belongs to, because a member belonging to neither is a word nothing can spend.
>
> **2. THE POOL DOOR IS `poolSqlExecutor(reason)`, EXPORTED FROM `packages/db` AND FROM NOWHERE ELSE.** It resolves the pool through `poolFromClient()`, which reads `client()`, so it is the pool every `transaction(handle, fn)` in that file already runs on. **It does not open one**, which is [ADR-084](ADR-084.md) section 2's ruling rather than tidiness: `pg` was chosen over postgres.js at a measured cost of 14 packages BECAUSE *"a transaction is per-connection, so a second driver is a second pool and that consequence stops being implementable"*.
>
> **3. `client()` IS NOT EXPORTED AND THIS ENTRY DOES NOT MOVE TOWARD EXPORTING IT.** Section 4 is what makes that more than an assurance.
>
> **4. THE TWO EXECUTORS SHARE ONE NORMALISER AND IT IS A RULING RATHER THAN A REFACTOR.** Section 5.
>
> **5. NOTHING IS WIRED, NOTHING IS SCHEDULED AND THE ONE-DOOR MODULE IS NOT WRITTEN.** [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 4 clause 4 is the reason, and it is still the reason after this row: the importer under `apps/worker/src` is the THIRD row of the three that entry said this sequence needs.

### 4. WHAT STOPS THIS BECOMING "RUN ANY SQL ON THE POOL", WHICH IS THE ONLY QUESTION THAT MATTERS HERE

**[ADR-331](ADR-331.md) SECTION 4 CLAUSE 3 SIZED THIS CORRECTLY AND IT IS QUOTED RATHER THAN SOFTENED**: *"A pool-shaped raw-SQL door runs arbitrary statements, unscoped, outside any transaction, on the money database. That is a bigger authority object than the transaction-bound one."* Four legs answer it. Three were available and the fourth had to be built, because a measurement said the obvious design was unsafe.

**LEG 1. THE WORD.** `'job-supervisor'` is refused past a cast at [`scoped-db.ts:4319`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)'s own guard, on `sqlExecutorOn`'s stated reason: so the parameter is not decorative. "Ran raw SQL outside every transaction" is a diff a reviewer reads. This is [ADR-084](ADR-084.md) section 5's *"forgot to scope" becomes "wrote the word system"*, one door over.

**LEG 2. THE PARTITION**, section 3 clause 1. The interesting half is the direction that is easy to miss: widening a vocabulary normally widens every producer that reads it, and here it widened neither.

**LEG 3. IT HANDS OUT ONE METHOD AND NOT A HANDLE.** The value is `{ executeSql }` and nothing else, which is the shape the three transaction authorities already return. There is no `connect`, no Drizzle handle, no schema, no key vocabulary; and `transaction()` has no overload taking this value, so **there is no argument position anywhere in `scoped-db.ts` where it becomes a scope**. That is the difference from `client()` stated structurally: `client()` hands out a handle a caller can do anything with, repeatedly; this hands out one statement at a time and cannot be composed into a unit of work AT THE ACCESSOR.

#### LEG 4, WHICH WAS GOING TO BE A SENTENCE AND IS A MECHANISM, BECAUSE THE MEASUREMENT REFUSED THE SENTENCE

**THE SENTENCE THIS ROW EXPECTED TO WRITE WAS "and it cannot be composed into a unit of work at the DATABASE either, because every statement runs on its own pooled connection". THAT SENTENCE IS FALSE AND IT WAS MEASURED FALSE BEFORE IT WAS WRITTEN.**

```
--- L. first: {"in_a_transaction":false,"pid":17678}
--- L. after a bare BEGIN through pool.query: {"in_a_transaction":true,"pid":17678}
--- L. and again: {"in_a_transaction":true,"pid":17678}
--- L. after a BALANCED one-string plan: {"in_a_transaction":false,"pid":17678}
--- L. idle clients in the pool: 1 total: 1
--- L. destroyed pid 17678 ; next checkout pid: 17679 in_a_transaction: false
```

`pg`'s pool returns a released client to its IDLE SET and hands the same backend to the next caller. **One `pg_backend_pid` for three statements, and the transaction opened by the first outlives it.** So a door built on `pool.query` lets a caller open a transaction and leave it open on a connection somebody else will check out, and **on this pool the next caller can be `transaction()` running a money write**: it would issue `BEGIN` into an existing transaction and its `COMMIT` would commit whatever the previous statement left behind. That is not a queue defect. **It is this door poisoning the accessor it lives in**, which is the exact class `client()` staying unexported exists to prevent, arrived at from inside `packages/db` rather than from an app.

**SO THE DOOR RELEASES WITH DESTROY WHENEVER THE BACKEND IS NOT IDLE.** The decision is read from `pg`'s own published `getTransactionStatus()` (`@types/pg` declares it `"I" | "T" | "E" | null`), which is the backend's last `ReadyForQuery` and not anything this file infers. A statement that opens a transaction and does not close it burns its connection instead of pooling it, and nothing it left behind, the transaction or a `SET` or a temp table, can be found by anybody else. A `null` after a completed statement can only mean the driver stopped answering, so the connection is destroyed FIRST and the failure raised after: **fail closed, then loudly**, which is `poolFromClient`'s own shape for Drizzle's `$client`.

**AND THE COST OF LEG 4 IS ZERO ON EVERYTHING THE ONE ADMITTED CALLER SENDS, MEASURED RATHER THAN HOPED.** `locked()` closes its own `BEGIN` inside ONE string, so the backend is idle when the statement returns and the connection is pooled like any other. Over the full run in section 6: **75 statements, 5 backends.** A door that destroyed unconditionally would have opened 75 connections against the money database for one supervise pass, forever, which is a real operational cost paid for a hazard no balanced statement has. **The direction of that error matters and [ADR-327](ADR-327.md) section 3.3 already named it: a too-wide grant is caught by review and a too-narrow one is caught by an outage.** Connection churn on the money database is the outage-shaped mistake here, and it is not taken.

### 5. Whether the two executors share a code path, decided rather than defaulted

**THEY SHARE ONE, `oneResultFrom` at [`scoped-db.ts:4158`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), AND BOTH PRODUCERS ROUTE THROUGH IT.**

**THE OBJECTION IS REAL AND IT IS ANSWERED RATHER THAN IGNORED.** Appendix `F2`'s rule of three says *"no shared helper until the third call site exists"*, and a helper extracted at two callers is exactly the speculative abstraction that section names as a tell. **What that rule is written against is a helper invented for a HYPOTHETICAL second caller.** This one has two REAL call sites on the day it lands, and what they share is not a convenience but a VENDOR CONTRACT: `IDatabase.executeSql` is ONE contract, read out of `pg-boss/dist/types.d.ts` by a case that already exists.

**THE DECIDING ARGUMENT IS WHAT THE DUPLICATE WOULD COST WHEN IT DRIFTED.** [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 7's finding is that the multi-statement answer was *"modelled by nothing"* and a measured defect could not turn a case red. A second copy of that flattening would reproduce that situation on the new half: the tree would carry two answers to one contract, and the case that watches it reads a producer BY NAME, so the copy nobody named would be the one that was wrong. **A semantic duplication whose divergence is invisible to the test that exists is not the same risk as a duplicated three-line helper**, and the rule of three is about the second.

**AND THE CASE MOVED WITH THE DECISION RATHER THAN BEING LEFT ASSERTING HALF OF IT.** [`surface.test.ts`](../../packages/queue/test/surface.test.ts)'s third case now reads `oneResultFrom` for the branch and then asserts that **both** `sqlExecutorOn` and `poolSqlExecutor` normalise through it, which is a stronger property than the branch appearing twice: a producer that stopped routing through it fails, and so does a producer that was added and never wired to it.

### 6. The pool executor, measured against the same estate

**THE SHIPPED DOOR, WRAPPED ONLY IN A COUNTER THAT DELEGATES EVERY CALL**, handed to `pgBossQueue` and driven end to end. Verbatim:

```
--- B. role on a pooled connection: {"current_user":"merit_app"}
--- B. start()
--- B. declareQueue()
--- C. enqueue inside a transaction that COMMITS returned: a job id
--- C. enqueue inside a transaction that ROLLS BACK returned: a job id
--- C2. jobs on the queue after one COMMIT and one ROLLBACK: 1
--- C2. the committed id is present: true
--- C2. the rolled-back id is present: false
--- D. leases cleared; waiting 65s for one supervise pass
--- D. failures observed: 0
--- D. statements seen: 75 (60 during the pass)
--- D. backends the pool opened for ALL of it: 5
--- D. {"name":"__pgboss__send-it","monitored":true,"maintained":true,"queued_count":0}
--- D. {"name":"adr-332-b","monitored":true,"maintained":true,"queued_count":1}
--- END. failures observed: 0
```

**ZERO FAILURES ACROSS A FULL SUPERVISE PASS.** Failures were counted from `unhandledRejection` and `uncaughtException`, because [`pgBossQueue`](../../packages/queue/src/pg-boss-queue.ts) publishes five methods and none of them is an emitter, so a caller cannot subscribe to the vendor's `error` event. That limit is [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 10 item 3's and it is unchanged here.

**BOTH PHASES ARE SHOWN TO HAVE RUN BY READING THE DATABASE RATHER THAN BY COUNTING STATEMENTS.** `maintain_on` and `monitor_on` were set to `NULL` before the pass, which is [ADR-327](ADR-327.md) section 3.3's trap cleared rather than rediscovered: the first supervise iteration takes an 86,400-second `maintain_on` lease, and a pass measured against it reports the maintain phase as unreachable. Afterwards every queue reads monitored AND maintained, and `queued_count` reads `1` for the queue holding the committed job. **That counter is written by `cacheQueueStats`, the exact statement whose rows [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) measured being lost**, so a green run here means something a statement count could not say.

**AND `enqueue` IS RE-MEASURED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE PATH THAT WORKED BEFORE AND THE ONE THING THIS ROW COULD BREAK.** Enqueued through `tx.sqlExecutor('job-enqueue')` inside a transaction that COMMITS, the job is on the queue; inside one that ROLLS BACK, it is not. **The contrast with section 2.3 is the whole entry in two lines**: the same two enqueues, against a constructor executor of the wrong lifetime and of the right one, and only the second pair behaves the way [ADR-006](ADR-006.md) requires.
