### 3. Both readers, re-derived, and the third one nobody had counted

**THE TWO SPELLINGS ARE QUOTED IN SECTION 1 AND THEY ARE REAL.** What section 5 of [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) did not measure is WHY each reader spells its source the way it does, and the answer is in the manifests:

| Deployable | Declares `@merit/db` | Reads | Spelling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `apps/worker` | **YES** (`workspace:*`) | a Drizzle driver row | camelCase |
| `apps/api` | **YES** (`workspace:*`) | a Drizzle driver row | camelCase |
| `apps/site` | **NO. It declares `@merit/rules-engine`, `next`, `react` and `react-dom`** | Merit's own HTTP response | snake_case |

**SO NEITHER READER CHOSE ITS SPELLING AND NEITHER CAN GIVE IT UP.** `apps/site` has no database to read; snake_case is the wire contract it is transcribing, not a naming taste. `apps/worker` has no wire; camelCase is what Drizzle returns. **A "canonical decoder" over the two is a decoder over two DIFFERENT inputs**, and two decoders over two different inputs are not `FM-16`: `FM-16` is one predicate stated twice, and _"what does `plan_version_sizes` look like after Drizzle"_ and _"what does it look like after JSON over HTTP"_ are two predicates that happen to share the name of their output type.

**AND `apps/api` IS ALREADY ON THE DRIVER SIDE, WHICH IS THE THIRD READER AND THE ONE THAT DECIDES THE ROW.** `readSize` at [`routes/catalog.ts:1242`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts) reads `size_cents: cents(row, 'sizeCents', PLAN_VERSION_SIZES),` off `rowsWhere` on the same `firm` handle the payout transaction would use. It returns a LOCAL `PlanVersionSizeRow` ([`routes/catalog.ts:305`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) which carries `price_cents` and `reset_price_cents` and omits the four floor-lock and daily-loss fields the engine's carries, so a count of the ENGINE type's producers cannot see it. **That is why [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) found two and there are three.**

**SO THE PAYOUT PATH NEEDS NO RENAME FROM ANYBODY.** It needs the camelCase read `apps/worker` already has, in a home it can reach.

### 4. The four shapes a canonical decoder could take, each refused on its own ground

| Shape | Refused because |
| --- | --- |
| **Canonical on snake_case (the column names)** | `apps/worker` and `apps/api` would each map a driver row into column names first, which is the transcription they already have, written twice more. Nothing is collapsed; a step is added to the two callers that were correct |
| **Canonical on camelCase (the property names)** | The engine's `PlanVersionSizeRow` field names ARE the column names by its own header (_"transcribed column for column from `0004_catalog.sql`"_, [`types.ts:587`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)), so the decoder's INPUT keys would stop matching its OUTPUT keys; and `apps/site` would rename against a wire contract it does not own |
| **One decoder parameterized by a key mapping** | A decoder that does not know its own source. The mapping becomes the thing nothing checks, and a typo in it is a silent wrong-column read on a money value: strictly worse than the two readers it replaces, because each of those is checked by its own compiler against one real source |
| **Two decoders, both in the engine** | `FM-16` relocated rather than closed, and the driver-side one puts `packages/db`'s property names inside the package `RI-01` exists to keep free of them |

**NONE OF THESE IS CHEAP AND NONE OF THEM BUYS ANYTHING**, which is what "the rename is not worth it" means here concretely: there is no arrangement in which one caller renames and the tree ends with fewer statements of one predicate than it started with.

### 5. What `plan` waits on, named: a HOME rather than a ruling

### 6. The cap schedule, which is the real `FM-16`, and why it is not taken either

**THE SPELLING RULING DOES NOT REACH INSIDE THE COLUMN.** `from_ordinal` and `cap_cents` are what Postgres stores, so every reader asks for the same two keys whatever it asked for the row:

| Reader | Row spelling | Blob keys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `toCapScheduleCents` ([`apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts:1320`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)) | camelCase | `from_ordinal`, `cap_cents` |
| `decodeCapSteps` ([`apps/site/src/catalog/adapter.ts:520`](../../apps/site/src/catalog/adapter.ts)) | snake_case | `from_ordinal`, `cap_cents` |
| `readCapSchedule` ([`apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts:1219`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) | camelCase | `from_ordinal`, `cap_cents` |

**SO THIS HALF IS ONE PREDICATE STATED THREE TIMES WITH NOTHING COMPARING THEM, ON THE VALUE THAT FIXES A PAYOUT CEILING.** That is `FM-16` by name, and it is the one thing in this area that a codec in `packages/rules-engine` could close without a rename and without knowing a property name: `SizeCapScheduleStep` is already the engine's exported type ([`types.ts:567`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)).

**THE CENSUS IS TAKEN BY THE QUOTED KEY AND NOT BY THE NAME, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THE WHOLE MEASUREMENT.** A file that reads `'cap_cents'` as a STRING is pulling it out of an untyped bag, which is a decode; a file that writes `.cap_cents` is reading a field off a value somebody already decoded. The broad question returns **eleven** files under `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src`, of which five are consumers (`apps/portal/src/view/eligibility.ts` and `packages/rules-engine/src/plan/resolve.ts` among them); the quoted-key question returns exactly the three above. Measured before the list was written down.

**AND IT IS NOT TAKEN HERE. THREE REASONS, AND THE THIRD IS THE ONE THAT DECIDED IT.**

1. **IT MOVES NO BLOCKER.** After it lands, `apps/api` still cannot produce a `PlanVersionSizeRow` from `catalogRowAt`'s `unknown`, because the row-level read is what section 5 refuses. The port waits on exactly what it waited on before.
2. **ITS RIGHT HOME IS INSIDE THE ROW DECODER THAT DOES NOT EXIST.** Whenever the row decoder lands, the cap-schedule decode goes inside it. Landing it standalone now commits a name on the engine's public surface, which [ADR-078](ADR-078.md) rules is _"a way for a caller to reimplement a rule slightly differently"_ and is the one export test this package applies to every addition, to a shape chosen before the question it serves has an answer.
3. **THE COLLAPSE IT WOULD REQUIRE IS A BEHAVIOUR-PRESERVING CODE MOTION THROUGH THE NIGHTLY FOLD.** `apps/worker`'s and `apps/site`'s cap readers are already CORRECT and already identical in behaviour, so routing both through the engine changes nothing either does. **The founder's `E2` read is the scarcest thing in this process, and spending one on `apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts` for a diff with no behaviour in it, in a session whose objective was a ruling, is spending it on the cheapest thing in the diff.** Row `286` said a ruling is a complete outcome; it did not say a ruling plus an unrelated refactor is a better one.

**AND WRITING THE CODEC WITHOUT THE COLLAPSE IS THE TRAP ROW `286` NAMED**, which is why neither half is taken: a fourth statement of the blob is `FM-16` on the money path, and [ADR-269](ADR-269.md) refused exactly that one port over for this same value.

**WHAT REPLACES THE CODE IS A CENSUS, SO THE FOLLOW-UP IS CHECKABLE RATHER THAN REMEMBERED.** `rule-state-producibility.test.ts` link 7 now holds the cap-schedule census at exactly three, names each of the three, asserts the list carries no `packages/` member, and asserts the divergence below as a live property. **A fourth statement turns it red and that is right; the day the collapse lands, the same case is what says so.**

### 7. What is reported rather than repaired

1. **`readCapSchedule` ([`apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts:1219`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) DIVERGES FROM ITS TWO PEERS, IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, ON THE MONEY. THIS IS THE SHARPEST FINDING IN THIS ENTRY AND `apps/api/src/**` IS OUTSIDE THIS ROW'S FENCE.**

   `apps/worker`'s `jsonCents` and `apps/site`'s `bigintCents` both admit a cents value as a **safe-integer** JSON number OR as a base-10 string of digits, and both REFUSE a number past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` rather than rounding it. `readCapSchedule` does neither. Its guard is `if (typeof cap !== 'number' || !Number.isInteger(cap))` at [`:1233`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts), and it converts at `return { from_ordinal: ordinal, cap_cents: BigInt(cap) };` at [`:1238`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts).

   **EXECUTED RATHER THAN REASONED ABOUT**, because a claim about a double is the kind this file has been wrong about before. `JSON.parse('{"cap_cents":9007199254740993}')` yields `9007199254740992`; `Number.isInteger` of it is `true`, `Number.isSafeInteger` is `false`, and `BigInt` of it is `9007199254740992n`. **One cent, silently, off a payout ceiling.** `Number.isInteger(1e20)` is likewise `true`. That assertion runs in the suite rather than living in this paragraph.

   **AND THE REFUSAL RUNS THE OTHER WAY TOO**: a base-10 string is rejected outright, and that is the rendering [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) ruling 5 ruled _"the only rendering that survives above the ceiling"_.

   **THE FILE IS NOT SAFE BECAUSE IT MENTIONS `Number.isSafeInteger` ELSEWHERE.** Its `cents` helper for the `bigint` COLUMNS does check it, which is why the suite asks the question of the FUNCTION and not of the file: a file-wide question reports this file safe on the strength of the reader that is not the one in question. That narrowing was made after the broad form passed on the unrepaired tree, and is recorded rather than quietly applied.

   **The repair is two words in one file and it needs a fence that reaches `apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts`.** The suite's assertion is written so that it goes RED on the repair, which is how this item closes.

2. **`apps/portal/src/api/types.ts` DECLARES `cap_cents` AND `payout_cap_cents` AS `number` ON A WIRE TYPE.** It is a declared wire contract rather than a decode, so it is correctly outside the census above, and `apps/portal` is outside this fence. It is registered because a cents value typed `number` on a wire type is the same narrowing of `INV-02` that item 1 turns into a rounded cap, one deployable over, and nobody has ruled it.

3. **`apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts`'s LOCAL `PlanVersionSizeRow` ([`:305`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) IS A THIRD DECLARATION OF THE SIZE ROW'S SHAPE**, carrying two columns the engine's deliberately omits and omitting four the engine's carries. It is not wrong for its endpoint. It is registered because whoever takes section 5's home will have to decide whether it collapses onto the engine's type or stays the catalogue's own, and that decision is cheaper made deliberately than discovered.

4. **`packages/db`'s `catalogRowAt` RETURNING `unknown` IS THE ROOT OF BOTH HALVES OF THIS ENTRY** and is out of fence. Section 5.

5. **[ADR-283](ADR-283.md) SECTION 7 ITEM 1's RETIREMENT IS STILL UNROWED.** The rules blob is still stated three times and link 7 still holds that census at three. This entry does not take it and does not renew it; it is that entry's follow-up and remains so.
