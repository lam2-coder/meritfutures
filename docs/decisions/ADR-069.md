## ADR-069: parity is the condition that makes read-only impersonation correct, and the eighteen gaps close at `owner` with the initiative recorded  (2026-08-20, status: accepted)

**[FOLD-04](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) said this ruling could not be written before the matrix existed, "because its content is the gap list". The matrix now exists**, as [the admin capability parity audit](../reviews/2026-08-20-admin-parity-audit.md) landed by session `I1` under [ADR-033](ADR-033.md), and its headline is blunt: **the admin console cannot do a single thing a trader can.** Thirty-four trader-side mutating actions, two with an `owner`-role admin equivalent, and every admin mutation in the corpus is enforcement, correction, configuration, conferral or annotation. **None of them is the trader's own act performed by an admin.**

- **Context:** **Actions taken through impersonation are attributed to the trader.** [FOLD-04:85](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) states it exactly: *"That corrupts the audit trail, and it weakens an evidence pack precisely where an evidence pack is worth most: a dispute, or a chargeback representment where Merit must show who did what. An admin-attributed action preserves provenance; an impersonated one destroys it."*

  **So parity is not a nicety that makes read-only tolerable. It is the thing that makes read-only correct.** A read-only impersonation session is defensible only if the work a support agent needs to do can be done from the admin surface, attributed to the admin, with a mandatory reason. **Where it cannot, read-only is not a principled boundary but a missing feature wearing one**, and the pressure to widen it arrives from a real support case rather than from a design argument. That is the failure this ADR is the guard against, and [`ADR-068`](ALLOCATION.md)'s non-disclosure ruling is weaker without it.

  **Merit is passwordless**, which is why the capability exists at all: [ADR-039](ADR-039.md) made OTP and passkeys the whole of trader authentication, so there is no password to share and no reset path a support agent can walk a trader down.

### 1. The audit's arithmetic, checked rather than accepted

**A ruling that takes its input's counts on trust is a ruling with one unchecked claim in its foundation.** The audit's own header states `34 = 2 + 18 + 4 + 10`. Recounting the four class columns of its section 4 tables, row by row:

| Class | Rows | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **E**, an `owner` admin equivalent exists today | 33, 34 | 2 |
| **GAP**, replicable admin-side and missing | 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 25, 26, 27, 29, 31 | 18 |
| **OQ**, may genuinely not be replicable | 18, 19, 30, 32 | 4 |
| **NOT-PARITY**, an admin equivalent would be a different and worse act | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 23, 24, 28 | 10 |

**It holds, and so does the grouping.** The audit's section 5 splits the eighteen into account recovery (4), money out (2), commerce (4), presence and contact (5), erasure (1) and affiliate (2), which is eighteen. **This ADR rules against those counts because they were recomputed, not because they were printed.**

### 2. The ruling, stated once and in full

> **RULED. Every one of the eighteen gaps closes with an `owner`-role admin route that performs the trader's act, ATTRIBUTED TO THE ADMIN, with a mandatory reason and a dual-timeline audit.** The eighteen routes are enumerated in [M06 section 11](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md). **No capability is granted to an impersonation session by this ruling**, which is the whole point of it: the admin surface gains the capability so the impersonated surface does not have to.

**Four properties, and none of them is optional:**

| Property | What carries it |
|---|---|
| **Attributed to the admin** | `admin_actions.actor`, which every mutating admin endpoint already writes ([API_CONTRACT section 8](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)) |
| **A mandatory reason** | `admin_actions.reason NOT NULL`, and [the design record](../architecture/data-model/admin_actions.md) is right that *"the `NOT NULL` is the whole control"* |
| **A dual-timeline audit** | The `admin_actions` row **and** an `events` row carrying `actor_kind = 'admin'`, which `GET /accounts/:accountId/timeline` projects to the trader |
| **On whose initiative** | `admin_actions.initiative` and `on_behalf_of_identity_id`, which is `SD-M6-11` and section 3 below |

### 3. `0043` is SPENT, and the delta is not the one the reservation named

**[ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) reserved `0043` as contingent**, on the ground that *"M06's `admin_actions` may already carry the shape"*, and required this session to confirm the delta before spending the number. **It was confirmed against [`0017_events_and_audit.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql) rather than recalled, and the answer is that most of the shape is carried and one half is not.**

| Property | Carried today? |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **Yes.** `actor`, `action`, `subject_kind`, `subject_id`, `reason NOT NULL`, `before`, `after`, `evidence_refs`, `ip` |
| **The trader half of the dual timeline** | **Yes.** `events.actor_kind` is `CHECK (actor_kind IN ('system','trader','admin','vendor'))` with `actor_id`, `identity_id` and `account_id` beside it, and the trader timeline is a projection of `events` |
| **On whose initiative** | **No. Nowhere.** Nothing in the schema distinguishes an admin acting **on** a trader from an admin acting **for** one |

**That third row is the delta and it is load bearing rather than tidy.** Both cases write `actor = <operator>` and a free-text reason, and the two claims a row can be making are different defences:

- *"Merit did this to the trader"*, which is enforcement or housekeeping.
- *"The trader asked and Merit did it"*, which is the only one that answers **why did you touch my account** in a dispute or a chargeback representment.

**A reviewer cannot reconstruct which was meant from free text, and free text is not a control.** That standard is this corpus's own, written on the column one line over.

**The vocabulary is not invented here, and the precedent is the audit's own existence proof.** Row 33 of the matrix is `POST /admin/accounts/:accountId/close`, whose [`CloseRequest`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) carries `kind: "enforcement" | "trader_request" | "operational"`. The audit calls that row *"the shape the other 18 gaps would take if they were closed"*. **This ADR takes it literally**: the same three values, in the same order, on the row every admin action already writes, so eighteen new routes inherit one discriminator instead of eighteen request bodies each inventing one.

**AND `CloseRequest.kind` HAS NO COLUMN ANYWHERE, WHICH IS THE FINDING THAT DECIDED THE CONTINGENCY.** It is a field on a request type. `account_status_history` ([`0007_accounts.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql)) carries `from_status`, `to_status`, `from_phase`, `to_phase` and a **nullable** `reason`, and no `kind`. So the corpus's one modelled trader-requested admin act lands its discriminator in the `after` jsonb or nowhere, and **cannot be queried as one**. Releasing `0043` would have left the parity closure resting on that.

> **RULED: `0043` is spent as `SD-M6-11`.** `admin_actions` gains `initiative` (`NOT NULL`, no default, `CHECK` over `CloseRequest.kind`'s three values) and `on_behalf_of_identity_id` (`NULL`, referencing `identities`), with a **biconditional** `CHECK` in both directions and a partial index that is the dual-timeline read.

**Both directions of the `CHECK` are asserted and the second one is why it is a `CHECK` rather than a convention.** Forward: a `trader_request` with no identity is a claim that a trader asked, with no record of which trader. **Backward: an `enforcement` or `operational` row carrying an on-behalf-of identity is an act against a trader dressed as an act for one**, which is exactly the misattribution this fold exists to prevent, arriving from the admin side instead of the impersonation side.

**Why it lands now, before any of the eighteen routes exists.** `admin_actions` is append-only ([`0026`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from `merit_app` and from `PUBLIC`) and its retention is forever ([DATA_MODEL](../architecture/data-model/README.md)). **A discriminator added after rows exist leaves every historical row `NULL`, and `NULL` is then ambiguous between "Merit's own act" and "written before the column existed".** The column is unambiguous only if it is never null, and it is never null only if it arrives while the table is empty. This is the last cheap moment, and E2 makes a merged migration unfixable rather than expensive.

**What is deliberately not in the migration.** No column records **how the requester was verified**. That is a support-process question this ADR leaves open rather than guessing at, and `reason` plus `evidence_refs` carry it as prose until somebody rules a vocabulary. **A `CHECK` naming values nobody has agreed on reads as a control in an audit and behaves as a coin flip in practice**, which [M06 AS-M6-06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) already says of controls that are theatre.

### 4. The factor substitution, which the ADR must state rather than leave to be reconstructed

**Nine of the trader routes in the matrix are C-27 sensitive actions requiring `passkey or dual_channel`** ([SECURITY C-27](../architecture/SECURITY.md), [API_CONTRACT section 11](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). The admin surface authenticates under a different token entirely: `admin_sso`, *"hardware-key SSO under C-08, which has no SMS path, ever"*.

**So closing a C-27-tagged gap admin-side is a SUBSTITUTION of factor, not a replication of it**, and the two claims are not the same claim:

| Surface | The claim the audit row makes |
|---|---|
| **The trader route** | *"The human holding this account proved possession on a second channel"* |
| **The admin route** | *"An operator holding a hardware key asserts that the human asked"* |
| **An impersonated write, for contrast** | *"The trader did it"*, which is simply false |

> **RULED: the substitution is accepted, and `initiative = 'trader_request'` IS the record of which claim was made.** The audit stated this as a finding and not an objection and it is treated the same way here: the operator's assertion carries less evidentiary weight than the trader's second-channel possession, and it carries **enormously** more than an impersonated write, which records a falsehood. **The dual-timeline entry on the trader's side is where a reader learns an operator acted**, per [FOLD-04:95](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md), and `events.actor_kind = 'admin'` is what carries it.

**This is the paragraph a reviewer two years from now needs and would otherwise have to reconstruct**, which is the audit's own reason for asking that it be written down.

### 5. Five rulings the gap list forces, each on a row that would otherwise close wrong

**Closing a gap is not always the same as adding a route, and five of them carry something a careless closure would drop.**

**Row 9, the phone-change ceremony, sets a money-path hold and the admin route does not waive it.** The trader route sets `withdrawal_hold_until`, a 48 hour external-withdrawal hold read by the payout and wallet paths ([API_CONTRACT section 4](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)).

> **RULED: an admin-opened phone-change ceremony sets `withdrawal_hold_until` identically.** Waiving it is exactly what pressure will ask for, on exactly the call where a compromised trader is the person on the line, and **a hold an operator can waive is not a hold**. The admin route inherits the side effect whole.

**Row 14, an admin-originated reset must not poison the risk signal it feeds.** Reset velocity is a risk signal ([API_CONTRACT section 4](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)), and an operator performing resets on behalf of traders would raise it against the traders.

> **RULED: the reset-velocity signal reads `events.actor_kind` and excludes operator-originated resets, or counts them separately.** No new column: `actor_kind` already distinguishes them and the detector has to read it.

**Row 22 is the one that cuts against closing casually.** [M16](../plans/M16-notification-center.md) makes `notification.preferences_changed` followed by a destination change a high-severity risk pattern ([EC-115](../edge-cases/EC-115.md), GS-193). **An admin-attributed preference change is indistinguishable from that pattern unless the actor is on the signal.**

> **RULED: the detector reads `actor_kind` before it fires.** Closing this gap without touching the detector would generate high-severity flags on Merit's own support staff, which is how a detector gets muted, which is [M06 AS-M6-03](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) arriving through a feature.

**Row 17 is the highest-consequence row in the matrix and it is dual controlled.** An admin-originated external withdrawal is money leaving the firm on an operator's assertion that the trader asked, on a route the audit names as *"the one most likely to generate pressure to widen impersonation"*.

> **RULED: `POST /admin/identities/:identityId/wallet/withdrawals` requires dual control**, resolved server side against `dual_control_approvals` by payload hash (`SD-M6-05`, `INV-M6-08`), and carries every control the trader route carries: KYC verified, destination outside its cooling window, name-match score, the $100 minimum and `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT`.

**And the fifth ruling is the one that keeps `AS-M6-06` standing**, because three of the eighteen routes look from a distance like the affordance that scenario says must not exist. [M06 AS-M6-06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) reads *"Identity changes have no admin path at all. There is no screen that edits a verified identity"*, and `DEP-M6-06` states the same boundary from M19's end.

> **RULED: no parity route writes a verified identity attribute, and rows 9, 10 and 11 OPEN A CEREMONY rather than editing a value.** Row 9's admin route cannot complete the phone change: completion is `POST /phone/verify`, which is **row 8 and permanently excluded**, so possession still has to be proved by whoever holds the handset. Row 11 adds an **unverified** channel, and [EC-115](../edge-cases/EC-115.md)'s ceremony holds unchanged: the existing contact is notified immediately and unmutably, and the new contact must verify before becoming usable.

**So an operator who runs all three has started a clock and proved nothing**, which is the distinction between an affordance a social engineer wants and one they cannot use. `AS-M6-06`'s target still does not exist. What exists is the ability to **start** a ceremony whose proofs are unchanged, for a trader who cannot start it themselves, which is the account-recovery group's entire reason for being.

**This amends no ruling, and the precedent is one route over.** [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/correct` is already *"compensating entries only, **dual controlled**, reason required"*. **Dual control on an admin money route is M20's existing shape rather than an extension of [ADR-010](ADR-010.md)'s sensitive set**, which matters because `ADR-067` is amending that set concurrently and this ruling deliberately does not touch it. **Row 16, payout origination, is NOT dual controlled** on the same asymmetry [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) uses for freeze: an internal payout request is re-evaluated against every gate before it settles and moves no money out of the firm by itself.

### 6. The ten NOT-PARITY rows are a declared exclusion list, and `GS-304` cannot pass without one

**The audit classed ten rows as NOT-PARITY and put them to the founder rather than ruling them.** They are ruled here, because `GS-304` is *"a coverage test rather than a behaviour test: it fails when someone adds a trader route and no admin equivalent"*, and **a coverage test with no exclusion list fails on ten rows forever, on arrival**. [STATE](../STATE.md)'s own sentence is that a gate which fails on arrival is a gate somebody switches off.

> **RULED: these ten are permanent, declared exclusions, each with its reason recorded in [M06 section 11](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md), and that list is `GS-304`'s exclusion set.** An addition to it is an amendment to this ADR and not a commit.

| Rows | Why an admin equivalent would be worse than the gap |
|---|---|
| 1, 2, 4, 5 | **Credential forgery.** Issuing a trader's OTP, consuming their challenge, or registering a passkey on their identity manufactures a login factor for an account the operator does not hold. **Worse than impersonation**, because the resulting session is indistinguishable from the trader's own with no impersonation record attached at all |
| 3 | **Elevation deletes C-27 if it can be asserted admin-side.** An operator holds neither a passkey nor the second channel on the trader's behalf |
| 6 | **Ending one's own session is not a support act.** The support-shaped version is row 7, which closes as a gap |
| 8 | **Phone verification's whole value is that the handset holder produced the code.** An admin-completed verification writes an [ADR-022](ADR-022.md) hard-link edge asserting a possession nobody demonstrated, which corrupts [M07](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md)'s graph rather than the audit trail |
| 23 | **Marking a notification read manufactures evidence that the trader saw it.** `read_at` is the input to the proof-of-notice query, so this is the audit-trail corruption the fold exists to prevent, arriving through the parity door instead of the impersonation one |
| 24 | **Impossible rather than forbidden.** The confirming half of a Discord link happens on Discord under the trader's own Discord identity ([M15](../plans/M15-discord-integration.md)) |
| 28 | **The journal is the trader's private reflection.** An admin writing in it is not a support action under any attribution. **The delete leg is a different act and closes as row 29** |

**Row 28 is why "an admin can do every trader action" is never stated in this corpus without its exception list.** Stated bare, it includes writing in a trader's diary.

### 7. The two open questions, each carrying a NARROW exception, neither pre-approved

**Blanket write access is not granted and neither exception is approved by this ADR.** [FOLD-04:101](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md): *"A capability nobody has enumerated is not a capability anybody can scope."* The enumeration is done, so these are scoped, **and scoped is not approved.**

#### `OQ-F4-01`, contract and terms acceptance. Rows 30 and 32, blocking rows 12, 13, 14 and 15

**The question: can an `owner` admin record acceptance of a ToS version on a trader's behalf, or is acceptance legally personal?** The audit widened it in two ways this ADR carries forward.

**First, there is no signature route, so the scope is the whole commerce group.** Acceptance is `accept_tos_version_ids` on checkout and reset, which makes this question a **blocker on four GAP rows** rather than an item beside them. Rows 12, 13, 14 and 15 are ruled to close in section 2 and **cannot be built until this is answered**, whatever is decided about the purchase itself.

**Second, `tos_acceptances` is append-only and revoked at the database role, and `INV-M3-09` makes it "the first artifact any enforcement dispute asks for"** ([M03](../plans/M03-billing-checkout.md)). **An admin-recorded acceptance is therefore permanent and is the row Merit produces in a dispute.**

**Proposed narrow exception, if and only if acceptance is ruled personal:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | `owner` role only |
| **What** | **Write-enabled impersonation scoped to the acceptance step alone.** Not to checkout as a whole and not to the session as a whole |
| **Audit** | Elevated: the impersonation record, the `admin_actions` row with `initiative = 'trader_request'`, **and a marker on the `tos_acceptances` row itself**, because that is the row a dispute reads and an unmarked row is a false claim about who accepted |
| **Control** | Dual control, on [ADR-010](ADR-010.md)'s footing |
| **Not proposed** | Any write capability beyond the acceptance step |
| **Cost if granted** | **A migration this ADR does not spend.** `tos_acceptances` is append-only, so the marker is a superseding file under E2 and belongs to the session that implements the exception |

**The alternative that avoids the exception entirely, recorded so the founder chooses between two things rather than approving one:** an admin-attributed purchase that records **no** acceptance, leaving the account unusable until the trader accepts on first login. **That is a product decision more than a legal one**, and this ADR does not choose it either.

#### `OQ-F4-02`, KYC assertion. Rows 18 and 19

**The question: can an `owner` admin initiate or re-initiate a KYC verification on a trader's behalf?** [FOLD-04](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) calls it *"a vendor question as much as a policy one"* and that holds.

**The act is narrower than it looks.** *"Merit never proxies documents"* (`INV-M19-07`), and `POST /kyc/session` returns a **hosted URL**, so an admin would be **creating a provider session, not making an identity assertion**. The assertion is still made by whoever appears in the provider's hosted flow. **That may make an admin-initiated session harmless and it may make it worthless, and which one it is depends on the provider's answer rather than Merit's.**

**Proposed narrow exception, if and only if provider policy permits it:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | `owner` role only |
| **What** | **Session creation and dispatch of the hosted URL to the trader's verified contact channel.** Not submission, which Merit cannot perform in any case |
| **Audit** | Elevated: `admin_actions` plus a `kyc_funnel_events` entry marking operator origination, so [M19](../plans/M19-kyc-identity.md)'s funnel telemetry does not count operator-initiated sessions as trader-initiated ones |
| **Control** | **Dual control NOT proposed**, on the reasoning [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) uses for freeze: the act is reversible and creates no obligation. **The asymmetry with `OQ-F4-01` is deliberate rather than an oversight** |
| **Not proposed** | Anything touching `POST /admin/sanctions/:id/review` or `POST /admin/dedupe-matches/:id/disposition`, which are adjudication and already exist with their own controls |
| **Prerequisite** | **A vendor answer, in writing, before this half is ruled.** [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) holds at `review` for exactly this shape under [ADR-005](ADR-005.md) |

**Rows 18 and 19 may rule differently and are kept separate.** Re-verification creates a new verification with a `verification_purpose`, and **a purpose an operator sets is not the same fact as a purpose a trader sets.**

### 8. All eighteen are `owner`-only at v1, and the `ops` boundary is not drawn here

**The audit records that the `ops` role's boundary will be tested by whatever this ADR adds**, since [API_CONTRACT section 8](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) gives `ops` *"read plus account actions, no config or role changes"*, and most of the account-recovery group reads naturally as an account action while most of the money-out group does not.

> **RULED: every one of the eighteen routes is `owner` only at v1.** `ops` and `readonly` receive none of them.

**Two reasons, and the second is the honest one.** Parity's condition is satisfied at `owner`: FOLD-04's requirement is that *an `owner`-role admin* can perform any trader action, so widening is a separate benefit rather than a missing half. And **each `ops` grant has a test count attached to it**: [M06 section 8.1](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) requires *"one per mutating route per role, enumerated from the router"*, so eighteen routes across three roles is what the D5 matrix grows by, and moving a route to `ops` changes which of those three assert a refusal rather than adding a row.

**The real ask is named rather than pre-refused.** A support organisation running at scale will want the **account-recovery group** at `ops`, because that is the group a first-line agent handles and routing every compromised-account call to the founder is not an operating model. **This ADR does not decide it and does not claim an `OQ-nn` identifier for it**, because [FOLD-04 section 8](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) owns that namespace and is not in this session's fence.

- **Alternatives considered.**

  - **Release `0043` and carry initiative in `admin_actions.reason`.** Rejected in section 3. Free text is not a control by this corpus's own standard, the discriminator is what an evidence pack turns on, and `admin_actions` is append-only and retained forever so the column cannot be added cleanly later.
  - **Release `0043` and add the column when the first parity route is built.** Rejected for the same reason and it is the more tempting version: by then the table has rows, and a nullable discriminator is ambiguous between two meanings exactly where the ambiguity is expensive.
  - **Spend `0043` on a wider shape**, adding a requester-verification vocabulary and an impersonation-session reference. Rejected: neither is decided, `ADR-068` owns the session type, and E2 makes a merged column unfixable. **The reservation was widened once and narrowed on reading, which is `0041`'s precedent one number over.**
  - **Grant write-enabled impersonation for the whole commerce group**, since `OQ-F4-01` blocks four rows at once and the exception would unblock them together. Rejected: it is the blanket grant FOLD-04 forbids in advance, and the acceptance step is the only part that needs it.
  - **Rule the ten NOT-PARITY rows as gaps and close them anyway**, on the ground that a complete matrix is simpler than a matrix with exceptions. Rejected in section 6: four of them are credential forgery, one manufactures proof of notice, one is impossible, and one is writing in a trader's diary. **A parity claim with no exception list is a claim nobody checked.**
  - **Widen the account-recovery group to `ops` now.** Rejected in section 8 as a decision with a test count and an unowned namespace, not as a bad idea.
  - **Rule dual control on all eighteen routes.** Rejected: it would make the account-recovery group unusable during the incident it exists for, and dual control is placed where money leaves the firm, which is row 17.

- **Consequences:**

  - **Eighteen new mutating admin routes exist as a specification and none exists as code.** They are specified in [M06 section 11](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md), which is the fold [FOLD-04](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) reserved this session for.
  - **The D5 matrix grows by eighteen routes across three roles**, by [M06 section 8.1](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s own rule rather than by a number written down. **No `M6-N-nn` ordinal is claimed here**, because that section deliberately replaced its counts with the list's own rule.
  - **`GS-304` becomes writable**, because it now has an exclusion list (section 6) and an enumerated action set (M06 section 11) to check coverage against. **It was not writable before this ruling** and no golden scenario is authored by it: `GS-300` to `GS-304` are already registered as [golden-scenario section 37](../testing/golden-scenarios/37-gs-300-to-gs-304-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md).
