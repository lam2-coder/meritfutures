## ADR-157: a read may narrow by a RANGE and by IS NULL and a write may not, the lock is a ROW lock rather than an advisory one, and the aggregate P7 asked for is refused on evidence  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-112](ADR-112.md) closed the predicate surface at a typed equality and named its own way out in the same sentence: *"There is no `OR`, no `IN`, no range and no `IS NULL`, and each absence is the same decision: a shape a caller can compose freely is a shape a caller can compose wrongly, and every one of them is a diff on this file with an argument attached when a caller needs it."* [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 3 is that argument arriving, and it arrives for three of the four absences at once.** This entry rules that two of them become a closed vocabulary of TERMS admitted on the READ path only; that the third, a lock, is a ROW lock taken through the accessor rather than the advisory lock two invariants name in prose, because an advisory lock has no way to be sent that is not `sqlExecutor`; and that the AGGREGATE [P7 section 10 item 1](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) asks this slice for is REFUSED, on the evidence that P7's own section 3.1 names a JOIN as its blocker and a scalar aggregate would not have served a single detector.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `157`, session **268**, dispatched as `claude/p5a-accessor` against [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 8's `P5-a` row. **MONEY PATH, [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) strict.** The number was RESERVED before dispatch and the reservation names it **REQUIRED**, on the ground [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) established: the `scopedDb(identity)` wrapper is what bounds a BOLA blast radius, and any widening of it is a ruling rather than a commit. **What is delegated is the SHAPE and what is not is the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read**, which is owed on every line this lands and is not discharged by this entry's own approval line.

- **Context:** three fences stand around [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) and this entry moves the innermost one. [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) created the wrapper as the only sanctioned accessor. [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) gave it a write path. [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) NARROWED that path to name exactly one row, deleting six unaddressed writes rather than deprecating them, on the argument that handing callers arbitrary SQL re-opens the surface `ADR-008` closed. [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) then ruled that **the vocabulary that moves is the TABLE and never the REASON**. `P5` is the first phase whose work is jobs and money, and a job is a range query over a clock; `P7` is the phase behind it and its plan states, in the document `P5-a`'s author was told to read, that its own need is larger than this slice's three admissions.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. A FILTER MAY CARRY A TERM AND A TERM IS A CLOSED VOCABULARY OF THREE SHAPES.** `atMost(v)` renders `col <= $n`, `atLeast(v)` renders `col >= $n`, `isNull()` renders `col IS NULL`, and there is no fourth. Both bounds are INCLUSIVE. This is [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) clause 3's move applied to a predicate rather than to a door: a closed vocabulary of HOW ONE COLUMN NARROWS, where every member removes rows from a read the caller already holds at that authority, and joining the list is a diff on this file with an argument attached.
>
> **2. A TERM IS RECOGNISED BY IDENTITY AND NEVER BY SHAPE.** The three constructors are the only producers in this workspace and each mints into a module-private `WeakSet`. A `jsonb` column can hold `{ term: 'at-most', value: 1 }`, and a shape check would read that stored value as a range and silently return a different set of rows than the caller asked for. **Under this rule a caller cannot hand-roll a term, cannot smuggle a `SQL` through one, and cannot receive one across a process boundary**, because nothing that was serialised is in the set.
>
> **3. A TERM READS AND NEVER WRITES, AND THAT IS ADR-112 CLAUSE 2 EXTENDED RATHER THAN AMENDED.** An ADDRESS stays equality only, so a term is refused in `rowAt`, `lockAt`, `updateAt` and `deleteAt` at all three authorities, and it is refused in an INSERT's values and an UPDATE's `SET`. The read-versus-write asymmetry is unchanged and its reason is unchanged: a filtered read narrows a read that already exists, and a term at non-unique columns on a WRITE is the multi-row write [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) forecloses, arriving through a new door.
>
> **4. THE LOCK IS A ROW LOCK, TAKEN THROUGH THE ACCESSOR, AND NOT THE ADVISORY LOCK THE PROSE NAMES.** `lockAt(key, at)` is `rowAt` plus `FOR UPDATE` on the same predicate; `lockScope()` locks the handle's own `identities` row and takes NO argument. The difference from an advisory lock is which door it goes through: `pg_advisory_xact_lock` can only be sent through `sqlExecutor`, which is the reach-around [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 11 rule 10 and [P7](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) section 11 rule 10 each foreclose by name. **A row lock says the same thing and says it with the tenancy conjunct attached**, which an advisory lock keyed on a uuid the caller supplies could never have.
>
> **5. THE LOCK IS ON THE TWO AUTHORITIES THAT AN INVARIANT NAMES AND ON NEITHER OTHER.** `ScopedTx` and `SystemTx`, because `INV-M5-07`'s lock is *shared with the batch* and a lock only one of two parties takes is not a lock. NOT `FirmTx`: a `firm` row belongs to nobody and no invariant in the corpus names a lock on one. NOT the read handles: a row lock is released at `COMMIT`, so a lock outside a transaction is released before the next statement runs and is a control that reads like one and is not one.
>
> **6. THE AGGREGATE IS REFUSED, AND THE REFUSAL IS ON EVIDENCE RATHER THAN ON SCOPE.** Section 5. `P7` asked for it in the entry this slice holds and the answer is recorded here rather than left for the first detector slice to discover.
>
> **7. `SqlExecutorReason` DOES NOT MOVE AND NEITHER DOES `SystemReason`.** One member and two, and this entry adds the assertion that watches the first, which nothing did before it.

### 3. Clause 1, and the shapes that are still refused

**Two of ADR-112's four absences become admissions and two do not, and the line between them is a property of a conjunction rather than a preference.**

| Shape | Disposition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **A range** | **ADMITTED**, inclusive, both directions | `P5-j`'s whole query is `freeze_expires_at <= now()` over three columns on two tables. Without it the hourly sweep reads EVERY `payout_requests` and EVERY `wallet_withdrawals` row every hour and filters in memory, and the nightly assertion does it again |
| **`IS NULL`** | **ADMITTED** | [`halts.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/halts.ts) has been paying for its absence on **every posting** since it was written, and says so in its own header |
| **`OR`** | **REFUSED**, unchanged | A conjunction can only REMOVE rows from the read the caller already holds at that authority. A disjunction can add them back, and the rows it adds back are the ones the tenancy narrowing removed |
| **`IN`** | **REFUSED**, unchanged | `OR`'s argument exactly, spelled shorter |
| **A STRICT inequality** | **REFUSED, and NAMED rather than omitted** | No P5 caller has one. The caller who might is `P5-g`'s cursor over `wallet_entries`, and an inclusive bound re-reads its boundary row rather than losing one, so the failure direction is a duplicate rather than a gap. **If that is unacceptable it is an argument `P5-g` makes, not one this entry makes on its behalf** |
| **`IS NOT NULL`** | **REFUSED** | The complement of an admitted term is not automatically a caller. Nothing in `P5` reads for the presence of a nullable column |
| **`LIKE`, and every pattern match** | **REFUSED** | A pattern is a shape a caller composes, which is clause 1 of [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) restated, and the search surface that would want one is `admin-reads.ts`'s and is a join first |

**A BOUND GOES THROUGH THE COLUMN'S OWN MAPPER AND IS NEVER ASSEMBLED BY HAND.** `atMost` is built with drizzle's `lte` for the same reason equality is built with `eq`: a `Date` handed to a `timestamptz` arrives as the string Postgres parses, and a bound written into an `sql` template would not. **The clock is the CALLER'S and never the database's**: the term renders `col <= $n` with a bound parameter, so a sweep passes the time it decided to sweep at and a fixture passes a fixed one. Rendering `now()` would put the database's clock into a money path the constitution keeps as data, and would make every expiry test unwritable.

### 4. Clause 3, and the hazard this entry CREATES rather than the one it closes

**Before this entry no caller held an object a filter treated specially. Now one does.** That is a new failure mode and it is this entry's to close rather than the first caller's to discover:

- **A term in an ADDRESS.** `updateAt('ledgerHalts', { id: atMost('h') }, ...)` reads like ordinary accessor use and would write every row whose id sorts below a string. It throws, on the one path every write and every addressed read already share.
- **A term in a `SET` or an INSERT's values.** `updateAt('ledgerHalts', { id }, { releasedAt: isNull() })` is somebody meaning *"clear this column"* and getting a row of JSON written into a `timestamptz`, or a silent success on a `jsonb` one. It throws, and the message says to write `null`.

**BOTH REFUSALS ARE AT RUN TIME AND NOT IN THE TYPE, AND THIS ENTRY SAYS WHICH HALF IS WHICH.** `RowFilter`'s value position is `unknown` because a registered column may hold a `Date`, a `bigint`, a `Buffer` or a row of JSON; narrowing it to exclude a term would refuse legitimate values on tables all over the registry. So the type cannot carry it, exactly as [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) records that `tsc` cannot carry uniqueness, and for a related reason: the fact that decides it is not in the type system. **The throw is the control and section 8 is it watched failing.**

### 5. THE AGGREGATE, WHICH IS THE QUESTION P7 PUT TO THIS ENTRY

**[P7 section 10 item 1](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) is addressed to this slice by name and its request is refused. The reason is not that it is out of scope. It is that an aggregate would not do what that item says it would do.**

> *"The read shape P7 needs, which is larger than `P5-a`'s three admissions ... A detector is a join and an aggregate, and `P5-a` is scoped to a range term, a null term and a row lock. The argument belongs in `P5-a`'s entry, because that is the slice that holds the file ... If `P5-a` lands without an aggregate, every wave-2 slice is blocked and the reach-around is one line."*

**The first half of that sentence refutes the second, and P7's own section 3.1 is where it does it.** Read against the detectors it names:

| Detector, in P7 section 3.1's words | Served by a scalar aggregate over one table? |
|---|---|
| `D-01`, *"a self-join on `fills` inside a two second window"* | **No. It is a JOIN.** The two second window is a RANGE, and this entry admits it |
| `D-02`, *"a rolling correlation over twenty trading days"* | **No.** A correlation is not `SUM` or `COUNT`; it is a window function or a computation in the runner |
| `D-03`, *"compares the variance of a summed series against a sum of variances across a discovered group"* | **No**, twice: a discovered group is a JOIN and a variance of a series is not a scalar aggregate |
| `D-14`, *"sums positions across a clique"* | **No.** A clique is a JOIN; the sum over it is the easy half |

**So the aggregate is not the blocker and admitting it would not have unblocked one wave-2 slice.** What it would have done is spend the widening, leave `P7` blocked on the join it is actually blocked on, and leave the next reader a foreclosure that reads as answered. **A widening that buys nothing and looks like it bought something is worse than the absence it replaces**, and the failure class is the one [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) invokes [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) for when it deletes six methods rather than deprecating them: a thing that reads like a control and is not one.

**THE SECOND ARGUMENT IS THE MONEY ONE AND IT IS FINDING 8.** Merit's money is `bigint` cents and [`post.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/post.ts) makes that a rule of the code and not only of the schema. Executed in this container: `SUM` over a `bigint` column returns `numeric`, `pg` hands a `numeric` back as a **string**, and the naive `Number()` on it is lossy above 2^53. So the first money aggregate through this accessor decides how a `numeric` crosses the boundary, and getting that wrong is a float in a financial path with every type in the workspace green. **That is a ruling of its own size and it is not a predicate widening**, and this entry declines to take it in passing on exactly the ground [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2 and [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) clause 5 both took.

**THE THIRD IS THAT THE ONE AGGREGATE WITH A LIVE CALLER IS NOT A DETECTOR'S.** Finding 6's seven admin bodies include `LiabilityResponse`, *"six aggregates over the whole book"* -- a FIRM-wide figure read at `operator-console` authority, whose reach question is about an unscoped read and not about a scoped one, and whose row is `P5-l`'s.

**WHAT P7 GETS FROM THIS ENTRY ANYWAY, stated so that "refused" is not read as "nothing".** `D-01`'s two second window IS a range term and it lands here; a detector can pull its window through `rowsWhere` and do the join in the runner. **What that costs is real and is named rather than waved at**: the rows crossing the boundary are the window's rather than the match's, so a detector over a wide window pays for every row it did not match.

**WHAT P7 MUST NOT DO, and this is the half `P7` section 11 rule 10 already wrote and this entry seconds from the other side of the file:** a wave-2 slice does not add a `SqlExecutorReason` member, does not import `pg`, and does not cast past a key type. **If the window read is not affordable, the remedy is an entry against this file that argues a JOIN construction on its own terms**, with the question that construction has to answer stated here so it is not discovered at the file: **a joined read has two tables and the tenancy narrowing has to hold on BOTH of them, or the accessor is a BOLA hole with an extra table in it.** That is a harder ruling than this one and it deserves its own.

### 6. Clause 4, and what a row lock is and is not

**`lockAt` runs every guard `rowAt` runs, in the same order, and then locks.** The two read the same predicate from the same builder, so *"a lock reaches exactly what the matching read reaches"* is a property of the code rather than of two call sites agreeing. Section 8 asserts it over every scoped table with a writable address.

**`lockScope()` TAKES NO ARGUMENT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF ITS SAFETY.** `identities` is the registry's only `root` and its rule names `id`, so `scopePredicate` renders `identities.id = $1` from the handle and the caller supplies nothing. There is no address here to point at somebody else. It is `INV-M20-01`'s per-identity lock, and `lockAt('accounts', { id })` is `INV-M5-07`'s per-account one.

**THREE HONEST DIFFERENCES FROM THE ADVISORY LOCK THE PROSE NAMES**, each stated rather than smoothed over:

1. **A row lock needs the ROW.** An advisory lock can be taken on a key whose row does not exist. Both callers here hold an identity or an account that exists by the time they lock, so the difference costs nothing today and would cost something to a caller locking ahead of a row it is about to insert.
2. **A row lock is WIDER than an advisory lock on the same key.** `FOR UPDATE` on an `accounts` row also blocks any other writer of that row, not only the parties who agreed to take the lock. **That fails in the safe direction** -- more mutual exclusion than asked for, never less -- and it costs throughput rather than correctness.
3. **`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on a `derived` table locks the OUTER row only.** The tenancy narrowing is an `EXISTS` over the parent and the parent's row is not locked by it. That is the correct semantics for an addressed lock and it is written down here because it is the kind of thing a caller assumes the other way.

**ONE STRENGTH AND NO OPTIONS.** `FOR UPDATE` only. `FOR SHARE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` have no caller. `NOWAIT` and `SKIP LOCKED` each change what a CONTENDING transaction does rather than what it sees, which is a decision a money path makes with an argument rather than a parameter somebody passes at a call site.

### 7. What this FORECLOSES, which is the part every later slice inherits

1. **THE AGGREGATE AND THE JOIN ARE BOTH STILL UNAVAILABLE, and after this entry that is a decision with a date on it rather than an absence.** Section 5. A slice that needs either writes an entry.
2. **A term cannot reach a write at any authority, permanently.** *"Mark every row past due"* is still refused, which is [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) foreclosure 2 unmoved: a caller reads the rows and writes them one at a time.
3. **A term cannot be constructed anywhere but here.** The `WeakSet` is module private and unexported, so a helper package cannot mint one and a term cannot cross a process boundary. A caller wanting to pass a filter through a port passes the columns and builds the term on this side.
4. **There is still no multi-row lock.** `lockWhere` does not exist. A caller holding a set locks them one at a time, in an order it chooses, and the deadlock that a badly chosen order produces is the caller's to reason about.
5. **`FOR UPDATE` is the only lock strength and there is no lock timeout in the accessor.** A contending transaction WAITS. A caller that must not wait has no way to say so, and `lock_timeout` is a session setting no accessor method reaches.
6. **A range is INCLUSIVE and there is no half-open interval.** A cursor built on this re-reads its boundary row.
7. **`sqlExecutor` remains the one door out of this file and it remains one member wide.** Three phases now have a rule saying so and this entry adds the assertion that watches it.
8. **The read handles gain nothing.** `scopedDb`, `systemDb` and `firmDb` are unchanged, which also keeps [ADR-084](ADR-084.md)'s two watched compile refusals proving what they were written to prove: `CI-01`'s brand seed declares a value carrying exactly `__brand`, `reason`, `identityId` and `rows()`, and a required method added to a read interface would make TypeScript report a MISSING PROPERTY and pass the seed on the wrong evidence.

### 9. The round trip, EXECUTED, which is the approval clause's other half

**Section 8 asserts what the accessor BUILDS. This is what a database DID with it, and it is reported as evidence rather than claimed as a control**, on [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) section 9's precedent and with its limitation restated: `ci.yml`'s `integration` job runs on bare `ubuntu-latest` with no services block, so a committed suite that needs Postgres reports nothing in CI, and a test that skips is worse than a test that does not exist.

PostgreSQL 16 is in this container. **All 49 migrations applied in order under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1`.** `ledger_halts` is the table, for [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) section 9's reason exactly: it is `owned` on `identity_id` with `PRIMARY KEY (id)`, which is the shape a caller of another identity's row would exploit, and it carries a nullable `released_at` and a `timestamptz` the range term can bound. Two identities, `A` and `B`. **The script is not committed**, for the reason above.

| # | Executed | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `A: rowsWhere('ledgerHalts', { releasedAt: isNull() })` | **A's live halt, and not A's released one** |
| 2 | the SAME filter through `scopedDb(B)` | **B's row and not A's.** One filter, two handles, two answers |
| 3 | `A: escalateAt: atMost(EARLY)` | the row AT the boundary, so the bound is inclusive as ruled |
| 4 | `A: escalateAt: atLeast(LATE)` | the other row, at the other inclusive boundary |
