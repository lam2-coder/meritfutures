## ADR-421: `ADR-303` limit 4 is NOT callerless, the typing was performed rather than argued about, and it is refused because it buys nothing a fenced row can spend  (2026-09-07, status: proposed)

**Row 421 of the 416s wave. Branch `claude/adr303-limit-4-typed-verbs`, off `claude/wave-416s` at `4bf323dc`, session 616.**

**THE DISPATCH'S ENABLING PREMISE IS FALSE AND THAT IS THIS ROW'S FINDING.** It said [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) *"took the one verb with a caller"* and left the remaining three CALLERLESS, and that typing them would therefore be *"cheap and safe"*. Not one of them is callerless. `lockAt`, the smallest, has **6** call sites in **5** files, every one of them on a money or an operator path.

**[ADR-303](ADR-303.md) LIMIT 4 SAYS SO ITSELF, IN THE FILE, AND THE DISPATCH'S SUMMARY INVERTED IT.** `CatalogRow`'s docblock reads: *"Whether the same treatment is owed to them is NAMED here and NOT RULED: that is a much larger surface, IT HAS REAL CALLERS, and it is a separate row."* The primary source states the opposite of the dispatch that cites it.

**AND [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) NEVER CLAIMED OTHERWISE.** Its section 12 item 4 says *"This row took the one verb with a caller"*, and its section 4 states what that means without ambiguity: *"[ADR-303](ADR-303.md) limit 4 taken for ONE VERB ON ONE HANDLE"*, the catalogue slice of `SystemTx.rowAt`. **The word CALLERLESS appears in the dispatch and in neither entry.** The misreading is a summary of a summary, which is the failure mode this corpus calls `FM-16` operating on its own decision record.

**THE TYPING WAS PERFORMED, TYPECHECKED TREE-WIDE, MEASURED AND REVERTED, RATHER THAN REASONED ABOUT.** All four verbs were narrowed to `(typeof TABLES)[K]['$inferSelect']` on all six handles, **28 signatures**. The result is section 8 and it is the reason for the refusal rather than an argument against it.

**THE MAPPING EACH READER WRITES, WHICH IS THE `FM-16` LIMIT 4 DESCRIBES.** The second pair of columns is the one that decides this row. ### 9. The refusal, and its four reasons in the order they carry weight

**`ADR-303` LIMIT 4 IS NOT SPENT BY THIS ROW.**

1. **THE AUTHORISATION RESTED ON A PREMISE THAT IS FALSE.** The dispatch permitted the typing because the remaining verbs were callerless. They are not, by 200 call sites, and the dispatch's own instruction for that case is to report the finding instead.
2. **IT BUYS NOTHING TODAY.** Section 8. Zero consumer errors is zero readers reached, and every mapping stays where it was. A diff that changes 28 signatures and removes no `unknown` from any reader is a diff that makes limit 4 read differently while the condition limit 4 describes is unchanged.
3. **THE ZERO WAS MEASURED AGAINST A TREE THAT IS NOT THE MERGE TREE.** Section 11.
4. **AND THE SHAPE [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) LANDED FORBIDS IT.** Section 7. A door is admitted where a caller exists; this fence has none.

**WHAT WOULD MAKE IT TAKEABLE, STATED SO THE NEXT ROW DOES NOT REDERIVE IT.** A row that holds `packages/db/src/**` **and** the port files it intends to retype, that retypes the accessor and at least one port together, and that can therefore show a hand-written mapping DELETED in the same commit. That is the admission rule [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) already honours, applied to this surface. **It is one row per port family and not one row for the accessor**, and the 25 files are the map of how many.

### 14. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.** Inherited as well as owed: [ADR-303](ADR-303.md) and [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) are both `proposed` and unsigned, and this row settles neither.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS WHETHER A REFUSAL IS THE RIGHT OUTPUT FOR A ROW DISPATCHED TO BUILD.** This row was told to type what could be typed, it found the typing available and free, and it did not take it. The argument is section 8's second number: free is not the same as useful, and a narrowing no reader can see spends a named limit to change how a docblock reads. **A founder who wants the accessor honest regardless of whether a reader benefits should say so, because that is a defensible position and this entry did not take it.**

  **THE SECOND IS THE ORDER OF THE REMAINING WORK, WHICH IS A JUDGEMENT ABOUT FENCES RATHER THAN ABOUT TYPES.** Section 9 says limit 4 wants one row per port family, each holding `packages/db` and an app directory at once. That is the same arrangement [ADR-413](ADR-413.md) needed and [ADR-416](ADR-416.md) executed, and it works; it is also the arrangement that makes two rows collide when the dispatcher issues them in the same hour, which is section 11. **How many such rows may run concurrently is not a row's call.**

  **THE THIRD IS THAT A DISPATCH INVERTED ITS OWN PRIMARY SOURCE AND THE ROW ONLY CAUGHT IT BECAUSE IT WAS TOLD TO READ AT SOURCE.** [ADR-303](ADR-303.md) limit 4 says *"it has real callers"*; the dispatch said the opposite and offered a reason to believe it. **The instruction to derive rather than accept is what stood between this and 28 signatures changed on a false premise**, and it is worth deciding whether that instruction should be in every dispatch rather than in some.

  **THE FOURTH IS THE ONE GOOD NEWS.** [ADR-303](ADR-303.md) wrote a compile-time control specifically so that a later session widening these verbs would be *"changing something recorded as deliberate rather than tidying an inconsistency"*. A wave later that is exactly what happened, and the control was the only thing in the repository that noticed. **It was watching one handle of three, and this row widened the watch to the handles the limit was already stated over.**
