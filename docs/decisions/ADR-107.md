## ADR-107: the provisioning saga admits nobody it cannot produce evidence for, and five of seven operations have no inverse  (2026-08-26, status: proposed)

**`INV-M2-13` is fail-closed provisioning and the failure it exists for is invisible: an account trading on a setpoint the platform never confirmed is indistinguishable, in every later read, from one that was. This entry rules that the admission is a VALUE WITH ONE PRODUCER rather than a check that refuses, that the saga is a sequence of transactions rather than one because [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) forecloses the alternative, and that compensation is forward-only over a vocabulary in which five of seven operations have no inverse.** It also reports a defect in the write path it runs through, executed rather than argued.

- **Origin:** [P3 wave 3](../plans/P3-wave3-modules.md) section 9's `P3-l` row and section 15's prompt, session **222**. **MONEY PATH**, [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) strict. The slice writes no route, no `packages/db` file and no migration.

- **Context:** [`packages/queue`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts) has published a `JobQueue` since [session 147](../sessions/2026-08-24-session-147.md) and its `enqueue` takes the caller's open transaction in the required first argument position. That file's header recorded that nothing in this workspace could produce one. [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) produced one. **This entry is the first caller [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s review criterion has ever been graded against.**

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE ADMISSION IS A VALUE WITH ONE PRODUCER, NOT A CHECK THAT REFUSES.** `SetpointConfirmation` is branded on a module-local `unique symbol` and `setpointConfirmation` is its only producer. `TradingAdmission`'s admitted arm carries one as a REQUIRED field, so **an admission cannot be constructed without the evidence** and the fail-closed exit is the shape rather than a branch.
>
> **2. THE EXIT IS A FOLD SEEDED AT A REFUSAL AND ITS LOOP HAS NO `else`.** `admitToTrading` starts refused and its only assignment is guarded by a non-null evidence value. Deleting the loop entirely leaves every account out of trading. **The refusal REASON is classified separately, on the side that has already refused**, so a bug in the diagnosis changes what an operator reads and cannot change whether an account trades.
>
> **3. `confirmed` IS EVIDENCE AND `confirmed_inferred` IS NOT, FOR EVERY OPERATION.** Not for `set_risk` alone. [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.6.2 records that `provisioning_queue_set_risk_never_inferred` names `set_risk` alone while the same argument reaches `disable_account` and `disable_entitlement`; **refusing every status but `confirmed` is the form of the rule that does not need extending when that finding is repaired**, and it needs no migration.
>
> **4. THE ANCHOR IS THE ACCOUNT'S CURRENT FLOOR**, which is `INV-M2-08` composed with `INV-M2-13` rather than a staleness rule somebody has to write. A confirmation is evidence about A FLOOR. An account whose floor moved is an account whose current setpoint is unconfirmed, automatically, and a superseded `failed` row naming an old floor neither admits nor blocks forever.
>
> **5. `entitle` TAKES THE EVIDENCE AS A REQUIRED ARGUMENT.** `INV-M2-15`'s ordering -- `set_risk` confirmed, then entitlement, then permissions -- **is the argument list rather than a step list**, and a caller holding no confirmation cannot call it at all.
>
> **6. THE SAGA IS A SEQUENCE OF TRANSACTIONS, ONE PER STEP, AND [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) RULED THAT RATHER THAN THIS ENTRY.** Section 8 item 5 of that entry forecloses nested transactions and names this slice's compensation path as the first place it could bite. **Within one step the `provisioning_queue` INSERT and the job enqueue ride ONE transaction**, which is [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s consequence intact.
>
> **7. COMPENSATION IS FORWARD-ONLY AND MOSTLY UNAVAILABLE, AND THE SAFETY IS NOT IN IT.** Five of the seven operations have no inverse. **Every compensation outcome, including compensation failing outright, leaves the account unadmitted** -- not because something noticed, but because admission needs positive evidence and none of them produces any.
>
> **8. THE WRITE AUTHORITY IS `SystemTx` AND THE REASON IS `'nightly-batch'`.** [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 8 item 2 requires this slice to say which it is. `provisioning_queue` is `derived`, so a scoped INSERT is a compile error and `FirmTx` does not serve it; [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.1 makes the provisioning queue drain **`ST-M2-9`, a stage OF the nightly batch**. **`SystemReason` is not widened and no second door is opened**; what the word does not cover is section 6.

### 4. Clause 7: compensation, stated as three answers rather than as a mechanism

**A saga is compensation, and compensation is where money paths rot. So which steps are compensatable, which are not, and what happens when compensation itself fails, are three questions this entry answers rather than three properties a reader has to derive.**

| Step | Compensatable | By what | If that fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| The enqueue, **uncommitted** | **YES, and it is the only true rollback in the module** | The caller's transaction. [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s transactional enqueue is what makes it total: the row and the job are on one connection, so an abandoned transaction loses both or neither | Nothing committed. There is no failure to have |
| The enqueue, **committed** | **NO by rollback. Forward only** | A counteracting intent, if the operation has one | The row stays, the account stays unadmitted |
| **The file, written** | **NO** | Nothing. The name is derived from the batch's payload hashes and a rewrite is the same bytes under the same name | n/a |
| **The file, delivered by SFTP** | **NO** | A forward `disable_*` intent. The act is the vendor's now | The account stays unadmitted |
| **The confirmation** | **NO, and it is not an act** | It is evidence. Absence of evidence is a refusal | n/a |

**The five operations with no inverse, each for a different reason:**

- **`create_user`.** A platform login is per human and is shared across every account that human holds -- `SD-M2-05` exists because "Rithmic bills per login-month per **user** ... not per account". Deleting a user to compensate one account's provisioning would revoke every other account that user holds.
- **`set_risk` and `set_permissions`.** Both are **setpoints rather than acts**. The compensation for a wrong floor is another `set_risk` carrying the right one, which is a re-push and not an undo; `INV-M2-08` says the pushed setting equals the account's **current** floor, so there is no earlier floor to return to that satisfies it.
- **`disable_account` and `disable_entitlement`.** These two **are** the compensations. Failing to disable an account is not repaired by re-enabling it, and [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.6.3 admits the asymmetry rather than smoothing it: "Revocation is always available. Restoration is contingent on `V-M2-15`."

**THE ORDER IS M02 SECTION 3.6's AND NOT THE REVERSE OF THE FORWARD ORDER.** `disable_entitlement` then `disable_account`, because "the entitlement is what costs money and what carries the platform login, so it goes first; if the second operation fails the account is already unable to reach the platform." **Reversing the forward order gives `disable_account` first, which leaves a live entitlement behind on exactly the failure the ordering exists for.**

**AND THE ANSWER TO THE THIRD QUESTION IS THE ONE WORTH SIGNING.** When compensation itself fails, **the account is in the state it was already in**: not admitted. That is not a recovery path and it is not luck. It is clause 1: admission requires a value only one function produces, and a failed compensation produces nothing at all. **A saga whose safety depended on its compensation succeeding would have no answer to this question.**

### 5. The finding: [ADR-102](ADR-102.md)'s system door cannot name a row, and this saga is the first caller that needed it to

**Rendered through the driverless `drizzle-orm/pg-proxy` handle ADR-102's own suite uses, on this branch's base:**

```
insert into "provisioning_queue" ("id", "account_id", ...) values (default, $1, ...) returning ...
update "provisioning_queue" set "status" = $1 returning ...
select "id", "account_id", ... from "provisioning_queue"
delete from "provisioning_queue" returning ...
```

**THE LAST THREE CARRY NO `WHERE` CLAUSE AND NO ARGUMENT WOULD GIVE THEM ONE.** [`scoped-db.ts:448`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), `:460` and `:470` each declare `where: SQL | undefined` in a **required** argument position, on `job-queue.ts`'s own reasoning that "an optional predicate is one a caller reaches the unsafe form of by leaving something out" -- and [`scoped-db.ts:685-708`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)'s `systemTx` passes `undefined` for all three, with no parameter through which a caller could supply anything else.

**So a saga that advanced one row from `written` to `delivered` through `SystemTx.update` would set EVERY ROW IN THE TABLE to `delivered`.** Under `provisioning_queue_delivered_has_timestamp` that write also fails, loudly, on the first row whose `delivered_at` is null -- which is the database catching it rather than the accessor, and only by luck of a constraint that exists for a different reason. On the `SELECT` there is no luck available at all: `SystemTx.rows('provisioningQueue')` is every account's rows.

**THIS IS WORSE THAN THE `semi-join` CLASS THAT ENTRY INHERITS AND NAMES AS OPEN.** ADR-102 section 3 says of it: "A read through it returns a subset; a DELETE through it deletes a subset and leaves the rest, which is a partially applied write with no error anywhere." **A predicate-free system UPDATE writes a SUPERSET**, which is the same defect in the direction that has no bound.

**IT IS NOT ROUTED AROUND.** `sqlExecutor` would run the statement; its vocabulary is one member and that member is `'job-enqueue'`. Using that word to run a status update is the deliberate door ADR-102 built so that a reviewer reads the diff, and this entry does not walk through it. **The consequence is in the types instead**: `ProvisioningAdvancePort` and `ProvisioningReadPort` are declared, are documented as blocked on their own declarations, and have no implementation in this repository.

**WHAT THE REMEDY IS, PRICED AND NOT TAKEN.** A `where` on the system door is a diff on [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), which is outside this fence and which five money-path slices program against; it is a superseding entry rather than a commit, and the shape it should take -- a required predicate argument, or a `byId` narrowing, or a fourth door -- is a ruling about what the system authority MEANS and not a repair. **`0048` stays free**: no migration is involved.

### 6. What clause 8 costs, stated against the entry rather than around it

**`'nightly-batch'` IS THE RIGHT WORD FOR THE STAGE AND IT IS DOING MORE WORK THAN ITS OWN COMMENT CLAIMS.** [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.1 makes the provisioning queue drain `ST-M2-9`, a stage of the nightly batch, so the word is honest. But section 3.6.1 records that **the provisioning CSV push runs every 15 minutes** ([CRON_INVENTORY](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md)), and a fifteen-minute cron reading a word that says "nightly" is a reason nobody has to justify joining. That is [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 7's own argument against the third `SystemReason` member it refused, arriving on the two members it kept.

**It is inherited, named, and not repaired here**, on that entry's own precedent for the same shape: widening or splitting `SystemReason` edits [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), which this fence does not hold.

### 7. What this FORECLOSES

1. **A `SetpointConfirmation` has one producer, permanently.** A second producer removes the entire control, and the removal will look like a convenience. This is [ADR-105](ADR-105.md)'s `cardLegOf` rule applied to a different value for the same reason.
2. **`confirmed_inferred` is never evidence, for any operation.** The day `create_account`'s inferred confirmation is wanted as evidence for something, this rule has to be split per operation, and the split is where [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) section 3.6.2's finding will have to be settled first.
3. **The payload is FLAT and the framing is FIXED.** Both fix every stored `payload_hash` forever, and there is no migration that repairs a framing change -- only a full rewrite of the column. Chosen now because the table has no rows, which is the only moment at which choosing costs nothing ([`hash.ts:303`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/hash.ts)'s identical argument).
4. **A batch carries ONE operation.** The file name names the operation and a CSV of mixed operations has no single column set. A caller with three operations builds three batches.
5. **The file name is assigned once and STORED.** `provisioningFileName` is not idempotent over time -- it carries `yyyymmdd_hhmmss` -- so a retry that recomputes re-uploads under a new name. `0007:280` calls the column "the idempotent name, assigned at batch build" and that column is the authority, not this function.
6. **The saga reads no clock.** `builtAt` is an argument everywhere it is needed, which is [`batch/ports.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts)'s rule one directory over.
7. **There is no UPDATE in this module.** Section 5. The transition is DECIDED here and APPLIED by something that can name a row, and today nothing can.
