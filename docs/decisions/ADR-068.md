## ADR-068: impersonation is a distinct session type that cannot elevate, so three of its seven refusals are ones the corpus already makes  (2026-08-20, status: proposed)

**Merit is passwordless, so there is no credential a support agent can be walked through and impersonation is the only support-visibility path into what a trader is actually seeing. This entry admits it as a read-only, money-blind, time-boxed session type. Its sharpest content REDUCES the ruling rather than extending it: an impersonation session never inherits or intercepts the trader's OTP or passkey, so it can never elevate, so [SECURITY](../architecture/SECURITY.md) `C-27` already refuses three of the seven blocked routes without any new machinery. Four refusals are new and three are inherited, and the count is stated plainly because a document claiming new enforcement where an old invariant already holds is how the old one gets removed later. The one requirement that genuinely needs schema is the session-type boundary, and it is a database constraint in [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql) rather than a middleware convention.**

- **Context:** [FOLD-04](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) section 4 specifies this capability and reserves this number. [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) is frozen, so nothing lands until this entry does. **AUTH, therefore MONEY PATH** under [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s regime table: its own session, plan mode, [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) strict. `ADR-069`'s capability parity is a **condition of section 3's non-disclosure ruling** and not a follow-on, for the reason section 3 gives. `ADR-069` and `ADR-067` are referenced unlinked throughout, because `CI-06a` fails on a link to a document that does not exist.

---

### 0. Why this exists at all, which is not obvious and is worth recording

**A reader five years from now will ask why a fintech built an impersonation feature, and the answer is a property of this system rather than a convenience.** [`0002_identity.sql:280`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) states it in the schema's own words:

> *"Merit is passwordless only, so THERE IS NO PASSWORD TABLE ANYWHERE IN THIS SCHEMA, by design. Adding one is a security architecture change requiring an ADR, not a convenience."*

[ADR-039](ADR-039.md) made OTP and passkeys the whole of trader authentication. **So there is no password to share, no reset link to walk somebody down, and no "what do you see on your screen" that survives a trader who cannot describe a screen.** Every other firm's support path runs through a credential. Merit deleted the credential. **Impersonation is what is left**, and building it is a consequence of `ADR-039` rather than a departure from it.

---

### 1. THE FINDING: three of the seven refusals are already made, and this entry adds nothing to them

[FOLD-04](../plans/FOLD-04-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md) section 4.1 lists seven routes an impersonation session must be refused: payout request, wallet spend, external withdrawal, payout-destination change, contact change of either kind, purchase, and KYC submission.

**[SECURITY:45](../architecture/SECURITY.md) `C-27` already refuses three of them:**

> *"No single factor, and specifically never SMS alone, is sufficient for a sensitive action: payout destination change, contact change of either kind, or external withdrawal. Each requires a passkey assertion or a dual-channel confirmation, which elevates the session rather than re-establishing it. A SIM-swapped session can see everything and change nothing."*

**Requirement 2 says an impersonation session never inherits or intercepts the trader's OTP or passkey.** An elevation is a passkey assertion or a dual-channel confirmation **as the trader**. An impersonation session has neither and can obtain neither. **So it cannot elevate, and every `C-27` action is closed to it by an invariant written before this entry was drafted.**

| | Route | Refused by | This entry adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | payout request | **explicit** | the refusal |
| 2 | wallet spend | **explicit** | the refusal |
| 3 | external withdrawal | `C-27` | **nothing** |
| 4 | payout-destination change | `C-27` | **nothing.** And no route exists, section 6 |
| 5 | contact change, either kind | `C-27` | **nothing** |
| 6 | purchase | **explicit** | the refusal |
| 7 | KYC submission | **explicit** | the refusal |

**Four explicit refusals. Three inherited.**

**Stating the split is not bookkeeping.** A future reader who finds seven refusals implemented in the impersonation middleware, and `C-27` implemented separately, has found what looks like a redundant control. **The redundancy is the point and the ADR has to say so**, because the alternative reading is that `C-27` is the copy and can be deleted. It cannot: `C-27` is what protects a SIM-swapped **trader** session, which has nothing to do with impersonation. **Two controls that happen to cover one route are not one control written twice.**

**The corollary is the honest one and it goes the other way.** If a later change ever gives an impersonation session a way to elevate, **three refusals disappear silently** and no test in this entry's list would fail, because the three inherited ones were never asserted against impersonation-specific code. That is why `M6-N-03`, `M6-N-04` and `M6-N-05` exist at all in [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) section 8.1a: they assert the **outcome**, so the outcome is pinned whichever control produces it.

### 2. The seven requirements, ruled, each with its enforcement site

| # | Requirement | Ruling and where it is enforced |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read-only and money-blind** | **Server-side rejection, never UI hiding.** The refusal is an authorization decision and the tests call the route directly, because a hidden button is not a control (`GS-300`). Four explicit refusals per section 1; three inherited from `C-27` |
| 2 | **A distinct session type** | **A database constraint, not a middleware convention.** `IMPERSONATION-C1` in [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql), **in both directions**, section 4. Initiated only from `ADMIN_ORIGIN` ([ADR-012](ADR-012.md)) under hardware-key SSO |
| 3 | **Time-boxed** | **30 minutes by default, configurable, with a hard database ceiling of 2 hours.** The ceiling is section 5 and it is what makes this a time box rather than a setting |
| 4 | **Visibly banner-ed** | A **persistent** banner for the whole session, **not a dismissible toast**. The surface is [M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md)'s and belongs to `I3`; this entry rules the property and does not write the component |
| 5 | **Reasoned** | A **mandatory controlled-vocabulary `reason_code` plus a non-blank `reason_detail`** at initiation, both `NOT NULL` in [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql). This mirrors [`0017:82`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql), *"NO UNEXPLAINED ADMIN ACTION, EVER. NOT NULL is the whole control"* |
| 6 | **Audited** | Actor, subject, reason, start, end, **and pages viewed**, the last in `impersonation_page_views`. Internal tier. A page view outside its session's box is **unwritable**, section 4 |
| 7 | **Not disclosed to the trader** | **Ruled: no trader-facing disclosure**, section 3. Internal tier only, and impersonation does not surface in a trader-audience evidence pack |

**Roles are closed and this entry does not widen them.** [API_CONTRACT:516](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) reads *"Roles: `owner` (all), `ops` (read plus account actions, no config or role changes), `readonly`."* Impersonation is an `owner` and `ops` capability; `readonly` does not get it, because initiating a session that appears to a subject as themselves is an action.

**`D5` is the matrix and `M6-N-nn` is the test identifier.** [M06:330](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) is the primary source. **Any document saying "a D5 test" is wrong**, and this is the third fold cycle in which a referral has said it.

### 3. Non-disclosure, and the argument against it, which is strong enough that omitting it would be dishonest

> **THE RULING. The trader is NOT notified that an impersonation session occurred, in-app or otherwise. Impersonation events are internal tier only and do not appear in a trader-audience evidence pack.**

The rationale is that this is an internal support-visibility tool, fully audited internally, and that a notification would be noise on a support contact the trader themselves opened.

**Now the counter-argument, stated at full strength because it is the requirement a future reader will challenge first.** Merit's whole differentiator is that **the trader can check the firm's work**: the transparency platform, the replay determinism, the evidence pack. **A silent view of a trader's account is the one place in this system where the firm sees and the trader cannot.** It is a deliberate exception to the property the product is sold on.

**Three things make it survivable, and all three are load bearing:**

1. **The compensating control is the internal audit trail**, and it is why requirement 6 includes **pages viewed** rather than stopping at start and end. An unnotified view that is not itself recorded in detail is not an exception to transparency, it is the absence of it.
2. **The exception's scope is exactly "read-only."** Non-disclosure is defensible for a view and is not defensible for a write.
3. **`ADR-069`'s capability parity is a CONDITION of this ruling and not a follow-on.** An `owner` admin must be able to perform any trader action **from the admin surface, attributed to the admin**, so that nobody ever needs impersonation to get work done.

**The failure mode this entry is really guarding.** If parity is not delivered, support will keep hitting work it cannot do admin-side, and **the pressure to write-enable impersonation becomes irresistible**. At that point a silent, unnotified, **trader-attributed** write path exists: the audit trail says the trader did it, the trader was never told, and the evidence pack Merit would rely on in a chargeback representment now asserts something false. **Read-only plus non-disclosure is coherent. Write-enabled plus non-disclosure is not, and the distance between them is one product decision made under support pressure.**

**`OQ-F4-03` is not answered here, and separating it is the point.** Evidence packs carry a declared audience including `regulator` (`SD-M6-04`). **A pack that omits impersonation for a trader audience and a pack that omits it for a regulator are different decisions**, and this entry rules only the first. The second is a legal question and belongs with the counsel sitting.

### 4. `IMPERSONATION-C1` and `C2`: what the database enforces, and why middleware was not enough

**`IMPERSONATION-C1`, the session-type boundary, in both directions.**

The trader authentication path resolves a bearer token by looking it up on `sessions`. [`0002_identity.sql:342`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) declares that column: `refresh_token_hash bytea NOT NULL UNIQUE`, commented *"Rotation on every refresh. The hash, never the token."* **So the whole question is whether a `sessions` row can ever exist carrying an impersonation token's hash.** [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql) answers it with a `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE` trigger on `impersonation_sessions` refusing a `token_hash` present in `sessions.refresh_token_hash`, **and its mirror on `sessions`**.

**Both directions, because one alone leaves an ordering hole.** A guard only on `impersonation_sessions` is satisfied by inserting the trader row second. The mirror closes it, and `GS-303` is the scenario: *"a database-level distinction under [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql), not a middleware convention. A token that can be replayed makes every other control on this list decorative."*

**The table also carries no `user_id`, no `auth_factor`, no `elevated_at` and no `elevated_by_factor`**, and that is structural rather than an omission. `C-27`'s elevation columns live on `sessions` ([`0029`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql), `SD-M4-04`). **There is no column on `impersonation_sessions` a trader-session lookup could resolve and no column an elevation could be written to**, so section 1's "it cannot elevate" is a fact about the schema rather than a rule somebody must remember.

**`IMPERSONATION-C2`: a page view cannot be recorded outside its session's box.** `viewed_at` must fall between `started_at` and `LEAST(expires_at, COALESCE(ended_at, expires_at))`.

**This is stronger than `GS-301`'s prose and the difference is worth naming.** `GS-301` says an expired session's next request is *"refused, not silently served."* A `CHECK` cannot refuse a request; it can only refuse a row. **So the constraint makes a request served after expiry UNAUDITABLE: the system cannot write the page-view row, and a system that served it fails loudly at the moment it tries to record it rather than quietly succeeding.** A control that turns a silent failure into a noisy one is doing the job an invariant can do from where it sits.

**Grants are revoked narrowly.** [`0026:174`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql)'s `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES` gives `merit_app` full DML on every table created afterwards, so append-only is a **revoke** and not a default. On `0032`'s and `0039`'s precedent, [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql) revokes `UPDATE, DELETE` on `impersonation_page_views`, `DELETE` on `impersonation_sessions`, which keeps the `UPDATE` the exit needs, and `ALL` from `merit_analytics`, because a row here names an admin actor beside a trader subject.

### 5. The 2 hour ceiling, which is the one parameter this entry proposes rather than transcribes

**Requirement 3 says "30 minutes by default, configurable" and stops.** That is not a time box.

> **THE RULING. The default is 30 minutes. The value is configurable. `expires_at` may not exceed `started_at + interval '2 hours'`, and the ceiling is a `CHECK` rather than a setting.**

**A configurable duration with no ceiling is a setting.** Whatever bound exists lives in whichever form or config row last set it, and the failure mode is not malice: it is a support lead raising the value during a bad week and nobody lowering it. **A bound that can be raised by the person inconvenienced by it is not a bound**, and the database is the only place to put one that cannot.

**The number is a launch candidate and it is the founder's**, under the standing [parameter-status ruling](gates/parameter-status-launch-candidates-versus-structural-rulings-founder-ruling-2026-08-14.md). **What is structural is that a ceiling exists and lives in the schema. The value 2 is proposed.** A session may not settle a number that bounds how long a firm can silently watch a customer.

### 6. `M6-N-04` guards a capability with no route, and it is labelled a tripwire so nobody mistakes it for coverage

**`grep destination` over [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) returns three hits and none of them is a route.** Two are the OTP challenge's `destination` field ([:81](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), [:83](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). The third is [:691](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), a **`D5` matrix row** reading *"Changing a payout destination from a session whose only factor is SMS OTP | `passkey or dual_channel` (C-27: payout destination change) | 403 `forbidden`."*

**So the capability is enumerated in the negative-authz matrix and the route that would perform it is unwritten.** A negative-authz test written against it today exercises nothing.

**That is the "control that exists and enforces nothing" class**, and it fails in the direction that does damage: an inventory shows seven blocked routes with seven tests and one of the seven is asserting against a 404. **`M6-N-04` is therefore written as a conditional and labelled a TRIPWIRE in the test body itself**, not only in this entry: it asserts that **if** a payout-destination route is ever added, an impersonation session cannot reach it. **Whoever writes that route owns converting it into a live test**, and that sentence belongs beside the assertion where that author will read it rather than in an ADR they have no reason to open.

### 7. What this entry does not rule, and one thing that belongs to nobody

**`OQ-F4-04`, `closed` identities.** `GS-302` rules `restricted`: **permitted for visibility**, and every money surface `restricted` already blocks stays blocked, because support needs to see a restricted account precisely when the trader calls. [`0001:27`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) declares `identity_status AS ENUM ('active', 'restricted', 'closed')` and [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) **refused** a fourth value, so a ruling written against a `suspended` identity would be unwritable. **`closed` is a support case that will happen and it is deliberately not ruled here.**

**`OQ-F4-01` and `OQ-F4-02`** are `ADR-069`'s: whether a contract signature is legally personal, and whether an admin-submitted KYC is worth anything to the provider. Neither is decided by this entry.

> **AND ONE ITEM BELONGS TO NOBODY, WHICH IS WHY IT IS RESTATED HERE RATHER THAN CITED IN PASSING. [ADR-062](ADR-062.md) section 1 rules the payout gate as `identities.status = 'active'`. [M20:62](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-06` enumerates the wallet-spend and external-withdrawal gate set with `identities.status = 'restricted'`, the `<>` shape. A `closed` identity fails `= 'active'` and passes `<> 'restricted'`, SO IT CANNOT REQUEST A PAYOUT AND CAN STILL SPEND ITS WALLET BALANCE. This is [ADR-062](ADR-062.md)'s own `OQ-062-01`, stated there in those terms.**

**It is not this entry's to fix.** The site is `INV-M20-06`, [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) belongs to `I3` and to `P5`, and the remedy `ADR-062` proposes is to move the other doors to `= 'active'` rather than to weaken the payout gate. **What this entry adds is the ownership fact: it has now been recorded by three sessions, it has a number, and no session's fence contains the file that would close it.** A finding recorded three times and owned by nobody is on its way to being treated as handled, and the difference between "open" and "unowned" is the difference between a queue and a hole. **Naming it here is the cheapest available correction, and it is not a substitute for somebody holding `M20`.**

---

- **Decision:** Impersonation is admitted as a **read-only, money-blind, time-boxed, reasoned, audited, banner-ed and undisclosed** session type, distinct from a trader session **by database constraint in both directions**. Four routes are refused explicitly, **three are refused by `C-27` and this entry adds nothing to them**. The time box carries a **hard 2 hour ceiling in the schema**, with the value proposed as a launch candidate. Non-disclosure is ruled, its counter-argument is recorded at full strength, and **`ADR-069`'s parity is a condition of it**.

- **Alternatives considered:**

  - **Middleware-only session typing** (rejected, section 4). Every other control on the list is decorative if a token can be replayed, and a convention is not a boundary.
  - **A `sessions` row carrying an `is_impersonation` flag** (rejected). It puts the impersonation token in the exact table the trader auth path queries, so the boundary becomes a predicate every caller must remember rather than a row that cannot exist.
  - **Notifying the trader** (rejected, section 3, and it is the strongest objection to this entry). It is coherent and it is what the product's own transparency argument points at. Rejected because the exception's scope is exactly read-only and the compensating control is a detailed internal record. **If write access is ever granted, this alternative must be re-opened**, and section 3 says so in those terms.
  - **A configurable duration with no ceiling** (rejected, section 5). It is a setting wearing the word "box".
  - **Writing all seven refusals as new impersonation-specific enforcement** (rejected, section 1). It would state that this entry enforces what `C-27` already enforces, and the reading a later engineer takes from two controls covering one route is that one of them is redundant.

- **Consequences:**

  - [`0042`](../../packages/db/migrations/0042_impersonation_sessions.sql) lands: two tables, three `CHECK` guards, two cross-table triggers, and narrow revokes. `SD-M6-10`.
  - [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) gains `SD-M6-10` and **claims `M6-N-01` to `M6-N-08`**, with the router enumeration continuing from `M6-N-09`. The claim landed in this session's **first** commit, because `I4` writes the same module concurrently and `M6-N-nn` has a rule instead of an allocation table.
  - **`GS-300` to `GS-303` are pinned by what was built.** `GS-304` is `ADR-069`'s.
  - **`I3` owns every surface**: [M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md)'s banner, and the rejection surfaces in [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md), [M19](../plans/M19-kyc-identity.md) and [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md). This entry writes no component.
  - **Nothing in the rules engine moves.** No gate, no rule, no fixture.
  - **`OQ-062-01` remains open and unowned**, section 7.

- **Founder approval: PENDING.** **Unsigned by design rather than by omission.** This is auth, therefore money path ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), [ADR-003](ADR-003.md)), it amends a frozen module plan, it rules that Merit may view a customer's account without telling them, and it proposes a parameter that bounds how long. It is not taken on a session's authority ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md)). **Recommended for acceptance, with section 5's number the founder's to set.**
