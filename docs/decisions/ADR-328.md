### 2. THE RULING, part one: why a REGISTER and not a grep

**THE OBVIOUS GATE IS A GREP AND IT IS WRONG, MEASURED RATHER THAN ARGUED.** A scan of `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src` for the phrasings that all four occurrences used returns **255 matching lines across 338 files** on this tree. Most of them are TRUE: an adapter nobody has written, a rail nobody has bought, a resolver that is genuinely absent, a port whose blocker is named beside it. **A gate that can only be satisfied by deleting honest prose is a gate somebody deletes**, and the first session under deadline is the one who does it.

**THE SECOND OBVIOUS GATE PARSES, AND IT FAILS IN THE DIRECTION THIS ROW IS ABOUT.** A check that decides which sentences it can adjudicate and stays quiet on the rest is a check whose blind spot is invisible, which is the defect one layer down.

**SO EACH CLAIM IS WRITTEN DOWN WITH THE ARTIFACT IT NAMES, AND THE CHECK ASSERTS THE ARTIFACT RATHER THAN THE SENTENCE.** Both registers are **WRITTEN AND NEVER COMPUTED**, which is `CI-06/gate-inventory`'s own rule for `INVENTORY_PROBES` and `UNPROBEABLE_ARTIFACTS` in [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs), and the reason is the same: a register a session can regenerate is a register nobody decided.

**WHERE IT LIVES, AND WHY.** This estate keeps registers in two places and the split is by CONTENT. A register of NUMBERS lives in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md), where `RI-29` reads it, because a number is claimed by prose and spent by prose. A register carrying PROBES lives in the file that runs them, which is where both of `gates.mjs`'s live. **An entry here carries an executable probe, so it belongs with the runner.** It is its own file rather than a block inside [`repo-invariants.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs) on `RI-11`'s and `RI-18`'s precedent ([ADR-138](ADR-138.md)); the dependency runs ONE WAY, as `RI-18`'s does and `RI-11`'s does not, so the `CHECKS` literal can import it without depending on an import order.

### 3. THE RULING, part two: the six legs, and which one is the point

| Leg | What it asserts | Goes red when |
|---|---|---|
| **1. SITE** | every registered site exists and carries its claim line **exactly once** | the sentence is reworded, moved or deleted and the register did not move with it, or the anchor stops identifying one line |
| **2. LIVE** | a `live` claim's artifact is **ABSENT** | **somebody lands it.** This is the leg that FAILS ON GOOD NEWS, and it is the row |
| **3. RETIRED** | a `retired` claim, quoted as history under `RI-14`, has its artifact **PRESENT** | a true sentence was retired, or the artifact went away again |
| **4. UNBINDABLE** | an entry with no probeable artifact carries a reason of at least 40 characters and names no artifact | somebody registers a claim as uncheckable without saying why |
| **5. FURNITURE** | every artifact is named by at least one claim | a probe runs for nothing, which is how a register grows a mechanism whose subject has gone |
| **6. THE SWEEP** | every line in `apps/*/src` or `packages/*/src` matching a registered needle **within two lines** of a word from the written absence vocabulary is a registered claim | a NEW sentence about an ALREADY REGISTERED artifact lands unbound |

**LEG 6 IS THE ONE THAT SCALES AND IT IS WHY THIS ROW EXISTS RATHER THAN A FOURTH HAND REPAIR.** Register the artifact once and every site naming it has to be accounted for. Section 6 shows it reporting ADR-327's site off ADR-326's registration, which is the row that did not happen.

**LEG 3 COSTS ONE BRANCH AND CATCHES THE DEFECT RUNNING BACKWARDS.** A sentence is quoted as history only because the tree falsified it, so a retired quote over an artifact that is still absent is a correction written ahead of the thing that justifies it.

**THE WINDOW OF TWO IS A MEASUREMENT AND NOT A CHOICE.** Comment prose in this repository wraps near column 78, so a sentence naming an artifact and asserting its absence routinely spans two lines. **Occurrence 3 is exactly that shape**: `pg-boss itself emits through` sits on one line and `THAT MIGRATION DOES NOT EXIST YET` on the next, so a LINE-scoped sweep misses it, and section 6 asserts that in a case of its own. At a window of 1 the sweep also misses [`schedule.ts:217`](../../apps/worker/src/schedule.ts), whose needle sits two lines below its absence word. At 3 it reaches [`pg-boss-queue.ts:36`](../../packages/queue/src/pg-boss-queue.ts), which says a boot-time install would land *"with no review and no migration number"* -- a sentence about a REFUSED ALTERNATIVE that asserts no absence at all, and therefore the first false positive. **Two is where the last true site arrives and the first false one has not.**

  **THE FIRST QUESTION IS THE ONE NO CASE IN THIS DIFF CAN ANSWER: IS A REGISTER SOMEBODY WILL KEEP.** Every leg here is mechanical and every leg was watched failing. **None of them can tell you whether the next session, at 11pm with a migration to land, adds its absence claim to a file in `packages/tooling` or quietly phrases the sentence so no needle reaches it.** The design leans on that not happening: leg 6 makes it hard for an artifact somebody already registered, and does nothing at all for a new one. **The honest alternative is a marker comment at each claim site**, which would make discovery total and would cost a sweep of the tree editing more than twenty files, which this row was told not to do. If the register is not kept, this check degrades into a permanent, correct, shrinking assertion about eleven sentences, and that is a real outcome rather than a failure mode I am dismissing.

  **THE SECOND IS WHETHER THE SWEEP'S SCOPE IS RIGHT, AND I THINK IT IS THE WEAKEST LINE I DREW.** It reads `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src` and excludes `docs/`, `test/`, `scripts/` and every `.sql` file. The `docs/` exclusion is defensible and has precedent ([ADR-212](ADR-212.md), `RI-15`, `RI-16`). **`scripts/` is not: occurrence 4 lived there, and it is registered by hand precisely because the sweep cannot see it.** Including `scripts/**/*.sql` would have caught it by discovery rather than by my having read ADR-327 first, and the cost is a second file-shape to parse. I chose the smaller change; a founder who thinks that is the wrong trade is looking at the right thing.

  **THE THIRD IS THE FIFTH SITE'S REPAIR, WHICH IS A JUDGEMENT ABOUT ONE WORD.** The old sentence said `@merit/db` supplies neither half. The new one says it supplies both, **and that one of them is unusable for the constructor path for a measured reason**. That is a more complicated sentence than the one it replaces, and complicated sentences are the ones that go stale next. The alternative was to say the supply exists and stop, leaving [ADR-327](ADR-327.md) section 9 finding 1 to be rediscovered by whoever writes the ONE DOOR. **I preferred the longer sentence because that finding cost a measurement to obtain and reads like a day lost if it has to be found again**, but a reader who wants refusal prose to carry one fact per paragraph should overrule it.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left to be found.** **The check binds a sentence to an artifact and an artifact is not a fact about the world.** `queue-door`'s probe asks whether any shipped source file imports `@merit/queue`; a door built by re-exporting the package from a file that imports it under a different specifier, or a `JobQueue` supplied by a hand-written adapter that never names the package, would satisfy the probe and leave three registered sentences claiming the work is not done. **That is the same weakness [ADR-324](ADR-324.md) named in its own approval block about `/conversion/i`, one layer up**, and it fails in the same safe direction: it misses work that landed rather than claiming work that did not. **What is new here is that the same weakness now applies to fifteen sentences instead of one**, which is what a control that scales buys and costs at the same time.
