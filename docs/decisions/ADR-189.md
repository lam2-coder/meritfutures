## ADR-189: the ninth ledger transaction is `LT-09`, it reverses `LT-06` when the rail is exhausted, and `0057` is taken because a reversal nobody checks is a promise  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[ADR-181](ADR-181.md) section 6 found a terminal state with no ledger posting and declined to mint the transaction that answers it. [ADR-187](ADR-187.md) minted the account it strands and recorded the finding as still open. This entry closes it.** `LT-09`, `wallet_withdrawal_failure`, is the exact negation of `LT-06`, posted in the same database transaction as `transferring --> failed`. **The mechanism was already ruled and is not redesigned**: `SD-M5-05` carries `reversal_of`, corrections are compensating entries and never updates, so `LT-06` is reversed rather than deleted, amended or adjusted in place.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `189`, session **347**, dispatched as `claude/ninth-ledger-transaction`. **`0057` was RESERVED CONDITIONALLY** and this entry states the condition's outcome up front: **the number is TAKEN.** Section 4 is why.

- **MONEY PATH. [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) READ IS OWED** on [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql) and **nothing here discharges it**: not the ruling being small, not the failure path having been posted end to end, and not this entry being merged. **Merging is not signing.** The entry ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line.

- **`0009`, `0027`, `0038`, `0052`, `0053`, `0054`, `0055` and `0056` are byte for byte unchanged, and none of `0056`'s three guards is touched.** `0056` merged one wave ago and moved `ledger_accounts_code_is_declared`, `LEDGER-C2`'s function body and `ledger_accounts_kind_matches_code` in one transaction. [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql) does not DROP, re-ADD, REPLACE or name any of the three. **The vocabulary stays closed at EIGHT and no ninth code is minted**: `LT-09` posts against `withdrawals_in_flight` and `trader_wallet`, which `0056` and [`0054`](../../packages/db/migrations/0054_identity_ledger_accounts.sql) already opened.

- **WHAT IS DERIVED AND WHAT IS JUDGED, separated before the argument starts.** That `LT-09` exists and reverses `LT-06` is DERIVED, in section 1, and the mechanism is ruled elsewhere. That `failed` is the only terminal state that strands anything is DERIVED, in section 2, state by state. **What is JUDGED is the SPELLING of the kind, and the decision to spend `0057` rather than return it**, which are sections 3 and 4. ### 2. What triggers it: the terminal states, enumerated and checked one at a time

**[STATE_MACHINES section 3.2](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) draws exactly three arrows into `[*]`**, at [`:204-206`](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md). Each is checked against whether `LT-06` has posted when the row arrives, because that is the only thing that decides whether anything is standing.

| Terminal state | Reachable from | Has `LT-06` posted? | What discharges the obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `settled` | `transferring` ([`:202`](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)) | **YES.** Past `approved` | **`LT-07`.** It debits `withdrawals_in_flight` and credits `firm_treasury` in the transaction that moves the cash ([M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1, [ADR-187](ADR-187.md)) |
| `failed` | `transferring` ([`:203`](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)) | **YES.** Past `approved` | **NOTHING, before this entry.** `LT-07` never posts because no cash leaves. This is the whole finding |
| `cancelled` | `requested` and `cooling` ONLY ([`:199-200`](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)) | **NO.** Both are BEFORE `approved` | **Nothing is needed.** `LT-06` never posted, so there is nothing to reverse |

**SO `LT-09` FIRES ON `failed` AND ON NOTHING ELSE, AND THE RULE UNDER THAT ANSWER IS THE ONE WORTH CARRYING: a terminal wallet withdrawal must leave nothing standing in `withdrawals_in_flight`, and the three states satisfy it three different ways.** `WD-C1` in [`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql) is stated as that rule rather than as *"`failed` needs a reversal"*, so it holds on all three and would hold on a fourth.

**`cancelled` IS SAFE BY THE DRAWING AND NOT BY A CONSTRAINT, AND THAT IS REPORTED RATHER THAN RELIED ON.** EXECUTED against the merged schema: `wallet_withdrawals` carries **zero** non-internal triggers before this entry and **seven** CHECK constraints, and not one of them polices a status transition. An `approved --> cancelled` UPDATE is refused by nothing in the database. **It does not become a hole here**, because `WD-C1` fires on `cancelled` as well and would catch exactly that case: a cancel after `LT-06` leaves the obligation standing and does not commit. **The enumeration is what made that worth doing**; a trigger written for `failed` alone would have left it open.

**A FOURTH TERMINAL VALUE WOULD NOT JOIN THE TRIGGER AND NOTHING WOULD SAY SO.** `WD-C1`'s `WHEN` clause names the three states as literals. That is the same class as `0027`'s vocabulary lists and it is stated in the migration's header for the same reason.

### 3. The identifier, the kind, the reference and the key

**THE IDENTIFIER IS `LT-09` AND IT IS CLAIMED PROPERLY.** [ADR-181](ADR-181.md) section 6 declined to mint one because *"a transaction id is a member of a declared identifier series, which `CI-06/identifier-series` refuses to let a citation invent"*. The `LT` series is declared in [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) with [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) as its register, file-wide, and a definition site is a table row whose first cell LEADS with the identifier. **So the row in section 2.1 is the mint**, the series runs `LT-01` through `LT-09` with no gap, and the gate passes on the amended document rather than in spite of it.

**THE KIND IS `wallet_withdrawal_failure`, AND THAT IS A JUDGEMENT.** The external leg names its postings for the transition each accompanies: `LT-06` is `wallet_withdrawal_approval` and `LT-07` is `wallet_withdrawal_settlement`. This one accompanies `transferring --> failed`, so it is `wallet_withdrawal_failure`.

> **`wallet_withdrawal_reversal` WAS THE AVAILABLE ALTERNATIVE AND IT HAS A REAL ARGUMENT.** `LT-03` is `payout_reversal` and is *"the exact negation of LT-01, with `reversal_of` set"*, which is this posting's shape one leg over, so the corpus already has a name for what this is. **It is refused because `reversal_of` on the row carries the mechanism already**, and a `kind` spelling it states one fact twice, which is [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 5's hazard on a column instead of a document. The external leg's two existing kinds name transitions and this entry keeps that family intact. **A founder who prefers the `LT-03` parallel is holding a defensible position**, and the cost of being wrong here is one string.

**THE REFERENCE TUPLE IS `('wallet_withdrawal', wallet_withdrawals.id)` AND THIS ENTRY RULES IT, BECAUSE NOTHING ELSE HAS.** Nothing in this tree posts `LT-06` or `LT-07`: [session 303](../sessions/2026-08-27-session-303.md) took `POST /wallet/withdrawals` and posted no ledger transaction anywhere, on asymmetry of harm. So the tuple is unwritten, and `WD-C1` cannot exist without it, because `ledger_entries` carries no withdrawal foreign key and `(reference_kind, reference_id)` is **the whole of the edge** from a withdrawal to its postings. **It is not invented**: [`payouts.ts:805`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) posts `LT-01` under `'payout_request'` and [`checkout.ts:1707`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) posts under `'purchase'`, each the singular of the table the posting is about. `LT-06`, `LT-07` and `LT-09` all carry it, and the ruling binds all three because `WD-C1` sums across them.

**THE KEY IS `` `wallet_withdrawal_failure <the withdrawal's stored key>` ``.** [ADR-175](ADR-175.md) clause 1 is the rule -- a key names the EVENT it posts and never the DOOR that reached it -- and its clause 3 is the spelling, ruled there for `LT-07`. Its clause 5 says in terms that `LT-02` and `LT-08` are NOT ruled by it, so **this entry extends clause 3 to a third posting rather than treating it as covered**, for the reason that clause gives: a key spelled for a posting whose slice has not been read is a key somebody argues with later. Bare is unavailable because `LT-06` claims it under a globally unique column ([ADR-175](ADR-175.md) finding 4, EXECUTED), and an endpoint prefix is the thing `LT-06` refused.

### 4. Whether anything can PROVE the obligation returned to zero, which is what decides `0057`

**THE DISPATCH PUT THIS AS A BINARY -- a constraint or a trigger takes the number, a posting builder plus a TypeScript assertion returns it -- and the honest answer is that the two halves prove different things and neither is sufficient.** `0057` is taken, and this section is the argument.

**THREE PROPERTIES HAVE TO HOLD FOR "THE OBLIGATION RETURNED TO ZERO" TO BE TRUE.** They are separated because they fail separately.

| # | Property | What can prove it | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The reversal is EXACT.** Equal, opposite, and against the same two accounts | **TypeScript, structurally.** [`reversalPosting`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts) swaps each transfer's two sides and carries the amount across. It negates nothing and builds no leg, so the negation follows from the same arithmetic that makes the original balance. **A partial reversal is not refused, it is UNAVAILABLE**: there is no parameter for an amount | `packages/ledger` |
| **2** | **It is posted AT MOST ONCE** | **A UNIQUE index, and nothing else in this database.** See below | `0057` |
| **3** | **It is posted AT LEAST ONCE, for every withdrawal that reached `failed`** | **A deferred constraint trigger, and nothing else in this estate.** See below | `0057` |

**PROPERTY 2 IS NOT HYPOTHETICAL AND IT WAS MEASURED BEFORE A LINE OF `0057` WAS WRITTEN.** [`0009:98-99`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) creates `ledger_transactions_reversal_of_idx` and it is **not unique**, read from `pg_indexes` rather than from the DDL. EXECUTED on the merged schema: a SECOND transaction naming the same `reversal_of`, under a different `idempotency_key`, **LANDS**. It drives `withdrawals_in_flight` to **+25,000** and `trader_wallet` to **-25,000**, and **the global sum stays 0**, so `INV-M5-04`'s zero-sum trigger passes, `LEDGER-C1` passes, and the unique `idempotency_key` does not see it because the second posting has a different key. **A TypeScript builder cannot close this**: exactly-once against a concurrent second driver is a database property. The index is DROPPED and re-created UNIQUE under its own name, which is [`0031`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql)'s idiom on `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx` and `SD-M5-08`'s on a constraint.

**PROPERTY 3 IS THE ONE THAT DECIDES THE NUMBER.** The defect this entry exists to close is *a state transition happened and no posting followed*. **Closing it with a posting builder reproduces it exactly**: a builder proves what a caller who called it built, and says nothing about a caller who did not. [STATE_MACHINES:213](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) names that failure mode in this table's own history -- the halt that was *"representable and entirely unenforced for four migrations"*, which *"arrived by writing half a mechanism and reading the other half as done"*. **A reversal nobody checks is a promise, and the corpus has already paid for one of those on this exact row.**

**SO `WD-C1` ASSERTS THE PROPERTY ITSELF RATHER THAN THE PROCEDURE.** At every terminal transition, the net of every `withdrawals_in_flight` entry over every transaction naming that withdrawal must be exactly **0**. It does not ask whether `LT-09` was posted, what its kind was, or who posted it. **It asks the question the trader would ask.**

**IT CARRIES A SECOND ASSERTION UNDER THE SAME ID, AND WITHOUT IT THE FIRST PASSES VACUOUSLY ON THE BUG.** A handler that transitions the row and posts nothing sums over zero rows and gets 0. [`0001:90`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) states the rule -- *"a gate that silently stops gating is worse than an absent one"* -- and a net over an empty set is how this one would. So a `failed` withdrawal that reached approval must name at least one `ledger_transactions` row. **Two assertions under one id is [`0038`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)'s own shape**, where `assert_adjustment_reversal_is_sound` raises `ADJ-C1` for six.

**THE PROXY FOR "REACHED APPROVAL" IS `source_provenance_summary`, IT IS A PROXY, AND ITS TWO WEAKNESSES ARE NAMED RATHER THAN LEFT TO BE FOUND.** `wallet_withdrawals` has no `approved_at`. What it has is [`wallet_withdrawals_approved_has_provenance`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) at `0011:192`, which requires the summary on every status except `requested`, `cooling`, `cancelled` and `failed`, so a row that passed `approved` carries one and keeps it. **First: `failed` is on that exemption list**, so an UPDATE that sets the status and clears the summary in one statement escapes assertion 2. **Second: [session 303](../sessions/2026-08-27-session-303.md) found that `'[]'::jsonb <> '{}'::jsonb` is TRUE**, so an empty array satisfies the proxy. **Assertion 1 is unaffected by both**: it reads the ledger and not the row.

**WHAT `0057` DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO.** It does not require a posting on `settled`: extending assertion 2 there would make `LT-07` mandatory at the settlement transition, which is a rule about a posting this entry does not rule and a slice this session does not hold. **The consequence is stated rather than hidden**: on `settled`, assertion 1 passes vacuously when nothing posted.

**AND THE HONEST LIMIT OF THE WHOLE MECHANISM.** A posting written under a DIFFERENT reference tuple is invisible to both assertions. `ledger_entries` has no withdrawal foreign key, so there is no join that does not go through `(reference_kind, reference_id)`, and no migration in this corpus has ever proposed one. **What holds the tuple still is that it is built in one place** -- [`reversal.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts) exports it as a constant rather than letting a call site retype it -- **and that [`ninth-transaction.test.ts`](../../packages/ledger/test/ninth-transaction.test.ts) holds that constant against the migration's own text**, so the day one moves without the other, the suite goes red.

**THIS IS THE FIFTH TIME THE POOL WOULD HAVE BEEN THE MECHANISM AND IT IS NOT.** [ADR-167](ADR-167.md) and [ADR-181](ADR-181.md) each returned a number unspent, and [`0055`](../../packages/db/migrations/0055_last_two_ledger_kinds.sql)'s row records that as the pool working. **This one is spent, and the test it is spent against is the one those entries state: a number is taken when the ruling needs schema.** Property 3 needs schema and nothing in TypeScript can carry it.

### 6. Findings this session found, which were not in the dispatch

**FINDING 1. [`0009:103-104`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql)'s COMMENT CLAIMS TWO RULES AND THE CONSTRAINT BENEATH IT ENFORCES ONE.** It reads *"A reversal may not reverse itself, and may not chain onto another reversal: a reversal of a reversal is an adjustment and should be posted as one"*, and `ledger_transactions_no_self_reversal` is `CHECK (reversal_of IS NULL OR reversal_of <> id)`. **EXECUTED: a transaction whose `reversal_of` names a transaction that is itself a reversal LANDS.** The chain rule is enforced exactly one table over, by [`0038`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)'s `assert_adjustment_reversal_is_sound` check 1 at `0038:323`, which cites this very comment as its authority. **It is REPORTED and not closed here.** It is a general ledger rule, its remedy is a trigger over `ledger_transactions` at large, and closing it from a withdrawal's fence would be this entry ruling the ledger on the way past. **`0057` closes the adjacent hole and not this one, and the distinction is argued rather than convenient**: a second reversal of one `LT-06` is on `LT-09`'s own path and is how THIS ruling's obligation fails to return to zero, while a chain needs a third transaction that no path in this ruling produces.

**FINDING 2. [ADR-187](ADR-187.md) SECTION 9 NOMINATES THE FAILED-WITHDRAWAL CASE AS THE CHEAPEST PLACE TO SEE WHETHER `LT-06`'s AND `LT-07`'s SLOTS ARE THE SAME ACCOUNT, AND IT IS NOT ONE.** That entry says its own most likely error is the single-account reading, and that the failure case is where it would show. **It does not show there.** `LT-09` reverses `LT-06` and therefore touches `LT-06`'s slot and no other; under a two-account reading the obligation created by `LT-06` still returns to zero and the account `LT-07` would have debited was never touched at all. **The failure path is silent on the question.** So is the settlement path, for a subtler reason: [ADR-187](ADR-187.md) section 6 row 4 reports the obligation at 0 after `LT-07`, but that posting was CONSTRUCTED on the single-account reading, and a two-account arrangement balances too and would report two equal and opposite permanent balances rather than a zero. **THE GOOD NEWS IS THAT THIS RULING DOES NOT DEPEND ON THE ANSWER**: `LT-09` is the exact negation of `LT-06` whichever account `LT-06` credited, and `WD-C1` sums the code the chart actually holds. **The question stays open and this entry moves it nowhere**, which is worth saying because a reader of [ADR-187](ADR-187.md) would otherwise expect this session to have settled it.

**FINDING 3. [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) SECTION 2.1's LEAD-IN SENTENCE SAYS *"one of these five shapes"* ABOVE A TABLE THAT HELD EIGHT ROWS BEFORE THIS ENTRY AND HOLDS NINE AFTER IT.** The sentence was already false in fact and the amendment makes it more so. **It is NOT corrected**, because this entry's fence over section 2.1 is additive and correcting prose in an approved plan on the way past is how a fence becomes advisory. It is a row for whoever holds `M05` next.

**FINDING 4. `wallet_withdrawals` HAS NO TRANSITION ENFORCEMENT AT ALL, AND THE MACHINE IS THE ONLY THING KEEPING `cancelled` HONEST.** Section 2. Measured rather than read: zero non-internal triggers, seven CHECKs, none of which looks at a status transition. `approved --> cancelled` is undrawn and entirely writable. **`WD-C1` narrows it without closing it**, exactly as [`0055`](../../packages/db/migrations/0055_last_two_ledger_kinds.sql) narrowed `0054`'s named hole: a cancel after `LT-06` is now refused, and a cancel before it is still as unpoliced as every other undrawn edge on this table.

**FINDING 5. `wallet_withdrawals_approved_has_provenance` EXEMPTS `failed`, WHICH IS THE ONE TERMINAL STATE WHERE THE SUMMARY IS EVIDENCE OF SOMETHING.** A `failed` row is always reached from `transferring`, so it always passed approval and always had a summary; the exemption lets it be cleared. It is what makes `WD-C1`'s second assertion a proxy rather than a fact. **Not repaired here**: narrowing it would supersede a constraint from [`0011`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) and [`0031`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql) and would additionally refuse a `requested --> failed` row, which is undrawn but not this entry's to forbid.

### 7. Consequences

- **[M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1 GAINS `LT-09` AND LOSES NOTHING.** The amendment is a new row and a new paragraph. **`LT-06`'s, `LT-07`'s and `LT-02`'s rows are not touched**, which is a stronger statement than the additive rule requires: this entry needed nothing from the open slots that they do not already say.

- **`INV-M5-15`'s Open Liability term, which [ADR-181](ADR-181.md) section 7 and [ADR-187](ADR-187.md) both left owed to [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md), gets a second reason to exist and stays owed.** Under this ruling the obligation is not only a term that must be counted; it is a term whose value is provably zero for every terminal withdrawal, which is the sentence a reconciliation would want. **This entry does not write it.**
