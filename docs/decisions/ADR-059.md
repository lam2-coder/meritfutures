## ADR-059: The three engine inputs group A is blocked on, framed as three questions, and the disposition of the five registry rows that no answer to them can free  (2026-08-18, status: proposed)

**Group A has been stuck since batch 9 and the reason has been re-derived four times without ever being put as a decision. This entry does not answer it. It states the gap as three separate questions, derives what each would cost by grepping the tree, checks whether the value each one needs EXISTS, and then recommends. Two of the three questions turn out to have already been answered in test code and never in the corpus. The third has no source anywhere in the tree. What has never been framed at all is what happens to the golden-scenario rows the three leave permanently unwritable, and that is what the recommendation is about.**

- **Context:** [`fixtures/README.md`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md) records batch 9's finding in its own words: *"Batch 9's objective named four rules ... as the group A work the eighty-three-session calendar had made writable. NOT ONE OF THE FOUR IS."* Batch 10 wrote no group A row. Session 58 and session 59 each re-derived the same table and each reported the same five rows still held. **Four independent re-derivations agreeing is not a decision, it is a decision being deferred**, and the deferral has a cost: `GS-001`, `GS-004`, `GS-030`, `GS-031` and `GS-035` sit in a denominator that says they are owed.

  **`R-06` is deliberately excluded from all three questions and is not in scope.** [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) line 426 pins it to `GS-035`, which [`fixtures/README.md`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md) states is *"payout at 23:59:59 versus batch at 00:05"*: it needs a **clock**, and [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) line 112's `INV-01` is *"The engine performs no I/O and reads no clock"*. A proposal that widened to include `R-06` would be proposing to break `INV-01`, so it is named here and left alone. `GS-035` is a group B row and always was.

---

### 0. The finding that reorders the three questions

**All three are blocked on DATA and OWNERSHIP. None is blocked on type design, and the tree already says so about two of them in a place the corpus never reads.**

[`rule-coverage.ts:120`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rule-coverage.ts) declares `DISCHARGED_ELSEWHERE`, four rules the engine does not compute, each with a stated reason. Two of them are questions 1 and 3 of this entry:

| Rule | The reason, quoted from `rule-coverage.ts` |
|---|---|
| `R-01` | *"ingest. `DailyMark` carries `fillCount` and no fill, so there is no execution timestamp to contain and no cast to refuse. A transcribed calendar does not change that: it adds rows, not a column on `CalendarDay`"* |
| `R-05` | *"`trading_calendar.session_open_at` and `session_close_at`, and `0032`'s constraints over them. `CalendarDay` carries neither column, so the timezone conversion R-05 forbids is unwritable here rather than merely unwritten"* |

[`implemented-rules.test.ts:57`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/implemented-rules.test.ts) asserts `COVERED_RULES.length + DISCHARGED_ELSEWHERE` equals the rule count, and line 83 states the position outright: **"NONE OF THE FOUR THAT REMAIN IS BLOCKED ON A FOUNDER RULING: every one is discharged by a layer that is not this engine."** [`rules-a-calendar.test.ts:22`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-a-calendar.test.ts) puts it harder: **"R-01 AND R-05 ARE NOT WAITING FOR THE DATA, THEY ARE WAITING FOR NOTHING."**

**That position is correct and it is stated in the wrong place.** It lives in two test files and in a fixture README. It is not in [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), which still lists `R-01` and `R-05` in the same rule table as the forty-six the engine computes, with a `Config` column and an `Arithmetic and operator` column filled in exactly as if the engine performed them (lines 421 and 425). **A reader who opens the plan document, which is the frozen specification, cannot tell which rules the engine is responsible for.** That is the gap. It is not that nobody knows the answer; it is that the answer has never been written where the specification is.

**So the three questions below are asked and answered honestly, and the answer to two of them is "the corpus already decided, silently".** The decision this entry actually puts in front of the founder is section 5's, and it is the one that has genuinely never been framed.

### 1. Question one: does `DailyMark` gain an INSTANT? (`R-01`, `GS-001`, `GS-030`)

**The type as it stands.** [`types.ts:723`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) declares `DailyMark` with nine fields: `tradingDay`, `openingBalanceCents`, `closingBalanceCents`, `highBalanceCents`, `lowBalanceCents`, `realizedPnlCents`, `adjustmentCents`, `fillCount` and `sourceHash` at line 734. **There is no timestamp on it.** `fillCount` is a count and not a fill, so there is nothing for a session-containment lookup to contain.

**What it would cost, derived by grep rather than estimated.** `git grep -l 'DailyMark'` over tracked `.ts`, `.mjs`, `.md`, `.json` and `.sql` files returns **43 files**, of which 26 are TypeScript:

| Where | Files |
|---|---|
| `packages/rules-engine/src/` | 7 |
| `packages/rules-engine/test/` | 12 |
| `packages/golden-loader/` | 2 |
| `apps/worker/` | 3 |
| `scripts/` | 4 |
| `docs/` | 12 |

**The number that actually costs is the construction sites, not the references.** `git grep -n 'sourceHash:'` over `.ts` returns **21 lines in 15 files**; one is the declaration in `types.ts`, so **20 object literals build a `DailyMark` today** and every one of them would need a new field. Two of those files are the golden loader (`loader.ts`, `run.ts`), which means the **fixture format gains a key**, which means every fixture in the directory is touched, which is why this is not a type change with a small blast radius even though it is one line in `types.ts`.

**Where would the value come from? It exists, and it is not Merit's to invent.** [`0013_ingest.sql:152`](../../packages/db/migrations/0013_ingest.sql) declares `executed_at timestamptz NOT NULL,   -- vendor execution time` on the fills table, and the very next line of that migration reads `-- RESOLVED THROUGH THE CALENDAR, never from the timestamp's UTC date.` [`rithmic/src/simulator/types.ts:225`](../../packages/rithmic/src/simulator/types.ts) carries the same instant as `readonly executedAtUtc: string;`. **So the instant `R-01` needs is present, at ingest, one module upstream, and the resolution `R-01` describes is already assigned there.**

**Purity check.** Adding a `string` field to `DailyMark` breaks neither `INV-01` nor `RI-07`: a field is not I/O and not a clock. **But the rule that field is for does.** `R-01` is *"a fill belongs to the trading day whose session contains its execution timestamp"*, and a containment lookup needs the session's bounds, which is question three. An instant on `DailyMark` and no bounds on `CalendarDay` gives the engine a timestamp it cannot resolve, which is the worst of the three outcomes: it looks like `R-01` is implementable and it is not.

**Answer: no, and it is already recorded as no.** The instant belongs to ingest, ingest already holds it, and `DISCHARGED_ELSEWHERE` already says so. What is missing is the same sentence in [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md).

### 2. Question two: does `halted` get a real VALUE, and from where? (`R-04`, `GS-004`, `GS-031`)

**This is the only one of the three that is not already answered, and the answer is that no publisher of the value exists anywhere in the tree.**

**The field is declared and the rule is implemented.** [`types.ts:222`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) carries `readonly halted: boolean;` under the comment *"R-04: on a halted session, day counters advance and win days do not."* `R-04` is in the engine's declared set, and [`rules-a-calendar.test.ts:239`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-a-calendar.test.ts) exercises both branches of it against a hand-built window with `{ halted: true }`, asserting `tradedDaysCount` advances either way while `winDaysCount` goes 1 and 0. **The rule works. Nothing about question two is about the rule.**

**Every writer of the field writes a constant, and there are two of them, not one.** The brief named the loader; the tree holds a second and it is the more serious.

| Writer | Line | What it writes |
|---|---|---|
| [`golden-loader/src/calendar.ts:158`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/calendar.ts) | inside `record.sessions.map` | `halted: false,` |
| [`db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs:1002`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) | the holiday row | `halted: false,` |
| [`db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs:1035`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) | the ordinary session row | `halted: false,` |

**The second writer is the PRODUCTION seed generator.** So it is not merely that no golden fixture can set `halted`; **no calendar Merit is able to build can set it either.** The database column exists and has since the catalog migration: [`0004_catalog.sql:331`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) declares `halted boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false`, under a comment at line 329 about a trader who *"cannot be penalised"* for a halted session. **A column with a default, two generators writing the default, and no input that could write anything else.**

**What it would cost.** `git grep -l 'CalendarDay'` returns **36 tracked files**, 20 of them TypeScript: 7 under `packages/rules-engine/src/`, 11 under `packages/rules-engine/test/`, 1 in the golden loader, 1 in `apps/worker`, 1 under `scripts/`, and 12 documents. **The construction sites are the cost again**: `git grep -n 'halted:'` over `.ts`, excluding the declaration, returns **26 lines in 13 files**, plus the two in `generate.mjs`. **But question two needs no type change at all**, which makes its blast radius the smallest of the three and its blocker the largest: the field exists, the rule reads it, and the entire cost is a record key, a loader mapping, and a source.

**Where would the value come from? Nowhere. This is the finding, and it is better than a design.**

[`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json) has ten top-level keys: `id`, `status`, `_status_note`, `provenance`, `coverage`, `session_rule`, `_holidays_note`, `holidays`, `early_closes`, `declared`. **There is no halts key and no slot for one.** The single occurrence of the string `halt` in that file is inside `_holidays_note`, in prose, and it is [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) section 4 declining to record something else: *"THE INTRADAY PAUSE IS NOT RECORDED AND THAT IS A DECISION."*

**And a halt is not a calendar fact, which is why the schema has no room for it.** [`GLOSSARY.md:32`](../GLOSSARY.md) defines the term: *"A trading day on which the market is halted or limit locked such that a trader cannot transact."* [`EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md:8`](../edge-cases/EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md) and the `GS-031` registry row both say *"halt or limit-locked session"*. **A limit lock is decided intraday, by the market, after the calendar for that day was loaded.** The CME Trading Hours tool publishes a schedule; it does not publish, and cannot publish in advance, the fact that a session locked limit-down. `session_rule` is a rule about opens and closes. `holidays` and `early_closes` are exceptions to that rule known in advance. **`halted` is a third kind of thing entirely and it was filed with the first two.**

**Purity check.** `INV-01` and `RI-07` are untouched: whoever supplies the value, the engine reads a boolean off a row it was handed. **The purity question is where the value is DECIDED, and every candidate answer is outside the engine.** Which is the point: the engine is not blocked, the source is missing.

**Answer: not from any source Merit currently reads, and probably not from the calendar at all.** The honest disposition is either a post-hoc correction path, which is [ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s calendar-correction shape and [ADR-047](ADR-047.md)'s watermark, or an admission that `halted` is a field Merit publishes a rule about and has no way to populate. **This entry does not choose between them**, because choosing needs a fact nobody has: whether any feed Merit will hold, vendor or exchange, reports a whole-session halt at all. `R-04` is published in the constitution's B4 answer, so it cannot simply be dropped.

### 3. Question three: does `CalendarDay` gain SESSION BOUNDS? (`R-05`, `GS-030`)

**The type as it stands.** [`types.ts:217`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) declares `CalendarDay` as exactly four fields: `tradingDay`, `isHalfDay`, `halted` at line 222, `sequence` at line 224.

**A test already exists whose entire job is to fail if this question is answered yes.** [`rules-a-calendar.test.ts:271`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-a-calendar.test.ts) builds `const CALENDAR_DAY_FIELDS: Record<keyof CalendarDay, true>` with the four names, asserts a real sample's keys match it at line 279, and asserts `toHaveLength(4)` at line 280. Its own comment at line 269 states why: *"a `session_open_at` added to `CalendarDay` is the exact change that would make R-05 an engine rule, and a runtime key check over a fixture would not see it arrive."* **So this question has a standing guard against it, written deliberately, and any yes answer must overrule that guard rather than trip it.**

**What it would cost.** The `CalendarDay` blast radius is section 2's 36 files. **The migration cost is zero, and this correction matters because the assumption runs the other way.** [`0004_catalog.sql:318`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) already declares `session_open_at timestamptz NOT NULL` and line 319 `session_close_at timestamptz NOT NULL`, and `0032` later made them nullable under a rewritten CHECK. **The columns exist. `R-05` needs no new migration, only a projection change**, and the projection is one `.map` in the golden loader plus one row shape in `generate.mjs`.

**Where would the value come from? The columns exist and the values do not.** This is the second finding that outranks a design.

- [`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json) carries `"status": "awaiting-transcription"`, `"holidays": null` and `"early_closes": null`. Its `_status_note` states the file is *"NOT LOADABLE AND NOT GENERATABLE"* and that `generate.mjs` refuses it on `source-not-transcribed` and on `exception-list-not-transcribed`. **The production calendar cannot be generated at all today.**
- `session_rule` is `{timezone: "America/Chicago", open_ct: "17:00", open_day_offset: -1, close_ct: "16:00"}`. **That is a RULE, not a set of per-session instants**, and [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) established on artifact evidence that the rule computes the wrong open for an absorbed session: trade date `2026-09-08` opened Sunday `2026-09-06`, where `open_day_offset: -1` computes Monday.
- The engine's own fixture calendar, [`fixtures/calendars/cme-2026.json`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/calendars/cme-2026.json), carries `"status": "partial"` and states in its own note that of 83 sessions, five are transcribed and **78 are synthetic**, so *"a reader who takes this calendar as a statement about the CME year is wrong about 94 percent of it."* Its session records carry two keys, `kind` and `trading_day`, and no instant of any sort.

**So a yes to question three would put two `timestamptz` columns on the engine's calendar row and have nothing true to fill them with for 78 of 83 days.** [ADR-058](ADR-058.md) makes it worse rather than better: the source publishes forward-only, so the transcription that would supply real bounds is not one session's work and never completes.

**Purity check, and this is the one place where a yes is genuinely dangerous.** [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) line 425 states `R-05` as *"Session bounds are stored UTC instants derived from CT session definitions"* with the arithmetic column reading *"DST is data. No arithmetic anywhere converts a timezone."* **Two `string` instants on `CalendarDay` do not breach `INV-01` or `RI-07` by themselves.** What breaches them is the next step: the moment the engine holds an open and a close, the containment comparison `R-01` wants becomes writable inside the engine, and the first author who needs a trading day from an instant has the ingredients for the timezone conversion `R-05` exists to forbid. **`RI-07` would not catch it**, because a string comparison reaches no Node builtin. **`merit/engine-purity` would not catch it**, because no import is involved. **The four-field assertion at line 280 is the only mechanism in the tree that sees this change arrive**, which is exactly what its author said it was for.

**Answer: no, and it is already recorded as no.** `R-05` is discharged by `0032`'s constraints over the two columns that already carry the instants. The projection to `CalendarDay` deliberately drops them.

### 5. The decision nobody has framed, which is what the five rows are

**Three questions with two settled answers and one unanswerable one leave five golden-scenario rows that this directory can never write.** They are not slow. They are not waiting. Under the answers above they are **permanently outside the golden fixture suite**, and the corpus has no way to say that about a row.

| Row | Rule | Why no fixture is possible |
|---|---|---|
| `GS-001` | `R-01` | The engine does not compute `R-01`. Ingest does |
| `GS-030` | `R-01`, `R-05` | Both discharged outside the engine |
| `GS-004` | `R-04` | The rule is implemented and no input can set the flag |
| `GS-031` | `R-04` | Same |
| `GS-035` | `R-06` | Needs a clock. `INV-01` forbids it. Group B, and it always was |

**Three costs follow, and each is already visible in the tree.**

**First, the denominator lies.** [`fixtures/README.md`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md) reports coverage as a share of M1's partition of 73 and says *"The remaining 35 are each on a named row below."* Five of those thirty-five are not remaining work. **A coverage figure that can never reach 100 percent, with no statement of what its true ceiling is, is a figure that will be read as a schedule.**

**Second, a fixture written for one of them would be asserted to FAIL rather than refused.** [`fixtures/README.md`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md) line 267 records the mechanism and how close it came: *"the four undeclared rules ... are cited by no fixture here, so every citation resolves ... a fixture citing `R-01` for the fill-containment flavour of the same day would have derived `inverted` and been asserted to FAIL."* **[ADR-048](ADR-048.md)'s per-fixture polarity is a rule about rules the engine has not implemented YET.** It has no vocabulary for a rule the engine will never implement, so it would classify a correctly-written `GS-001` as an expected failure and go green on it. That is a fixture asserting nothing while looking like coverage, which is `L-06`'s objection.

**Third, and this is why it keeps recurring: each batch re-derives it.** Batch 9's table, session 58's table, session 59's table and section 1 to 3 of this entry are four independent derivations of one fact, each costing a session's reading, each agreeing. **The fact is stable. The record of it is not, because it lives in batch notes rather than in the specification.**

> **RECOMMENDATION 1. [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) section 3.5's rule table gains a column stating, per rule, WHICH LAYER COMPUTES IT.** Four rows read something other than the engine, and the four are `R-01`, `R-05`, `R-11` and `R-20`, exactly as [`rule-coverage.ts:120`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rule-coverage.ts) already holds them. **The source is the test file and the direction is one way**: the plan document is made to agree with the assertion that already runs, not the reverse. This is the whole of questions one and three.

> **RECOMMENDATION 2. The golden-scenario registry gains a disposition for a row no suite can execute**, and `GS-001`, `GS-030` and `GS-035` take it. **It is not "blocked" and it is not "deferred".** The M1 partition denominator drops from 73 to the rows that are actually owed, and the excluded rows are listed with the layer that discharges each, so the exclusion is auditable rather than a smaller number.

> **RECOMMENDATION 3. `GS-004` and `GS-031` are NOT excluded, and are held on a named open question instead.** `R-04` is implemented, published in the constitution's B4 answer, and blocked only on a source. **The open question is: does any feed Merit will hold report a whole-session halt or limit lock?** Until it is answered, the two rows are blocked on a retrieval, which is a different state from the three above and must not be collapsed into it.

> **RECOMMENDATION 4. `generate.mjs`'s two constant `halted: false` writes gain a comment naming this entry**, so the next reader of the production seed generator finds the reason at the line rather than in a batch note. **No behaviour changes.** [`0004_catalog.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) is not amended: a merged migration is never edited, only superseded ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md)), and a column whose default is correct does not justify one.

**No `CI-06` letter is claimed here, and the gate that would fit is named rather than written.** A check that every rule in [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)'s table appears in exactly one of `IMPLEMENTED_RULES` or `DISCHARGED_ELSEWHERE`, and that the layer column agrees with which, is checkable in one comparison and is the shape [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) prefers over care. **[ADR-034](ADR-034.md) and [ADR-036](ADR-036.md) both rule that a letter is claimed in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) before the artifact exists, and this entry is not that artifact.** The letter after `r` is free and the session that writes the gate takes it.

### 6. The argument AGAINST the recommendation

**Stated at full strength, because an alternatives section that is a formality is the shape [ADR-014](ADR-014.md) was overruled from.**

**The strongest objection is that RECOMMENDATION 2 makes a coverage number go up by deciding some rows do not count, and that is the oldest bad move in test reporting.** Every excluded row is a scenario somebody wrote down because they wanted it verified. `GS-001` is fill containment: it is `R-01`, and `R-01` is the rule that catches a fill landing in no session at all, which [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) established is a **real defect on real CME data**, not a hypothetical. Marking it "not this suite's" and watching the denominator fall is indistinguishable, from the outside, from marking it done. **The corpus has a standing warning about exactly this: [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)'s `FM-17`, that a control which is noisy gets muted, and [ADR-058](ADR-058.md) applied it to an alarm four days ago.** An excluded row is a permanently muted one.

**The answer this entry gives is that the row moves rather than disappearing, and the answer is not fully satisfying.** Nothing in the recommendation says where `GS-001` gets verified instead. Ingest owns `R-01`; M02 is held at `review` by [ADR-005](ADR-005.md) pending the vendor call; there is no M02 fixture suite to move the row into. **So RECOMMENDATION 2, taken alone, moves five rows out of a suite that exists and into a suite that does not.** A founder could reasonably rule that no row leaves group A until the receiving suite is named, and that ruling would be defensible on the same `FM-17` grounds this entry cites.

**The second objection is to RECOMMENDATION 1, and it is a scope objection.** [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) is frozen. Adding a column to its rule table touches fifty rows of the money path's specification to record something two test files already assert and one gate could check mechanically. **The cheaper move is the gate alone**: write the check, let it fail if the two ever disagree, and leave the frozen document alone. **The counter-argument is that a gate comparing a plan document to a test file needs the plan document to state the fact before it can compare anything**, so the column is the gate's prerequisite rather than an alternative to it. That counter-argument is sound and it does not make the edit small.

**The third objection is that question two is the only genuinely open one and this entry does not answer it.** It says a halt is not a schedule fact and stops. **A founder who wanted a decision rather than a framing would be right to say the framing is the easy half.** The defence is that the deciding fact, whether any feed Merit will hold reports a session halt, is not in the tree and could not be derived from it, and that guessing it is how the reconciliation session's three worst errors happened. **But "I could not find it" and "it does not exist" are different claims, and only the first is proven here.**

- **Alternatives considered.**

  **Widen `DailyMark` and `CalendarDay` and unblock all five rows** (rejected in sections 1 and 3: the values do not exist for either, so the fixtures would be written against invented instants, which is `TR-01`'s prohibition by name. And section 3's purity risk is real: the four-field assertion at [`rules-a-calendar.test.ts:280`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-a-calendar.test.ts) is the only mechanism in the tree that would see the change arrive).

  **Answer question two by putting a `halts` list in the calendar source schema now** (rejected: it is a slot with nothing to put in it. `holidays` and `early_closes` are already `null` and the file already refuses to load. Adding a third unpopulated exception list to a source that is `awaiting-transcription` adds a refusal reason and no capability, and it would assert that halts ARE a schedule fact, which section 2 argues they are not).

  **Wait for the calendar transcription** (rejected on four independent derivations, three of them not this session's: [`fixtures/README.md`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md)'s batch 9 table, session 58's, session 59's and section 1 to 3 above. The transcription moved `GS-003` and `GS-032` and moved none of the five. **This is the alternative that has actually been taken, four times, and it is what "blocked since batch 9" means**).

  **Write the gate from section 5 and no ADR** (rejected: the gate compares [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) to `rule-coverage.ts` and M01 does not carry the fact yet, so there is nothing to compare. Amending a frozen document needs an ADR rather than a commit, which is the constitution's own rule and the reason this file exists).

  **Rule `R-04` unimplementable and remove it from the declared set** (rejected: it is published in the constitution's B4 answer and restated in [`GLOSSARY.md:32`](../GLOSSARY.md), [EC-013](../edge-cases/EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md) and [M14](../plans/M14-loyalty-retention.md)'s `GS-185`. Merit has told traders what happens on a halted session. Undeclaring the rule because the input is missing would make the published promise unenforceable, which is a larger decision than this entry's and one nobody has asked for).

  **Fold `GS-004` and `GS-031` into the same exclusion as the other three** (rejected in RECOMMENDATION 3: their blocker is a retrieval and the other three's is a layer boundary. Session 55 recorded what happens when rows with different blockers are filed together: *"GS-004 AND GS-031 WERE FILED BESIDE GS-003 AS IF ONE THING BLOCKED ALL FOUR"*, and the transcription then moved two of the four. Collapsing them again would repeat the exact error this entry is documenting).
