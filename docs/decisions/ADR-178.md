## ADR-178: the flag queue sorts by corroboration first, the contract's severity-then-age is kept as the order within a band, and the depth goes on the wire because the assertion cannot check a key it cannot see (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**Two `approved` documents ordered one queue differently and the collision was not a reading error.** [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 8 said *"Sorted by severity then age"* and [`routes/admin-reads.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-reads.ts)'s `assertFlagOrder` enforced that FLAT across the page; [M07](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md)'s `AS-M7-03` clause 3 says the queue sorts by the number of INDEPENDENT detector families implicated on an identity. A corroborated page inverts severity BY CONSTRUCTION, so the route refused the adapter [`P7-i`](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) built on every page where the control did any work.

**THIS ENTRY RULES THAT BOTH SENTENCES ARE KEPT AND ORDERED, AND THE ORDERING HAS A PRICE THAT IS PAID ON THE WIRE.** `AS-M7-03` clause 3 stands UNAMENDED and takes the first key. API_CONTRACT section 8 is AMENDED IN PLACE: its "severity then age" is the order WITHIN one corroboration band, which is verbatim what the adapter's second and third keys already do. `assertFlagOrder` is RETARGETED and not deleted: it goes from two keys to three and now enforces the control it used to refuse. **`FlagListItem` gains `corroboration_depth`, and that field is the price of the ruling rather than an addition to it: without it the assertion cannot check the key it is asserting, and the only honest move left at the boundary is to delete the assertion, which is weakening a gate to pass it.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `178`, session **325**, dispatched as `claude/flag-queue-sort`. Found by `P7-i` ([#425](https://github.com/lam2-coder/meritfutures/pull/425)) and reported rather than resolved, because `admin-reads.ts` is `P7-b`'s file and that session held no number.
- **NOT A MONEY PATH.** No migration number is allocated to this session and none is needed; section 5 says why the key is not a column. `SystemReason` gains no member, `SqlExecutorReason` gains no member, no `pg` import, no cast past a key type, `packages/db` untouched.
- **No port is wired.** `apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts` is untouched and its `{declared, wired, blocked}` triple stays `{21, 6, 15}`.
- **Both sentences were read at their own sources on this branch** rather than taken from the dispatch or from the allocation row, and one claim carried by the record came back different. Section 1 finding 7.

### 2. THE RULING

> **Clause 1. `AS-M7-03` CLAUSE 3 GOVERNS THE FIRST KEY AND IS NOT AMENDED.**
>
> The queue sorts by the number of independent detector families implicated on an identity, descending, before any other key. **The ground is that this sentence is a CONTROL and the other is a CONVENTION**, and the difference is not a preference: `AS-M7-03` names an attack, an adversary who needs no access to Merit to run it, a failure mode (*"the operator learns that D-01 means nothing, and the actual ring runs underneath it"*), and a golden scenario that pins it. API_CONTRACT section 8's sentence states an order and no ground at all. **When a convention and a control disagree, the convention is the one that can move without anything breaking that was not already broken.**

> **Clause 2. API_CONTRACT SECTION 8 IS AMENDED IN PLACE, AND ITS SENTENCE IS SCOPED RATHER THAN DELETED.**
>
> [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3's form, which `CI-06f` enforces. The section now reads *"Sorted by corroboration depth, then severity, then age"*, and states in the same place that the old sentence is KEPT as the order **within one corroboration band**. **Nothing the contract promised stops being true; it acquires a scope it never had.** Finding 7's correction is written into the amendment: the band is a DEPTH and not an identity, because two identities at the same depth interleave. The directions the section never stated are stated: most corroborated first, then most severe first, then oldest first.

> **Clause 3. `FlagListItem` GAINS `corroboration_depth`, AND THIS CLAUSE IS THE PRICE OF CLAUSE 1 RATHER THAN AN ADDITION TO IT.**
>
> **Two things break without it and they break quietly.** `assertFlagOrder` cannot enforce a key that is not on the values it is handed, so the only honest options at the boundary are to delete the assertion or to leave it enforcing the second key as though it were the first. Deleting it is weakening a gate to pass it. And an operator is shown a severity 3 above a severity 5 **with nothing on the row that explains it**, which is `AS-M7-03`'s own concern arriving from the other side: a queue an operator cannot read is a queue an operator stops reading, and attention is the resource that attack spends.
>
> **SO THE ASSERTION GOT STRONGER RATHER THAN WEAKER, AND THAT IS THE TEST OF THIS RULING.** It went from two keys to three, it refuses a page that ranks one loud detector above three agreeing ones, and it still refuses every page it refused before inside a band. A ruling that made the boundary check less is a ruling that lost.

> **Clause 4. `assertFlagOrder` IS RETARGETED, EXPORTED, AND ASSERTED ON THE PAGE RATHER THAN ON ITS OWN CHARACTERS.**
>
> Before this entry, `P7-i`'s suite pinned the refusal by reading the SOURCE TEXT of `admin-reads.ts` for `'if (previous.severity < current.severity)'`, because a module-private function gave it no other reach. **A string match is a test of a spelling.** The function is exported, and the suite now hands it the `GS-120` page and asserts it is accepted, and hands it two bad pages and asserts each is refused for its own key.

> **Clause 5. `P7-i`'s SUITE ASSERTED THE INVERSION AS A FACT AND THAT ASSERTION IS INVERTED HERE, DELIBERATELY, AND NOT DELETED.**
>
> The fact was true and this entry makes it false, which is what an ADR moving an approved document does. The case is not removed: it keeps `GS-120`'s fixture, keeps the observation that the page still inverts severity (which is WHY the ruling was needed), and adds that the route now accepts it. **A deleted case would have left the ruling unpinned at exactly the point it is load bearing**, and the file's own header now records what section 8 used to assert and why it changed.

> **Clause 6. NO COLUMN, NO INDEX AND NO MIGRATION, AND THE PERFORMANCE QUESTION IS REPORTED RATHER THAN REACHED FOR.**
>
> Finding 10. `risk_flags_queue_idx` now serves the SECOND key; it is neither dropped nor superseded and `0008` is byte for byte unchanged. The depth is computed in memory over rows the adapter already reads, and `FlagQueueCost` returns what that cost. **`corroboration_depth` is therefore NOT filterable and NOT a cursor component a client may compose**, and the contract says so at the field. Whether a queue at real volume needs a materialised depth is a question with no answer until there is a corpus, and inventing an aggregate for it here would be exactly what `ADR-157` section 5 refused.

### 3. The shapes refused, and what each one costs

**The dispatch named three shapes and mandated none. All three were taken seriously and two of them are genuinely available.**

| Shape | Refused because | What it costs to take it anyway |
|---|---|---|
| **API_CONTRACT wins outright; `AS-M7-03` clause 3 is amended** | It retires a control to preserve a convention, and the control answers an attack that needs no access to Merit | `GS-120` fails and is deleted or weakened. `M07` section 3.2's counter loses its third part. **The adversary picks the top of the operator's queue by firing one detector at strangers**, and every flag they use is technically correct, so nothing in the system reads as wrong |
| **Two orders for two audiences: a severity queue and a corroboration queue** | **This is the strongest of the three and it is why this row is the longest.** It costs nobody anything on paper: both documents stand, both audiences are served, no sentence moves | It restores the attack IN FULL on the surface that carries it. `AS-M7-03`'s failure is *"the operator learns that D-01 means nothing"*, and an operator who has a severity-ordered queue on the same screen works the poisoned one whenever it looks busier. **A control an operator can switch off by clicking a tab is not a control.** It also costs two cursors over one ordering, and `decodeCursor` (`flags.ts:514`) already refuses a cursor from a different ordering by its component count, so the second queue needs its own cursor vocabulary and its own way of being handed the wrong one |
| **The contract sentence is narrowed to a PAGE-LOCAL guarantee** | A page-local guarantee is not a guarantee | `assertFlagOrder` runs per page, so a queue severity-ordered within every page and not across pages passes every check and is unreadable as triage. **It would produce exactly the artefact the old assertion existed to prevent, while continuing to pass the assertion** |
| **Keep the flat assertion and have the adapter re-sort into it** | It is clause 1 lost by another route | The adapter would compute corroboration, order by it, and then throw the order away at the boundary. The control would exist in a file and in no page |

**The shape taken is the second one done honestly:** `AS-M7-03` wins the key, the contract is amended rather than contradicted, and the field that makes the new order checkable is added in the same change. **Clause 3 is what separates this from simply ruling for one document and deleting the other one's enforcement.**

### 6. The costs this ruling carries, stated rather than discovered

1. **`corroboration_depth` is computed and no column holds it.** A client cannot filter by it, cannot sort by it locally and get the server's answer, and cannot compose a cursor from it. The contract says so at the field.
2. **The depth can move between two page requests.** `flags.ts`'s cursor header already ruled that direction: a detector firing between pages can make a flag REPEAT on a later page, which is chosen over the orderings that can DROP one. **A dropped flag in a risk queue is the case nobody worked.** This entry inherits that and does not re-open it.
3. **`risk_flags_queue_idx` no longer serves the first key.** No index serves it. Nothing is dropped and nothing is superseded, and clause 6 says why no aggregate was reached for.
4. **A client that hard-coded severity-descending will render this queue wrong.** Finding 8 measures that no such client exists in this tree, and finding 9 measures that the route cannot serve one. **That is the cheapest moment this ruling will ever be available, and it will get more expensive every week.**

### 7. Approval

**Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.**

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

**THE CENTRAL QUESTION HERE IS A PRODUCT DECISION AND NOT A DERIVATION, AND THIS ENTRY SAYS SO PLAINLY.** Section 1's findings are `grep`s and `sed`s and need no judgement. **Which of two approved documents is the contract is not derivable from either of them**, because each is `approved`, each is internally coherent, and the disagreement is about what the flag queue is FOR. This entry ruled it on the ground that a control outranks a convention, which is an argument and not a proof.

1. **Is the admin flag queue a TRIAGE queue or a CASE queue?** This entry ruled it a case queue: corroboration first, so three families agreeing outranks one firing loudly. **The founder is the operator of this queue.** A founder who opens it every morning to work the worst thing first is describing a triage queue, and for that reader `AS-M7-03` clause 3 belongs in a digest or a second view rather than in the primary sort. **No file can answer that. It is a description of how the founder intends to work.**
2. **Should `FlagListItem` grow a field that no column holds?** Clause 3 says yes and section 6 item 1 is the price: a computed field on a wire type is one a client will eventually try to filter, sort or page by, and each of those is a support conversation. **The narrower alternative is to rule for `AS-M7-03` and let `assertFlagOrder` be deleted**, which costs the boundary check and the operator's explanation, and this entry judged that worse. A founder who disagrees is choosing a smaller contract over a checkable order.
3. **Is a second, severity-ordered view owed to the operator later?** Section 3 refuses it HERE, on the ground that a control an operator can switch away from is not a control. **That refusal is strongest while the queue has one reader and weakest the day it has a team**, and the day it has a team is a decision about the firm rather than about this file.
4. **Finding 7 corrects a sentence in `STATE` and in session 318's log**, which is the record correcting itself. The correction is stated in both places rather than quietly overwritten, and whether a superseded reconciliation should be struck through or left standing beside the correction is a convention this corpus has not settled.
