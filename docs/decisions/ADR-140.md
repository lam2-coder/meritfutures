## ADR-140: the identity-status term of `G-ELIGIBLE` is a named refusal and never a gate result, and the contradiction this session was dispatched to rule was CLOSED six days earlier  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**The dispatch's premise was that after [ADR-062](ADR-062.md) the payout gate reads `identities.status = 'active'` while [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s `INV-M20-06` blocks on `= 'restricted'`, so a `closed` identity cannot request a payout and can still spend its wallet. IT WAS TRUE AND IT IS NOT TRUE NOW.** [ADR-075](ADR-075.md) amended `INV-M20-06` and `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED` to `= 'active'` on 2026-08-21 and closed `OQ-062-01` by name. **This entry does not re-rule a closed question.** It rules the half nobody has: `G-ELIGIBLE` names a term that **nothing in this tree can evaluate and nothing in the contract can report**, and the route that pays a trader cannot be written without deciding where that refusal lives.

- **Origin:** session **252**, `claude/routes-payouts`. The number was **RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH and REQUIRED** ([ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row 140). The reservation's stated reason is stale by six days; the requirement is not, and section 2 is why.

- **MONEY PATH. `E2` APPLIES.** This is the route that pays a trader. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is claimed on every line this lands and is the founder's.

### 2. What IS open, and it is why `ADR-140` is still REQUIRED

`G-ELIGIBLE` conjoins twelve terms. **Two of them can be evaluated by no function in this repository, and the corpus has already ruled the shape for one of the two.**

| Term | A field on `ExternalGates`? | A cell in `EligibilityResponse.gates`? | Its refusal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `identities.status = 'active'` | **NO** | **NO** | **Unruled. This entry** |
| `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT` | Yes, `hasPayoutInFlight` | **NO** | Ruled: `PayoutEvaluation.noPayoutInFlight`, and `409 conflict` |

**Both halves of the first row were read at source.** [`packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)'s `ExternalGates` declares five fields: `accountStatus`, `kycState`, `payoutsFrozen`, `reconBlocked`, `hasPayoutInFlight`. [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s `EligibilityResponse.gates` declares ten: `account_active`, `kyc_verified`, `not_frozen`, `recon_clear`, `traded_days`, `win_days`, `buffer`, `consistency`, `cadence_gap`, `minimum_amount`. **Neither set contains the identity status, and `account_active` is the ACCOUNT's `status` and phase, which is a different column on a different table.**

**The second row is the precedent the first one needs.** The engine met this exact shape one term over and answered it in a comment that reads like it was written for this entry: R-38 *"is not in `gates` and that is API_CONTRACT's shape ... it is computed, it binds through `contextEligible`, and it is REPORTED ON ITS OWN FIELD rather than being smuggled into a published shape that does not have a slot for it."*

**The identity status cannot even take that answer**, because there is no evaluator to report beside. So it is placed outside the evaluation entirely.

### 3. THE RULING

> **1. The identity-status term of `G-ELIGIBLE` is enforced as a NAMED REFUSAL, evaluated BEFORE anything about the account is read.**
>
> **2. It is answered `identity_restricted`, status 422**, which is [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 2's existing code.
>
> **3. It is NEVER expressed as a gate result.** No eligibility snapshot is taken, no gate breakdown is returned, no `payout_requests` row is written, and no `payout.blocked` is emitted.
>
> **4. The predicate is `status === 'active'` and NOT an enumeration of what is refused**, so it covers `closed` as well as `restricted` and continues to cover whatever the enum gains next.

**Four supports, in order of weight, each a primary source.**

1. **The contract has nowhere to report it, so the alternative is a wrong answer rather than a different one.** `payout_not_eligible` is defined as *"Gates not satisfied; body carries the full gate breakdown"*, and the breakdown has no cell that could carry this. **A restricted trader would receive a refusal in which every gate passes**, which is a support ticket by construction and, worse, a record. Under clause 3 there is no such record to correct later.
2. **`INV-M5-23` is this exact placement argument, decided for a different refusal, by the founder's own corpus.** [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 3.6 puts the impersonation refusal *"before section 1.1's first step, beside the authentication check rather than inside the evaluation. Nothing about the account is read, because reading it is the act being refused"*, on the ground that the eligibility path *"is built for a trader who did not qualify"* and routing another refusal down it *"would put a false eligibility story on a real account, emit `payout.blocked` about a human who did nothing, and leave both in the record that M06's evidence pack later exports as fact."* **A restricted or closed human is not a trader who did not qualify either.**
3. **The engine cannot be made to evaluate it inside this session's fence.** Adding a term to `ExternalGates` widens a money-path contract that [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), [M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md) and [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) each render, and `INV-23` keeps context out of the replayed state while `SD-06` is the column split enforcing it. **That is an amendment with its own ruling and it is not this one.**
4. **The code already exists and the table it lives in is closed.** [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 2: *"checkout, payouts, wallet spend, external withdrawal and affiliate settlement all refuse with this one code rather than with five near-synonyms."* **Inventing a sixth spelling is a ruling nobody took.**

**Clause 4's form is [ADR-062](ADR-062.md) section 1 inherited rather than re-argued.** `= 'active'` and `<> 'restricted' AND <> 'closed'` are equivalent over today's three-member enum and **stop being equivalent the moment a fourth value lands**, at which point the enumerate-the-refusals form fails OPEN on an outbound door. That was ADR-062's closest runner-up and its stated reason for rejection.

### 4. The argument AGAINST, and the one place this entry is genuinely uncomfortable

**`identity_restricted`'s own name says *restricted*, and a `closed` identity is not restricted.** Clause 2 answers one code for two facts whose remedies differ: a restriction is reversible by a documented restore ([ADR-041](ADR-041.md)), and closure, whatever it turns out to be, is not. A client rendering from the code alone shows a closed trader a remedy that does not exist for them.

**Three answers were available and the other two are worse.**

- **A new code, `identity_closed`.** It is the honest shape and it requires amending [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), which is a frozen document outside this fence and whose code table is closed by design. **A route inventing a code the contract does not define is the precise defect section 2 records M03 committing**: *"the plan refused with a code this contract did not define, which is the shape where an implementer either invents a second spelling or reuses the nearest existing code."*
- **`403 forbidden`, which is [`checkout.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)'s incumbent shape for the same predicate.** Rejected, and the reason is checkable rather than aesthetic: on this surface a `403` is what an unelevated session receives and it arrives carrying `required_factor` so the client can offer the ceremony ([ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 4). **A client meeting a 403 here would offer a passkey prompt to a human whose problem no factor solves.** Section 2's sentence also puts payouts on `identity_restricted` in terms, so **`checkout.ts` is the file that diverges from the contract and this route is not.** That divergence is REPORTED in section 6 and is not repaired here.

**So the imprecision is accepted, the `detail` string carries the fact the code cannot, and `OQ-140-01` records what is owed.**

> **`OQ-140-01`. OPEN.** Should [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 2 gain a code distinguishing a CLOSED identity from a RESTRICTED one, given that the two remedies differ and one of them does not exist? **It is a contract question and not a route question**, it is owned by whoever next holds that document, and it is numbered here because a divergence that falls between two sessions is a divergence neither one closes.

**The second objection is that `closed` may be unreachable and this entry is enforcing against a ghost.** [ADR-062](ADR-062.md) section 7 raised it and answered it, and the answer has not moved: `G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` **is named by the identity machine and defined nowhere**, so nobody can prove a `closed` identity holds no funded account. **A gate defended by an undefined guard is defended by nothing.**

### 6. Consequences

- **[`apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) exists**, and `gateIdentityStatus` is clause 1 through 4 in five lines. **It is the first code to gate an EXTRACTION door on the identity status, and it is NOT the first code to gate any door on it.** [`checkout.ts:894`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) reads `cap.identityStatus !== 'active'` on the PURCHASE door and has since it landed. **[Session 162](../sessions/2026-08-24-session-162.md)'s measurement of zero hits across `packages/` and `apps/` is 2026-08-24 and is superseded by that file**; a first draft of this bullet cited it as though it still held, which is the class of error [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) names, caught by reading the primary source rather than the citation.
- **`POST /accounts/:accountId/payout` posts `LT-01` through [`packages/ledger`](../../packages/ledger/src/index.ts)**, and the route holds no ledger arithmetic. **The table's three entries are written as two transfers and therefore FOUR entries**, because [ADR-104](ADR-104.md) ruling 1 makes a one-debit two-credit posting unrepresentable by the same construction that makes the imbalance unrepresentable. The two debits are both against `trader_withdrawable`, in the same direction, which `posting()` admits by name, and they sum to `approved_cents`. **`INV-M5-03` is asserted at that seam** rather than assumed, because nothing runs between the engine returning a split and the route turning it into two legs.
- **A live deployment answers `503` on both routes**, and the reason is a wall this entry names and does not move: `postTransaction` takes a `LedgerTx`, only [ADR-102](ADR-102.md)'s `SystemTx` satisfies it, and `SystemReason` is `'nightly-batch' | 'operator-console'`. [`packages/ledger/src/tx.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/tx.ts) states it: *"A CHECKOUT POSTING FROM `apps/api` IS NEITHER OF THOSE WORDS ... On the WRITE side the gap is open."* **`SystemReason` IS NOT WIDENED HERE.**
- **`INV-M5-23`'s impersonation refusal is NOT implemented and nothing asserts it.** `AuthSession` carries six fields and none is a session type, so there is nothing on this surface to refuse on. M05 section 3.6 writes the corollary out for exactly this case: *"this refusal disappears silently and no assertion in this module fails, because nothing here asserts it."* **Reported.**
- **No `payout.requested`, `payout.approved` or `payout.held` is emitted.** Nothing in `apps/api/src` writes an event and a route inventing a sink would be deciding where the catalogue lives. **Reported.**
- **[`checkout.ts:894`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) carries TWO separate defects on one line and this entry reports only the second of them.** Session **247**'s `OQ-135-03` reports the **predicate**: that line refuses anything not `active` while [M03](../plans/M03-billing-checkout.md)'s `INV-M3-15` refuses only `restricted`, so a code line has answered [ADR-075](ADR-075.md)'s still-open `OQ-075-01` on whether a `closed` identity may purchase. **This entry reports the CODE**: it answers `403 forbidden` where [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 2 puts checkout on `identity_restricted` in terms, and section 4 is why this route does not follow it. **Neither is a duplicate of the other and neither is repaired here**: that file is outside both fences.
- **`ADR-140`'s reservation reason in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) is corrected in place** and the row now says what the entry rules.
- **The `adr_count` spans in [INDEX](../INDEX.md) and [STATE](../STATE.md) move**, generated rather than typed, required by `CI-06g` and outside the stated fence for the reason [ADR-061](ADR-061.md) recorded.

### 7. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.**

> **A `closed` identity is refused a payout with `identity_restricted` and no gate breakdown, and the refusal is decided before the account is read; an `active` identity on the identical request is approved and posts `LT-01`.**

Both halves executed through Fastify's real router. **Eight seeded mutations were watched failing and removed**, among them the one that turns the door into `<> 'restricted'`, which reddens only the `closed` case and is the witness this entry turns on, and the **negative control** that makes the door refuse every identity, which reddens thirteen.

**MONEY PATH.** The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is claimed on every line this lands and is the founder's. **NOT MERGED.**

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Section 4 already names the place this entry is uncomfortable and `OQ-140-01` carries the question forward; what a read decides is whether the discomfort is acceptable in the meantime. **One refusal code answers for two facts whose remedies differ**: a restriction is reversible by a documented restore and closure, whatever it turns out to be, is not, so a client rendering from the code alone offers a closed trader a remedy that does not exist. The entry accepts that rather than invent a code a frozen contract does not define, and both alternatives are priced beside it. **The second judgement is clause 3**, that the term is NEVER expressed as a gate result: no snapshot, no row, no event. It is the right shape if a restricted human is not a trader who did not qualify, and it means an operator investigating a refusal finds nothing in the record at all, by design. **The third is clause 4's predicate form**, inherited from ADR-062 rather than re-argued: `= 'active'` covers whatever the enum gains next, and the enumerate-the-refusals form would fail OPEN on an outbound door the day a fourth value lands.
