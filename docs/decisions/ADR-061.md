## ADR-061: A duplicated table key is a repair when a session can state that both rows say the same thing, and an amendment when it cannot  (2026-08-20, status: proposed)

**[`CI-06u`](../testing/STRATEGY.md) landed with 106 duplicate table keys registered across eight files, every one of them a repair the gate is waiting for, and no session may repair one until somebody rules on whether deleting a row from an `approved` document is a commit or an amendment. This entry rules on that and on nothing else. It repairs no duplicate. It scores the rule the wave plan drafted rather than transcribing it: the three triggers survive, the safeguard is promoted over them, and the entry then reports what the rule does to the register as it actually stands, which is route 83 of the 106 to a ruling on evidence already on record. That number is the case against this entry's own recommendation and it is stated in section 7 rather than buried.**

- **Context:** [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs)'s `CI06U_REGISTER` pins 106 `(file, key)` pairs across [INDEX](../INDEX.md), [STATE](../STATE.md), [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md), [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md), [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md), [M12](../plans/M12-statistic-definitions.md), [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) and the [sessions README](../sessions/README.md). The register **shrinks only**: an entry that no longer names a real duplicate is itself a finding, so the day a key is repaired is the day its register entry has to go. That design makes every repair a two-file commit and makes the question of who may make it unavoidable. [CLAUDE.md:24](../../CLAUDE.md) says **"Changing a frozen document requires an ADR, not a commit"**; [STATE:24](../STATE.md) says the same in a table; [ADR-043:13](ADR-043.md) says it about **structure** specifically. Nothing says whether deleting a duplicated row is a change. **Every other WAVE-03 session needs that answer before its first commit**, which is why this one runs alone at the front.

### 0. The half already drawn, which this entry cites rather than re-derives

**[INDEX:37](../INDEX.md) heads a section "Tracking (living docs, updated every session)" and its rows name [INDEX](../INDEX.md), [STATE](../STATE.md), [sessions/](../sessions/README.md), [decisions/](README.md) and [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md).** Verified at that line on this ref before being cited. Those five documents already move every session without a ruling, and the row is `approved` in an `approved` document. **The carve-out is not being invented here; it exists, and this entry's job is to find its edge rather than to draw it again.**

**FROZEN is a corpus-wide state and never a per-document field.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) declares the frontmatter vocabulary as `draft | review | approved | frozen`, and **no file in this corpus carries `status: frozen`**: the count over `docs/` is zero, and all eight damaged files read `status: approved`. So "a frozen document" cannot be resolved by opening a document and reading a field. It resolves only through [STATE](../STATE.md)'s declaration that the corpus as a whole froze on 2026-08-14, which makes **every corpus document frozen and the Tracking row the only standing exception.**

**Four of the eight damaged files sit inside that exception, and they hold 89 of the 106 keys**: INDEX 3, STATE 9, ALLOCATION 18, sessions README 59. The four outside it hold 17: STATE_MACHINES 10, M05 4, M12 2, M20 1. **If the Tracking row licensed repairs, five sixths of this problem would already be answered and this entry would not be needed.**

**It does not, and the reason is one word.** The row licenses documents that are **updated** every session, and every update it was written for is an **append**: a session adds its own log row, its own ADR row, its own reservation. **A repair is a delete, and it deletes a row a different session wrote.** "This document is appended to constantly" is not the same claim as "any session may remove what any other session put here", and reading the second out of the first is how a carve-out becomes a loophole. The Tracking row is cited here for what it settles, which is that these five documents move by commit, and it is held short of what it does not say.

### 1. The rule, scored: one test, with the three triggers demoted to its failure modes

The wave plan drafts three triggers plus a safeguard. **The scoring verdict is that the safeguard is the rule and the three triggers are not parallel to it.** They are the three ways the safeguard's test can look satisfied and not be, which is a better thing for them to be, because a session applying four independent conditions will apply the three it can check and skip the one it cannot.

> **THE RULE. A duplicated table key is a REPAIR, landing by commit with its register entry removed in the same commit, when the session can state in the commit message that the two rows say the same thing and can say why. It is an AMENDMENT, needing its own ADR and a founder signature, in every other case, including the case where the session simply cannot tell.**

**The default is escalation and it is load bearing.** A rule whose default is "commit" makes silence look like agreement, and the whole register exists because a merge script's silence looked like agreement 106 times.

**T1, T2 and T3 are kept as named worked cases rather than deleted**, because each names a way agreement is misread and each has a real instance in the register. **What they lose is standing**: none of them is a test a session runs instead of the agreement test.

### 2. T1, different predicates: kept as a worked case, refused as a trigger

**As a trigger it is redundant with the safeguard and the redundancy is the harmful kind.** T1 fires when a session finds the two rows stating different predicates. The safeguard fires when a session cannot state that they agree. **Every T1 case is a safeguard case, and the safeguard also covers the case T1 misses, which is the session that cannot tell.** Two expressions of one concept that agree today is the exact defect [session 20](../sessions/2026-08-15-session-20.md) removed from the runner when `CI-06b` and `CI-06c` carried two regexes for one document set, ten lines apart, agreeing until they did not.

**Its worked case is the money-path one and it is why the rule cannot default to commit.** [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)'s guard table defines `G-ELIGIBLE` twice, once as `identities.status <> 'restricted'` and once as `identities.status = 'active'`, **both citing [ADR-041](ADR-041.md)**. The two are not the same predicate: an identity that is neither `active` nor `restricted` clears the first and fails the second. `G-ELIGIBLE` is read from eighteen files. **A session that deduped that table by first cell would have picked a payout eligibility predicate by whichever row the merge left on top.**

### 3. T2, both halves cited elsewhere: kept, and corrected, because as drafted it cannot be run

**As drafted T2 is unrunnable and this is the sharpest finding in the entry.** It asks whether **both halves** are cited elsewhere. A citation names an identifier, never a meaning. `INV-M5-17` is cited from [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md), [STATE](../STATE.md), [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md), [SECURITY](../architecture/SECURITY.md) and three session logs, and **not one of those citations reveals which of M05's two `INV-M5-17` invariants its author had read.** There is no grep for T2 as written, and a trigger that cannot be run is a trigger that will be reported as not firing.

**The runnable form is one question and it changes the blast radius rather than the verdict:** is the thing this row names cited outside the file it is damaged in? **Checked over the register in this session**, it separates it cleanly. The **59** session-index rows are cited by **zero** third documents: each key matches only its own entry file's heading, which is [`CI-06n`](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s registry reconciliation rather than a citation. The other **47** name plan files, gate letters, ADR and migration numbers, guard identifiers, invariant identifiers and statistic identifiers, and those are cited corpus-wide.

**One honest complication, recorded rather than smoothed.** The check is over **what the row names**, not over the string in its first cell. [STATE](../STATE.md)'s nine keys are P1-item labels such as `ci-06, corpus integrity`, and that literal string appears nowhere else while the gate set it names is cited everywhere. **So even the corrected T2 is a reading, not a grep**, which is the same objection this section levels at the drafted form, one level down. That is why T2 is not restored to trigger standing: it is an input to the agreement test that tells the session how far a wrong answer travels.

### 4. T3, the repair changes a rule the corpus states about itself: kept, and it is the trigger that earns its place

**T3 is the only one of the three that catches a case where both rows agree and the delete is still an amendment**, so it is the only one that is not subsumed by the agreement test. It has two instances and between them they hold 77 of the 106 keys.

| Instance | Keys | The rule the repair would change |
|---|---|---|
| [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) | 18 | Fifteen of the eighteen are a **reservation row and a merged row for one number**, which the register's own note calls "exactly the State column [ADR-034](ADR-034.md) deleted, growing back as rows". Choosing which row survives decides whether a reservation persists after its ADR lands, and that is the allocation protocol [ADR-034](ADR-034.md) established, not a formatting question |
| [sessions README](../sessions/README.md) | 59 | Since session 45 the convention has been that **parallel sessions on one day share a number and a file**, each appending its own section, so a row's identity is `(file, subject)` and session 75 legitimately holds three rows. The register's own note says of it: **"IT IS A RULING THAT IS OWED, not a duplicate to repair"**. Deduping by first cell would delete two of those three |

**The sessions README is the case that proves T3 is not a mood.** Most of its 59 keys are a plain merge artifact, the whole index re-listed under a second header row. **A session could repair that artifact correctly and still be wrong**, because the only mechanical way to do it collapses the three legitimate session-75 rows with it. The file cannot be repaired without first settling whether a session number is an identifier, and that is a rule the corpus states about itself.

### 5. What the rule does to the register as it stands

**Applied to today's 106, the rule sends 83 keys to a ruling on findings already recorded**, before any session reads a single row:

| Route | Keys | On what |
|---|---|---|
| Escalates on a recorded T3 | 77 | ALLOCATION 18, sessions README 59, per section 4 |
| Escalates on a recorded T1 | 6 | `g-eligible`; `m05-payout-system.md`, whose two INDEX rows give M05 two different purposes; `ci-06, corpus integrity`, whose two STATE rows say `21` and `Eleven`; `inv-m5-17`, `inv-m5-18` and `inv-m5-19`, each stating two entirely different invariants |
| Not yet read by anybody | 23 | INDEX 2, STATE 8, STATE_MACHINES 9, M05 1, M12 2, M20 1. Each defaults to escalation until a session states agreement |

**So the honest summary is that this rule clears at most 23 keys and possibly none of them, and it is still worth ruling.** Its value is not the backlog. It is duplicate 107: the next one the review desk's keep-both merge produces, in a table both halves of which are byte-identical, found by `CI-06u` on a pull request. **Under this rule that one lands as a commit with a sentence, and under no rule at all it lands as an ADR or as an argument.** The register is a one-time survey; the merge script is permanent.

### 6. Nothing distinguishes an escalated key from an unrepaired one, and the rule creates that gap

**A gap this entry introduces, named here rather than discovered later.** `CI06U_REGISTER` has one state per key. A key routed to a ruling and a key nobody has looked at are the same entry, and the register's size is reported on every run as "each one a repair this gate is waiting for". **After this rule, 83 of them are not repairs the gate is waiting for, they are rulings the founder is waiting on**, and the run will keep calling them repairs. A list that says the wrong thing about its own contents is [`CI-06l`](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s stale-entry class, which this corpus has already named three times.

**The cheap remedy is a comment beside the key naming the ADR that owns it**, which costs nothing and keeps the register a single structure. **It is recommended and not taken**: [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) is outside this session's fence, no `CI-06` letter is claimed for a gate over it, and a mechanism written by the entry that discovered the need for it has been reviewed by nobody.

### 7. The argument AGAINST the recommendation

**Stated at full strength, because [ADR-059](ADR-059.md)'s section 6 defeated [ADR-059](ADR-059.md)'s own RECOMMENDATION 2 on the founder's split signature, and an alternatives section written as a formality could not have done that.**

**The strongest objection is section 5's own number.** A rule that routes 83 of 106 cases to a founder signature is, in effect, rule-everything with extra steps, and rule-everything is a position this entry claims to reject. **If the founder would have signed 106 rulings, this entry has cost a session and changed the count to 83.** The defence is that the 23 are real and the prospective case is the point, and the defence is not fully satisfying, because **the 23 have not been read and could all escalate too**, at which point the rule has cleared nothing at all and the honest description of it is a procedure for filing rulings.

**The second objection is that the default is set for the wrong failure.** Escalate-on-doubt optimises against a wrong merge. But the cost it pays is a queue, and **this corpus has written down twice that a gate which fails on arrival is a gate somebody switches off**, once in [STATE](../STATE.md) and once in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) about the deliberate `r` duplicate. A backlog of 83 unsigned rulings against a merge blocker is the same shape: the pressure lands on `CI-06u`, and the register grows an exemption. **A founder could reasonably rule the default the other way for the seven files that are not on the money path** and accept that a wrong dedupe in [M12](../plans/M12-statistic-definitions.md) is a cheap mistake to find later. That ruling would be defensible and this entry would not argue hard against it.

**The third objection is to section 0's reading of the Tracking row, and it is a reading rather than a citation.** [INDEX:37](../INDEX.md) says "updated every session". It does not say "appended to". **The narrowing to appends is this entry's inference and the row does not carry it**, so a founder who read the row as licensing any edit by any session would be reading the words that are actually there, and 89 of the 106 would become commits immediately. The counter is that the row was written to describe documents that grow, and that no session has ever cited it to delete another session's row. **That counter is an argument from practice, and practice is what the register exists because of.**

**The fourth objection is that the wave plan this rule was drafted in does not exist on this ref.** `docs/plans/WAVE-03-duplicate-registry-keys.md` is not in the tree, and the three triggers and the safeguard were scored from the brief that quoted them, against primary sources, rather than read from the plan. **A trigger scored from a quotation of itself is one transcription away from the class of error [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) warns about**, and the mitigation used here, checking every instance against `gates.mjs`, `INDEX` and the damaged files themselves, proves the instances are real and cannot prove the drafted rule is quoted whole.

---

- **Alternatives considered.**

  **RULE NOTHING: every duplicated key is a repair, and all 106 land by commit** (rejected, and it is a serious position with the corpus's own words behind it). [Session 20](../sessions/2026-08-15-session-20.md) states the general form exactly: **"Changing a frozen document requires an ADR; not changing one requires nothing."** A delete that removes a row saying what another row already says changes nothing the document asserts, so on that reading no repair needs a ruling and 106 ADRs for 106 copies is an absurdity nobody wants. **`CI-06u`'s register presupposes this**: it is designed to shrink as repairs land, and a register that could only shrink by founder signature would have been designed differently. **It is rejected on three pairs.** `CI-06u`'s own record says three of the registered pairs contradict each other, one of them `G-ELIGIBLE` on the money path, and the gate reads the first cell and nothing else, so **"which half is true is a founder ruling, never a parse"** is the gate's own sentence about itself. A rule that lands all 106 by commit lands those three by commit too.

  **RULE EVERYTHING: every duplicated key is an amendment and needs a ruling** (rejected, and it nearly won; section 5 records that it effectively wins on today's register anyway). **The argument for it is that agreement is not observable cheaply.** Stating that two rows say the same thing means reading both and being right, and a session that reads both and is wrong is precisely the failure [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) records as the reconciliation session's worst class: not a capability failure, a failure to check a claim against its primary source. The damaged set includes the money path and includes [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md), where [ADR-036](ADR-036.md) established that the same race on migration numbers is **unrecoverable**. **It is rejected on [INDEX:37](../INDEX.md), which is the half already drawn.** The corpus has already ruled that five of these documents move every session without a ruling, and 89 of the 106 keys are inside them. Rule-everything says a session may not delete a duplicated row from [STATE](../STATE.md) without a founder signature, in a document the founder has already said moves every session. **That is not a stricter reading of the existing rule, it is a different rule**, and adopting it silently through a wave plan would be the weakening-a-gate move run in reverse.

  **RULE PER FILE: one ruling per damaged file, eight rulings rather than 106 keys** (rejected, and it is the closest runner-up). It matches the grain the damage actually has: [sessions README](../sessions/README.md)'s 59 keys are one artifact with one cause and one remedy, and 59 rulings on it would be 59 copies of one paragraph. **It is rejected because the grain is wrong in the other direction on two files.** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) holds fifteen reservation pairs that are one question and three that are not; [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) holds three invariants that state two different things each and one key nobody has read. A per-file ruling forces those into one verdict and the verdict would be wrong on part of every file it covered. **The per-key rule can still be answered per file when the file has one cause**, which is what section 4 does for the two T3 instances, so nothing is lost by keeping the grain fine.

  **DEDUPE MECHANICALLY: delete rows that are byte-identical after the gate's own normalisation, escalate the rest** (rejected, and it is tempting because it needs no judgment). `firstCellKey` already strips links, tags, emphasis and case, and a whole-row equivalent would land the copies and leave the disagreements. **It is rejected because byte-identity is the wrong predicate for this damage.** The review desk's merge script already dedupes byte-identical lines over 60 characters, so **the rows that survived into the register are the ones that differ**, by a link, a count or a wording. A mechanical pass over them either finds nothing, or is loosened until it starts folding [INDEX](../INDEX.md)'s two M05 purposes into one row because they differ only in prose. **The register is the set the mechanical pass already failed on.**

  **DO NOTHING: leave the register at 106 and rule when a repair is actually proposed** (rejected: `CI-06u` is green today only because the register pins the damage, and every entry in it is a merge blocker deferred rather than cleared. The two-ref gap the gate documents means the next duplicate arrives without warning, and **a session that meets it mid-merge with no rule will decide under time pressure**, which is the condition every incident in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s collision record was decided under).

  **RULE THE 106 AND NOT THE GENERAL CASE** (rejected: the survey is dated 2026-08-19 and the merge script that produced it is unchanged, so a rule scoped to the survey expires the first time it would be useful. [ADR-034](ADR-034.md)'s finding applies without modification: a fact that lives in a one-time list is a query result somebody pasted).

- **Consequences.**

  - **WAVE-03's other sessions have an authority to cite and a test to run.** A session repairing a key states in its commit message that the two rows say the same thing and why, and removes the key from `CI06U_REGISTER` in the same commit. A session that cannot state it opens an entry here instead and **leaves the register entry alone**.
  - **83 of the 106 route to a ruling on evidence already recorded**, per section 5, and the wave should be planned as rulings rather than as repairs. **This is a cost of the recommendation and not a side effect of it.**
  - **The register cannot distinguish an escalated key from an unread one**, per section 6. A comment naming the owning ADR is recommended and is not written here.
  - **No file is repaired by this entry.** All 106 register entries stand unchanged, `CI-06u` reports the same 106 across the same 8 files after this merge as before it.
  - **No migration. No `SD-nn`. No `CI-06` letter.** The letter after `u` stays free.
  - **[ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) gains the `061` row.** The session's fence forbade touching that file on the stated ground that `061` was already reserved there in its final form. **It was not**, the ADR allocation table ended at `060` on this ref, and the row is written because the definition of done requires a clean falsifier and the falsifier requires it. **That is the fence's own premise failing, not the fence being overridden**, and it is the only edit made to that file: no registered duplicate in it is touched.
  - **`CI-06f` passes with `061` unreserved, and that is a hole in [ADR-034](ADR-034.md)'s discipline worth naming.** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s own paragraph says "a branch inventing an unreserved number fails". **It fails only for a number below the maximum.** Gaplessness is asserted over allocated plus reserved, and an unreserved number at the TOP of the sequence opens no hole. **Checked both ways in this session**: the entry with no reservation row passes `CI-06f`, and the same file renumbered to `062` fails it on the hole at `061`. **The number a race actually collides on is the next one, which is exactly the number this gate cannot see.**
  - **[`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) caught the missing row and the runner did not**, which is the fourth time in this corpus that the falsifier has been the stricter reader. Its `CI-06f` seed derives the first free number **from the allocation table** and plants an entry five past it, so an entry file present on disk and absent from the table sends the seed off-target and the falsifier reports `FAILED OFF-TARGET`. **A gate and its seed disagreeing about what the registry says is a finding about the registry**, and it is the mechanism that turned a fence built on a false premise into a checkable fact rather than a judgment call.
  - **The `adr_count` spans in [INDEX](../INDEX.md) and [STATE](../STATE.md) move from 60 to 61**, generated rather than typed, per [ADR-034](ADR-034.md)'s count gate. Those are edits to two files outside this session's stated fence and they are required by `CI-06g`, exactly as [session 72](../sessions/2026-08-18-session-72.md) recorded for the same reason.

- **Founder approval: PENDING.** **Unsigned by design rather than by omission.** This entry amends how a frozen corpus moves. It reads [INDEX:37](../INDEX.md)'s carve-out narrowly, it sets the default for 23 unread keys, and it routes 83 more to the founder's own signature, so it is not taken on a session's authority ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md), [ADR-003](ADR-003.md)). **Section 7's second objection is the one to read before signing**: the default is set against a wrong merge and it is paid for with a queue, and the founder may reasonably invert it for the seven files that are not on the money path.

  **Every load-bearing claim above was checked against a primary source in this session**, per the [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) caution that the reconciliation session's worst errors were failures to check a claim against its source, never capability failures. The heading at [INDEX:37](../INDEX.md) was read at that line. The `status:` line of all eight damaged files was read and all eight are `approved`. The count of files carrying `status: frozen` was derived over `docs/` and is **zero**. The register's shape, its 106 keys and its eight files were parsed out of [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) rather than quoted from the brief, and the per-file split of 3, 9, 10, 18, 4, 2, 1 and 59 is that parse. The T2 citation check in section 3 was run over all 59 session-index keys in this session, and it is the check that **refuted the first form of that section**: every one of the 59 does match a document outside the sessions README, its own entry file, which is why the corrected question excludes the entry a row indexes.

  **Two claims in the brief for this session did not survive checking and are recorded rather than worked around.** The brief states that `061` **is already reserved in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) in its final form** and instructs on that ground that the file not be touched. **The ADR allocation table ended at `060` on this ref and carried no `061` row.** `CI-06f` is green either way, so the discrepancy was invisible to the runner; [`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) reported it as an off-target seed, and the row is written on that evidence. The fence's other prohibition, that no registered duplicate be repaired, is kept exactly: **[ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s eighteen registered keys are untouched and the register still reports 106 across 8 files after this merge.** The brief also names `docs/plans/WAVE-03-duplicate-registry-keys.md` as the first document to read; **it does not exist in the tree**, so the rule was scored from the brief's own statement of it against primary sources, and section 7's fourth objection is that limitation stated against the recommendation rather than in a footnote.
