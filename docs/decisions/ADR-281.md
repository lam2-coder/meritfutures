THE CALENDAR HALF IS DISCHARGED AND THE CHOICE IS NOT THE QUESTION.** A third obstruction stands, it has never appeared on this entry, and it is on the field the entry says waits on nothing.

So the entry's sixteenth-revision summary, that `plan` waits on nothing, is **false**, and this is the third time this entry has named the second-cheapest blocker.

### 3. The binding obstruction, derived rather than inherited

**THE FIELD HAD NO DOCBLOCK.** `PayoutSubject` declares three fields. `state` carries four paragraphs, `gates` carries five, and `plan` carried **one line and no explanation** for the whole life of this port. The entry's summary sentence said it waited on nothing, on [ADR-233](ADR-233.md)'s authority, and nothing anywhere checked that.

**WHAT ADR-233 ACTUALLY BOUGHT.** `ScopedTx` gained `catalogRows`, `catalogRowsWhere` and `catalogRowAt` over a closed list of five `firm` keys that includes both plan tables. A catalogue read hands out **rows**. `plan_versions.rules` is a `jsonb` column, so the row hands out a blob.

**WHAT `resolvePlan` REQUIRES.** [`packages/rules-engine/src/plan/resolve.ts:184`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/resolve.ts) declares `resolvePlan(rules: PlanRulesJson, size: PlanVersionSizeRow): ResolvedPlan`. The size row is column-typed and is not the problem. `PlanRulesJson` is a decoded document with six groups of published parameters, and turning the blob into one is a decoding.

**THE CENSUS, OVER THE TREE RATHER THAN OVER A LIST.**

| Asked | Read at |

### 4. The empty table, ruled rather than assumed

The row said both arms are defensible and did not presume. **They are not equally available, and the reason is a fact about this route rather than a preference.**

**THE ARM THAT WOULD WIRE NEEDS A REFUSAL PATH THAT DOES NOT EXIST.** `ruleStateOn` has exactly two outcomes, a `RuleState` and a throw, and `RuleStateAbsent` is correct: the nightly fold has not closed that day, and there is no value to answer with. But `subject()` returns `PayoutSubject | null` where `null` is section 1's **404** for a resource the caller does not own, so an absent row cannot be expressed in the return type. It leaves as a throw. And `unwiredOrThrow`, the route's one error funnel, reads:

```ts
if (!(err instanceof PayoutBackendUnwired)) throw err;
```

So the throw escapes to the transport and the trader is answered **500**.

**A 500 IS NOT AN HONEST ANSWER.** The port's own standing rule, quoted by three other entries in this list, is that a live-looking route in front of an arm that cannot answer is worse than an honest 503. A 500 is that shape with an internal error attached: it tells the trader nothing, it pages an operator about a condition that is not a fault, and it is emitted on the endpoint that approves payouts.

**AND [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) RULING 12 IS THE STANDING RULE THAT DECIDES THIS ARM.** [ADR-226](ADR-226.md) and [ADR-229](ADR-229.md) permit wiring when the last gap is a thing **the deployment sets**; ruling 12 narrows it with the sentence this file quotes in its own header: *"a composition that does not exist is not such a gap."* An unrun nightly job **is** a thing a deployment does. A refusal arm mapping an absent row to a problem document API_CONTRACT permits **is not**; it is code somebody writes in `apps/api`. Both certificate ports expired on a slice somebody had to write rather than on a variable, and this one is no different.

**SO THE EMPTY TABLE IS NOT DEFENSIBLE AS A WIRING TODAY, AND IT IS ALSO NOT WHAT BINDS.** Even with that arm built and the job run, `plan` refuses. The order matters for whoever holds the next row: **the decoding is the larger purchase and the refusal arm is the smaller one**, and neither alone wires this port.

### 8. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**RULING 2 IS THE ONE TO ARGUE WITH.** The row said in terms that a door which exists and is not reached from the refusing path is not a discharge, and **nothing in this tree reaches this door**, because nothing implements `PayoutTx` at all. This entry rules the clause discharged anyway, on the ground that the clause claimed the read could not be MADE and it can. A reviewer may hold that a door with no caller is exactly the thing the row warned about. **The defence is that the alternative makes the clause unfalsifiable**: it would stay live until the port wires, at which point it is discharged by the wiring rather than by the door, and the door's landing would never have been recorded anywhere.

**AND RULING 3 RESTS ON A CENSUS RATHER THAN ON A PROOF OF IMPOSSIBILITY.** What is measured is that no decoder is reachable today. A reviewer could hold that a backend could read the blob positionally, as `sensitiveFields` already does in this deployable, and hand `resolvePlan` a value cast into shape. **That is refused rather than overlooked, and the refusal is `FM-16`**: a positional read of the blob that fixes every cents value a payout is decided against, on the money path, with nothing comparing it to the worker's. It would also not type-check without a cast, and a cast is where the second statement would hide.

**THE 500 IN RULING 5 IS DERIVED FROM ONE LINE AND NOT WATCHED.** No test in this session drove a request through a wired backend to see the status code, because wiring one to observe it is the widening the row forbids. The line is unambiguous and the case asserts it, but the claim is about what `unwiredOrThrow` does with a throw rather than about a response somebody measured.
