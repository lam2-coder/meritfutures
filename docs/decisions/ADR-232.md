## ADR-232: The approval edge exists and is dual controlled above `500000` cents on an operator's hand alone, and it is not what ends a per-trader lockout  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**Two findings were dispatched as one row because they meet at one edge, and a third was found underneath them that moves the answer.** `requested --> approved` and `cooling --> approved` are drawn in [STATE_MACHINES section 3.2](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) and nothing in this tree performed either; the founder's dual-control threshold of `500000` integer cents ([ADR-228](ADR-228.md) section 1) was undischarged on every payout path. **Both are true, both were re-derived at their own files before a line changed, and the edge is now written, guarded and bounded.**

**THE THIRD FINDING IS THAT THE FIRST ONE'S CONSEQUENCE WAS WRONG.** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `232`, the dispatch, and [`wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts)'s `BLOCKED` entry for `useWithdrawalBackend` all read the missing approval edge as the thing standing between a wired backend and a permanent per-trader lockout, and all three ask for a test in which an approved withdrawal *"leaves `OPEN_WITHDRAWAL_STATUSES`"*. **It does not leave it. `approved` is one of the four members of that list** ([`wallet-withdrawals.ts:288-293`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts), which is `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx`'s own predicate at [`0011:205-207`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)), so an approved withdrawal is found by `gateNoInFlight` exactly as a requested one is and the identity is refused its next withdrawal by the same 409. **Building the approval edge does not shorten the blocked entry by a word.**

### 2. The edge, written down

**`decideApproval` is total and pure and `driveApprovals` is the transition over one transaction**, both in [`wallet-withdrawals.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts). The decision takes rows and returns a decision, so every guard term is reachable from a test with no database, and the writing half is one accessor call.

**A hold is not a `Refusal`.** A guard that does not hold is *"not yet"* rather than *"no"*: there is no request to answer, no reply to send and no status code that would be honest about it, and reusing the HTTP refusal would mean this transition could only ever be driven by a request. `ApprovalHold` has eight members and two of them are not "not yet": `not_approvable`, which is a caller pointing the transition at a row the machine does not admit, and `dual_control_required`, which is the control.

**THE TWO GUARDS SHARE EVERY TERM BUT THEIR ORIGIN STATE, AND THE DRAWING DOES NOT SAY SO.** [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)' guard table gives `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED` four terms, *"KYC `verified`, destination outside its cooling window, provenance summary present, and `identities.status = 'active'`"* ([ADR-075](ADR-075.md)), and gives `G-COOLING-ELAPSED` one, *"the window elapsed"*. **Read literally that lets a withdrawal leave `cooling` on the clock alone**, with KYC expired and the identity restricted, because the strict guard is drawn only on the arrow out of `requested`. **The looser reading is REFUSED**: the `cooling` arm carries every term of `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED` and the clock as well. **A guard that gets weaker the longer you wait is a queue an attacker joins.**

**`payouts_frozen` is added and is not drawn.** `gatePayoutsFrozen` is already on the creation path, and a freeze that stops a trader opening a withdrawal while permitting the one they opened yesterday to be approved is a control with a doorway in it.

**The composition is tested for PRESENCE and not re-parsed.** The term is `wallet_withdrawals_approved_has_provenance`'s own condition, `source_provenance_summary <> '{}'::jsonb AND earliest_credit_at IS NOT NULL` ([`0011:192-195`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)). Re-parsing the composition here would be a second reader of a value this module wrote, and the `CHECK` is the thing that has to agree with it.

### 3. Whether a trader's own withdrawal should ever wait on an operator. **IT SHOULD NOT**

**This is the question the row left open and it is ruled here, against the threshold on this arm and in favour of it on the other.**

**[ADR-069](ADR-069.md) section 5 says both things and a reader who takes only one of them gets the wrong answer.** Row 16, payout origination, is *"NOT dual controlled"* on the asymmetry [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) uses for freeze: *"an internal payout request is re-evaluated against every gate before it settles and moves no money out of the firm by itself"*. **Row 17 is the opposite ruling on the neighbouring act**: `POST /admin/identities/:identityId/wallet/withdrawals` *"requires dual control"*, resolved against `dual_control_approvals` by payload hash, because an admin-originated external withdrawal is *"money leaving the firm on an operator's assertion that the trader asked"* ([M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) row 17).

**Row 16's ground splits when it is carried onto this edge, and only half of it reaches.** The half that reaches is re-evaluation: `decideApproval` re-runs every gate the creation path ran, at the moment of approval, against rows read inside the transaction. **The half that does not reach is the important-sounding one**, because a wallet withdrawal is exactly money leaving the firm. **So row 16 is not the precedent and this entry does not lean on it.**

**The ruling rests on three grounds of its own.**

**FIRST, AND IT IS DECISIVE: THERE IS NO FIRST HAND FOR A SECOND HAND TO CHECK.** Dual control is a control on a human decision. Both guards on this edge are predicates a machine evaluates and **neither names a human**: `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED` is four facts about the row and the identity, and `G-COOLING-ELAPSED` is a clock. An approval that satisfied them was not decided by anybody, so requiring a second decider is requiring a second signature on a document nobody signed.

**SECOND, THE FRAUD ROW 17 GUARDS IS NOT THE FRAUD ON THIS ARM.** Row 17 exists because an operator asserts that the trader asked. On the trader arm **the trader's own `C-27`-elevated session is that assertion**: [API_CONTRACT section 6.2](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) requires `passkey or dual_channel` on this endpoint, and the threat it leaves is account takeover, which is answered by the step-up factor, the KYC chain, the 48 hour destination cooling window and `withdrawal_hold_until`. **A second operator adds nothing to any of those.** They cannot see whether the person holding the passkey is the trader.

**THIRD, THE PRODUCT COST IS REAL AND ONE-DIRECTIONAL.** A trader withdrawing $6,000 of an already-settled wallet balance would wait on two Merit operators being awake, on a path where every gate is machine-checkable. **A trader locked out of their own money by a control meant to stop operator fraud is a worse product than the fraud**, and the corpus already has that instinct: [ADR-067](ADR-067.md) section 5 scopes this control to *"compromise resistance, not insider resistance"*, and a compromised **trader** session is not what dual control resists.

> **RULED: the TRADER arm of the approval edge takes no dual control at any amount.** `approved_by` is `NULL` on a machine approval and `0070`'s constraints are written over that column, so the threshold is unreachable on this arm **by design rather than by omission**, and the DDL says so in its own comment.

**AND THE ADMIN ARM IS NOT WEAKENED TO A THRESHOLD.** [ADR-069](ADR-069.md) row 17 requires dual control on admin **origination** at every amount. **Origination is a different act from approval**, that route does not exist in this tree, and when it lands its own constraint is what binds it. This entry does not touch that ruling and must not be read as putting a `500000` floor under it.

### 4. So the threshold binds an operator's hand, and `approved_by` is the column that tells the hands apart

**The founder's question was *"above what payout amount should a second human have to approve it"* and the act it describes is APPROVING.** What `0070` binds is therefore an operator **approving** a withdrawal, at or above `500000` integer cents.

**The comparison is `>=` and not `>`, and that is not a reading of the English.** [`0038:235-238`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql), the only dual-control comparison this estate had before `0070`, is `amount_cents < threshold OR approval IS NOT NULL`, so the threshold amount **itself** requires the second key there. `0070` carries the identical comparison and `dualControlRequired` carries it a third time, **because a route that disagreed with its own `CHECK` would fail at `COMMIT` rather than refuse, which is a 500 where a hold belongs.**

**THE CEILING ARRIVES WITH THE COLUMN, WHICH IS THE WHOLE LESSON OF THE LAST TWO MIGRATIONS.** `0038` shipped `dual_control_threshold_cents` on `2026-08-16` with `> 0` as its only bound; [session 418](../sessions/2026-08-29-session-418.md) drove a **$1,000,000** credit past every `CHECK` on that table by naming a threshold no adjustment could reach; [`0068`](../../packages/db/migrations/0068_dual_control_threshold_ceiling.sql) bounded it on `2026-08-29`. **`0070` declares the column and `wallet_withdrawals_dual_control_threshold_ceiling` in the same statement.** A writer may name a **lower** threshold, which can only require more approvals, and may not name a higher one, which can only require fewer. **Tightening is free and loosening is a migration**, which is deploy access rather than the route access [ADR-067](ADR-067.md) section 5 assumes compromised.

**A ceiling and deliberately not an equality**, for `0068`'s reason and for the reason the column is per row at all: a `CHECK` binds historical rows, so `= 500000` would make every row written under an earlier threshold unrepresentable the first time the number moved.

**THE FOREIGN KEY IS NOT THE CONTROL AND `assert_withdrawal_dual_control_is_real` IS.** A reference proves a row exists and nothing about what it says. The trigger asserts that the approval names **this** withdrawal (`subject_kind`, `subject_id`), that it is in status `approved` rather than `pending`, and that its `requested_by` is the withdrawal's own `approved_by`. **The second-person rule is deliberately NOT restated**: binding the operator of record to `requested_by` makes `dual_control_approvals_second_person` ([`0016:250-252`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql)) the rule that refuses a self-approval, and **one control composed with beats two copies that drift.**

**`payload_hash` is unread and it is named as owed rather than invented.** `SD-M6-05` makes the hash the thing that pins **what** was approved, and a withdrawal row is not a payload: the bytes an operator approved are the admin request body, which the origination route this tree does not have would supply.

### 6. Why no door drives the edge, and why that is a smaller gap than the one it replaces

**The transition is written, guarded, tested and called by nothing.** That is stated here rather than left to be noticed, and the obstruction is checkable rather than argued.

**AN APPROVAL POSTS `LT-06`.** [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 2.1 names `LT-06`'s kind `wallet_withdrawal_approval`, *"The external leg's APPROVAL"*, and `INV-M6-15` states the consequence in the panel: Open Liability *"does not move when a wallet withdrawal is approved"* precisely because the wallet term falls and the obligation rises in the same posting. **A posting commits in the SAME transaction as the state change that caused it** ([`0057`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql)'s header item 3, [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)). **And no door in `apps/api` may open the ledger authority that posting needs**: [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) declined to give `SystemReason` a member for it and [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) clause 2 ruled that the handle is not the missing thing, because the only value satisfying `LedgerTx` is generic over every table in the estate.

**So the choice was between an edge with no driver and a driver that approves without posting.** The second marks money as leaving while the wallet still shows it, and today nothing double-spends it only because `gateNoInFlight` refuses the second withdrawal anyway. **A control whose safety depends on a different control refusing is the half of a mechanism this corpus has already paid for twice**, most recently in `0011`'s own halt, which was *"representable and unenforced for four migrations"*. **The edge is written and the driver is owed to the slice that lands the posting.**

**THE ENTRY SUPPLIES ITS OWN DECISION PROCEDURE AND `RI-20` RUNS IT.** `wiring.test.ts`'s corrected reason states that `grep -rn driveApprovals apps/api/src` returns **2** lines, which are the transition and one docblock reference. **The day a door calls it, that number moves and the reason goes red**, which is the trap this file exists to set rather than a sentence a reader has to trust.

  **THE ONE OBJECTION WORTH CHECKING IN A MINUTE.** The trigger binds the withdrawal's `approved_by` to the approval row's `requested_by`, which means **the operator who raises the dual-control request must be the operator who then performs the approval**. That is deliberate and it is what makes `0016`'s own second-person `CHECK` do the work. **The cost is that a third operator cannot execute an approval two others agreed on**, and if your console wants that shape the binding has to move to a column this table does not have.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **The whole operator arm is unexercised by any route.** No admin withdrawal origination or approval endpoint exists, so `approved_by` is written by nothing today and every case in section 8 that involves an operator was executed by hand against the database rather than through a door. **The constraints are live from the moment a writer exists and not before**, which is exactly what [ADR-228](ADR-228.md) said about `account_adjustments` one migration ago, and it is the same shape arriving on the same day.
