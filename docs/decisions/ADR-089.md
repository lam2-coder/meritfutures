## ADR-089: six Railway services, and `portal-api` was a fused process name rather than an origin label  (2026-08-24, status: proposed)

**[ADR-083](ADR-083.md) made the API its own deployable and left the deployment topology naming four services for a fifth deployable that ships twice.** This entry closes that, and the closure is two rulings rather than one: how many Railway services the architecture requires, and what the compound name `portal-api` was ever naming. **The second is the one that decides anything.** A ruling that renamed the service without deciding whether the name described an ORIGIN or a PROCESS would have changed a string and left the question live. **Non-money: no migration, no `SD-nn`, no engine change, no route, and nothing is provisioned.**

- **Origin:** [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) section 7, which reported both defects against a document outside its fence and said of the second *"Recorded so the INFRA session decides it rather than inherits it."* [Session 144](../sessions/2026-08-23-session-144.md) carried it forward as an owed repair. This is that session.

- **Context:** [INFRA](../architecture/INFRA.md) is `approved` and frozen, so it moves by ADR and not by commit. What moves is section 2's service list, one sentence of section 3's third hard rule, and one clarifying sentence in section 13.2. **Everything else in that document is untouched**, including every hosting choice [ADR-007](ADR-007.md) made, which this entry does not reopen.

### 2. The ruling

> **1. Six Railway services. `site`, `portal`, `api`, `admin`, `api-admin`, `worker`.**
>
> **2. `apps/api` deployed twice is TWO Railway services and not one service with two instances**, because the two deployments differ in a value INFRA scopes per service, sit on two origins, and sit behind two Cloudflare rule sets.
>
> **3. `portal-api` is a STALE NAME. It named one process serving the portal and the API, which is the fusion [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) section 3 refused. It is not, and never was, a label for the shared origin.** It becomes `portal`, and the API half of it becomes the separate service `api`.
>
> **4. [INFRA section 13.2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s origin sentence and [OVERVIEW section 7](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md)'s zone box stay exactly as written**, because those are an origin and an edge zone. **Only [INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s list, which is a list of processes, was wrong.**

---

### 3. Two deployments of one codebase are two SERVICES, and the alternative is refused on a variable rather than on taste

**The tempting economy is one Railway service scaled to two instances**, and it is worth refusing explicitly rather than skipping, because it is cheaper on paper and it is where a reader who has absorbed *"one codebase deployed twice"* naturally lands. Three independent chains close it, and the first is decisive on its own.

**One: the variable.** [`resolveSurface`](../../apps/api/src/surface.ts) reads `MERIT_API_SURFACE`, has no default, and throws on unset. The two deployments differ in exactly that one value. [INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s own why-cell names *"per-service environment variables"* as a property the platform is being bought FOR, and [section 7](../architecture/INFRA.md) repeats it: secrets are *"injected as environment variables at runtime, **scoped per service**"*. **A scope stated per service cannot distinguish two instances inside one service.** So one service running two instances hands both instances the same `MERIT_API_SURFACE`, both resolve the same surface, and there is no operator deployment at all, or no public one.

> **That failure is silent, which is why it is the one to name.** Both instances start cleanly and both serve a coherent route set. If the shared value is `operator`, the public origin serves `/admin/*`. **[API_CONTRACT section 12](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s required 404 for `/internal/*` from the public origin becomes a 200**, and ADR-083's structural argument, that the 404 is the router's rather than a check somebody maintains, is dead without a single error anywhere.

**Two: the origins.** `api` answers on the portal's origin and `api-admin` answers on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`. Instances of one service are one service's ingress, load balanced behind one set of domains; the two API deployments are not interchangeable behind one hostname, because which one answers is the whole ruling.

**Three: the control.** [INFRA section 3](../architecture/INFRA.md) hard rule 3 gives `ADMIN_ORIGIN` *"its own Cloudflare rules and its own IP allowlist (C-08)"*, and section 3 continues *"Cookie scope, CORS, and the CSP never span the two origins."* A single service spanning both origins is a single object holding both rule sets, which is the sentence read backwards.

**What the alternative would have bought is worth stating so the trade is visible: one service, one deploy, one log stream, one bill line.** It is refused because the thing it merges is the thing being kept apart.

---

### 4. `portal-api` named a PROCESS, and this is the half the ruling turns on

The steelman is real and must be answered rather than waved at: **`portal-api` may have been shorthand for the shared origin all along**, in which case [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) is right that nothing moves and the finding is a naming preference. **It is not.** Five readings, taken in the documents themselves:

1. **The table's unit is a service, and its justification is process-shaped.** The row's why-cell buys *"per-service environment variables, private networking between services, no OS to patch."* Environment variables, private network addresses and an OS are properties of a running process. **An origin has none of the three.**
2. **INFRA already has a place for origins and it is not section 2.** Section 13.2 is the domain and origin ruling, and it is where `meritfutures.com` and `app.meritfutures.com` are written. **Section 2's table never writes a hostname**, not once, in any row.
3. **INFRA uses both nouns in one sentence and keeps them apart.** Section 3 hard rule 3: *"deployed as its own service ON `ADMIN_ORIGIN`."* A service is deployed on an origin. They are not the same word in this document.
4. **The origin reading is not even internally consistent across the four names.** If a name in that list were an origin, `admin` would be the admin origin. Under [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) **two** services now run there, `admin` and `api-admin`, so an origin-naming convention had already collapsed inside its own list before this ruling touched it. Under the process reading every one of the four names is exactly one `apps/*` directory. **`portal-api` is the only compound and the only name that is not a directory**, and it is the only one that fuses two containers section 2 of [OVERVIEW](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md) draws separately.
5. **The fusion it names is the one ADR-083 refused, in the same words.** [API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md): the UI surfaces are the API's clients with *"no privileged back door."* ADR-083 section 3: *"A surface that CONTAINS the API has a privileged back door by construction."* **A service name that fuses them is the deployment-topology spelling of the thing that argument forbids.**

> **[ADR-083](ADR-083.md) says both of these and only one of them is right, and the correction is small and exact.** Section 3 reads *"All three remain true under this ruling, because sharing an origin and sharing an edge zone are not sharing a process."* **The stated reason has two limbs and it is offered over three claims.** The origin limb covers INFRA section 13.2. The edge-zone limb covers OVERVIEW section 7. **INFRA section 2 is a list of processes and neither limb reaches it.** Section 7 of the same entry then says so outright: *"a service name that describes one thing and is named for two."* **This entry rules that section 7 is the accurate half, and it amends section 3's blanket sentence to the two documents its own reason covers.** Nothing in ADR-083's argument for a separate deployable depends on that sentence; that argument stands on API_CONTRACT section 1 and on the three arrows, both untouched here.

**So the answer to the question as posed: STALE NAME.** `portal-api` described a process that served both the portal and the API, which is what [ADR-007](ADR-007.md)'s prose *"portal API"* meant on 2026-08-13, when the deployables were four and the API had nowhere else to live. ADR-083 made the deployables five. **The name was accurate for a four-deployable world and is inaccurate for a five-deployable one**, which is exactly what stale means.

### 5. What the rename costs, measured, and why the cost ADR-083 named has not been incurred

[ADR-083](ADR-083.md) section 7 declined the rename with *"renaming it is not free: it is a Railway service identity."* **That is true of a service that exists, and no such service exists.** Measured on this tree: **no `railway.json`, no `railway.toml`, no Railway configuration file of any name anywhere in the repository, and no deploy step in any of the four workflows** in [`.github/workflows/`](../../.github/workflows). The Railway project is unprovisioned. **The cost the entry named is real and is entirely in the future**, and it begins on the day the project is created.

**Today's cost is six strings, one of which is a definition.**

| Site | Kind |
|---|---|
| [`apps/portal/src/index.ts:41`](../../apps/portal/src/index.ts) | **the definition.** ``export const SERVICE = 'portal-api' as const`` |
| [`apps/portal/test/service.test.ts:7`](../../apps/portal/test/service.test.ts) | assertion pinning the definition |
| [`apps/portal/test/surface.test.ts:39`](../../apps/portal/test/surface.test.ts) | assertion pinning the definition |
| [`apps/portal/package.json:6`](../../apps/portal/package.json) | manifest description |
| [`apps/portal/src/index.ts:12`](../../apps/portal/src/index.ts) | comment, citing INFRA section 2 as its source |
| [`apps/api/src/index.ts:12`](../../apps/api/src/index.ts) | comment, quoting INFRA section 2 as a document that reads the other way |

**None of the six is in this session's fence, and that is stated as a cost rather than hidden as a scope note.** Between this entry landing and a session holding `apps/portal`, INFRA says `portal` and the tree says `portal-api`. **That is [ADR-074](ADR-074.md)'s defect, opened deliberately inside the entry that closes an instance of it**, and the honest thing is to name it, bound it and hand it over, which section 7 does. It is bounded because two of the six sites are already correct in substance: both comments cite INFRA as their authority, so both become true again the moment their quotation is refreshed, and [`apps/api/package.json:6`](../../apps/api/package.json) already reads *"Railway services: api (public origin) and api-admin (ADMIN_ORIGIN)"*, so the tree already carries the six-service reading in the deployable that ships twice.

**`api-admin` is kept rather than renamed to `admin-api`, and the reason is the ruling above.** It is pinned by [`surface.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/surface.test.ts) under a signed ADR, so moving it costs a test outside this fence for nothing. It is also the better name on this entry's own argument: `api-admin` sorts beside `api` and reads as **the second deployment of one codebase**, while `admin-api` sorts beside `admin` and reads as **the admin app's API**, which is the fusion this entry exists to undo, reintroduced one origin over.

### 6. What this forecloses

| Foreclosed | What it costs to reopen |
|---|---|
| **One Railway service with two instances serving both API surfaces** | Section 3. Reopening it needs a variable scope below the service, which INFRA does not claim the platform has, and the failure mode if it is wrong is a silent 200 where API_CONTRACT section 12 requires a 404 |
| **A `portal-api` service, under any spelling** | Ruling 3. Reopening it puts the API back in the portal's process and reinstates the back door API_CONTRACT section 1 forbids. **The name is the only thing left of that topology and this is where it ends** |
| **Naming a Railway service after an origin** | Ruling 4 and section 4 reading 4. The convention already contradicts itself on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`, where two services run. **Service names are `apps/*` directory names plus a surface suffix, and nothing else** |
| **Writing the real admin hostname beside `api-admin` when the project is provisioned** | [ADR-012](ADR-012.md), unchanged and reasserted here because deployment topology is precisely where a placeholder gets resolved by helpfulness. `api-admin` names the service that runs there without naming where there is |
| **A seventh service, or a third API surface** | `API_SURFACES` is `['public', 'operator']` and [`surface.ts`](../../apps/api/src/surface.ts) says why in its own words: *"a third surface is a ruling, not a value."* This entry is the topology half of that sentence |

**What stays open, deliberately.** How the portal reaches the API, browser to the shared origin or server to server over Railway's private network, is **not ruled here** and does not need to be: both are clients of the same contract and neither is a back door. It is named so that a later session recognises it as an open choice rather than as something this entry settled by omission.

### 7. Consequences

- **[INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s service list reads six**, and a new **section 2.1** carries the service-to-codebase-to-origin table with `MERIT_API_SURFACE` per row. The hosting choices in that table are untouched.
- **[INFRA section 3](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s third hard rule now names both services on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`.** It read *"The admin app is deployed as its own service on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`"*, and two services run there now. **The control itself survives unchanged and the reason is worth recording: the Cloudflare rules and the IP allowlist are scoped to the ORIGIN, so they covered `api-admin` on the day it was ruled into existence.** What was wrong was the sentence's inventory, not its protection, and the two are different repairs.
- **[INFRA section 13.2](../architecture/INFRA.md) gains one sentence and loses none.** Its origin ruling is correct as written; the sentence added says that *"portal and API"* on one origin is two services and points at section 2.1, because that sentence is the one a future reader would otherwise use to re-fuse them.
- **[ADR-007](ADR-007.md)'s `Decision:` line still reads *"the four services (site, portal API, admin, worker)"* and is superseded IN PART by this entry**, on the count and the enumeration only. **Its hosting ruling, Neon plus Railway plus Cloudflare plus an S3-compatible private bucket, is untouched and is not reopened.** That file is outside this fence, so it is reported here rather than edited, and the amendment it needs is one sentence citing this entry. **Same shape as ADR-083's `RI-04` consequence, one document over.**
- **[ADR-083](ADR-083.md) section 3's *"All three remain true"* is amended to the two documents its own stated reason covers.** Section 7 of that entry is the accurate half and is unamended. That file is outside this fence and the repair is one clause.
- **Six code sites still read `portal-api` and every one is listed in section 5.** The definition is [`apps/portal/src/index.ts:41`](../../apps/portal/src/index.ts) and the other five follow it. **This is the highest-value follow-up this ruling creates**, and it is one session holding `apps/portal` plus a two-word quotation refresh in `apps/api`.
- **`RI-04`'s `DEPLOYABLES` literal is NOT this entry's to fix and is being fixed concurrently.** [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) section 7 named it, and a session holding [`repo-invariants.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs) is adding `api` to it now. **When that lands, `RI-04`'s list is five DEPLOYABLES and this entry's list is six SERVICES, and the two numbers are correct together**: `api-admin` is a second deployment of the fifth deployable and not a sixth directory under `apps/`. Stated because two lists of near-identical names differing by one is exactly the pair a later reader reconciles by making one of them wrong.
- **[OVERVIEW](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md) is not edited.** Section 3's container table rows codebases and rowed `apps/api` under [ADR-083](ADR-083.md); section 7's zones are edge zones. **Both are already right and a change to either would be this entry over-reaching.**
- **No migration, no `SD-nn`, no new `CI-06` letter, no gate change and no `falsify` case.** `0047` stays free, the gate count does not move, and the scope-case count does not move.
