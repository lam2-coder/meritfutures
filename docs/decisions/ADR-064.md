## ADR-064: A session number is an allocation and not an identifier; identity is `(log file, section heading)`, the renumber is declined rather than defaulted into, and a hole is an unspent allocation  (2026-08-20, status: accepted)

**This rules the identity function of [the session registry](../sessions/README.md), which is the one registry in this corpus whose key was never defined.** The ruling has been owed since session 45, when parallel sessions first began sharing a number, and it is stated here as a ruling rather than performed as a merge repair because the two available answers differ by about thirty renumbered sessions and every cross-reference to them.

- **Context:** **Two problems shared one file and they needed different answers.**

  **The merge.** `docs/sessions/README.md` carried the whole session index **twice**, under a re-inserted header row at line 119 with no blank line between the copies, which is the shape [`CI-06u`'s own scope case](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) names as the reason `markdownTables()` reads a maximal run of pipe lines rather than splitting on a delimiter. 236 lines, 213 body rows, 83 distinct keys. **Neither copy was a superset.** Copy 1 alone held sessions 60, 61, 62 and 64; copy 2 alone held 63, 65 to 69, 71 to 82 and 86 to 88. `CI-06n` asserts in both directions that every entry file has a row and every row resolves, so deleting either copy fails it. **The repair was a union** ([ADR-061](ADR-061.md)), and the two copies agree on the subject set of **all 58 keys they share**, so the union lost nothing and had nothing to rule on.

  **The convention.** After the union, **19 keys still head more than one row**, and they are not merge damage. Since session 45 the convention has been that parallel sessions on one day **share a number and a log file**, each appending its own `##` section. Sessions 31, 33, 49, 50 and 56 hold four sections each; twelve more hold three or two. `CI-06u` reads the first cell of a row and calls a repeated first cell a duplicate, so **the convention and the gate are in conflict and one of them must give.**

  **The measurement that decides it.** Across the 83 session files the merge read, there are **121 `##` section headings**, and the union index carries **121 rows over 83 distinct first-cell keys**. The row count and the section count agree file by file in **83 of 83 files** after one repair, described next. **A registry with 121 entries and 83 keys does not have a key.** (This session's own log makes it 84 files, 122 sections and 122 rows on the ref that carries this ADR; the ratio is the argument, not the totals.)

  **The one collision, and it cost an entry.** [`2026-08-16-session-33.md`](../sessions/2026-08-16-session-33.md) holds **four** sections and had **three** rows. Its sections at `:70` and `:110` are two genuinely different sessions that both ran FOLD-02 session 4, and their headings differ **only** by the `(money path)` marker that this index drops on the way from heading to row. Once the marker was dropped the two rendered identically, one row was written for both, and **the fourth session disappeared from the registry while every gate stayed green.** `CI-06n` cannot see it: the file has a row, so the file is covered.

  **The holes.** There is no session 35 and no session 70. Session 70 was allocated by [WAVE-01 section 4](../plans/WAVE-01-post-freeze-parallel-sessions.md) to `R1`, the duplicated-registry-rows session, and **`R1` never ran**, as [STATE](../STATE.md) and [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) both record. Session 35 has no record anywhere in the corpus: it is a number nothing claims and nothing spent.

---

### 1. The ruling: identity is `(log file, section heading)`, and the number is neither

**A session number is a day-and-slot allocation.** It names when a session ran and which log file it appends to. It does not name the session, it has never named the session, and since session 45 it has demonstrably named up to four of them at once.

> **The identifier of a session is the pair `(log file, `##` heading)`.** The heading is the identifier's second component **verbatim, including any `(money path)` marker**. A row in the session index is that identifier rendered; its **first cell is a pointer to the file, not a key.**

**The marker is part of the identity and not decoration, and session 33 is the proof.** Two sessions whose only distinguishing text was the marker collapsed into one row the moment the marker was dropped. This index has dropped it elsewhere too, and everywhere else it happened to be harmless because the rest of the heading still differed. **It was harmless by luck, not by construction.**

**Consequently the session index legitimately carries several rows under one first cell, and that is a property of the registry rather than a defect in it.** `CI-06u` reads first cells as identities and is right to do so on every other table in this corpus. On this one it is reading a pointer.

### 2. Numbers are NOT made unique, and the reason is stated rather than assumed

The alternative was ruled on directly, because defaulting into it would have been the larger and less reversible act.

**Renumbering** would give each of the 121 sessions its own number, split the 19 shared files, and make the first cell a real key. The register entry would go to **zero** rather than 19.

**Rejected, for three reasons, in order of weight:**

1. **It rewrites history to satisfy a gate.** 121 sessions ran under the numbers they ran under. Session 33's four sections were four sessions that all called themselves 33, in their own logs and in every document that cites them. Renumbering makes the corpus say something that was not true at the time.
2. **The cross-reference surface is large and unbounded by this fence.** Session numbers are cited in plans, in STATE, in ALLOCATION's provenance clauses, in ADR bodies and inside other session logs. A renumber that misses one leaves a citation pointing at a session that no longer exists, and **`CI-06a` cannot see it**: a prose mention of "session 49" is not a link.
3. **It is a side effect of a merge repair.** This session's fence is the index and the ruling. Thirty renumbered sessions is its own session with its own fence, and it should not arrive attached to a deduplication.

**This ADR does not forbid the renumber; it forbids doing it silently.** If a later session wants unique numbers, this ruling is what it supersedes, and it must supersede it deliberately.

### 3. What stops a fourth session taking a number that already names three

**Nothing does today, and the honest answer is that nothing in this ruling changes that.** What the ruling changes is that the collision is now **detectable**, because the identity function is written down.

**The stop is mechanical and it is named here rather than built here** ([ADR-065](ADR-065.md) rules that a new gate takes a slug and not a letter):

> **`CI-06/session-index-matches-sections`** — for every session log, the `##` headings in the file and the rows in [`docs/sessions/README.md`](../sessions/README.md) that point at it are in **one-to-one correspondence**, matched on the heading's subject. A section with no row is a session that vanished; a row with no section is a session that never ran.

That gate is what would have caught session 33 on the day it happened, and it is the only thing in this ruling that is load-bearing against the next collision. **It is owed and it is not written**, and until it is, this registry is protected by a convention and a reading rather than by a control. That is stated as a real constraint, not a note.

**It is a control this corpus prefers on its own reasoning.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) records that the reconciliation session's three worst errors were failures to check a claim against a primary source, and that the remedy is a mechanical assertion rather than a bigger model. A session heading is the primary source and the index row is the claim; this gate is the assertion between them.

### 4. `CI-06u`'s register holds 19, and what it decays to

**The `docs/sessions/README.md` entry goes from 59 keys to exactly 19**: sessions 31, 32, 33, 40, 42, 45, 47, 48, 49, 50, 51, 52, 54, 55, 56, 57, 58, 59 and 75. The other 40 were the merge and they are repaired.

**The 19 stay in the REGISTER and not in an exemption, and the distinction matters.** The register's own rule is that it shrinks only and that an entry naming nothing is a finding, so a permanent residue sitting there forever would turn it into the exemption list it was built to avoid. **These 19 name a repair that is still owed; it is simply not the repair the register assumed.**

> **The destination is the gate's dimension-header mechanism, not the register.** `CI-06u` already exempts a table whose first column is a **dimension rather than an identity** — `From` on a transition table keyed by `(From, To, Guard)` is the argued case, and it is exactly this table's shape: the first cell is one component of a two-part key. The session index cannot reach that mechanism today **because its first-column header is empty**, which the register comment states. Giving the table the header `Session` and adding `session` to `DIMENSION_HEADERS` takes the entry to zero **without renumbering anything**.

**That is two lines and it is deliberately not taken here.** `DIMENSION_HEADERS` is corpus-wide: it would exempt every future table headed `Session` from the merge gate, and widening a merge gate's scope inside the session that is repairing a merge is the shape of relaxation the working agreements forbid. **It is owed work for the session that writes `CI-06/session-index-matches-sections`**, which is the control that makes the exemption safe to grant, and the two should land together.

### 5. A hole is an unspent allocation, and it is recorded where allocations live

**A gap in the session numbers means one thing: a number was allocated and the session never ran.** It is not an error and it is not a missing file to go and write. Session 70 is the worked example — allocated by WAVE-01 to `R1`, and `R1` never ran.

**But an unexplained hole and an unspent one are indistinguishable from the index**, which is the actual defect. Session 35 has no record anywhere in the corpus of who held it or what it was for. **A registry with unexplained holes and repeated numbers is not a registry**, and the index cannot fix that, because the index only ever contains sessions that ran.

**The remedy is section 6.** A hole is legible only against an allocation table, and this is the last registry without one.

### 6. The allocation table: `docs/sessions/ALLOCATION.md`

> **The session-number allocation table lives at `docs/sessions/ALLOCATION.md`, beside the registry it allocates for, mirroring [`docs/decisions/ALLOCATION.md`](ALLOCATION.md).** It is unwritten, so this reference is deliberately not a link: `CI-06a` fails on a link to a document that does not exist, which is the constraint [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3 names. One row per number. The row carries the branch or wave that claimed it, what the session was for, and whether it ran. **A number is claimed in the table before the log file is written** ([ADR-034](ADR-034.md)'s rule, unchanged), and when a claimed number's log lands the row is **amended in place** rather than joined by a second row ([ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3).
>
> **A shared number gets one row and lists its holders**, because the ruling in section 1 is that the number is the allocation and the holders are the sessions.

**It is named here and it is not written here.** Backfilling 88 numbers is a transcription job against 83 files and several wave plans, it is the kind of work that must not be interleaved with a ruling, and it has a natural owner: **the session that writes `CI-06/session-index-matches-sections`**, which needs the same file-by-file read to build its fixture. Until then the holes stay as they are and this ADR is the only record of what they mean.

### 7. The filename format, and a premise correction

**The brief for this session recorded that `gates.mjs` "matched `session-\d{2}\.md` until 2026-08-20" and that "that cap is lifted". It is not lifted.** On `origin/main` today the sessions registry predicate at [`gates.mjs:218`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) reads:

```js
entry: (f) => /^docs\/sessions\/\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}-session-\d{2}\.md$/.test(f),
```

`git log -S` puts that expression in the ADR-043 split commit and shows no later change to it. **The cap is live.**

**What it costs, at session 100.** `CI-06n` builds `onDisk` from files matching this predicate and filters README rows through the same predicate. A `session-100.md` file matches neither. So the file yields **no "entry file with no row" finding** and its row yields **no resolution check**: the registry's only completeness gate goes silent in **both directions** for every session from 100 on, and the `onDisk.length === 0` sentinel does not trip because the other 83 still match.

> **The format is `YYYY-MM-DD-session-NN.md`, zero-padded to a minimum of two digits and unbounded above.** The predicate is to be widened to `\d{2,}` **before session 100 is allocated**, and the allocation table in section 6 is where that deadline becomes visible.

The corpus is at 88 allocated numbers. This is close, it is checkable, and it fails silently, which is the combination that argues for writing it down now.

- **What the record said, and what the tree says.**

| Claim | Where | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The session index is duplicated and one copy can be deleted | the shape of every other ADR-061 repair | **False here.** Neither copy is a superset; a delete loses 25 sessions and fails `CI-06n` |
| The two copies might contradict each other, as `M05` and `STATE_MACHINES` did | [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s `CI-06u` row | **False.** All 58 shared keys carry identical subject sets, so the union had nothing to rule on |
| 59 of the register's keys are duplicates awaiting repair | [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs)'s `CI06U_REGISTER` | **40 were. 19 are the convention**, and they need a different mechanism, not a repair |
| Identity in this registry is `(file, subject)` | the register comment on this entry | **True, and it was never written down**, which is how session 33's fourth entry was lost |
| `gates.mjs`'s two-digit session cap is lifted | this session's brief | **False.** `gates.mjs:218` still reads `session-\d{2}\.md`; section 7 |
| Session 70 is a missing log | readable from the index alone | **False.** It is an unspent allocation; `R1` never ran |

- **Alternatives considered.**

  - **Renumber, and make the number the identifier.** Rejected in section 2 on three grounds, the first of which is that it rewrites what was true at the time. Not forbidden; it must be its own session and it must supersede this ADR deliberately.
  - **Delete one copy of the index.** Rejected: neither copy is a superset, and 25 sessions live in exactly one of them.
  - **Leave the 19 in the register permanently and call the matter closed.** Rejected in section 4. A residue that never decays turns a register into an exemption list, which is the failure the register's own ratchet exists against.
  - **Add `session` to `DIMENSION_HEADERS` in this session and take the entry to zero.** Rejected in section 4, and it is the tempting one. The exemption is corpus-wide and the control that makes it safe is not written yet. **Granting a merge gate a wider exemption from inside a merge repair is the shape the working agreements forbid**, whatever the argument for it.
  - **Split the shared files, one file per session, keeping the numbers.** Rejected: it produces `2026-08-16-session-33b.md`, which is a renumber wearing a suffix. It carries every cost of section 2 and adds a filename format no predicate matches.
  - **Write `docs/sessions/ALLOCATION.md` here.** Rejected: 88 rows of transcription against 83 files, interleaved with a ruling, in a session whose fence is the index. Named and owned in section 6 instead.

- **Consequences:**

  - **The index is one table**, 121 rows over 83 keys as merged and 122 over 84 once this session's own log is appended, ordered by session number and, within a number, by the order the sections appear in the file. The ordering is now a stated rule rather than the residue of two merges: copy 1 ended `59, 60, 59, 61, 64, 62`.
  - **Session 33 gains its fourth row**, quoting the heading verbatim with its `(money path)` marker. **This is the only entry the corpus had silently lost**, and the only file of 83 where sections and rows disagreed.
  - **The duplicated prose header is repaired.** Two headers stood at `:9` and `:13`, contradicting each other on recoverability: "from this log alone" against "from its own file alone, one file per session since [ADR-043](ADR-043.md)". **ADR-043 makes the second current** and the first is dropped. It is the same keep-both mechanism as the table below it and it was in no register, because `CI-06u` reads tables and this was prose.
  - **`CI-06u`'s register drops from 59 keys to 19**, and `docs/sessions/README.md` remains the only file in it. The gate's note line changes accordingly.
  - **Two controls are owed and neither is written**: `CI-06/session-index-matches-sections` and the `\d{2,}` widening. The second has a deadline the corpus will reach.
  - **`ALLOCATION.md`'s `064` row is left unlinked**, contrary to [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3, which says the session that lands the artifact links the reservation. **ALLOCATION is outside this session's fence** and ADR-065 is itself unsigned. The link is owed to whichever session next holds that file.
  - **[STATE](../STATE.md):1806 says the register holds "97 known duplicate keys across 7 files".** It was stale before this session and is staler after it; the gate prints the truth on every run. STATE is outside this fence and the correction is owed.
  - **The falsification harness stops depending on this register being non-empty.** [`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs)'s `CI-06u/a-repaired-register-entry-is-itself-a-finding` anchored on the first live register entry, which is a seed pinned to a defect the corpus is allowed to repair -- the class the founder rider of 2026-08-15 forbids, and the third anchor this one case has outgrown. Emptying the register on today's tree was measured: `gates.mjs check` reports `CI-06u` failing with 130 findings and `falsify.mjs` reports 5 problems, the first being `SEED IS STALE`. The seed now **builds its own two-rows-under-one-key table and registers it in the seeded copy**, so the case holds on a tree whose real register is empty. **The gate's own falsification was tied to the defect the gate exists to remove, which meant the wave's success was what would end it.**

- **Founder approval: GRANTED 2026-08-21.** Given by the founder in session 95 as an **explicit delegated approval**, not as a line-by-line read. **Recorded that way deliberately**: [E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) reserves the line-by-line read for the money path, this entry is not on it, and a signature claiming more than was actually done would put a false attestation into the record this corpus exists to be. **Safe to grant because the ruling is in force and was exercised while unsigned**: session 95 appended a second `##` section to its own log under exactly this identity function, and the declined renumber is the cheaper of the two options by the count this entry measured rather than recalled. Every count in this entry was measured on the tree rather than carried from the brief, and one of the brief's premises did not survive that check: section 7.
