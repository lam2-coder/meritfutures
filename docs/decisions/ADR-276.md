## ADR-276: one word, two temporal types, and the estate already tells them apart in six of nine places, so the finding is the three that say nothing rather than the collision  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-272](ADR-272.md) clause 5 ruled that a name which is silent about its temporal type *"MUST STILL DENOTE ONE TYPE ACROSS THE ESTATE"*, named `effective_from` as the estate's one violator, and left the disambiguation OWED with a fence of its own. This row is that fence, and it asked the prior question the owed item did not: whether nine tables spelling one word two ways is a DEFECT or a CORRECT DISTINCTION spelled the same way.**

**IT IS A CORRECT DISTINCTION, THE NINE ARE RIGHT AS THEY STAND, AND NO RENAME AND NO MIGRATION IS OWED.** The two types answer two different questions and the question is decidable at each table from its own DDL without reading a single comment: **must two supersessions of one subject inside one calendar day be distinguishable?** Where they must, `effective_from` is a `timestamptz` and is the sole non-subject member of the primary key. Where they need not, it is a `date`.

**AND ADR-272 CLAUSE 5'S OWN STATED GROUND IS FALSE ON SIX OF THE NINE, WHICH IS THE FINDING THIS ENTRY ADDS.** That clause grounded the harm on *"no reader can tell which without opening the migration."* Six of the nine already carry `**Unit: wall clock**` in their design record, and they carry it because `CI-06m` REFUSES a `date` column that declares no unit. The three `timestamptz` columns carry nothing, because `CI-06m`'s scope is `date`. **The collision is not the defect. The asymmetry is**, and it is repaired by writing three sentences rather than by moving nine columns.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `276`, session **467**, branch `claude/one-column-two-types`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED AND NO `RI-nn` IS MINTED.** `0074` is the highest on disk, read at the moment of branching, and `git diff origin/main -- packages/db/migrations` is empty on this branch. `packages/db/migrations/**` and every `src/` file are out of fence by the row's own words and neither is written.

- **NOT MONEY BY CONTENT AND NOT MONEY BY BLAST RADIUS.** No DDL moves, no reader moves, no value moves, and nothing executable changes: the diff is design records, this entry and the registries. **`E2` is neither claimed nor discharged.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. Merging is not signing.

- **THE ROW'S TRAP IS UPHELD RATHER THAN WORKED AROUND.** No column is renamed to match a convention, no migration is proposed to close a vocabulary gap, and the tidy rule that would have made all nine one type is refused in section 4 with the cost of taking it measured rather than asserted.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE NINE ARE CORRECT AS THEY STAND. NO COLUMN IS RENAMED, NO MIGRATION IS OWED, AND [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) SECTION 6'S `effective_from` DISAMBIGUATION CLOSES HERE AS A DOCUMENTATION RULING.** The row offered this as a complete and welcome outcome and it is the one the sites support.
>
> **2. THE DISTINCTION IS THIS, AND IT IS DECIDABLE FROM THE DDL ALONE: MUST TWO SUPERSESSIONS OF ONE SUBJECT INSIDE ONE CALENDAR DAY BE DISTINGUISHABLE?** Where they must, `effective_from` is a `timestamptz` and is the sole non-subject member of the primary key, with no `version` column beside it. Where they need not, it is a `date`, and the row's identity is carried by an explicit `version` (four of the six), by the day itself (`contract_specs`), or by nothing because the table holds one row per subject (`geo_restrictions`).
>
> **3. THE GROUND FOR CLAUSE 2 IS THE SCHEMA'S AND THE ROUTES', NOT THIS ENTRY'S.** [`0074:150`](../../packages/db/migrations/0074_firm_parameters.sql): *"Two rows for one parameter at one INSTANT would make 'which number was in force' ambiguous, and the primary key is what makes that unrepresentable."* [`admin-wallet.ts:1834`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) refuses with *"The grain is `(identity_id, effective_from)` and a limit is an APPEND, so two writes at one INSTANT collide"*, which says in the same breath that two writes on one DAY do not. **A `date` on those three tables would make the second act of a working day a primary-key collision**, and `SECURITY C-23`'s scenario is exactly a limit tightened during an incident and relaxed after it.
>
> **4. THEREFORE [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) CLAUSE 5 IS NARROWED AND NOT OVERTURNED, AND IT IS NARROWED ON ITS OWN STATED GROUND.** That clause is *"A NAME THAT IS SILENT MUST STILL DENOTE ONE TYPE ACROSS THE ESTATE"*, and the harm it names is *"no reader can tell which without opening the migration."* **The harm is the second sentence and not the first.** One name may denote two types where the estate says which at the reader's seat, and may not where it says nothing. Clause 5 is amended to read: **a silent name that denotes more than one temporal type must declare which one it holds at every site, in the design record, or be renamed.**
>
> **5. SIX OF THE NINE ALREADY SATISFY CLAUSE 4 AND A GATE IS WHY, NOT A CONVENTION SOMEBODY FOLLOWED.** `CI-06m` refuses any `date` column in the migration set whose design record carries no `**Unit: <token>**` from a closed vocabulary of three ([`gates.mjs:2725`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs)). All six declare `**Unit: wall clock**`, and on this tree the gate resolves **56 `date` columns over 3 tokens: 36 trading day, 18 wall clock, 2 rail clock, 0 unresolved.**
>
> **6. THE THREE `timestamptz` COLUMNS GET THE SAME DISCLOSURE IN PROSE AND DELIBERATELY NOT AS A `**Unit:**` MARKER.** Section 6. `CI-06m` reads that marker on `date` rows only, so a marker written on a `timestamptz` row is checked by nothing while looking exactly like a declaration that is. **That is `CI-06m`'s own "a marker is not a mention" lesson run backwards** and this entry refuses to plant it.
>
> **7. THE READER'S SEAT IS [data-model/README](../architecture/data-model/README.md) SECTION 1 AND IT NAMES ONE OF THE THREE UNITS.** Its Types bullet reads *"Trading dates: `date`, and always the exchange trading day"*, and the gate one layer down counts **20 of 56 `date` columns that are not one.** The bullet is not false about trading dates; it is silent about the other twenty, so a reader who stops there has no reason to look for a unit token at all. Amended, with the vocabulary named and `CI-06m` cited as what enforces it.
>
> **8. NOTHING IS RULED ABOUT THE API SURFACE HERE AND THE COLLISION HAS ALREADY ARRIVED THERE.** Section 7. `effective_from` is a live response field twice, a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` on a PUBLIC page and an RFC 3339 instant on the admin surface, and [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2 reaches neither because the field name carries no suffix. `apps/api/src/**` and [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) are both outside this row's fence. **It is the largest thing this entry leaves owed and it is named rather than implied.**
>
> **9. `RI-26` LEG 2 STAYS, AND ITS EXCEPTION'S GROUND IS NOW WRONG IN TWO WAYS.** Section 8. The leg is still the only thing that would catch a TENTH table adopting this name with a third disposition. Its entry must stop saying the collision is a defect awaiting a repair, and it cites [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) clause 4 where the clause that rules `effective_from` is clause 5. `packages/tooling/**` is outside this fence and the edit is specified rather than taken.

### 3. Why the discriminator is the day-grain question and not the four answers that fit worse

Four candidate rules were tested against all nine before clause 2 was written. Three of them fit seven or eight and were discarded for that reason, because a rule that fits eight of nine is a rule with an unexplained exception, and an unexplained exception is where the next session goes wrong.

| Candidate | Fits | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| "config is a `date`, an operator's act is a `timestamptz`" | 8 of 9 | `price_floors` is set through the dual-controlled publish path and is as much configuration as a contract spec. The noun does not decide it |
| "a table with a `version` column dates in days" | 7 of 9 | `contract_specs` and `geo_restrictions` have no `version` column and are both `date` |
| "`effective_from` in the primary key means `timestamptz`" | 8 of 9 | `contract_specs` puts a `date` in the key at [`0004:299`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) |
| **"two supersessions inside one day must be distinguishable"** | **9 of 9** | -- |

**The winning rule explains the three near-misses rather than surviving them.** `contract_specs` puts a `date` in its key precisely because one spec per symbol per day is a true constraint about the exchange rather than an accident of typing: the key is doing the ruling. `geo_restrictions` needs no version discriminator at all because it holds one row per country. `price_floors` is configuration by content and an act by grain, and the grain is what the primary key is made of.

**And it is the only one of the four a future session can apply without this entry.** The other three need a judgement about what a table IS. This one is answered by reading a `PRIMARY KEY` line.

### 4. The tidy rule, its cost measured, and why it is refused

**The tempting move is to pick one type for `effective_from` and supersede the other six or three.** It is one sentence, it makes `RI-26` leg 2's exception disappear, and it is wrong in both directions.

**Making all nine a `date`** takes the instant off `wallet_spend_limits`, `price_floors` and `firm_parameters`, and the cost is not a migration count: it makes the SECOND spend limit set on an incident day unwritable, against a `C-23` control whose whole scenario is a compromised session. The route already refuses that write with a message naming an instant ([`admin-wallet.ts:1834`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts)), and `0074` chose the type in a header that argues it ([`0074:150`](../../packages/db/migrations/0074_firm_parameters.sql)). **A convention would be buying legibility with a control.**

**Making all nine a `timestamptz`** is worse and quieter. It invents a time of day for a counsel ruling, a contract specification and a published methodology, none of which has one, and `INV-M12-07`'s forward-only rule would start comparing an invented instant against a trading day. That is [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 4's forbidden derivation written into the schema instead of into a helper.

**Six merged migrations on one side and three on the other**, five of the nine money-adjacent, to buy a property the estate already has in six places and can have in the other three for three sentences. **The row named this as its trap and the measurement is what refuses it rather than the row's say-so.**

### 5. `statistic_definitions` is `wall clock` and is compared against a trading day, and that is sound for a reason worth writing down

This is the one place in the estate where a `wall clock` `effective_from` meets the exchange calendar, and it looked like a defect until it was read.

[`ports.ts:777`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts) states the adapter's obligation as *"`effective_from <= asOfTradingDay`"*, and `asOfTradingDay` is a `TradingDay`. A `wall clock` day and a trading day are different units under [ADR-042](ADR-042.md), so the comparison mixes them.

**It is sound because it is an ORDER COMPARISON and not an ARITHMETIC.** Every trading day is a calendar day, `<=` between two `YYYY-MM-DD` values is the same order on both, and the question being asked, *"had this definition taken effect by the day being published"*, is answered correctly whether or not the definition's start date was a session. A definition effective on a Saturday is in force on the Monday that follows, which is the intent.

**Where it would stop being sound is named here so it is not rediscovered:** any expression that ADDS to or SUBTRACTS from `effective_from` in days, or that counts days between it and a trading day. Those are trading-day arithmetic on a wall-clock value and they are wrong on roughly 104 days a year, which is `CI-06m`'s own header sentence about `interval '48 hours'`. **No such expression exists today; `effectiveDefinitions` has no adapter yet.** The obligation is stated in the port's own doc comment, which is where the adapter's author will read it.

### 6. What is written, where, and why the three sentences are prose rather than a marker

**The disclosure goes where a reader already is.** [data-model/README](../architecture/data-model/README.md) section 1 gains the temporal-column rule and the three-token vocabulary, because section 1 is what a module author reads before opening a table's file. The nine table files each get the disposition in the `effective_from` row's own `Why` cell, because that is the cell a reader is looking at when the question occurs to them.

**The three `timestamptz` rows do NOT get a `**Unit:**` marker and the reason is mechanical.** `CI-06m` collects `date` columns from the DDL and looks for the marker on those rows only. A marker on a `timestamptz` row would be read by no gate, could carry an invalid token forever, and would be indistinguishable from the six that ARE checked. `CI-06m`'s header records that its first version matched a token anywhere in the row and three rows passed by accident, which is the same failure in the other direction. **A declaration nothing reads is prose wearing a control's clothes**, so the three carry a plain sentence that says which clock and why, and claims nothing more.

**Widening `CI-06m` to `timestamptz` was considered and is not taken.** It is a gate change, `scripts/corpus/gates.mjs` is outside this fence, and it is not obviously right: a `timestamptz` is already unambiguous on the {instant, day} axis that [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) rules, and the unit question for an instant is a different question that no ruling has asked. Section 8 records it as an option rather than as an owed item, because nobody has established it is needed.

### 7. The collision has already arrived at the API surface, and that is the largest thing left owed

**`effective_from` is a live response field twice and the two are different temporal types.**

| Surface | Field | Type on the wire | Contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GET /public/methods/:statCode` | `effective_from` | bare `YYYY-MM-DD`, guarded by a `DAY` regex at [`public-methods.ts:456`](../../apps/api/src/routes/public-methods.ts) | [API_CONTRACT:315](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), *"`YYYY-MM-DD`, always future at write time"* |
| `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/spend-limit` | `effective_from` | RFC 3339 instant, `toISOString()` at [`admin-wallet.ts:1818`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts), returned at [`:1884`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) | [API_CONTRACT:1400](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) and [`:1407`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), no shape stated |

**Both are `readonly effective_from: string`.** [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2 refuses a `*_at` field carrying a day and a `*_day` field carrying an instant, and reaches neither of these, because the field name carries no suffix. **[API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s Time rule has the same blind spot**, which [ADR-274](ADR-274.md) section 5 already found from the other side.

**AND THE ADMIN ENDPOINT ACCEPTS THE PUBLIC PAGE'S SHAPE.** `instantField` parses with a bare `new Date(value)` at [`admin-wallet.ts:1077`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) and refuses only what is unparseable. **Measured, not reasoned:** `new Date('2026-09-01')` returns `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z` at `TZ=UTC` and at `TZ=Asia/Kolkata` alike, because a date-only ISO string is UTC by specification. **So a caller who learned the field's shape from the public methods page and sent a day gets a silently promoted instant on the append-only record of what limit was in force when.** It is accepted, stored, echoed back with a time of day it never carried, and nothing anywhere reports it.

**This is [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) clause 5's harm, executable, on the surface rather than in the schema**, and it is the reason clause 4 above narrows that clause instead of retiring it. `apps/api/src/**` and [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) are outside this row's fence and neither is touched. It is section 8's first owed item and it is the one worth a row.

### 9. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**CLAUSE 2 IS A RULE DERIVED FROM NINE OBSERVATIONS AND IT IS NOT A LAW.** Nine is a small set, six of the nine have no reader at all, and a tenth table could arrive whose day-grain answer is "no" and whose type is `timestamptz` for a reason none of these nine carries. **What the rule buys is that such a table would be visible**: `RI-26` leg 2 reports any new type for this name, and the entry it reports through now names a disposition rather than a defect. A reviewer who thinks clause 2 is a pattern rather than a principle is arguing with the right thing.

**THE STRONGEST CASE AGAINST CLAUSE 1 IS `contract_specs`, AND IT IS NOT MADE ABOVE.** That table has `effective_from` in its primary key with no version column, which is the timestamptz set's exact shape, and it is a `date`. This entry rules that the key is deliberately encoding one-spec-per-symbol-per-day, and **the migration does not say so in as many words**: [`0004:293`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) says only *"Versioned because specs change. `effective_to` null means current."* The reading is the best available and it is an inference. **If the exchange ever publishes two specs for one symbol on one day, this table cannot record it**, and that is a property worth a founder's eye whatever is decided about the name.

**AND SECTION 5 RULES A COMPARISON SOUND ON A PORT THAT HAS NO IMPLEMENTATION.** `effectiveDefinitions` is a contract and a caller and no adapter. What was checked is the predicate as written in the doc comment. **An adapter that renders `asOfTradingDay` through anything other than a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` would break the argument**, and nothing checks that today.
