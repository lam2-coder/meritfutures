### 3. What `RI-35`'s first leg actually demands, read at source

Leg 1 reads the file named by a claim's `site`, splits it on newlines, and counts the lines that CONTAIN the registered `claim` string. **One is the only passing answer.** Zero is a finding that says the sentence moved, was reworded or was deleted; two is a finding that says the anchor does not identify one site. **No line number is stored anywhere**, which is the property that makes re-anchoring possible at all and also the property that makes it delicate: the substring has to occur verbatim at the new site AND be the sentence that carries the claim, and only the first half is machine-checkable.

**THAT SECOND HALF IS WHY THIS ROW READ BOTH FILES END TO END RATHER THAN GREPPING.** A substring that occurs at a new site while belonging to a different argument would satisfy leg 1 and quietly rebind the check to a sentence nobody chose.

### 4. The `site`, which is honest to move and is refused anyway

#### 4.1 The anchor exists at the new home and it is the right sentence

The registered anchor is the string `` `makeEventSink` is called by NO file ``. Derived at reporting time, it occurs **exactly once** in `apps/api/src/events.ts`, the registered site, and **exactly once** in `packages/ledger/src/events.ts`, the producer's home. **It is not a coincidental substring at the home.** The paragraph it sits in continues that every call to the factory in this tree is in a suite, that `UNWIRED_EVENT_SINK` is therefore the only sink any deployment could reach, and then names the binding outright: it says `RI-35` registers that absence as `event-sink-caller` and turns RED at that sentence the day it stops being true. **That is the claim sentence, written as one, at the producer.**

So the honest anchor DOES exist at the new site and the re-anchoring is a real repair rather than a nearby sentence pressed into service. **It was then performed and measured.** With `site` changed to `packages/ledger/src/events.ts` and nothing else touched, `repo-invariants.mjs` reports **35 of 35 invariants hold**, `RI-35` included.

#### 4.2 And it reddens a suite this row may not write

`apps/api/test/event-placement.test.ts` asserts that this register still spells the old path. It is not an accident and the file says so at the assertion: the comment above it states that the `site` is the old path, that moving it was outside ADR-410's fence, and that **the day the register's site moves, that expectation is what tells the row the sentence may follow it.** It is a tripwire, deliberately laid, and this row tripped it exactly as designed.

Measured with the `site` moved and nothing else: that file reports **1 failed, 19 passed**, the one failure being the `toContain` on the old path. The register edit was then reverted and `sha256sum` on `packages/tooling/checks/absence-claims.mjs` returned the same digest before and after.

**SHIPPING THE MOVE MEANS SHIPPING A RED SUITE, AND THAT IS WORSE THAN SHIPPING THE FINDING.** [ADR-389](ADR-389.md) ruled exactly this trade one wave over and reverted a page rather than take it. Editing the suite is forbidden: `apps/**` is outside this row's fence, row 413 holds `apps/api/src/admin-source/**` and `apps/api/src/payout-backend.ts` and holds no test, and row 414 holds `apps/worker/**`. **No row in this wave owns that file.**

#### 4.3 The finding under the finding: the sentence is carried twice

**THE TREE ALREADY HAS TWO FILES CLAIMING TO CARRY THE BOUND SENTENCE AND EXACTLY ONE OF THEM CAN BE RIGHT.** `apps/api/src/events.ts` says the register anchors that claim to ITS path, which is TRUE today. `packages/ledger/src/events.ts` says `RI-35` turns RED at ITS sentence, which is **FALSE today**: the register points at the other file, so a rewording at the home would be invisible to leg 1. Moving the `site` swaps which of the two is lying and repairs nothing on its own.

**Nothing in the shipped check reports this**, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight: leg 1 reads only the site it is told about, leg 6 sweeps only registered needles and this artifact registers none, so a second copy of a bound sentence is outside every leg by construction. It was found by reading both files.

| file | what the row that takes this must do | whose it is today |
|---|---|---|
| `packages/tooling/checks/absence-claims.mjs` | change the one `site` string | **this package's.** The change is prepared, measured green, and the register now says in a comment why it is not taken |

**TWO OF THE THREE ARE `apps/**` AND THE THIRD IS THIS ONE**, which is why a row holding only `packages/tooling` cannot finish the repair its own package was assigned. That is the specification, corrected, rather than an estimate.

Its scope is `shippedSources` plus `scriptSources`. | where | lines | what it is |

#### 5.1 The old figure does not reproduce, and the move is not what broke it

The entry read *"exactly seven lines in the shipped scope: six are the declaring module's own header and declarations, and the seventh is `apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts`'s `EVENT_SINK_BLOCKER`"*. **`git log -S` names exactly one commit that wrote that sentence, `8d10e86c`**, and a `git worktree` at it puts the same instrument over the tree the author had.

| instrument, at `8d10e86c` | result |
|---|---|
| raw lines, shipped scope | **11 over 2 files** |
| raw lines, shipped scope plus `scripts/` | **11 over 2 files** |
| lines with comments stripped, either scope | **4 over 2 files** |
| leg 6's own reader, an absence marker on the line and a needle in the window | **2** |

**NOT ONE OF THEM IS SEVEN.** The partition is recoverable, though, and that is what makes it a finding rather than a mystery: at that commit the declaring module carries four header lines and two declarations, which is the "six", plus the one in `batch/adapter.ts`, which is the "seventh". **What it silently drops is four more lines in the declaring module's own BODY**: three `{@link}` references in jsdoc and one refusal string that quotes the install. So the figure was **four short the day it was written**, under the instrument its own words describe, and every wave since has read a number that was never true.

**THE MOVE IS THEREFORE NOT THE DEFECT AND THE REPAIR IS NOT AN ADJUSTMENT.** ADR-410 section 9 says the move "changes that figure" and it does, but a row that had taken the delta and added it would have shipped a second wrong number with a fresher date. **The figure is derived from scratch and anchored to a commit in the sentence itself**, which is the remedy [ADR-034](ADR-034.md) states and the shape two entries in this same register already use.

#### 5.2 What is bound rather than reported, and why it is not the integer

Eight cases are added to `packages/tooling/test/absence-claims.test.ts`, and **the integer is deliberately not one of them.** A case asserting `23` against the live tree would go red the day any row rewraps a comment under `apps/worker/src`, and row 414 is moving exactly those files this wave, so **a case that fires on a rewrap is a case somebody deletes rather than answers**, which is a weakening by a longer road.

**WHAT IS ASSERTED IS THE ARGUMENT, AND IT IS A NARROWER SIGNAL THAN THE NUMBER.** Every line the two names reach must be in one of the four places the entry accounts for: the declaring module, the barrel, the compatibility module, or somewhere under `apps/worker/src`. A reached line in a FIFTH place is a needle arriving where the entry does not account for it, **which is the shape of a real install landing**, so the case still fails on good news. It was watched RED under a seeded file at `apps/api/src/zz-seed-fifth-place.ts` naming the writer, which reported that path and line, and the seed was deleted.

The double-carriage of section 4.3 is bound too: the anchor must occur exactly once at the old path AND exactly once at the home. Watched RED by reseating one word of the sentence at the home, which reported `hits: 0` against `hits: 1`; the file was restored from a copy taken before the seed and `sha256sum` returned the same digest on both sides. **A `git checkout --` restore was deliberately not used**, on ADR-410's own recorded lesson that it is the wrong instrument on a tree carrying uncommitted repairs.

### 6. ADR-410 section 7's owed registration, and whether this row agrees with it

**IT IS LANDED IN `sweptBy`, WHICH IS THE FIELD SECTION 7 NAMED.** The registration states the false positive in the probe's own terms: its proxy for a value position is `TRANSACTION_EVENT_WRITER` followed by `.`, `,` or `)`, and **a name inside a re-export SPECIFIER LIST is followed by a comma while being a binding position and not a value position at all.**

**AND IT IS WATCHED RATHER THAN CLAIMED, WHICH IS THE PART SECTION 7 COULD NOT DO FROM WHERE IT STOOD.** A registered false positive nobody has seen fire is a paragraph. Four cases now stand around it, all using the SHIPPED probe rather than a copy of it:

| case | fixture | probe |
|---|---|---|
| the false positive, fired | a barrel publishing the writer inside a multi-name list | **`present`**, which is the artifact reported existing over a tree where it does not |
| the counterfactual | the same barrel, same name, published on a statement of its own | **`absent`** |
| the asymmetry | the FACTORY name in that same list | **`absent`**, because its shape is a call and not three punctuation marks |
| the power that must not be lost | a real install, through the factory and separately through a member access on the writer | **`present`** both ways |

**THE VERDICT: YES, IT IS A FINDING ABOUT THE CHECK, AND THIS ROW WOULD PUT IT MORE STRONGLY THAN SECTION 7 DOES.** Nothing about a barrel makes a re-export a value. The proxy conflates two syntactic positions that TypeScript itself distinguishes, and the only reason it could not fire before is that the producer had never been published from a package, which is a fact about the estate's history and not about the check being right. Section 7's own sentence, that the probe "cannot tell a publication from an install", is exact.

**WHAT THIS ROW ADDS IS THE PART THAT DECIDES HOW URGENT IT IS: WHAT HOLDS THE GREEN TODAY IS A LINE BREAK.** `packages/ledger` publishes the writer on a statement short enough that prettier leaves it on one line, so the next character after the name is a space. **Add a second name to that statement and prettier reflows it into a list, puts a comma after this one, and flips the artifact to `present`, turning `RI-35` red at a sentence that is TRUE, with nobody having edited the register, the probe, or any file this check names.** The correctness of a repository invariant currently rests on a formatter's line-width heuristic and a comment asking the next reader not to disturb it. That is registered in `sweptBy` in those words.

**IT IS REGISTERED AND NOT REPAIRED, AND THE REASON IS THAT EVERY REPAIR IS A WIDENING THIS ROW WAS FORBIDDEN.** Excluding the barrel is a second exclusion. Reading the specifier list is a parser. Both are changes to a check in the direction of making it easier to satisfy, on a wave whose rule about that is sharper than usual. **The registration is the honest disposition for a known false positive and it is what this register carries `sweptBy` for.**

### 10. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.** [ADR-410](ADR-410.md), whose section 9 this row answers, is itself `proposed` and unsigned on this row's base.

- **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST DECISION IS WHETHER A REGISTER'S REPAIR MAY BE SPLIT ACROSS A FENCE AT ALL.** ADR-410 assigned this `site` to `packages/tooling` and the assignment was not wrong so much as incomplete: the binding it names is enforced from `apps/api/test`, which no reading of the register would reveal. **This is the second consecutive row where the fence and the objective were in contradiction and only running the work found it**, and the instrument both times was performing the change and reading the failures. A founder pricing this programme should note that the diagnosis cost one suite run on each row and that nothing in the corpus currently makes the tripwire visible from the file it protects.

  **THE SECOND IS WHETHER `RI-35` WANTS A LEG FOR A SENTENCE CARRIED TWICE.** Section 4.3 is outside every existing leg by construction, not by oversight. It is also cheap to state: a registered anchor should occur once in the tree and not once in the file. **A founder should decide whether that is worth a leg**, because the failure it prevents is a check silently watching the wrong copy of its own sentence, which is the defect `RI-35` exists to catch happening inside `RI-35`.

  **THE THIRD IS THE ONE WITH A DATE ON IT.** A repository invariant is currently green because a formatter chose not to wrap a line. Section 6 says exactly which line and exactly what would flip it. **A founder should decide whether to buy the widening**, and should know that the alternative on offer is what this row shipped: a paragraph, four cases, and a request that nobody add a second name to one export statement.

  **AND THE FOURTH IS THE ONE THIS ROW WOULD KEEP IF IT KEPT ONE.** Two derived figures in one register were measured and both were wrong, one of them from the commit that wrote it, through eight waves of readers who each had every reason to trust it. **The corpus's answer to a stale count is to anchor it to a moment, and this row did that.** It is not obviously enough. A figure nothing re-derives is a figure that decays at the rate the tree moves, and this register has no mechanism at all for noticing.
