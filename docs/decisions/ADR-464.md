## ADR-464: the pricing idiom gets a register and a checker, the population is forty-eight and not forty-one, and the entry that derived the forty-one published a command that misses three of them (2026-09-08, status: proposed)

**Row 464 of the 448s wave. Branch `claude/price-register`, off `origin/claude/wave-464s` at `74eb0fd`, session 659. [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) section 10 item 4 taken, second option.**

**THE POPULATION IS FORTY-EIGHT OVER EIGHT ENTRIES AT THIS BASE AND IT WAS DERIVED HERE.** [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) found forty-one over seven at `3f40646a`, and the difference is not the arithmetic it looks like. Seven of the eight are that entry's own seven, because [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) prices things and is now in the tree. The eighth number is the finding: **the derivation command [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) section 2 publishes returns thirty-eight over those seven entries and not forty-one.** Its census table is right and the command printed under it is short by three, because the command requires an asterisk against the label and three of the prices it scored are written unbolded. A register transcribed from either that command or that table would have inherited a hole or a stale total, which is the error this whole wave is about, so this row's checker counts the corpus on every run and pins no number anywhere.

**WHAT LANDS IS A REGISTER PLUS A CHECKER AND NEITHER HALF IS THE WHOLE.** [PRICE_REGISTER.md](PRICE_REGISTER.md) lists every labelled price with an obligation, an accuracy and a basis. [`price-register.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/price-register.mjs) derives the population, the anchors and the paths, compares them against that table in both directions, and **refuses to derive a state.** The division is the whole design and section 4 is the argument for it: a script that claimed to know whether a price is still owed would be inventing the reading [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) spent a row producing.

**THE SHAPE IS THE ONE THE DISPATCH SET AND IT WAS NOT ARGUED AGAINST.** One checker under `packages/tooling/checks/`, on [`write-guard-set.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/write-guard-set.mjs)'s precedent, running through `CI-02` by way of its own suite file. **No `CI-06` letter and no `RI-nn` is minted; gates stay at 33 and invariants at 35.** No workflow file is touched.

### 2. How the population was derived, and the three prices ADR-461's own command cannot see

**THE COMMAND IS STATED SO IT CAN BE DISAGREED WITH.** The derivation is in [`price-register.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/price-register.mjs) and runs on every invocation, but its shell equivalent at this base is:

```
grep -rnoE '(\*\*)?\bPrice( of the [a-z]+)?:' docs/decisions/ADR-*.md
```

with matches inside a backtick code span or a fenced block discarded. **That returns 48 clauses over 8 entries**: [ADR-445](ADR-445.md) 7, [ADR-448](ADR-448.md) 7, [ADR-451](ADR-451.md) 7, [ADR-452](ADR-452.md) 7, [ADR-454](ADR-454.md) 4, [ADR-458](ADR-458.md) 4, [ADR-459](ADR-459.md) 5, [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) 7.

**THE FIRST SEVEN COLUMNS REPRODUCE [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) SECTION 2's TABLE TO THE ENTRY.** That is the cross-check that matters, and it was run before anything was written down: a derivation that agreed on the total and disagreed on the distribution would mean two different populations of the same size.

**WHAT DOES NOT REPRODUCE IS THE COMMAND THAT ENTRY PUBLISHED.** Section 2 gives it as

```
grep -ohE '\*\*?Price[^*]{0,40}?:' docs/decisions/ADR-*.md
```

and `\*\*?` requires at least one asterisk immediately before the label. **Three of the forty-one prices that entry scored are written unbolded and the command cannot see any of them**: [ADR-445](ADR-445.md):139, item 8.3, scored HOLDS; [ADR-448](ADR-448.md):159, item 8.5, scored HOLDS and discharged; and [ADR-458](ADR-458.md):198, item 11.4, scored HOLDS. Run over those seven entries at this base the command returns **38**. All three files are byte-identical between `3f40646a` and this base, checked with `git diff --stat`, so this is not drift: **the census read forty-one clauses and the command under it reads thirty-eight.** The table is the sound half and the command is the one nobody re-ran.

**THIS IS NAMED AND NOT REPAIRED.** [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) is a dated record and it is outside this row's fence. It is recorded here because a later row that takes the published command as the definition will build a smaller population than the census it thinks it is extending.

**THE IDIOM THIS ROW USES IS WIDER BY EXACTLY THAT AND NARROWER IN TWO PLACES.** Wider: bold is not required, because the corpus does not consistently apply it. Narrower: a match inside a code span or a fenced block is a QUOTATION and not a price. That second rule is load-bearing rather than tidy. [ADR-461](ADR-461.md):5 and [ADR-461](ADR-461.md):44 quote the label four times between them while discussing the idiom, this entry quotes it repeatedly, and without the exclusion the population would grow every time somebody wrote about pricing. **The scope is `docs/decisions/ADR-*.md` and nothing else**: a price quoted into [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) by a dispatcher or into a session log by the row that wrote it is a quotation of a price set in an entry, not a second price, and both currently exist.

### 3. The anchor, and why it is a line rather than a label

**A ROW IS KEYED `ADR-NNN:LINE` AND ITS SECTION AND ITEM ARE DERIVED BESIDE IT.** The section is the nearest preceding `### N.` heading; the item is a leading `N.` **on the price's own line** and nowhere else.

**THE SAME-LINE RULE WAS FORCED BY A CASE AND NOT CHOSEN.** A first derivation carried the last numbered item forward across paragraphs and put [ADR-458](ADR-458.md):104 at `6.3`. [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) cites it as **section 6 with no item**, and reading the entry settles it: the clause sits in a paragraph that FOLLOWS the numbered list, opening `**IT IS NOT REPAIRED HERE AND THE REASON IS THE CONSTRAINT, NOT THE APPETITE.**` With the rule corrected, **the derivation reproduces all forty-one of [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) section 3's section-and-item labels, one for one, including that one.** The join between the two was run on `(entry, where)` and every one of the forty-one matched; the only unmatched anchors are [ADR-461](ADR-461.md)'s own seven, which no census has scored.

**THE ANCHOR IS A LINE BECAUSE A DATED RECORD'S LINES DO NOT MOVE.** [ADR-386](ADR-386.md):169 rules that a pointer in a dated record is named rather than repaired, which means a line in an entry that has been written is stable in the way a heading number is not. It also means the register goes red rather than silently wrong if one ever does move, which is the behaviour [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) section 7 item 1 found missing when [ADR-459](ADR-459.md)'s prose pointer `:270` drifted to `:264` with nothing able to see it.

### 4. The state vocabulary, and the line between what a script decides and what a reader does

**[ADR-461](ADR-461.md)'s VERDICT COLUMN IS TWO QUESTIONS IN ONE CELL AND THIS ROW SPLITS THEM.** That entry's table carries `HOLDS`, `HOLDS, discharged`, `WRONG` and `UNCHECKABLE`. `HOLDS, discharged` is a compound because two independent things are being said: the work was taken, and the price described it correctly. They vary independently and the census contains the proof. **[ADR-445](ADR-445.md) 8.1 is WRONG and its work was TAKEN**: the guard that landed is `refuseGeneratedColumn`, in a different place and a different shape from the one priced. **[ADR-445](ADR-445.md) 8.2 is WRONG and half its work is still owed.** One column cannot carry both.

So the register carries two, each with a closed vocabulary and a stated default:

| Column     | Values                                        | Default      | What the default claims |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------- |
| Obligation | `OPEN`, `DISCHARGED`, `WITHDRAWN`             | `OPEN`       | nothing                 |
| Accuracy   | `HOLDS`, `WRONG`, `UNCHECKABLE`, `UNASSESSED` | `UNASSESSED` | nothing                 |

**`UNASSESSED` IS THE STATE THIS ROW ADDS AND IT IS THE ONE WORTH HAVING.** [ADR-461](ADR-461.md)'s four values have no cell for a price nobody has read, because that entry read all forty-one. Seven prices in this tree have never been checked by anybody: [ADR-461](ADR-461.md)'s own. Without `UNASSESSED` the register would either omit them or score them, and both are worse than saying so. **The register's most useful column on the day it lands is the one that says which load-bearing claims are unread.**

**WHAT THE CHECKER DECIDES**, and it decides nothing else:

1. **Leg A, the population.** Every derived price has a row and every row names a derived price, in both directions, with no anchor listed twice. Derivable, and it is the motivating failure: a price nothing can see is the whole of [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) section 7 item 2.
2. **Leg B, the anchors.** Each row's section and item are the ones the entry gives, derived independently on every run. Derivable, and it is what catches a row copied from another entry's table rather than derived at this base.
3. **Leg C, the paths.** Every repo path a price clause links resolves, and where the link label ends `:NNN` the file is at least that long. Derivable. An unresolved path must be declared with a `SETTLED` or `OPEN` reason, on [`write-guard-set.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/write-guard-set.mjs)'s precedent, because a price may legitimately name a file whose creation is the thing it buys.
4. **Leg D, the states.** Every value is in its vocabulary, and every value other than the two defaults carries a basis naming an entry that exists. **This leg checks that a reading was WRITTEN DOWN and cites somebody. It does not check the reading.**

**WHAT ONLY A READER DECIDES.** Whether a price is still owed is a question about work, not about text: [ADR-458](ADR-458.md) 11.1's expression-shaped guard was answered by a `void` call at [`scoped-db.ts:2211`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), which no diff of the clause would reveal. Whether a price describes the tree took [ADR-461](ADR-461.md) a whole row. **A checker that guessed either would be a green check that is not checking anything**, which is the failure `RI-30`'s own header names, and the register would be worth less than the prose it replaced. So the states in [PRICE_REGISTER.md](PRICE_REGISTER.md) for the forty-one are [ADR-461](ADR-461.md)'s verdicts, cited row by row to its section 3, 4, 5 or 6, and the obligations for its ten WRONG prices are read off its section 4 one at a time. **This row ran no second census and claims none.**

**NO TOTAL IS WRITTEN IN THE REGISTER.** The tallies are printed by the checker on every PASS, which is the only place they cannot rot. Section 6 quotes the line.

### 5. What ADR-461 section 10 item 4 asked for, and which half of it landed

The item priced two options and this row took the second, as dispatched:

> **Price: a `CI-06` letter or an `RI-nn` asserting that every labelled price names a path that exists, which is the cheapest useful leg and would have caught nothing in this census, or a register span listing all 41 with their state, which would have caught six of the ten.**

**BOTH LANDED, AND THE FIRST IS FREE ONCE THE SECOND EXISTS.** Leg C is the cheapest useful leg, built as a leg of the register's checker rather than as a `CI-06` letter or an `RI-nn`. That placement is the dispatch's fence decision and its reason is arithmetic rather than taste: rows 462, 463 and 465 are each reporting the gate and invariant counts from their own branches, and a row that moved either count would turn three clean integrations into a reconciliation.

**THE ITEM'S OWN CLAIM ABOUT WHAT A REGISTER WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IS NOT ASSESSED HERE AND ITS ROW SAYS `UNASSESSED`.** _"Would have caught six of the ten"_ is a counterfactual about a control that did not exist, and no state of this tree makes it true or false. Scoring it would be exactly the error this entry is built to make harder.

**ITS OBLIGATION IS `DISCHARGED` AND THE BASIS IS THIS ENTRY.** The register describes the tree it ships in, which is the rule that makes it maintainable, and on this branch the register is in the tree.
