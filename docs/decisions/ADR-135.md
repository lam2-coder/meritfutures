### 3. The ruling on the residue, with both directions priced

> **CLAUSE 2. THE CREDIT IS POSTED. [ADR-067](ADR-067.md) SECTION 7 STANDS UNAMENDED, [`0038`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) IS NOT SUPERSEDED, AND AN ADJUSTMENT AGAINST A `closed` IDENTITY REMAINS PERMITTED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.**
>
> **WHAT IS REFUSED IS THE SILENCE. A CREDIT TO A `closed` IDENTITY IS AN OBLIGATION MERIT CAN RECORD AND CURRENTLY CANNOT PAY, AND THIS ENTRY IS WHERE THAT IS WRITTEN DOWN.**

**Direction A, refuse the credit against `closed`. REFUSED, and the reason is not the price.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it would buy** | No obligation is ever created that no door can discharge. The book never carries a liability with no exit |
| **What it costs** | [ADR-067:179](ADR-067.md)'s own sentence, and it is the right sentence: *"Refusing here would mean the one state in which a trader can least argue is the state in which Merit's error becomes permanent."* A reconciliation error found after closure would be uncorrectable in the direction it was made |
| **The mechanical cost** | A **superseding migration** against a merged money-path file, plus the assertion at [`account-adjustments.test.ts:260`](../../packages/db/test/account-adjustments.test.ts) that `0038`'s executable DDL contains no `identities.status`. **This session has no migration number**, so Direction A is not even writable from here |
| **Why it is refused** | **A refusal to post is not a refusal to owe.** The obligation exists whether or not the row does. Refusing the row removes only the **record** of it, which is the one thing the whole of [ADR-067](ADR-067.md) exists to create, and it hides the liability from [M06:85](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) `P-M6-01` rather than preventing it. **The cautious-looking option is the one that makes the book less true** |

**Direction B, post the credit. TAKEN, and here is what it costs, in full.**

1. **An obligation in Open Liability that no door will pay.** [M06:85](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) `P-M6-01` defines Open Liability to include `sum(wallet_balance_cents)` across identities, so the credit is in the figure the moment it posts, and it stays there.
2. **[M20:65](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-09` is reduced to a promise the door set cannot keep.** *"A wallet balance is payable on demand forever, and dormancy never forfeits it"*, and its enforcement cell calls a forfeiture clause on money already earned **the single most brand-destroying term available to this firm**. For a `closed` identity, `INV-M20-06` is now that clause, arriving through a consistency sweep rather than through a decision. **[ADR-075:40](ADR-075.md) uses that exact argument to refuse moving the affiliate door and does not apply it to the wallet door it moved.**
3. **The trader can see a credit and cannot take it**, which is the precise inversion of the thing Merit sells. The differentiator is that the trader can check the firm's work; a visible, unreachable credit is the firm's work checked and found undeliverable.

**All three are accepted. None is discounted and none is called small.**

> **CLAUSE 3. THE ACCEPTED COST IS BOUNDED BY A FACT ABOUT THE TREE RATHER THAN BY A HOPE, AND THE FACT IS THAT `closed` HAS NO WRITER.**
>
> `closed`'s only inbound edges are `active --> closed` and `restricted --> closed`, both labelled `G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` ([STATE_MACHINES:329](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) and [:330](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)), and **[STATE_MACHINES:353](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) states that `G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` "is named by this machine and is defined nowhere"**. ### 4. The fact nobody has, and this entry declines to invent it

> **CLAUSE 4. THE QUESTION "WHAT DOES MERIT OWE A `closed` IDENTITY" CANNOT BE ANSWERED TODAY, BECAUSE THE CORPUS DOES NOT SAY WHAT CLOSURE IS.**

**[STATE_MACHINES:353](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) records the disagreement in its own words:** *"the corpus does not agree on what it closes: [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) says closure for cause is 'terminal and **per account**', while this drawing routes an **identity** to `closed`. Those are two different acts."*

**The two readings give opposite answers to the residue, which is what makes this a missing fact rather than an unmade choice.**

| If `closed` means | Then a stranded credit is |
|---|---|
| **Termination for cause**, at the end of an enforcement path | Arguably correct, or at least arguable. Merit's own goodwill toward a terminated account is a policy question with two defensible sides |
| **A trader who voluntarily left** | **A forfeiture on money already earned**, which [M20:65](../plans/M20-wallet.md) calls the single most brand-destroying term available to this firm |

**Those are not the same person and one predicate answers for both.** Choosing between them here would be a definition of `G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` inferred from what an adjustment happens to need, which is **one ruling's convenience used as evidence about another**, the defect [STATE_MACHINES:353](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) refuses in that same sentence and routes to an ADR rather than an edit. **This entry does not take it.**

**What happens in the meantime is clause 2, and the meantime is priced rather than assumed empty.** The credit posts, the obligation is visible in Open Liability from the moment it is taken on, and it is unextractable while the identity is `closed`. **That is a decision with a cost and it is stated as one.**
