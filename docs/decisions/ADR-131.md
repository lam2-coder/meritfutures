## ADR-131: the `CI-06<letter>` series is CLOSED at `w`, the last letter is retired UNUSED, and the retirement is a gate rather than a convention  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-065](ADR-065.md) section 5 ruled the successor identifier a slug and closed nothing.** Nine slug gates have been written since that ruling and every one of them took a slug **by discipline alone**: no gate in this runner would have reported a twenty-fourth letter, and the letter table would have accepted the row. That is [ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s finding wearing a convention's name, in the registry ADR-065 was written to retire.

- **Origin:** `OI-17`, session **243**. The number was **RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH and CONDITIONAL** ([ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row 131): an entry is owed only if the retirement turns out to be a decision rather than a rename. **It is a decision, and section 2 is the one it turns on.**

- **NOT MONEY PATH.** No migration, no ledger, no engine and no auth surface is touched. The subject is a naming registry and the artifacts are this entry, one gate, one seed and the rows that record them.

- **This entry does NOT rename any existing gate**, and section 4 rules that out on the record rather than on the cost. `CI-06a` through `CI-06w` keep their names exactly as they are.

---

### 1. The count, recomputed at source rather than taken from the dispatch

The dispatch said one free letter and instructed that the figure be recomputed. It was, from [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) and [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) rather than from any prose:

| Reading | Measured | How |
|---|---|---|
| Letters the runner implements | `a` through `w`, twenty three | every `id: 'CI-06<letter>'` in `gates.mjs` |
| Letters the ALLOCATION table claims | `a` through `w`, twenty three | the letter table's first cells, `a` to `j` as a range plus `k` to `w` |
| Implemented and unclaimed | none | the two sets are equal |
| Claimed and unimplemented | none | the two sets are equal |
| Free in `a` to `z` | `x`, `y`, `z` | three |
| Free inside `nextFreeLetter()`'s scan | `x` | one |

**The dispatch's figure is right and its unit is the thing to be careful about.** [`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs)'s `nextFreeLetter()` scans `a` to `x` and throws `seed anchor exhausted` when all of them are claimed. So the one free letter is **one letter inside the harness's scan**, and `y` and `z` sit outside it by construction.

### 2. The letter is not spendable, which is what makes the first of the three answers unavailable

**`nextFreeLetter()` returns `x`, and the `CI-06p` seed writes a row claiming TWO PAST what it returns.** One seed therefore consumes `free`, `free + 1` and `free + 2`, which is `x`, `y` and `z` today. ADR-065 section 5 already stated the consequence and reserved `x` to the harness on it:

> **There are zero allocatable letters. The next gate to claim one breaks the falsification harness for `CI-06p`**, which is the gate that guards this very table.

**So answer 1, spend the letter on the next gate and retire the series after, is not an option this session may choose.** There is nothing to spend. The letter that looks free is the falsifier's headroom, and a gate that claims it does not take a name, it removes the ability to watch `CI-06p` fail. **Never weaken a gate to pass it** cuts against spending it, and it cuts hardest here because the gate that would be weakened is the one guarding the registry being retired.

**The closure is what makes that headroom permanent, and this is the argument FOR closing today rather than a consolation.** With the series closed at `w`, `nextFreeLetter()` returns `x` on every future tree and the `CI-06p` seed writes `z` on every future tree. That case can no longer go stale. Before the closure it was one reservation row away from dying, and it would have died the way `OI-21` records the `CI-06u` seed dying: silently, on a tree where the gate itself was working.

### 3. The ruling

> **The `CI-06<letter>` series is CLOSED. Its members are `a` through `w` and it has no twenty-fourth. `x`, `y` and `z` are RETIRED UNUSED and belong to `falsify.mjs`. Every gate written from here takes a slug, `CI-06/<subject>`, per [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) section 5. The existing letters keep their names.**

**Answer 2 of the three, and the retirement is of a letter rather than of a name anybody is using.** The registry stops allocating today; the twenty three names it already allocated stay allocated forever, because they are cited in a corpus that cannot be rewritten (section 4).

**The letter table stays in ALLOCATION and stops being an allocator.** It is now a decoder: twenty three rows saying what each spent letter checks, which is the one job a letter identifier cannot do for itself and the reason the table was created. Deleting it would strand every citation in the corpus with nothing to resolve against.

### 4. Why answer 3, renaming the letters to slugs, is refused, and it is refused on the record rather than on the bill

**The bill first, because it is the argument the dispatch asked to have costed.** Measured on this tree with `grep -roE "CI-06[a-w]\b"` over `.md`, `.mjs`, `.ts`, `.yml` and `.json`:

| Reading | Measured |
|---|---|
| Citation sites | 2,563 |
| Files carrying at least one | 283 |
| Of those, session logs | 145 |
| Of those, `docs/decisions/` | 60 |

**That bill alone would not settle it.** This corpus has paid for sweeps before, and 283 files is a long session rather than an impossible one.

**What settles it is that 145 of the 283 are RECORDS, and a rename cannot reach a record without falsifying it.** [ADR-118](ADR-118.md) ruled that a section headed by a date or a session number is a record of a measurement made that day, and `CI-06/derivable-counts` implements the rule as its third exclusion: rewriting such a section **makes it say something it did not say**. A session log reading *"`CI-06n` passed on this tree"* is a statement about a gate that was called `CI-06n` on 2026-08-19. Renaming it to `CI-06/registry-readme-rows` would put a name into the record that did not exist when the measurement was taken.

**So a rename has two endings and both are worse than the letters standing.** Sweep the records and the corpus loses the property it exists to have. Skip the records and the corpus decodes two namespaces forever, with the letter table still needed to resolve the old one, which is the whole cost of the rename bought and none of its benefit.

**A third consideration, smaller and concrete.** Session **244** is live in `gates.mjs` tonight reconciling `CI-06n`'s BODY with its title. A rename of `CI-06n` in this session collides with that work in the same file. It is not the reason for this ruling and it is recorded so a reader does not have to wonder whether it was.

### 5. The mechanism, because a convention nobody enforces is prose

**The deliverable is `CI-06/closed-letter-series`, a gate, and it takes a slug because the ruling it enforces is why slugs exist.**

**The closed set is a WRITTEN STRING in `gates.mjs` and it is never computed**, which is [ADR-074](ADR-074.md) section 2's rule applied to a registry rather than to an identifier series. A gate deriving its closed set from the letters the runner currently implements would admit every new letter **on the commit that added it** and report the series closed at whatever the tree happens to hold. That is a control that cannot fail.

**Four assertions over three inputs, and the three inputs are the three places a letter can be taken.**

| # | Assertion | Why it is the one that catches a claim |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | No `CI-06<letter>` gate id in the runner is outside the closed set | the artifact |
| **2** | No row of ALLOCATION's letter table claims a letter outside the closed set | **the earliest.** [ADR-034](ADR-034.md) rules the claim precedes the artifact, so a reservation row lands before any gate is written |
| **3** | No row of [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md) section 4.4 heads a letter outside the closed set | the document a reader consults first |
| **4** | Every member of the closed set is still claimed by a row | **the other direction**, and it is what keeps the written constant load bearing |

**Assertion 4 is not tidiness.** Three subset assertions over sets that only shrink are all satisfied by a tree that has lost its inputs entirely. Assertion 4 fails on that tree, which is the property `CI-06u`'s register has and a bare subset check does not.

**It is not `CI-06p` and the distinction is worth stating.** `CI-06p` asks whether the three registries **agree with each other**. Agreement cannot express a closure, because a letter claimed in the runner, in the table and in STRATEGY agrees with itself perfectly. This gate asks what they may say **at all**.

**Watched failing before it was claimed to work**, per [STRATEGY section 4](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s own criterion, and the seed is in `falsify.mjs` so it runs on every pull request rather than once. The seed plants **the act the closure forbids**: an allocation row claiming a letter past the end of the series. **Both halves are derived from the runner's gate ids and neither names a letter**, because a seed carrying `x` by literal would stop being a violation the day the series moved and would stop silently.

### 6. What this amends

| Where | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) section 5 | *"it must land before any twenty-fourth gate is written"*, a precondition with no enforcement | landed. The precondition is now a gate and cannot be missed |
| [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s letter table | an allocator with one letter left | **a decoder. CLOSED at `w`**, no further row may be added |
| ALLOCATION's `OI-17` row | **Open** | **Closed** by this entry |
| [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md) section 4.4 | twenty three letter rows and six slug rows | one row added for the closure gate. **No existing row is renamed** |
| ADR-065 section 4's displaced gate | *"recorded as unlettered and it is not lost"* | unchanged, and now correct permanently rather than pending: there is no letter for it because there are no letters |

- **Alternatives considered.**

  - **Spend `x` on the next gate and close the series after.** Refused in section 2 and it is the one that reads reasonable. There is no letter to spend: `x` is `falsify.mjs`'s scan boundary and the `CI-06p` seed needs it plus two.
  - **Rename the twenty three letters to slugs.** Refused in section 4, on the record rather than on the bill. 145 of the 283 citing files are dated or session-headed records, and [ADR-118](ADR-118.md) rules that rewriting one makes it say something it did not say.
  - **Close the series and write no gate.** Refused. It is the state the corpus has been in since ADR-065 section 5, and it produced nine slug gates by discipline plus a table that would still have accepted a twenty fourth letter. A convention nobody enforces is what ADR-042 named.
  - **Extend `nextFreeLetter()` to `z` to buy three more letters.** Refused, and it was already refused by ADR-065 section 5. It buys one gate, removes the harness's headroom, and treats exhaustion as the defect when exhaustion is the symptom.
  - **Delete the letter table now that it allocates nothing.** Refused. 2,563 citations resolve against it, and a decoder is exactly what a one-character identifier needs and cannot supply for itself. It stays and stops growing.
  - **Put the closed set in ALLOCATION and have the gate read it.** Refused, on ADR-074 section 2's reasoning and `CI-06w`'s: a scope read out of the document it polices moves when somebody edits that document. The written constant and the implementation are then the same line and cannot drift apart.

- **Consequences:**

  - **`CI-06p` keeps its falsification case permanently.** With the series closed, `nextFreeLetter()` returns `x` and its seed writes `z` on every future tree. This is the concrete gain and it is the reverse of what spending the letter would have done.
  - **The corpus now has two live gate namespaces and that is the settled end state rather than a transition.** `CI-06<letter>` is closed and historical; `CI-06/<subject>` is open and unbounded. A reader meets both forever, and the letter table is why the first one still decodes.
  - **[STATE](../STATE.md)'s `OI-17` paragraph is NOT edited and that is deliberate twice over.** It sits under `## Session 76: ... (2026-08-20)`, which ADR-118 rules is a record of what session 76 measured that day, and STATE is outside this session's fence. The disposition lives in the `OI-17` register row, which is the allocator.
  - **Two implemented gates still have no STRATEGY section 4.4 row**, `CI-06/table-row-width` and `CI-06/derivable-counts`. `CI-06p` deliberately does not require one, so nothing reports it. It is named here as observed rather than repaired: the rows belong to whoever owns that inventory, and adding rows for other sessions' gates is outside this fence.
  - **No founder approval is claimed.** This entry is `status: proposed` and unsigned. The ruling is enforced rather than trusted: `CI-06/closed-letter-series` passes clean and fails dirty on this branch, and the fail was watched rather than asserted.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Two of the decisions here are judgements rather than derivations. **That the corpus ends with TWO live gate namespaces permanently** (section 4) refuses the rename on the record rather than on the bill: the bill is a long session and not an impossible one, and what settles it is that most of the citing files are dated or session-headed records a rename would falsify. The consequence is that every future reader meets both spellings and the letter table is kept forever as a decoder. Whether that is the right end state, or whether the records should have been swept and the property ADR-118 defends spent once, is a judgement no measurement in section 4 decides. **That the last letters are RETIRED UNUSED** (section 2) spends the registry's remaining headroom on keeping a falsification case alive rather than on a gate, and the argument is that the gate whose seed needs that headroom is the one guarding this very table. The entry calls the first alternative the one that reads reasonable, which is the sentence to read hardest.
