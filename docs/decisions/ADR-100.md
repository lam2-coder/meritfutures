## ADR-100: `derived` is REFUSED where the row carries its own identity column and refused again on a nullable edge, and the refusal is an assertion rather than a sentence  (2026-08-25, status: proposed)

**[Session 202](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-202.md) wrote a `derived` rule on `wallet_entries` through `ledger_transactions` and the estate agreed with it: `pnpm run typecheck` at exit 0 with zero `error TS`, and [`scoped-db.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/scoped-db.test.ts) green at 78 of 78 on that branch and at 101 of 101 on `main`.** The rule terminates at an identity, names a foreign key the DDL declares, and answers **whose ledger accounts appear on this transaction** rather than **whose wallet holds this entry**. The two agree only while no transaction touches two identities' accounts, which nothing in the schema enforces and which double entry makes the ordinary case rather than the exception. **`wallet_entries.identity_id` is `uuid NOT NULL` on the row and a `derived` rule was available anyway.**

**This is the BOLA failure [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) scoped the accessor to bound, arriving through the accessor itself.** [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) already found the shape once in the direction that makes a slice smaller: `affiliate_commissions` fell out of the registry because `DerivedRule.via` is typed `TableKey` and cannot name unregistered `attributions`. **That was the type system refusing a wrong rule. This entry is about the type system permitting one.**

- **Origin:** [session 202](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-202.md)'s two landmines, dispatched as one ruling, session **212**. Session 202 reported both and repaired neither, on the standing rule that a transcription rules nothing.

- **Context:** The registry's vocabulary ([`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) answers exactly one question, **HOW does a row reach an identity**, and its four classes partition the answers. Nothing in it asked **WHETHER `derived` was allowed to be the answer** on a row that already carries its own identity column, and nothing asked whether the hop a rule names is even PRESENT on every row. Both gaps return rows for the wrong identity, or a strict subset of the right one, with no error anywhere. **[ADR-092](ADR-092.md) and [ADR-096](ADR-096.md) both concluded that the direction these fail in is SILENCE**, and both of these fail open.

### 2. The ruling

> **1. `derived` IS UNAVAILABLE WHERE THE ROW ITSELF DECLARES A COLUMN `REFERENCES identities(id)`.** A derivation on such a row can only answer a DIFFERENT question from the one that column already answers, and it answers it by returning rows rather than by raising. The table is registered `owned` on that column, or it is left unregistered.
>
> **2. `derived` MAY NOT TRAVERSE A NULLABLE EDGE.** The column that CARRIES the edge must be declared `NOT NULL` by the DDL. There is no admissible `derived` reading of a nullable edge: the database has declared the relationship optional, and a scope rule may not answer *"whose row is this"* with silence on the rows where it is absent.
>
> **3. `DerivedRule` GAINS NO `nullable` FIELD. `OwnedRule.nullable` IS CHECKED AGAINST THE DDL INSTEAD, FOR THE FIRST TIME.** Section 5 is why, and the short form is that the corpus does not need a second field nothing reads.
>
> **4. THE RULING IS THE ASSERTIONS AND NOT THIS PAGE.** Clauses 1, 2 and 3 exist as five tests in [`scoped-db.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/scoped-db.test.ts), under `a class is REFUSED as well as declared`. **A future session meets this entry as a red test naming its own row, never as a paragraph it was supposed to have read.** [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) ruled that prose is not a control, and section 6 states why the prose alternative was refused here specifically.
>
> **5. THE READER IS THE FOLD AND NEVER THE `CREATE TABLE`.** [ADR-094](ADR-094.md) ruled that a table is read AS OF THE LAST MIGRATION. Every clause above reads the folded column set, and section 7 records what that exposed about the readers that do not.

### 4. What a nullable hop MEANS, which is the question the candidate does not cover

**A nullable hop is a rule whose answer to *"whose row is this"* is `NULL` on some rows, and SQL has exactly one behaviour for that: the row falls out of the predicate.** So the rule does not raise, does not warn, and does not return the row to anybody. **The person who owns it is told it does not exist.**

**That is strictly worse than the firm-parent case the registry already refuses.** A `derived` rule through a `firm` table THROWS at read time -- `plan_version_sizes`' own `why` says so, and `scopePredicate` recurses until the chain terminates or it refuses. **A nullable edge terminates perfectly well. It simply terminates for a subset.**

**The three legitimate `nullable: true` rows in the registry show why the axis belongs to `owned` and not to `derived`.** `ledger_accounts`, `integration_dispatches` and `offers` each carry a NULLABLE identity column, and in every one **the NULL is a declared discriminator with a CHECK constraint behind it**: `offers_identity_scope_matches` makes it biconditional, `ledger_accounts.scope` is CHECKed to `('firm','identity')` and tied to the column, and `integration_dispatches` states the same shape. **The database declares what a NULL MEANS there, and filtering `identity_id = $1` returns exactly the rows the scope names.** A nullable hop has no such declaration: the NULL means the relationship was optional, and nothing says what the row's tenancy is instead.

**So the answer to "does `derived` need a `nullable` field" is no, and the reason is not economy.** A field could only record what the DDL already declares, and the one admissible value would be `false`. **The nullability that matters is read from the migrations by clause 2, which is the primary source, and stating it in the rule as well would put the expectation on the same side as the thing it checks.** That is [session 145](../sessions/2026-08-23-session-145.md)'s failure exactly, quoted by the suite in its own words: *"the render test took its expected column FROM THE RULE and so was asserting the code against itself."*

### 5. `OwnedRule.nullable` is stated by thirty-four rules, read by nothing, and was checked by nothing

**This was found while answering section 4 and it is load bearing, so it is stated rather than folded into the argument.**

- **`scopePredicate` does not read it.** [`scoped-db.ts:84-89`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) says so in its own comment: *"A NULLABLE identity column needs NO second predicate. SQL NULL never equals anything ... Adding `IS NOT NULL` here would look careful and assert nothing."* The `owned` branch is `eq(column, identityId)` whether the flag is `true` or `false`.
- **No assertion compared it to the DDL.** `grep -n nullable packages/db/test/scoped-db.test.ts` returned **zero** before this entry, over 101 tests and 34 declarations.

**So the field was a comment with a type.** Adding a second one to `DerivedRule` would have added a second comment with a type. **Clause 3 goes the other way and makes the existing one checkable**, at the cost of four lines, and it passes on all 34 today with `wallet_dormancy` included -- **the one row whose `NOT NULL` is carried by `PRIMARY KEY` rather than by the words**, which [session 202](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-202.md) recorded as reading its flag off the key. The check reads `PRIMARY KEY` as implying `NOT NULL` for that reason, and section 8 watches the leg being necessary.

### 6. Why the instrument is mechanical, and why a compile-time refusal is IMPOSSIBLE for clause 1

**[CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) is explicit: *"Prefer a new CI gate over a bigger model whenever the error is checkable."* [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) ruled prose is not a control. Section 1 is the evidence in this specific case: [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) already names the `sessions` trap in prose, in its first twenty lines, and the trap is writable.** A ruling that added a ninth paragraph to that header would have been the eighth `why` field's fate in section 3.

**Clause 1's predicate cannot be a type, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of effort.** It reads *"the DDL declares a column `REFERENCES identities(id)` on this table"*, and **TypeScript has no access to the migrations.** The only way to make it a compile error would be to state the fact a second time in TypeScript -- a declared list of which tables carry identity columns -- and that list is:

1. **the exact hazard [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 5 measured on `SQL_NAME` and `DDL_NAMES`**: two hand-kept statements of one fact, where deleting from one left `tsc` at exit 0 and the suite green at 25 tests against a baseline of 26; and
2. **the rule asserted against itself**, since the list would be written by the same session writing the rule.

**This is [ADR-084](ADR-084.md)'s own division of labour and not a departure from it.** Totality is a compile error because `keyof` can see it; drift is an assertion because `tsc` cannot read SQL. **Which class a rule may take is a fact about the DDL, so it lands on the assertion side, and the assertion reads the migrations rather than any restatement of them.**

**The prose alternative is refused. A `docs/edge-cases/` entry is refused for the same reason and is outside this fence besides. A per-table exemption list is foreclosed by section 3: there is nothing to exempt, so a mechanism carrying an exemption facility would be carrying a facility for its own future weakening.**

### 7. The finding this exposed, which is NOT repaired here

**Every identity-column reader in [`scoped-db.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/scoped-db.test.ts) written before this entry stops at the `CREATE TABLE` body, and one assertion's stated claim is therefore FALSE about this tree while the test is green.**

`no firm table carries a column referencing identities, so the class is not hiding one` reads `ddlColumnDefs`, which parses one `CREATE TABLE`. **`admin_actions.on_behalf_of_identity_id uuid NULL REFERENCES identities(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT` is added by `ALTER TABLE` in [`0043`](../../packages/db/migrations/0043_admin_attributed_actions.sql) and is invisible to it.** Folded over all 47 migrations, `admin_actions` is the **only** firm table that reaches `identities` directly -- one of 31 at this entry's base and one of 33 on the merge result -- and it reaches it on a column the assertion cannot see.

**`admin_actions` is CORRECTLY `firm` and nothing about its registration is in question.** Its own `why` argues the point better than the assertion does: `0043` sets that column exactly when `initiative = 'trader_request'`, by a biconditional CHECK, *"so a scoped read would return the actions Merit took FOR a person and silently omit every enforcement action it took AGAINST them."*

**The repair is NOT made here and the omission is deliberate.** Pointing that assertion at the fold turns it RED on `admin_actions`, and the only ways to green are an exemption list or a restatement of what the `firm` class promises -- **a ruling about a different class, made in passing, in the same commit, on the money path, and not the subject the founder's `E2` read is being asked for.** The remedy is one line and is named rather than left to be rediscovered: **the assertion takes `identityColumnsOf(...)`, which this entry adds, and the `firm` claim is restated to permit a NULLABLE identity column with a stated reason.** [ADR-096](ADR-096.md)'s `RI-08` and [ADR-098](ADR-098.md)'s `RI-09` are the precedent for naming a check and leaving it for the session that holds its subject.

**A second, smaller instance is named with it:** the `owned` column assertion at `every owned rule names a column the DDL declares REFERENCES identities(id)` has the same CREATE-only reader. **It is sound on this tree today** -- zero of the 34 `owned` columns is `ALTER`-added, measured -- and it will stop being sound the first time one is.

### 10. Alternatives considered

- **Prose in [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)'s header.** Refused on evidence rather than on principle: the header ALREADY names the `sessions` trap and section 1 seeded that exact trap successfully. Refused again on [ADR-042](ADR-042.md).
- **A `nullable` field on `DerivedRule`.** Refused on section 4 and 5: the one admissible value would be `false`, the field would restate the DDL on the side that is being checked, and the existing field of that shape turned out to be read by nothing. **It would also have edited fourteen table rows in a file three concurrent sessions are transcribing into**, which is a cost paid for a constant.
- **A per-table exemption list.** Foreclosed: section 3 measured zero refusals, so the facility would exist only to be used later.
- **Making clause 1 a compile error.** Impossible without restating the DDL in TypeScript (section 6), and the restatement is [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 5's measured hazard.
- **Refusing `derived` outright wherever ANY nullable column reaches an identity anywhere on the row.** Rejected as over-broad: the clause that matters is about the edge the rule TRAVERSES, and a nullable column the rule does not name says nothing about the rows the rule returns.
- **Leaving it to the founder's `E2` read.** Rejected on [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s own finding: *"Escalating the model does not fix that class of error; reading the source and adding a mechanical assertion does."* Three seeds passed a full green estate, and a reader looking at one rule cannot see what fourteen others do.

### 12. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED. MONEY PATH, and the `E2` line-by-line read is owed on it.** This entry changes what the scoped accessor will REFUSE for every table registered after it, which is a decision about the boundary [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) created the accessor to hold.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Clause 2 refuses a nullable edge **outright**, with no admissible exception, and the cost is stated rather than hidden: **a future table whose only path to an identity is a nullable FK cannot be registered `derived` at all.** It stays unregistered, which is unreachable, which is safe -- and which is also a table no trader can read through the accessor until somebody rules on it per-table, exactly as `identity_links` and `attributions` wait today. **Whether that price is right on the money path, or whether a stated-and-justified nullable hop should be admissible the way a nullable `owned` column is, is a judgement about how much readability this corpus should trade for a refusal that cannot be argued with.**

  **One checkable clause, and it is a COMMAND rather than a COUNT.** Seven signed approval clauses have drifted on their enumeration -- [ADR-077](ADR-077.md), [ADR-078](ADR-078.md), [ADR-083](ADR-083.md), [ADR-093](ADR-093.md), [ADR-095](ADR-095.md), [ADR-096](ADR-096.md), [ADR-097](ADR-097.md) -- and in every one the RULING held while the ENUMERATION did not. **The counts in section 3 are true at `fe05a2e` and WILL go stale as the registry grows from 80 toward 111; nothing here asks a signature to stand behind them.**
