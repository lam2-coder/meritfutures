## ADR-077: there is no 25 hour session and there never was a 23 hour one either in the sense the registry means; the DST hour lands in the WEEKEND GAP, and nothing in the tree computes from any of it  (2026-08-22, status: proposed)

**[ADR-076](ADR-076.md) section 5 recorded that `GS-030`'s and [EC-012](../edge-cases/EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md)'s *"23 hour and 25 hour"* arithmetic is falsified, and deliberately repaired neither**, because both documents are FROZEN and both were outside that session's fence. This entry is the repair. **[Session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) landed the half that IS assertable** as one green test over all six transitions in coverage; what remains is the half the tree refutes, and that is the arithmetic.

- **Context:** three documents recite the clause and **no two of them recite it the same way**, which is the first finding and is why this entry rules them separately rather than as one sentence appearing twice.

  **Every number below was measured by this session against the committed generator**, not inherited from [ADR-076](ADR-076.md) section 5 or from [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md). Both of those are reproduced and both hold; the measurement here is **wider than either** and it turned up one thing neither reported, in section 3.

- **Decision:** the clause is struck and replaced in both registries, **separately**, and the correction is ruled **NOT load bearing** on the evidence in section 5 rather than on the assumption that a wall-clock number could not matter.

## 2. What the tree says: 782 sessions, every one 23 hours, and no 25 anywhere

**The transcribed session rule is the primary source and it is one object.** [`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json)'s `session_rule` reads `"open_ct": "17:00"`, `"open_day_offset": -1`, `"close_ct": "16:00"`, `"timezone": "America/Chicago"`, and its own `_note` says it is [P1 S-E section 3.1](../plans/P1-SE-trading-calendar.md)`:42` verbatim: *"every weekday inside coverage that is not a holiday, 17:00 CT on the prior calendar day to 16:00 CT on the trading day."*

**17:00 CT to 16:00 CT the next day is 23 hours, every day of the year.** It is 23 because it is a 24-hour cycle with a one-hour break, and **not because anything happened to a clock.**

### The measurement, and its one honest limitation stated first

**The committed real source cannot be run and this is worth recording, because it is why every measurement in this corpus has been synthetic.** [`generate.mjs`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) refuses [`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json) with `source-not-transcribed`: the file declares `"status": "awaiting-transcription"` and carries `"holidays": null`. **So no one can measure the real CME calendar today**, this session included, and what follows is the transcribed RULE applied over whole calendar years rather than a statement about what CME published.

Three whole calendar years, `2026-01-01` to `2028-12-31`, run through the committed `build()` with `holidays: []` and `early_closes: []`:

| Session length | Count |
|---|---|
| **23 hours** | **782** |
| 24 hours | **0** |
| **25 hours** | **0** |
| anything else | **0** |

> **782 of 782. There is no 25 hour session. There is no 24 hour session. Every session produced by the transcribed rule is 23 hours, on a transition week and on every other week alike.**

**This reproduces and widens both prior measurements.** [ADR-076](ADR-076.md) section 5 measured **two** transitions and **sixteen** sessions; [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) widened it to **six** transitions and **60** sessions. This is 782 and the answer does not move.

### Why no session's length can change, stated as instants rather than as reasoning

Both US transitions fall at **02:00 CT on a Sunday**. The measured gap each one falls in:

| Transition | Kind | Friday's close | Sunday's reopen |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-03-08` | spring forward | `2026-03-06T22:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2026-03-08T22:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |
| `2026-11-01` | fall back | `2026-10-30T21:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2026-11-01T23:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |
| `2027-03-14` | spring forward | `2027-03-12T22:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2027-03-14T22:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |
| `2027-11-07` | fall back | `2027-11-05T21:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2027-11-07T23:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |
| `2028-03-12` | spring forward | `2028-03-10T22:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2028-03-12T22:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |
| `2028-11-05` | fall back | `2028-11-03T21:00:00Z` (16:00 CT) | `2028-11-05T23:00:00Z` (17:00 CT) |

**02:00 CT Sunday is inside every one of those gaps, and inside no session. The market is shut when the clock moves.** A session's two endpoints are both CT wall times, both on the far side of the transition from each other only in the sense that the gap between them contains it, and **a transition strictly between a close and the next open changes neither endpoint's wall time and therefore changes neither's UTC instant relative to the other.**

**The transition day itself carries no row at all**, which the measurement confirms for all six: it is a Sunday, and [`generate.mjs:990`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) skips Saturday and Sunday because *"the Sunday 17:00 CT open is MONDAY's session opening on the prior calendar day, which is the whole reason `open_day_offset` is -1."*

## 3. WHERE THE HOUR ACTUALLY GOES, which no prior entry measured

**If no session's length changes, the hour has to be somewhere.** It is, it is measurable, and it is **the weekend gap**. Over the same three years, the gap between each session's close and the next session's open:

| Gap | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **1 hour** | 625 | An ordinary weeknight: 16:00 CT close to 17:00 CT open. **Unchanged by DST** |
| **49 hours** | 150 | An ordinary weekend: Friday 16:00 CT to Sunday 17:00 CT |
| **48 hours** | **3** | **The three spring-forward weekends**, and there are exactly three in coverage |
| **50 hours** | **3** | **The three fall-back weekends**, and there are exactly three in coverage |

> **The DST hour is real and it is 48 and 50, not 23 and 25.** It lands in the weekend gap, where the market is shut, and it is the only quantity in this whole area that a transition actually moves.

**This is the sentence the registry was reaching for and missed by a whole quantity.** The row does not say a wrong number about a session; **it says a number about a session when the thing that moved was the gap between sessions.** That is why re-deriving the arithmetic would not have found it: the arithmetic was applied to the wrong subject.

**These two numbers are deliberately NOT written into either registry row**, and section 6 says why: nothing asserts them, and an unasserted number in a frozen registry is the exact defect this entry exists to remove. They are recorded here, where they are measured and cited, and a row may claim them the day a suite executes them.

### One session in this corpus is NOT 23 hours, and it is not a DST artifact

**Stated because "every session is 23 hours" is false as a claim about the calendar and true only as a claim about the ordinary rule.** [ADR-055](ADR-055.md)'s absorbed session opens **before** the holiday it was absorbed from. Run over the generator suite's own committed `ABSORBED` fixture ([`trading-calendar-generator.test.ts:66`](../../packages/db/test/trading-calendar-generator.test.ts)), a Thursday closure absorbed into a Friday that is also an early close:

| Trading day | Open to close | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| `2026-11-25` | `2026-11-24T23:00:00Z` to `2026-11-25T22:00:00Z` | 23 |
| `2026-11-26` | **no session.** A holiday is an absence, not a row | n/a |
| `2026-11-27` | `2026-11-25T23:00:00Z` to `2026-11-27T18:15:00Z` | **43.25** |
| `2026-11-30` | `2026-11-29T23:00:00Z` to `2026-11-30T22:00:00Z` | 23 |

**So session lengths in this corpus DO vary, and they vary on holidays and early closes.** None of that is DST, none of it is 25, and **nothing makes a session an hour longer**: the only mechanism that lengthens one is absorption, which lengthens it by a day and a bit. The precise true statement is **no session length anywhere in this corpus is a function of a DST transition**, and it is stronger than "every session is 23 hours" as well as being correct.

## 4. THE CONSTITUTION CARRIES A DIFFERENT PREMISE AND IT IS ALSO FALSE. This entry records it and does not rule it

**The number is not the constitution's.** [Appendix B4 item 1](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md), at line 264, reads verbatim, em-dash and all:

> `1. DST transition day — session spans clock change; day boundary must follow exchange calendar, not wall clock.`

**"23 hour and 25 hour" appears nowhere in the constitution.** The whole file contains no `23`, no `25` and no `hour` in this sense. **The two numbers were invented in the transcription into the registries**, which is a finding about how the row got here and not merely a curiosity: a reader who assumed the numbers were the founder's would have treated them as unamendable.

**And the constitution's own premise is falsified by the same measurement.** *"Session spans clock change"* is false of the transcribed CME session rule, for section 2's reason: the transition falls in the weekend gap and no session spans it.

> **THIS IS WHY THE ERROR SURVIVED, and it is the one sentence in this entry worth carrying forward.** The constitution's CONSEQUENT is correct and is the entire design: *"day boundary must follow exchange calendar, not wall clock."* Its ANTECEDENT is false. **A false premise supporting a true and independently justified conclusion is invisible to every reader who checks the conclusion**, and every reader checked the conclusion, because the conclusion is the interesting part.

**RECORDED AND NOT RULED.** [`MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) is read-only and moves only through this directory ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)), and it is **outside this session's fence**. This entry does not amend it and does not propose wording for it. What it does is stop a future session from re-deriving the finding: **the antecedent is false, the consequent is right, and correcting the antecedent changes no design.** A follow-up entry may take it; section 8 rows it as owed.

## 5. THE SCOPE QUESTION, asked before the ruling and answered against the tree

**The ruling is one line and the scope is the hard part.** Does correcting this arithmetic change any `INV`, any `R-nn`, or any trading-day count the engine reads? Or is it a statement about wall-clock hours that nothing computes from?

### 5.1 The three rules that cite `GS-030`

| Rule | Its text | Does it read a DURATION |
|---|---|---|
| **`R-01`** [M01:465](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) | *"A fill belongs to the trading day whose session contains its execution timestamp"*, arithmetic column *"Session containment lookup. Never a UTC date cast"* | **No.** Containment is `open <= t < close`. It reads both endpoints and computes no interval. `Computed by` is **Ingest**, outside the engine |
| **`R-05`** [M01:469](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) | *"Session bounds are stored UTC instants derived from CT session definitions"*, arithmetic column *"DST is data. No arithmetic anywhere converts a timezone"* | **No.** It states where the bounds come from and says nothing about the distance between them. `Computed by` is **Calendar data** |
| **`FM-14`** [M01:839](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) | *"DST transition handled by arithmetic instead of data -> a duplicated or missing trading day at the boundary"* | **No. It is a COUNT**, and [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) asserted it green over all six transitions |

**`R-02`** is the only other calendar arithmetic in group A and it is the one a reader worries about: [M01:466](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) reads *"Gap counting is `calendar.sequence` subtraction, never date arithmetic."* **`sequence` is a dense integer index and a wall-clock hour cannot reach it.**

### 5.2 The engine cannot see a session bound at all

[`types.ts:217`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)'s `CalendarDay` carries **four** fields and they are `tradingDay`, `isHalfDay`, `halted` and `sequence`. **There is no open, no close and no duration**, which is the same fact [ADR-059](ADR-059.md) question three settled and [ADR-076](ADR-076.md) section 5 ground one re-used. **`INV-01`** ([M01:112](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)) closes the other door: *"The engine performs no I/O and reads no clock."*

### 5.3 Every site in the tree that reads a session bound, and what each does with it

**The check is a grep and it is bounded**, which is what makes this an answer rather than a belief. Over `*.ts`, `*.mjs` and `*.js` outside `node_modules`, plus the migrations, the reading sites are:

| Site | What it does with the pair |
|---|---|
| [`0004_catalog.sql:338`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) and [`0032:122`](../../packages/db/migrations/0032_trading_calendar_holidays_coverage_revisions.sql) | `session_close_at > session_open_at`. **ORDERING, not duration.** No `CHECK` anywhere bounds the interval |
| [`generate.mjs:1118`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) | the same ordering predicate, re-asserted before a review sees the file |
| [`session.test.ts:99`](../../packages/rithmic/test/session.test.ts) to [`:100`](../../packages/rithmic/test/session.test.ts) | `at >= open` and `at < close`. **CONTAINMENT**, `INV-M2-05`, and it is `R-01`'s shape in M2's simulator |
| [`eod-report.ts:152`](../../packages/rithmic/src/simulator/eod-report.ts) | `parseInstantUtc(session.sessionCloseUtc) + options.deliveryLagSeconds`. **An offset from ONE endpoint.** The other endpoint is not read |
| [`types.ts:57`](../../packages/rithmic/src/simulator/types.ts), [`canonical.ts:175`](../../packages/harness/test/canonical.ts), [`canonical.ts:97`](../../packages/rithmic/test/canonical.ts) | a field declaration and two fixture literals. **No computation** |

> **NOTHING IN THE TREE SUBTRACTS A SESSION OPEN FROM A SESSION CLOSE.** The only expression that puts both endpoints in one comparison is `close > open`, in two migrations and one generator check, and it is an ordering predicate that 23, 25, 43.25 and every other positive length satisfy identically.

### 5.4 The answer

> **NOT LOAD BEARING.** It moves no `INV`: no invariant in the corpus is stated over a session length, and the engine that would have to evaluate one reads no clock and holds no bound. It moves no `R-nn`: the three rules that cite `GS-030` read the endpoints for containment and provenance and none reads the interval. **It moves no trading-day count**, and that is the half [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) already asserted green: one trading day and one row across each of the six transitions, which this correction leaves exactly as it stands.
>
> **It is a statement about wall-clock hours that nothing computes from, and it is being corrected because it is false and because a reader trusted it, not because a rule depends on it.**

**The one thing this entry does NOT clear**, stated because the briefing that produced it is right that a silent consequence is the danger: **correcting `GS-030` changes what pins `R-01` and `R-05`**, since `GS-030` is in both `Pinned by` cells. **The pin survives**, because what is struck is a false predicate and what remains is the count and the UTC-instant claim, which is what a pin on `R-05` should have said all along. **If a reader disagrees with that, the disagreement is about section 6's replacement wording and not about section 5**, and it is cheap to settle before signing rather than after.

## 6. THE RULINGS, taken separately because the two documents say different things

### 6.1 `GS-030`, [`04-gs-030-to-gs-051:7`](../testing/golden-scenarios/04-gs-030-to-gs-051-the-appendix-b4-battery.md)

> **RULED.** *"The 23 hour and 25 hour sessions"* is **struck and kept**, on [ADR-052](ADR-052.md)'s idiom as applied at [M01:1150](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md): *"The struck clause is kept rather than deleted, because the value was ruled on a reason that did not hold and that is worth being able to find."* It is replaced by **"The sessions either side of a DST transition"**, and the row gains the clause that makes it about DST at all: **"and their identical 16:00 CT close resolves to UTC instants an hour apart"**.

**"and one mark" is KEPT and is not silently dropped.** It is true, since a `DailyMark` is one per account per trading day and the trading-day count is one, and **it is UNASSERTED**: [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) records that `DailyMark` carries no instant and `EngineInput` carries no calendar, so the mark half is not this suite's to execute. **Deleting it would narrow a frozen row's obligation under cover of an arithmetic repair**, which is the move this entry exists to make impossible.

### 6.2 `EC-012`, [`EC-012-to-033:7`](../edge-cases/EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md)

> **RULED, and it is a different row.** The same clause is struck and replaced with the same wording. **`EC-012` carries no "and one mark" half and none is added**, because the edge-case registry states decided behavior rather than test coverage and the mark obligation is `GS-030`'s. **Its third clause, *"No `new Date()` arithmetic exists in engine code"*, is untouched and is not part of this repair.**

**The 48 and the 50.** They are section 3's finding, they are true, they are measured, and **nothing executes them.** Writing them in would repeat, in a corrected form, the exact defect being corrected: **a number in a frozen registry that no suite checks and no rule reads.** They live in this entry, cited to the run that produced them, and a row may claim them the day a case asserts them.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **Delete the clause rather than strike it.** Rejected. [ADR-052](ADR-052.md)'s idiom at [M01:1150](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) is that a value ruled on a reason that did not hold is worth being able to find, and **this clause has been recited by four documents**; a reader meeting one of the other three needs to be able to land on the strike and see what happened. A deletion makes the next rediscovery cost what this one cost.
- **Rule the two rows as one, since the clause is "the same sentence".** Rejected in section 1, on the evidence that **it is not the same sentence**: `GS-030` obligates a mark and `EC-012` does not. One ruling would have carried `GS-030`'s obligation into `EC-012` or dropped it from `GS-030`, and neither is a repair anybody asked for.
- **Correct the number rather than the subject: say "the 23 hour sessions" and drop the 25.** Rejected, and it is the tempting one. **23 is right for a reason that has nothing to do with DST**: every CME weekday session is 23 hours, transition week or not, which is why the number survived four transcriptions unchallenged. A row saying "the 23 hour sessions each produce one trading day" would be **true, checkable and still about the wrong thing**, and the next reader would still believe a transition does something to a session.
- **Write the 48 and the 50 into the rows, since they are the corrected arithmetic.** Rejected in section 6.3. The defect was never the value; it was an unasserted number in a frozen document. **Replacing a wrong unasserted number with a right unasserted number leaves the mechanism intact.**
- **Also amend [section 39](../testing/golden-scenarios/39-fixture-status-and-blockers.md)'s blocker cell, which recites the clause a fourth time.** Rejected on the fence: that file is **[session 127](../sessions/README.md)'s** and an edit here would collide with it. What it owes is stated in section 8, and it is more than a re-quote: [session 124](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-124.md) found the cell **understated in one direction and overstated in the other**, since the count half IS now stated in `packages/db/test` and the duration half is not merely unstatable but false.
- **Amend the constitution's B4 item 1, whose premise section 4 falsifies.** Rejected, and it is the rejection to read. The file is read-only and outside this fence, **and the correction is genuinely larger than a wording fix**: the antecedent is false while the consequent it supports is right and independently justified, so an amendment has to decide whether to restate the premise or to delete it, and that is a founder's call rather than an arithmetic one. **Recorded in section 4 and left.**
- **Say nothing, since nothing computes from the number.** Rejected. Section 5's answer is the OUTPUT of the check and not a reason to skip it: the clause is recited by four documents, cited as a `Pinned by` for two money-path rules, and was already the stated reason a registry row sat `blocked`. **A false sentence that nothing computes from is exactly the sentence that stays false longest.**
- **Wait for the real CME source to be transcribed and measure that instead.** Rejected. Section 2 records that [`generate.mjs`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) refuses the committed source on `source-not-transcribed` and that `holidays` is `null`; **the retrieval is not scheduled and the clause is false against the transcribed RULE**, which is the artifact both registries actually describe. Waiting would hold a correct repair behind an unrelated blocker.
