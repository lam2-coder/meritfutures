## ADR-302: three readers of one payout ceiling, one of them wrong on the money in both directions, collapsed onto a single codec in the diff that wrote it  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**The row's first instruction was to re-derive the divergence at source before repairing it. IT WAS RE-DERIVED AND IT HOLDS, and it is LARGER than the dispatch said by one field.** `plan_version_sizes.payout_cap_schedule_cents` was decoded three times in three deployables that cannot import one another, nothing compared them, and `apps/api`'s copy differed from its two peers on the money in both directions: it **ADMITTED a `cap_cents` past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` and handed back the rounded double**, and it **REFUSED the base-10 string [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) ruling 5 blessed** as the only rendering that survives above that ceiling. **The field the dispatch did not name is `from_ordinal`, which the same reader held to `Number.isInteger` where both peers held it to `Number.isSafeInteger`.** Section 2 has all three at `file:line` as they read today.

**THE RULING IS THE COLLAPSE. `decodeCapScheduleCents` ([`packages/rules-engine/src/plan/cap-schedule-codec.ts:218`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/cap-schedule-codec.ts)) states the predicate once and ALL THREE READERS WERE RETIRED IN THE SAME COMMIT THAT ADDED IT.** That is not sequencing hygiene: `ADR-299` section 7 named the alternative in advance, and a codec written without the collapse is a **FOURTH** statement of the payout ceiling, which [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) refused and [ADR-269](ADR-269.md) refused before it for this same value.

**THE COLLAPSE IS NOT SYMMETRIC AND THE ASYMMETRY IS THE POINT.** `toCapScheduleCents` ([`apps/worker`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)) and `decodeCapSteps` ([`apps/site`](../../apps/site/src/catalog/adapter.ts)) are behaviour-preserving deletions to the byte on what they admit and refuse. **`readCapSchedule` ([`apps/api`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) is not**: it gains two refusals it did not have and loses one it should never have had, which is why this row is worth an `E2` read and the other two would not have been.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `302`, session **492**, branch `claude/one-of-three-rounds`. **`ADR-299` section 7 specified this row whole** and the row transcribed it; the two agree and nothing here departs from the entry.

- **MONEY PATH, and the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is OWED.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **NO MIGRATION, NO `RI-nn` MINTED, `packages/db/**` UNTOUCHED, `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` NOT WIDENED, AND THE SIZE ROW NOT TAKEN.** The size row is `ADR-299` ruling 1's and row `303`'s fence. All five are measured in section 10 rather than intended.

- **A SECOND FINDING, IN THE GATE THAT WAS SUPPOSED TO CATCH THIS.** Two of the three per-file needles in link 7's cap-schedule census quoted a line that **exists verbatim a second time in the same file**, in the basis-point reader, so those two would have stayed green on a file whose cents reader had been deleted. Section 7.

### 3. The ruling

**ONE EXPORTED FUNCTION, IN THE ENGINE, WITH ALL THREE CALLERS.**

`decodeCapScheduleCents(value: unknown, at = '$'): readonly SizeCapScheduleStep[]` ([`:218`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/cap-schedule-codec.ts)). It takes `unknown` and means it, reads both keys **by name and none by iteration** (`M01` section 1.4), returns the engine's own type so the day `SizeCapScheduleStep` grows a key it fails to compile rather than silently dropping it, and admits cents on `jsonCents`' terms: a safe-integer JSON number **or** a base-10 string of digits, **refusing anything past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` rather than rounding it**.

**`packages/rules-engine` IS THE ONLY PLACE ALL THREE ARROWS ALREADY POINT.** It declares no workspace dependency at all (`RI-01`), the three deployables cannot import one another, and this is where [ADR-250](ADR-250.md) put `gates-codec.ts` and [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) put `rules-codec.ts` on exactly this argument. [ADR-078](ADR-078.md) is what admits the export rather than what withholds it: the limit *"exists to stop reimplementation"* and *"where withholding causes reimplementation, the limit is being applied against its own purpose"*. **Three implementations existed and one had diverged. Withholding here would not have prevented a decoder; it would have left the defect live.**

**`CapScheduleCodecError` IS THE SECOND EXPORTED NAME AND IT IS NOT A SECOND FUNCTION.** `ADR-299` section 7 says *"one exported function"* and this entry reads that as the count of functions, on `EngineGatesCodecError`'s and `PlanRulesCodecError`'s precedent, both of which ride beside their codec for the reason both record: **a refusal a caller must be able to catch is a refusal a caller must be able to name.** The engine's surface goes from thirty-three names to **thirty-five**, nineteen functions to **twenty**, error classes from seven to **eight**, asserted at `evaluate.golden.test.ts`.

**THE ENCODE DIRECTION IS NOT EXPORTED AND DOES NOT EXIST**, on `rules-codec.ts`'s ruling unchanged: nothing in this repository writes the column from a `SizeCapScheduleStep[]`, and an encoder nobody calls is a second statement of the shape with no caller to keep it honest.

### 5. What a diverging value costs, traced to the trader rather than asserted

**THE COLUMN CAN HOLD EITHER SHAPE TODAY AND NEITHER THE SCHEMA NOR THE WRITE PATH STOPS IT.** [`0004_catalog.sql:168`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) is `payout_cap_schedule_cents jsonb NOT NULL` with **no CHECK on its contents**, and the admin write path checks the value is an array and passes the steps through untouched ([`admin-writes.ts:1767`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)). So both halves of the divergence were reachable rather than theoretical.

**THE STRING HALF IS THE ONE THAT WAS COSTING SOMETHING, AND IT COSTS THE WHOLE PUBLIC CATALOGUE.** A `cap_cents` stored as `"9007199254740993"` is read exactly by `apps/worker` and by `apps/site`, and `readCapSchedule` refused it, so `GET /public/plans` answered **500 for every plan** while the nightly fold ran fine on the same row. After this diff all three read it.

**THE ROUNDING HALF IS NARROWER THAN "a trader is paid against a rounded cap", AND SAYING SO IS THE HONEST VERSION.** The endpoint publishes only the ordinal-1 rung, selected by `capAtFirstOrdinal` ([`:652`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)), and renders it through `centsToJson` ([`:516`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)), which refuses a `bigint` past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` with `CatalogMoneyError`. Every integral double that is not a safe integer is past that bound in absolute value, **so on the ordinal-1 rung a second guard caught the rounded value and answered 500 for a different reason.** On any rung at ordinal two or above, the rounded value was **admitted, carried, and then silently dropped** because that rung is never rendered. **So no rounded cap reached a trader through this endpoint today.** What the defect actually was is a money reader that rounds on the money path, one guard away from serving the result, on a value whose whole purpose is a payout ceiling, with `apps/worker`'s copy of the same predicate refusing it correctly. **`INV-02` and `CLAUDE.md`'s "no floats in financial paths" are the reason it is repaired, and neither of them is conditional on a downstream guard happening to exist.**

### 6. The trap, named in advance and disarmed by ordering

`ADR-299` section 7 and the row both said it: **the codec without the collapse is a fourth statement, and a session that ran out of context after writing it would have made the tree worse.**

**IT WAS DISARMED BY MAKING THE CODEC AND ALL THREE RETIREMENTS ONE COMMIT.** `7cb2f70` adds `cap-schedule-codec.ts`, exports it, and deletes `toCapScheduleCents`, `decodeCapSteps` and `readCapSchedule`, all three call sites repointed. There is no commit on this branch at which the tree holds four statements of this predicate. The tests, which are where a session is most likely to run out of context, landed second, in `a2f685a`, on a tree that was already correct.

### 7. The census's own defect, found while satisfying it

**LINK 7's CAP-SCHEDULE CENSUS HELD THE COUNT AT THREE AND NAMED EACH READER. TWO OF THE THREE NEEDLES WERE NOT UNIQUE TO THE READER THEY NAMED.**

It quoted `from_ordinal: jsonInteger(jsonField(source, 'from_ordinal', where), ...)` for `apps/worker` and `from_ordinal: integer(field(source, 'from_ordinal', at), ...)` for `apps/site`. **Both lines exist VERBATIM a second time in the same two files**, in `toCapScheduleBp` and `decodeCapScheduleBp`, which decode `plan_versions.rules.phase_funded.payout_cap_schedule` in **basis points** and have nothing to do with this column. Deleting the cents reader from either file left its `toContain` green. **This was measured rather than reasoned about**: the first run of the rewritten case failed on the worker's surviving `cap_bp` reader, which is how it was found.

The `'cap_cents'` quoted-key sweep beside them **was** sound and is what actually held the census, so the gate was not blind, only two-thirds redundant. The needles are now `cap_cents` lines, which are unique, and the two basis-point readers are asserted **present** so a later session does not read this collapse as having reached them. **They are ADR-283's to collapse and not this row's.**

### 9. Where this entry is most likely wrong

1. **THE ERROR CLASS.** Section 4 argues the change is unobservable because nothing branches on it. That is a claim about today's callers, verified by reading them; a future handler that wants to distinguish "the row is malformed" from "the response is malformed" now gets one class for both in `apps/site`. The precedent is real and merged, and it is still the judgement in this diff most worth a second opinion.
2. **`from_ordinal` TIGHTENED WITHOUT BEING DISPATCHED.** The row and `ADR-299` name only `cap_cents`. Holding the ordinal to `Number.isSafeInteger` is what both peers already did, so the codec could not have been written both ways without diverging from one of them, but it IS a refusal `apps/api` gains that nobody asked for. It is reported here rather than buried.
3. **"NO ROUNDED CAP REACHED A TRADER."** Section 5's trace is over this endpoint and today's tree. `readCapSchedule`'s output fed `readSize` ([`:1211`](../../apps/api/src/routes/catalog.ts)) and nothing else in this deployable, which was measured; a second consumer added later would have inherited the rounding.
4. **THE CODEC IGNORES UNDECLARED KEYS.** `rules-codec.ts` section 2's ruling, applied to a two-key array where the argument is thinner: this array is `0004`'s in full, so refusing a stray key would arguably be right here where it is wrong there. All three retired readers ignored them, so ignoring is the behaviour-preserving choice, and that is the whole of the reason.

### 12. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.**

This is a **build** row on the **money path**, and unlike a ruling row what is owed is a read of the **diff**, because one of the three legs changes behaviour.

- **Decision:** `plan_version_sizes.payout_cap_schedule_cents` is decoded **once**, by `decodeCapScheduleCents` in `packages/rules-engine`, and all three deployable readers were retired in the commit that added it. A `cap_cents` past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` is **refused rather than rounded** and a base-10 string of digits is **admitted exactly**, everywhere, which is what `apps/worker` and `apps/site` already did and what [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) ruling 5 ruled. `apps/api` gains two refusals and loses one.
- **Consequences:** [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) section 7 item 1 is **CLOSED**. Link 7's cap-schedule census reads **ONE** and its divergence case asserts the repaired property in both directions. The engine's export surface grows by two names. The rules-blob census is untouched at three and is still [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) section 7's row. **Nothing is wired and no wiring count moves.**

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

**THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS SECTION 4's ERROR CLASS**, because it is the only unrequested observable change in the diff and it lands in two deployables that did not have a defect. The precedent is `decodeEngineGates`, merged and unwrapped in both directions since [ADR-250](ADR-250.md), and the alternative was three catch-and-rethrow blocks to keep in sync. **If that trade reads differently to somebody who did not write it, the repair is three small wrappers and no part of the ruling moves.**

**THE SECOND IS SECTION 5's HONESTY ABOUT THE ROUNDING**, which is the part of this entry most likely to be read as a walk-back of the dispatch. It is not: the reader really did admit and round the value, exactly as [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) reported and `ADR-299` ruled. What section 5 adds is that on the ordinal-1 rung `centsToJson` caught the rounded result one step later and answered 500 for a different reason, so the trader-visible harm today was the **base-10 string** half rather than the rounding half. **A reader who thinks that makes the row smaller should weigh it against the string half, which took the entire public catalogue down on a row both peers read fine.**

**THE THIRD IS SECTION 7, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT OUTLIVES THIS ROW.** A census gate whose per-file needles are not unique to the thing they name is a gate that reports a count it cannot actually see. Two of three were in that state, in the file the corpus points at when it wants to know what this deployable can produce. **The repair here is local. Whether the same pattern is load-bearing in the other censuses in that file is a question this row did not have the fence to ask.**
