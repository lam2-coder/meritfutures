## ADR-191: a row that reaches an identity two DIFFERENT ways is scoped by both, and the sixth class is `either`  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) spent thirty-eight lines on one table and named the thing it was not allowed to do about it.** `events` carries [`identity_id uuid NULL REFERENCES identities(id)`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql) at `0017:41` and [`account_id uuid NULL REFERENCES accounts(id)`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql) at `0017:42`, with **no CHECK tying them and neither one required**. All five members of the scope vocabulary were tried against that shape by two sessions and every one is either refused by a mechanical assertion or silently lossy. **This entry adds the sixth: `either`, one nullable identity column of its own beside one nullable foreign key to a row that carries one, with the predicate their DISJUNCTION.** `events` is registered under it and is a member of `ScopedTableKey`, which `pair` is not.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `191`, session **351**, dispatched as `claude/events-sixth-scope-class`. The reservation is amended in place under [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3 rather than answered with a second row. **`ADR-191` was RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH**, which is that file's remedy for the day three sessions took `103` and is why no number was chosen here.

- **Context:** it runs after [ADR-106](ADR-106.md), which is the precedent for what a class costs and whose own closure claim this entry falsifies; after [ADR-101](ADR-101.md), whose two clauses are what refuse the near-miss; and after [ADR-092](ADR-092.md), whose rule that a table is registered by the first session that needs it is why this one waited sixteen sessions for a session with a number.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH BY FILE, and `0058` IS RETURNED TO THE POOL UNSPENT.** No migration is written, no migration is edited, no transaction is opened, no ledger account is named, no `SystemReason` member and no `SqlExecutorReason` member is added, no `pg` is imported and no cast past a key type is written. Section 10 is why the number was not needed, and it is a measurement rather than economy.

- **THE ENTRY SHIPS `proposed` WITH AN UNSIGNED APPROVAL LINE.** What is **derived** is the shape, the split of the trader timeline across the two legs, the eight-table measurement in section 3, and the two candidate constraints in section 10. What is **judged** is clause 5 -- that an `either` table is SERVED by the scoped accessor where a `pair` table is refused -- and section 6's disposal of the payload objection. Section 12 is that question.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every claim below was read at its own file before it was relied on. Two of the dispatch's premises did not survive and they are findings 1 and 2 in section 11.

### 1. THE RULING

> **1. THERE IS A SIXTH SCOPE CLASS AND IT IS `either`.** A row carrying ONE nullable column declared `REFERENCES identities(id)` beside ONE nullable foreign key to a table that itself reaches an identity reaches that identity through EITHER of them, and on any given row through at most one. `EitherRule` names the identity column, the via table, the local and foreign columns and the traversal; both columns are asserted against the DDL and both are asserted NULLABLE.
>
> **2. THE PREDICATE IS THE DISJUNCTION AND NEITHER HALF MAY BE LOST.** `column = $1 OR EXISTS (via WHERE foreign = local AND <via's own predicate>)`. An `owned` rule on the identity column drops every row reached the other way and a `derived` hop drops every row reached the first way; both halves are read on one screen and section 4 is the count.
>
> **3. A ROW REACHING NEITHER LEG BELONGS TO NO IDENTITY AND IS RETURNED TO NOBODY, and it needs no third predicate.** SQL NULL never equals anything, so the equality drops those rows and the `EXISTS` drops them again. **This is the first class in the registry whose `firm` half is a property of the ROW rather than of the table**, and those rows are reached through `systemDb(reason)` like every other firm row.
>
> **4. THE LEGS CARRY NO PRECEDENCE.** Guarding the derived leg with `column IS NULL` would guarantee no row ever reaches two identity uuids, and it is REFUSED: the rows the legs disagree on are the rows a HARD MERGE produces, where the two uuids are one person, and the guard would hide a merged person's account-level history from the survivor. Section 5.
>
> **5. AN `either` TABLE IS A MEMBER OF `ScopedTableKey`, and that is where this class parts from `pair`.** [ADR-106](ADR-106.md) clause 2 excludes a pair table because every row a disjunction returns carries the OTHER party's identity uuid out of a `NOT NULL` column. Here the identity column is this person and the account is this person's, so **no row this predicate returns discloses a second party through a tenancy column**. That ground is ABSENT rather than outweighed.
>
> **6. A DERIVATION CHAIN MAY TERMINATE AT AN `either` TABLE**, unlike at a `pair` or a `firm` one, because an `either` rule CONSTRUCTS a predicate rather than throwing. Section 7 records the two columns this makes newly writable and refuses both on their own ground.
>
> **7. THE ROW'S TENANCY COLUMNS ARE NOT ITS PAYLOAD, AND A SCOPE RULE STATES WHICH ROWS REACH AN IDENTITY AND NOTHING ABOUT WHAT IS INSIDE ONE.** That sentence is `idempotency_keys`' own, out of this same migration set. What is different about `events` is that its `jsonb` can name a THIRD PARTY, and section 6 rules on that difference rather than on the whole objection: the class admits the table, the two event names are named, and the projection that owns them is named with them.

### 2. `events` is registered `either`, and the four other members were tried first

| Member | Against this shape |
|---|---|
| `owned` on `identity_id` | **COMPILES**, renders a correct-looking predicate, binds the identity, and **drops every account-level row in silence.** This is the wrong answer two sessions declined to write and it is the one section 8's assertion now refuses mechanically |
| `derived` through `account_id` | Refused by [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) **clause 1**, because the row carries its own identity column, and again by **clause 2**, because the edge is nullable. Would drop every identity-level row if it could be written |
| `pair` | Needs a SECOND IDENTITY column. This row's second column is an **ACCOUNT**, and both columns are nullable besides, where `pair` asserts both `NOT NULL` |
| `firm` | Refused by the suite's own *"no firm table carries a column referencing identities"*, which is the `firm` class's only promise. Passing it under `firm` means relaxing that assertion, which is weakening a gate to pass it |
| `root` | `identities`' alone |

**The rule that landed**, at [`scope.ts:1262`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts):

```ts
events: {
  class: 'either',
  column: 'identity_id',
  via: 'accounts',
  localColumn: 'account_id',
  foreignColumn: 'id',
  traversal: 'hop',
  why: ...
}
```

`hop` rather than `semi-join` because an event names ONE account and `accounts.id` is that table's primary key, so the traversal cannot multiply the row. The chain terminates one hop out: `accounts` is `owned` on `identity_id`, `nullable: false`.

### 5. Why precedence is refused, which is the alternative that looks strictly safer

**The obvious hardening is to make the row's own column authoritative when it is populated:**

```
identity_id = $1 OR (identity_id IS NULL AND EXISTS (...))
```

That guarantees no row ever reaches two different identity uuids, at the cost of one conjunct. **It is refused, and the reason is a fact about this table rather than a taste.**

`events` is **APPEND-ONLY, no UPDATE, no DELETE, retention forever**, which is [`0017:25`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql)'s own comment. A **hard merge repoints ownership into the surviving `identities` row** -- [`plan_size_unlocks`](../architecture/data-model/plan_size_unlocks.md)' design record says so in those words, and `purchases`' own `why` in the registry says `identity_id` and `user_id` *"CAN DIFFER AFTER A MERGE"*, which is that repointing seen from the other side. `accounts.identity_id` moves with it. **This table's `identity_id` does not, because nothing may update it.**

So an account-level event written before a merge names the **merged** identity on the row and the **survivor** through the account. Under precedence that row is handed to the identity that no longer logs in and hidden from the person who owns the account. **That is `purchases`' own refusal in this registry -- *"a strict subset of a merged person's purchase history, silently"* -- arriving one table over.** Under the plain disjunction it is returned to both, and after a hard merge both ARE one person.

**THE RESIDUAL IS NAMED.** A row whose two legs reach two genuinely different people would be returned to both. The only mechanism in this corpus that re-parents an account is a merge, so the corpus names no way to produce one; nothing enforces that, and it is registered in section 11.

### 6. The payload objection, ruled rather than resurrected and rather than waved

**The objection ran:** a row whose own tenancy column is correct still names a DIFFERENT identity inside `jsonb` -- `kyc.dedupe_hit` carries `matched_identity_id` at [EVENTS:60](../architecture/EVENTS.md) and `identity.merged` carries `merged_identity_id` at [EVENTS:50](../architecture/EVENTS.md) -- and [`INV-M4-06`](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md) at `:46` is that the portal *"never receives another identity's data to filter client side"*. [Session 195](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-195.md) concluded that registering the table *"would make those rows readable and leave the projection as the only control, which is the position [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) scoped the accessor to stop relying on"*.

**The corpus had already ruled the adjacent question the other way, three times, and `scope.ts` demoted the objection on that ground before this session opened.** `idempotency_keys`, registered out of this same migration set, says in its own `why` that *"a scope rule states which ROWS reach an identity and nothing about what is inside one"*. `risk_flags` says *"REGISTERING A TABLE MAKES IT READABLE AND NOTHING ELSE, and this rule answers HOW A ROW REACHES AN IDENTITY rather than who may read it"*. `evidence_packs` says *"REGISTERING THIS TABLE MAKES IT READABLE AND NOTHING ELSE -- the redaction profile is a property of the export and no scope rule enforces it"*.

**What survived is the distinction `scope.ts` itself names, and this entry rules ON it.** `idempotency_keys.response_body` holds THIS person's stored response; an `events` payload can hold a THIRD PARTY's uuid. **That is real and it is not a scope question, for three reasons that were each checked at source:**

1. **THE SET IS TWO NAMES AND IT WAS READ RATHER THAN REMEMBERED.** A sweep of every payload in the catalogue for a field naming a second identity returns exactly `identity.merged` and `kyc.dedupe_hit`, and no third.
2. **NEITHER IS CONSUMED BY `TL`.** `identity.merged` is `FEED, RISK, EVID, ALERT`; `kyc.dedupe_hit` is `RISK, ALERT, EVID`. **The corpus's own catalogue already excludes both from the only trader-facing consumer it defines**, so the surface `INV-M4-06` is about does not receive them by the catalogue's own construction.
3. **THAT EXCLUSION IS A PROJECTION AND IT IS ALREADY WRITTEN DOWN AS ONE.** [M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md) at `:259` says `GET /accounts/:id/timeline` is *"Trader-safe subset only. The portal never receives detector internals or other identities' ids, so it cannot leak them (INV-M4-06)"*, and [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) types `TimelineItem.kind` as *"event_name, trader-safe subset only"*. **The trader read of this table was a projection in the corpus before it was one in this entry.**

**SO THE CLASS ADMITS THE TABLE AND THE OBLIGATION IS PLACED RATHER THAN DISSOLVED.** `scopedDb(identity).rows('events')` is `SELECT *` and would return the payload; the endpoint that serves a trader is the one that must project, and it is named. **What is NOT claimed is that a scope rule enforces it**, which is the sentence three registered tables already carry and the one this entry declines to invent an exception to.

### 7. What registering this table makes WRITABLE, which is ADR-106's shape arriving again

**Two columns in the tree declare `bigint NULL REFERENCES events(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT` inline in their `CREATE TABLE` body**, found by grepping the migrations for `REFERENCES events(` rather than by remembering: `integration_dispatches.event_id` (`0018:89`) and `discord_announcements.event_id` (`0019:252`).

**Before this entry a `derived` rule through `events` COULD NOT BE WRITTEN AT ALL**, because `DerivedRule.via` is typed `TableKey` and `events` was not one. **After it, the rule compiles at every call site.** That is exactly what [ADR-106](ADR-106.md) section 10 reports about `affiliate_commissions` once `attributions` became a key, and it is why the two comments that recorded the type refusal are repaired in this diff rather than left to read as reasons.

**Both are refused, on grounds that never depended on the type:**

- `integration_dispatches` -- *"the event is Merit's own fact and this row is the DISCLOSURE of it, so a chain through `events` would answer a different question from the one the breach and privacy requests ask"*. Its own rule is `owned` on a nullable `identity_id`, which [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) clause 1 makes refuse the derivation outright.
- `discord_announcements` -- **the row is Merit speaking**, `firm`, and the causing event is not its tenancy. The edge is nullable besides, so clause 2 refuses it a second time.

**The `why` on `integration_dispatches` said *"and it is unregistered besides"* and that half is gone.** It is repaired in the same diff, because a reason resting on a fact this entry falsifies is the drift class this package exists to keep to one.

### 8. Eight assertions, and the one with teeth reads the DDL rather than the rule

| Assertion | What it refuses |
|---|---|
| **the shape reader finds the either tables and refuses the ones that merely resemble them** | The **non-vacuity guard**, in both directions at once. It must find the registered member AND it must refuse every table carrying both edges whose identity column is `NOT NULL`. A reader that stopped matching would find nothing on both sides and stay green, which is [ADR-106](ADR-106.md)'s clause-4 hazard on a wider set |
| **a row with a NULLABLE identity column beside an account edge is registered either, never owned** | **THE CLAUSE WITH TEETH.** It reads the migrations and refuses the wrong answer two sessions declined to write. `owned` on `identity_id` compiles, so no type can see this |
| **every either rule names an identity column and a foreign key the DDL declares** | A rule asserted against itself. Both legs are read out of the folded DDL |
| **every either rule stands on TWO nullable columns** | [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) clause 2 **inverted rather than evaded.** A `NOT NULL` identity column means the owned leg already covers every row and the class is `owned`; a `NOT NULL` edge means the same the other way. The class REQUIRES what clause 2 refuses, of both legs, and that requirement is what makes a nullable edge admissible here at all |
| **the either predicate is a DISJUNCTION naming both legs, and binds the handle's identity on each** | A rule that dropped a leg passes every assertion above: the fields would still be right and the DDL would still declare them. This is where the SQL is read, and it pins `or` rather than `and` and `[IDENTITY, IDENTITY]` rather than one binding |
| **the legs carry no precedence** | Clause 4, mechanically. A predicate that grew an `IS NULL` guard is red |
| **an either table has a scoped reading** | Clause 5's runtime half, beside a type-level witness (`const EITHER_KEY_IS_SCOPED: ScopedTableKey = 'events'`) which is the half vitest cannot see at all |
| **an either rule is a TERMINAL for a derivation chain** | Clause 6, asserted on the empty set today so that a session adding the first such rule inherits it |

**Three existing assertions had to widen and each widening is strictly stronger than what it replaced**: the tenancy-column count grew a fourth answer, and two write-side sweeps that read the SINGULAR tenancy column now read the PLURAL, so `events` is checked on both legs where the singular would have read `undefined` and checked nothing.

### 9. THE COUNTEREXAMPLE THIS ENTRY WAS HANDED, ANSWERED RATHER THAN CLOSED

**[`apps/api/src/events.ts`](../../apps/api/src/events.ts) states one in its own header and calls it *"the counterexample any sixth class has to answer"*.** `payout.freeze_expiring`'s catalogue payload is `{ payout_request_id, flag_id, expires_at, lead_hours }`: an event about one trader's frozen payout whose payload names **neither** tenancy column.

**The answer is that the payload and the ROW's tenancy columns are different things, and the corpus already works that way.** EVENTS.md says of `wallet.credited` that *"`wallet_entries` carries no `account_id` of its own, so `account_id` on a payout credit is resolved through `reference_id` at write time and is carried because TL in section 2's legend is a per-account view and would otherwise have to join to find one"*. **A producer resolves the row's tenancy at write time from what it has**, whatever the payload happens to name, and a `payout.freeze_expiring` producer holds a `payout_request_id` that names an account.

**So a row of that event carrying neither column is a PRODUCER defect and not a hole in the class**, and under clause 3 it belongs to no identity and is returned to nobody -- which is the correct reading of what the DDL says about it.

**IT IS THE SHARPEST OPEN ITEM IN THIS ENTRY AND THE REASON IS THAT THE FAILURE IS SILENT.** The row is simply never in the trader's timeline; nothing raises, nothing is logged, and the absence is indistinguishable from the event not having happened. **No scope class can fix that, and section 10 is why no constraint can either.**

### 10. `0058` IS RETURNED TO THE POOL, and the reason is two candidate constraints that were written out and rejected

The reservation took the number conditionally, on the ground that *"a ruling that says what a row's tenancy IS may need the database to say it too"*. It does not, and the alternatives were priced rather than skipped.

| Candidate | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| `CHECK (identity_id IS NOT NULL OR account_id IS NOT NULL)` | **Forbids the firm rows the class exists to admit.** A MID health change and a vendor file load are events about the firm, and clause 3 is that they belong to nobody. This constraint makes them unwritable |
| A trigger asserting `identity_id` agrees with `accounts.identity_id` | **FALSE ON PURPOSE after every hard merge**, which is section 5. `accounts.identity_id` is repointed into the survivor and this table is append-only, so the disagreement is the expected state of every pre-merge row. A probe asserting it would fire on every merged account's history |
| `CHECK (subject_kind NOT IN ('identity','account') OR ...)` | The nearest row-local discriminator, and it **does not catch the actual counterexample**: `payout.freeze_expiring`'s subject is a payout request, which is an object. `subject_kind` carries no CHECK constraining its vocabulary either, so the predicate would depend on an unenumerated set |

**The obligation section 9 names is per-EVENT-NAME and the catalogue is 117 rows.** A `CHECK` over an event-name list puts the catalogue in the database and needs a migration per event; **migrations are sacred and a constraint that has to be superseded whenever a product ships an event is the wrong instrument**, not merely an expensive one.

**So the class is expressible in `packages/db/src` alone and the number returns unspent**, which is what the reservation said it would do before the fact, on [ADR-167](ADR-167.md)'s and [ADR-181](ADR-181.md)'s precedent. `0058` is the next number and the pool is empty. **`0009`, `0027`, `0038`, `0052` through `0057` are byte for byte unchanged, and no migration is edited at all.**

### 12. Alternatives considered

**Refuse `ScopedTableKey` and serve `events` through `systemDb(reason)` alone, exactly as `pair` is served.** Seriously considered, and it is the conservative reading. **Refused because [ADR-106](ADR-106.md) clause 2's ground is absent rather than outweighed**: a pair row's counterparty arrives AS the tenancy column, so it cannot be projected away without projecting away the thing that made the row reachable, and an `events` row's tenancy columns name one person. Taking it would also leave M04's timeline with no door at all -- a request handler is neither `'nightly-batch'` nor `'operator-console'`, which is [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 7's standing hole -- and would pay that cost for a disclosure the tenancy columns do not carry.

**An optional second column on `OwnedRule`.** [ADR-106](ADR-106.md) refused the same shape for `pair` and the reason transfers exactly: it makes the two-path case a variant of the one-path case, so every assertion written for `owned` silently spans both, and section 8's clause-with-teeth **could not be written at all**, because a rule naming one column of two would be a well-formed `owned` rule. **The class is what makes the refusal expressible.**

**Precedence between the legs.** Section 5, refused on the hard-merge case.

**Leave the table unregistered and rule only the class**, which is [ADR-094](ADR-094.md)'s precedent and [ADR-106](ADR-106.md)'s named alternative. **Refused because the cost is bounded and measured here**: the fallout is one registry entry, two cases in one source file, eight new assertions and three widened ones, all of which were executed. And a class with no member fails the suite's own *"every class in the vocabulary has at least one member, so none is vacuous"*, which is the assertion that made both the fifth member and this one impossible to add quietly.

**Take `0058` and constrain the shape.** Section 10, three candidates, all rejected on measurement.

### 13. Consequences

- The registry is **107 of 114**, and **63** are served by `scopedDb`.
- `ScopeClass` has six members and the arithmetic is restated over PATHS: one path (`root`, `owned`, `derived`), two of the same kind (`pair`), two of different kinds (`either`), or none (`firm`). **What a seventh would be is registered as UNMEASURED rather than asserted at zero**, which is the honest difference between this closure claim and the two before it.
- `packages/db` goes **583 to 593 cases**; the suite **5,288 to 5,298 passed / 6 skipped**. **The delta is +10 and only eight of them are written**: registering a table adds its two per-table drift cases, the column-set comparison and the type-and-nullability one, which is `DDL_NAMES` being derived from `TABLE_KEYS` doing its job.
- **`listEvents` and the admin event feed are unblocked and unwritten.** Section 11 item 4.
- **`0058` returns to the pool unspent** and the ALLOCATION row says so.
- **Every `either` row whose two tenancy columns are both NULL is a firm row**, reachable through `systemDb(reason)` and through nothing else, and that is a per-ROW fact the type system cannot express.
