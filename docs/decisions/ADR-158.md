## ADR-158: the wallet surface enters the contract, and every row that disagreed with a plan was written from the CHECK  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 3.3 measured [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) as carrying no wallet surface at all and named the rows it is owed. This entry writes them.** The contract is `approved`, so the change is an ADR and not a commit ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)), which [ADR-153](ADR-153.md) established this week for a single wrong verb. **The reservation was CONDITIONAL on the contract and the schema disagreeing. They disagree in seven places and the largest of them is not on any wallet row**: `payout_requests_hold_is_complete` is a biconditional, so releasing or enforcing a held payout NULLs the whole record of the hold, and the contract had no field for the fact.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `158`, session **269**, dispatched as `claude/p5c-contract`, `P5-c`. **MONEY PATH BY CONTENT AND NOT BY FILE.** This entry writes no code, opens no transaction and claims no migration number; what it specifies is the door cash leaves through. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is owed on the routes these rows produce and is not discharged here.

- **The reservation's premise mis-describes the slice, and the row is amended in place.** It reads *"transcription against a frozen contract mints nothing until the contract and the schema disagree"*, which is the rule for a slice that transcribes the contract INTO code. `P5-c` writes INTO the contract, and a change to an approved document is an ADR unconditionally. [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 8's own `P5-c` fence says so: `docs/decisions/ADR-1NN.md` (new, **REQUIRED**). **The number was always going to be spent** and the conditional was the wrong instrument, which is worth recording because five reservations in this range carry the same wording.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every figure below is a `grep`, a `git show` or a run, quoted at the file it came from, and the two dispatch claims that were checkable are checked in section 6.

### 2. THE RULING

#### Clause 1. The rows go in a `6.n` SUBSECTION and in section 8, and no top-level section is created

Section 6.1's own paragraph is the authority and it was written for this: *"Section 12 is cited by NUMBER ... so inserting a section ahead of it would renumber it and break every one of those citations silently"* ([ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 2). The trader rows are `### 6.2`, under section 6 because [ADR-019](ADR-019.md) made `POST /accounts/:accountId/payout` credit this balance: `GET /payouts` and `GET /wallet` are the two halves of one movement. The operator rows append to section 8, which needs no new number at all.

#### Clause 2. `provenance` is on CREDITS and a debit's meaning is `cause` and `reference_id`

Finding 3. The column is `NOT NULL` on a table that stores debits, and its three members are the credit list by the DDL's own heading. Neither `payout`, `refund_wallet_funded` nor `correction` describes the `LT-06` debit that funds a withdrawal or the `LT-08` debit that funds a purchase, so **every wallet debit in this schema is written carrying a class that does not describe it.**

`WalletEntry` is therefore a union discriminated on `direction`: `WalletCredit` carries `provenance` as the closed three-member union, and `WalletDebit` carries none. What a debit means is `cause`, *"the business event, human readable"*, and `reference_id`, whose own declaration enumerates `purchase` among its referents. **A single optional field would have collapsed the two**, and a client reading a debit labelled `payout` would render a credit class on a withdrawal, which is [ADR-113](ADR-113.md)'s `required_disclosure` failure in the opposite direction.

**The schema half is a finding and is not repaired here.** A direction-conditional CHECK, or a debit vocabulary, is a superseding migration, and [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 7 forbids this slice a migration number. Section 6 records it.

#### Clause 3. `entry_id` is a DECIMAL STRING, and it is the first non-uuid identifier this contract carries

Finding 4. A `bigint` reaching the wire as a JSON `number` is the defect [ADR-122](ADR-122.md) refuses inside the digest, in its own words: it *"would admit a value above `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` that has already lost digits by the time the digest sees it"*, and [ADR-104](ADR-104.md) clause 5 refuses a `number` for money at runtime as well as at compile time. The field is a string of decimal digits, a client must not parse it, and it is also the anchor the opaque cursor is built over.

#### Clause 4. Section 1's idempotency list gains the external leg, because the schema already required it

Finding 5. `wallet_withdrawals.idempotency_key` is `NOT NULL` under a unique `(identity_id, idempotency_key)`, so **a withdrawal without a key is unwritable**, not merely undesirable. A contract sentence that closed the required list at three while adding an endpoint whose table refuses a null key would have shipped a contradiction this entry created. The sentence is amended and states that the schema is the reason.

#### Clause 5. `composition` is NULLABLE at creation, and P-1 is a flag on the withdrawal rather than a hold on the balance

Finding 6. [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) section 3.3's sequence reads request, compose FIFO, provenance check, `LT-06`, which puts the composition at request time. The CHECK puts it at approval and **explicitly permits an empty one at `requested` and `cooling`**. So `composition` is `null` on the common creation response, and a client typing it non-null renders an empty breakdown on the one screen where the breakdown is the point.

**P-1 does not subtract from `withdrawable_cents`.** [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md)'s `P5-g` row names *"withdrawable-now versus held with the rule that holds it (`P-1`, `P-3`)"* and [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) section 3.4 is more precise: P-1 *"routes the withdrawal to review"* and *"spending that value inside Merit is unaffected"*. It is `provenance_review: boolean` on the withdrawal and it is absent from `GET /wallet`'s `holds`, which carry P-3 alone.

#### Clause 6. `available_at` is `null` under `chargeback_window`, and the absence is named rather than filled

Finding 14. P-3 holds payout credits whose funding purchase is still inside the card networks' dispute window, and no landed column carries that window's end for a purchase. `earliest_credit_at` is Merit's own clock on the credit; the networks' clock on the purchase is what P-3 needs, and `affiliate_commissions.chargeback_window_ends_on` is that column existing for the identical rule one rail over. `OQ-M20-02` asks the length and is open; `DEP-M20-03` is the dependency that would make it computable and is unbuilt.

**Adding a chosen number of days to `earliest_credit_at` would be a number this repository invented**, on [ADR-139](ADR-139.md) clause 3's rule that a backend returning plausible values is a fixture serving real traffic. The field states absence. Under [ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s vocabulary it would also be `wall clock` standing in for `rail clock`, which is the substitution `affiliate_commissions` avoided by storing the date.

#### Clause 7. The release and the enforcement carry the hold they just erased, read BEFORE the write

Finding 9, and it is the largest disagreement this entry found. [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) section 4 says `release` *"posts the stored decision unchanged and re-evaluates nothing"*, which reads as though the row keeps its record of the hold. **It does not.** `payout_requests_hold_is_complete` requires all five hold columns to be `NULL` at any status other than `held_pending_review`, so the instant the release succeeds the row stops being able to say it was ever held, and `GET /payouts` correctly returns `hold: null` for it.

So `PayoutReleaseResponse` carries `released_hold` and `PayoutEnforceResponse` carries `enforced_hold`, both read before the write, and the contract states plainly that **the durable record is the [`admin_actions`](../architecture/data-model/admin_actions.md) row's `before`**, which section 8 already requires of every mutating admin endpoint. Without the field the caller performs an act whose justification is unreadable in the same response.

`ordinal_released: true` is on the enforcement response for the same reason: finding 10 makes it a fact of the schema, and the alternative is an operator believing an enforcement burned a rung off a finite ladder.

#### Clause 8. The correction row declares dual control, and the widening is stated rather than hidden

Finding 18. [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) says the endpoint is dual controlled and `C-10` closes the dual-control set at four items naming one endpoint. **The row is written WITH dual control**, because that is the fail-closed direction on an admin write that moves a trader's money and because rule 13 of [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 11 forbids weakening a gate to pass it. **Reconciling `C-10`'s set with this row is owed to an ADR against [SECURITY](../architecture/SECURITY.md) and is not settled here**, which is the shape [ADR-113](ADR-113.md) clause 4 used when it refused to invent a fourth `C-27` action: the difference is that there the plan asked for nothing and here the plan asks for the stronger control.

#### Clause 9. `GET /wallet` on an empty wallet is `0` and not a `404`

[ADR-139](ADR-139.md) clause 6 makes an account with no `rule_states` row ABSENT, because a zero balance beside a zero floor renders as an account sitting on its breach line. **The asymmetry is deliberate and the reason is that here absence means exactly zero**: no credit and no debit has ever been written, `INV-M20-09` makes the balance payable on demand forever, and a `404` on a wallet tells a trader they do not have one.

#### Clause 10. Promotional credit is never a field on any row this entry adds

[`0011`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) header item 3: the loyalty perk is `promotional_credit`, lives in `promotional_credit_grants`, and **must not** be in the wallet's closed provenance list, `ADR-025`'s literal wording having been overruled at the FREEZE gate. A `promotional_credit_cents` field beside `balance_cents` is one client-side addition away from `AS-M20-01`, so the wallet screen composes two reads and no response here mixes two kinds of money.

#### Clause 11. `GET /admin/wallet/reconciliation` reports BOTH comparisons

Finding 12. `INV-M20-10` names the entries-versus-ledger assertion. `0011` built a second one and said so: the stored running balance exists so that a divergence from the recomputed one is a **detectable tamper indication**. An endpoint reporting only the invariant leaves that indicator unread, so the row carries `divergence_cents` and `balance_divergence_cents` as separate figures.

**Float is its own object and never enters a coverage numerator.** [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 5.3 resolves the three-way reading in `P-M6-07`'s favour: float is exposure in the DENOMINATOR and never reserve in the numerator, `AS-M20-08` being the misreading itself. A single combined figure would make the alarm unwritable.

#### Clause 12. The spend limit is an APPEND, and `0` is a writable value that means no spend

Finding 13. The primary key is `(identity_id, effective_from)`, so a new limit is a new effective-dated row and the previous one survives as the record of what was in force when; two writes at one instant collide on the key and the contract returns `conflict`. `effective_from` carries no default, so the caller states it. `set_by` is `NOT NULL` and comes from the admin session, never from the body.

`daily_cents: 0` is admitted by `CHECK (daily_cents >= 0)` and means **no wallet spend at all**, not "no limit". **There is no value meaning unlimited**: the absence of a row is what unlimited looks like, so an endpoint that removes a limit is a different thing from this one and does not exist.

### 4. Alternatives considered

- **A new `## 7. Wallet` section.** Refused by section 6.1's own recorded reason and [ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 2: sections 12 and 13 are cited by number and every citation would break silently.
- **`provenance` on every entry, typed as the closed union.** Refused on clause 2: it puts a credit class on a debit and makes the contract assert something the data cannot mean.
- **`provenance: ... | null` on a debit.** Refused: the column is `NOT NULL` and `null` on the wire would misdescribe the row rather than describe it honestly. The union does both jobs at once.
- **`entry_id` as a `number`.** Refused on clause 3. It is the only shape here that loses data silently.
- **Computing `available_at` from `earliest_credit_at` plus a window.** Refused on clause 6. It is a number this repository would have invented, against an open question that asks for exactly that number.
- **Writing `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/correct` without dual control.** Refused on clause 8: weakening a control because the control's register is stale is the move rule 13 names.
- **Repairing `payout_requests.approved_at` or `GET /payouts` in this change.** Refused. Section 6 records both. `GET /payouts` is an existing row, [ADR-140](ADR-140.md)'s session implemented against it, and moving it is a second ADR against work in flight.
- **Adding a section 11 rate-limit row for `POST /wallet/withdrawals`.** Refused: no approved document states a number and inventing one for the cash door is the same class of error as clause 6's date. It is named as owed in section 5.
- **Amending [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) or [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) to match the schema.** Refused, and it is the tempting one: both are `approved`, so moving a sentence is a second ADR against a second document, and neither sentence is wrong. They are about different moments, which is [ADR-113](ADR-113.md) section 3's finding recurring.

### 6. Findings reported and not repaired, recorded here because the entry is where a reader will look

**1. `payout_requests.approved_at` is `NOT NULL` and section 6 types it nullable.** Finding 15. [ADR-040](ADR-040.md) made `PayoutListItem.approved_at` nullable with the note *"null while held: the hold is PRE-approval"*, and `payout_requests.approved_at` is `timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()` and was never altered. `0031` says explicitly that it kept *"every existing NOT NULL and every existing CHECK on this table intact"*, so a held request carries an `approved_at` equal to its insertion time: **a false approval time on the one state where the trader is being told nothing has been approved.** The route file's `readPayouts` is still a port with no implementation against the real column, so this bites whoever wires it. Repairing it is either a superseding migration or a second edit to `GET /payouts`, and both are outside this fence.

**2. `wallet_entries.provenance` has no member that describes a debit.** Clause 2. The repair is a direction-conditional CHECK or a debit vocabulary, and it is a migration.

**3. `wallet_withdrawals` cannot store the ToS clause `ADR-040` requires a halt to carry.** Finding 7. [ADR-040](ADR-040.md)'s own sentence is *"Both audit-logged with a cited flag and a ToS clause"*, and the external leg has three freeze columns where the internal leg has five hold columns. Today the clause would have to live in `admin_actions` alone, which is a different guarantee from the one `payout_requests_hold_is_complete` gives the internal leg.

**4. `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT` is enforced by the database on the leg that moves no cash and by the application on the leg that does.** Finding 8. [`0010:195`](../../packages/db/migrations/0010_payouts.sql)'s own reason for the unique index is that *"the engine is not the only writer"*, and that reason applies at least as hard to a withdrawal.
