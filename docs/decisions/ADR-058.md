## ADR-058: The calendar source publishes forward-only, so a full forward year never exists and the six-month horizon alarm can never be cleared  (2026-08-18, status: proposed)

**[ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s `OQ-SE-02` is amended a second time. [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) section 5 amended it on its MECHANISM and deliberately left its REASON standing, because the reason is a claim about the publisher and the only thing then available to test it was a paste. Four committed artifacts now test it, and it does not survive. This ruling strikes the reason, strikes the number the reason justified, and recalibrates the alarm the number was paired with.**

- **Context:** `OQ-SE-02` reads, in full: *"current year plus two, horizon alarm at six months"*, and its stated reason is *"`0004`'s own `COMMENT ON TABLE` says 'seeded years ahead, reviewed annually', and **the current year plus two is about as far as CME publishes**, so it is the honest maximum rather than a chosen one."*

  Both halves of that sentence are about a publisher nobody had observed. **Five artifacts have since been retrieved from CME Group's Trading Hours tool and committed to [`packages/db/src/seed/calendars/`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/README.md), and their own date columns settle it.** ADR-055 declined to rule from the paste and was right to; this rules from the artifacts, which is the condition ADR-055 set for reopening it.

---

### 1. The finding, read off the artifact date columns and nothing else

Every artifact in the directory was retrieved on **2026-08-17**. Four are holiday windows. Their column headers, read out of `xl/sharedStrings.xml` rather than off the filenames:

| Artifact | Its own date columns | Window opens, relative to retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| `...-2026-08-16-to-2026-08-18-all-products...` | `2026-08-16`, `2026-08-17`, `2026-08-18` | **minus 1 day**, and the window contains the retrieval date |
| `...-2026-09-06-to-2026-09-08...` | `2026-09-06`, `2026-09-07`, `2026-09-08` | **plus 20 days** |
| `...-2026-11-25-to-2026-11-27-thanksgiving...` | `2026-11-25`, `2026-11-26`, `2026-11-27` | **plus 100 days** |
| `...-2026-12-24-to-2026-12-26-christmas...` | `2026-12-24`, `2026-12-25`, `2026-12-26` | **plus 129 days** |
| `...-2026-12-31-to-2027-01-02-new-year...` | `2026-12-31`, `2027-01-01`, `2027-01-02` | **plus 136 days** |

**Three facts follow from the table and no others are claimed.**

- **F-A. Every holiday window in the directory begins strictly after the retrieval date.** Not one covers a holiday that had already passed.
- **F-B. The furthest date any committed artifact reaches is `2027-01-02`, 138 days after retrieval.** The furthest holiday inside one is `2027-01-01`, at 137 days.
- **F-C. The tool is not literally forward-only, and the qualification matters.** The all-products export reaches back one day, to `2026-08-16`, inside a three-day window that contains the retrieval date. So the picker permits a window straddling today; what no artifact shows is any reach back to a holiday months gone.

**The four exports also carry their own generation stamp**, which is the strongest form of the retrieval claim available: `docProps/core.xml` gives `dcterms:modified` as `2026-08-17T03:29:56Z`, `04:43:22Z`, `04:43:33Z` and `04:44:34Z`. All five share the identical `dcterms:created` of `2020-05-18T15:33:05Z`, which is the export template's and not a retrieval fact. **The all-products file is the one exception and it is stated rather than smoothed over**: its `modified` is `2021-10-13T13:16:40Z`, so that export path does not restamp, and its retrieval date rests on the filename and the founder's record alone.

**What is NOT established here, named because the ruling must not be read as resting on it.** The [work-list paste](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/reference/cme-holiday-page-2026-globex-schedule.pasted-2026-08-17.md) lists the `2026 CME Globex Trading Schedule` as **eleven** rows ending `1 January 2027`, of which exactly these four were still ahead on 2026-08-17 and **seven had passed**. That arithmetic closes, and it agrees with the calendars README and with ADR-055's `FINDING 2`. **Its only source is a file whose own first line reads "NOT AN ARTIFACT."** The count is therefore recorded as context and the ruling below is written so that it holds whether the number is eleven or ten.

---

### 2. What it falsifies

> **"The current year plus two is about as far as CME publishes."**

**Whatever CME publishes, what is RETRIEVABLE is bounded ahead and shrinks daily.** On 2026-08-17 the best reach in the directory is 138 days. Tomorrow it is 137, and the day after 136, because the far end is a **fixed date** (`2027-01-01`) while the near end is today. The reach does not hold steady at two years, or at any duration: it **decays monotonically to the year boundary and resets when the next schedule appears**. It is a sawtooth, and `OQ-SE-02` describes it as a constant.

**A full forward year therefore never exists to transcribe.** The maximum honest `coverage.to` on 2026-08-17, under ADR-055's evidence bound and assuming the holiday list itself had been retrieved that day, is `2027-01-01`: **four and a half months, on the best day of the year for it.** Half a year later the same procedure yields weeks.

**And the seven holidays already passed are not merely untranscribed, they are unreachable.** F-A says no artifact covers one and the tool offered none. So even the *current* year cannot be completed retrospectively from this source. `OQ-SE-02` asks for three years from a tool that will not serve one.

**One half of the sentence does survive and is not disturbed.** `0004`'s `COMMENT ON TABLE` does say "seeded years ahead, reviewed annually". That is Merit's own comment about Merit's own table, and this ruling makes the review cadence real rather than annual. It is quoted here so the next reader does not go looking for it in the struck text.

### 3. Coverage under an incremental source

**[ADR-055](ADR-055.md) already ruled the binding constraint and this ruling adds nothing to it:**

> `coverage.to` may not exceed the last date whose exceptions are transcribed from a committed artifact.

**What section 1 changes is the standing of the number beside it.** ADR-055 wrote "`OQ-SE-02` is amended on its mechanism, not on its number", which leaves "current year plus two" in the corpus as a target the evidence bound has not yet reached. **Section 2 makes it a target that cannot be reached, ever, from this source.** A target nobody can attain and an alarm nobody can clear are the same defect, and the corpus should not carry one in order to spare the other.

> **RULING 1. `OQ-SE-02`'s coverage NUMBER is struck along with its reason. There is no ruled coverage depth. Coverage is exactly what the evidence supports, which is [ADR-055](ADR-055.md)'s bound, and the only planning number is a RETRIEVAL CADENCE.**

**The interaction the brief asks about is this, and it is the whole of it: an evidence bound plus an incremental source produces a coverage horizon that is a consequence of the retrieval schedule and of nothing else.** Under a source that published two years at a time, coverage depth and retrieval cadence are independent, and ruling the first is meaningful. Under this source they are the same variable seen twice. **So the corpus should hold one of them, and it should hold the one Merit controls.** Merit cannot decide how far the exchange publishes. Merit decides when it goes and looks.

**The failure this prevents is concrete and is the one the file already guards against once.** A reader who finds "current year plus two" in a frozen ADR and `coverage.to: null` in the source file has a documented instruction to write `2028-12-31`. `generate.mjs` would refuse it on the evidence bound, and **that reader would experience a correct refusal as a generator defect**, because a frozen ADR told them the value. The calendars README already anticipates the move and answers it with "**Do not raise it to reach `coverage.to`. Lower `coverage.to` instead**". Striking the number removes the instruction rather than adding a second warning about it.

### 4. The horizon alarm is unsatisfiable, which is `FM-17` by name

`OQ-SE-02` pairs the coverage number with an alarm: **coverage running less than six months ahead warns.** It is specified in three merged places, [`0032`](../../packages/db/migrations/0032_trading_calendar_holidays_coverage_revisions.sql) lines 301 and 369, the [`trading_calendar_loads`](../architecture/data-model/trading_calendar_loads.md) model, and an index built for its read.

**Six months is 181 to 184 days. Section 1 F-B measures the source's best reach at 138.**

> **The alarm fires on the best state attainable on the day it was measured, and every state after it is worse until the next schedule is published.**

That is not a threshold set slightly too high. It is a threshold **above the ceiling**, so no retrieval, no transcription, no review and no load can clear it. It fires on a green calendar and on an empty one identically.

**This is [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)'s `FM-17` exactly, and the corpus names the consequence in its own words:** *"the self-audit gets disabled 'temporarily', which is how it dies"*, and one module over, *"the ones most likely to be muted are the noisiest ones, which are usually noisy because they are firing on something real that nobody has had time to fix."* An alarm that fires forever is muted, and what it was also going to say, on the day it mattered, is muted with it.

**ADR-055 is not wrong and its sentence needs one qualification.** It wrote that the alarm "gains its purpose" from the evidence bound: *"At six months against an evidence-bounded horizon it fires when the transcribed calendar actually runs short."* **True of the direction and false of the number.** Against a declared-but-empty two-year tail the alarm could never fire; against an evidence bound it can never STOP firing. **The bound moved the alarm from one unreachable end of its range to the other, and the six was carried across unexamined**, which is the drift `CI-06g` exists for in generated spans and which no gate covers in prose.

### 5. What the alarm means when no action can clear it

**The six was a miscalibration. The thing under it is not, and the tempting repair is the wrong one.**

Lowering six months to thirty days does not fix the class. The source's reach troughs at the year boundary: with the schedule ending `2027-01-01`, a reading taken on `2026-12-20` has twelve days of coverage and **no threshold above twelve clears**. Any constant duration is unsatisfiable at the trough, because Merit does not control the ceiling. **A number cannot be chosen that is both useful in August and attainable in December.**

**So the defect is not the value. It is that ONE alarm answers TWO questions and reports them identically.**

| Question | Clearable by Merit? |
|---|---|
| **Is there evidence available that we have not transcribed?** | **Always.** Go and retrieve it |
| **Is coverage about to run out under the batch?** | **Only if the exchange has published further.** Otherwise by nothing at all |

An operator who mutes the first because it is chronically noisy mutes the second, and the second is `F-4`: *"an exhausted calendar is indistinguishable from an unbroken holiday: every counter quietly stops advancing, no rule fires, nothing breaches, nothing becomes eligible, and nothing raises."*

> **RULING 2. The horizon alarm splits in two, on two keys, and neither threshold is six months.**
>
> **`calendar.retrieval_due`.** Fires when the calendar is due a retrieval under RULING 4. **Cleared by a retrieval and a transcription, always, by definition.** S3, ordinarily suppressible.
>
> **`calendar.coverage_runway`.** Fires when `coverage_end_day` is fewer than the ruled runway ahead of the batch's next trading day. **Not always clearable.** S2.

> **RULING 3. An alarm that no action can clear is not a request for work. It is a COUNTDOWN TO A RULED REFUSAL, and it states the date.**
>
> `F-4` already decided what happens at the end of coverage: **the batch fails closed.** So when `calendar.coverage_runway` cannot be cleared, it is not reporting a fault and it is not asking anyone to try harder. It is reporting that **Merit stops computing marks on a known date**, and the only open question is who decides what to do before then.
>
> Therefore: **the alert text names `coverage_end_day` as a date**, not as a shortfall in days. **It escalates to a named owner rather than repeating**, and the owner's decision is written down. And **a suppression on it may not carry an `expires_at` later than `coverage_end_day`.**

**That last clause is the mechanical part and it follows from a control that already exists.** [`alarm_suppressions`](../architecture/data-model/alarm_suppressions.md) makes `expires_at` mandatory precisely so that, in the corpus's own words, *"'temporarily off' [becomes] a dated fact"* rather than a lie. **Bounding this alarm's mute by the very date it is counting down to is that idea applied once more**: the alarm may be silenced while somebody works the problem, and it cannot be silenced past the day the problem arrives. It needs no new table and no new column, only a predicate on insert.

**It is deliberately NOT added to the four unsuppressible alarms** ([ADR-040](ADR-040.md)). Those four mean Merit cannot safely pay anyone or has stopped paying and nobody is being told. A short calendar with a known end date and a named owner is neither, and the honest limit is already recorded where that set is defined: `alarm_suppressions.alarm_key` carries no CHECK, so nothing in the schema refuses a row muting any of them either.

### 6. The cadence, and why it is per-holiday rather than periodic

**A retrieval cadence needs a publication cadence to match, and the corpus contains exactly one statement of one.** The same work-list paste, which carries the same "NOT AN ARTIFACT" caveat as its holiday count:

> The page also carries a note that CME finalises hours **roughly two weeks before each holiday** and that the schedule is subject to NYSE and SIFMA input.

**Read against the artifacts, that note is the more serious finding of the two in this ruling, and it is not the one the coverage question was about.** The Thanksgiving export was taken **100 days ahead** and it carries exact times: for trade date `2026-11-27`, `12:15 Trade Date: 2026-11-27 (CLOSED)` under Equities and `13:45 ... (CLOSED)` under Energy. **Those are precise, well-formed, transcribable values for a schedule the publisher says is not final for another eighty-six days.**

**There are two horizons and `OQ-SE-02` conflates them.**

| Horizon | What it bounds | Reach on 2026-08-17 |
|---|---|---|
| **Dates** | which days are closures | 137 days, decaying to the year boundary |
| **Hours** | whether `close_ct` and `absorbs_into` are FINAL | roughly 14 days, and provisional beyond it |

**Merit transcribes hours, not dates.** `F-3`'s `close_ct` is the latest close across six products, and [ADR-055](ADR-055.md)'s `absorbs_into` carries a session's explicit bounds. Both are hours. **So the binding horizon is the second one, and it is two weeks.**

**The exposure is one-directional, which is the only reassuring thing about it, and it is `F-3`'s own asymmetry.** A close that moves EARLIER than transcribed leaves the recorded session a **superset** of the real one, and `R-01` only asks whether a fill fell outside, so a superset is safe. A close that moves LATER, a holiday that is REMOVED from the schedule, or an `absorbs_into` that turns out to be an object where `null` was transcribed, each leaves the recorded session a **strict subset**, and a fill in the uncovered part **lands inside no Merit session at all.** That is `R-01`'s condition arriving on ordinary trading, which is precisely the failure [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) exists to close on the open end and `F-3` on the close end. **A provisional value is a third door into the same room.**

> **RULING 4. The calendar carries TWO retrieval obligations, and only one of them is periodic.**
>
> **DISCOVERY, periodic.** Retrieve the holiday page and extend `evidence_to` as far as it then permits. It is what finds next year's schedule and what makes `calendar.retrieval_due` clearable. **Recommended: quarterly, plus one mandatory retrieval in the first half of December**, because section 2's sawtooth troughs at the year boundary and the December pass is the one that either catches the next schedule or proves it is not there yet.
>
> **CONFIRMATION, per holiday.** For every holiday inside coverage, a retrieval inside its finalisation window and before it arrives, whose result is diffed against what is already transcribed. **A DISAGREEMENT IS A CALENDAR CORRECTION**, and [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) already rules what one is: no dependent rows means an ordinary data change, dependent rows means an incident with a prior image and a replay, under [ADR-045](ADR-045.md)'s trigger.
>
> **A periodic cadence alone cannot discharge the second obligation, and this is why it is ruled separately.** A monthly sweep can read a holiday at 20 days out, provisional, and not read it again until 10 days after it has traded. **The confirmation retrieval is scheduled off the calendar's own rows**, so the calendar schedules its own re-verification, and the number of retrievals a year is the number of holidays plus the discovery passes.

**The lead time is a launch candidate, not a structural ruling**, under the standing [parameter-status ruling](gates/parameter-status-launch-candidates-versus-structural-rulings-founder-ruling-2026-08-14.md). **Recommended: 7 trading days before the holiday**, which sits comfortably inside "roughly two weeks" while leaving room for `OQ-SE-04`'s two blind readers, the diff, and a re-run if they disagree. **The structural half is that a confirmation retrieval exists at all and that a disagreement routes to the correction path.** If finalisation turns out to be three weeks rather than two, the number moves and nothing above it does.

**The ruling names its own dependency rather than hiding it.** The two-week figure is read off a document that disclaims being evidence. **The first confirmation retrieval is what tests it**: Labor Day `2026-09-07` is the nearest holiday, its hours were exported on 2026-08-17 at 20 days out, and a re-retrieval inside the window either agrees, in which case the provisional value was stable, or disagrees, in which case this section is worth more than the coverage finding above it. **Either outcome is recorded in the calendars README beside the artifacts**, and it is the cheapest possible test of the premise.

### 7. The production seed is a launch dependency, not a build blocker

**These two facts together decide it:** the seven passed 2026 holidays are unreachable from this source (section 2), and every holiday's hours are provisional until roughly two weeks out (section 6).

> **The production calendar cannot be both COMPLETE and CORRECT at any single moment before launch.** Transcribed early it is provisional. Transcribed late it is short. **There is no session that finishes it.**

That is not a reason to delay anything. It is a reason to stop modelling it as a task with a done state.

> **RULING 5. [P1 S-E3](../plans/P1-SE-trading-calendar.md)'s production seed is reclassified. It is a LAUNCH dependency carrying a standing retrieval obligation, and it is NOT on the build's critical path.**
>
> **What must be true at launch:** coverage reaches past the first trading day by at least the ruled runway, every holiday inside it has been confirmed or is scheduled for confirmation before it arrives, `evidence_to` is honest, and both `calendar.` alarms are wired.
>
> **What must be true to keep building:** nothing. The engine is a pure function of the calendar it is handed and cannot read one for itself, by `types: []`, `merit/engine-purity` and the `RI-01` manifest check. **What the engine's tests read is a separate question and is deliberately not ruled here**; a sibling session holds it.

> **RULING 6. The obligation lives in [CRON_INVENTORY](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md), and it has never had a row there.**
>
> Checked line by line before it was claimed: the scheduled table carries seventeen jobs and none is the calendar; the calendar-work table carries eight items, quarterly and 90-day and annual, and none is the calendar. **The word "calendar" appears in that document only as the name of the section.** So `OQ-SE-02`'s promise that "the annual review joins CRON_INVENTORY" was never kept, and by section 6 an annual review would not have been the right row anyway.
>
> **Discovery is a calendar-work row**, because it has a named owner and a written result and no completion signal a machine can miss. **Confirmation is a SCHEDULED row with a dead-man switch**, because it is derived from data, it recurs on dates nobody chooses, and it is exactly the shape that document's opening rule was written for: *"a scheduled job that stops being scheduled is invisible."*

**One line in `OQ-SE-02` is now stale for a second reason and it is corrected in passing.** It says CRON_INVENTORY "today carries 90-day and quarterly rows and no annual one." **It carries two annual rows**, admin domain renewal and breach-notification contacts. The claim was true when written and the document moved under it, which is the ordinary way a restatement rots and is why the calendar row is ruled by predicate rather than by cadence name.

### 8. Consequences

- **[ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s `OQ-SE-02` section is amended in place** to strike the coverage number and its reason, to replace the six-month alarm with RULING 2's two, and to correct the CRON_INVENTORY sentence. **The ruling is not deleted, it is superseded in place with its original text quoted**, which is how [ADR-052](ADR-052.md) and [ADR-055](ADR-055.md) both handled a reversed reading, and the original reason is what makes section 2 legible.
- **[P1 S-E](../plans/P1-SE-trading-calendar.md) line 255 carries `OQ-SE-02`'s recommendation verbatim** and moves with it. Line 136 restates the alarm and the "no annual one" claim, and moves with it as well. **Both are restatements of the ADR rather than independent sources**, which is `ADR-037`'s class.
- **[CRON_INVENTORY](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md) gains two rows**, per RULING 6. The scheduled row needs a dead-man signal name and a severity; the calendar-work row needs an owner and a written result.
- **[`trading_calendar_loads`](../architecture/data-model/trading_calendar_loads.md)'s `coverage_end_day` row cites the six-month alarm** and is amended to cite RULING 2 instead. **Its index is unaffected and correct**: `(coverage_end_day DESC, coverage_start_day)` serves the runway read whatever the threshold is.
- **[`0032`](../../packages/db/migrations/0032_trading_calendar_holidays_coverage_revisions.sql) is NOT amended.** Lines 301 and 369 name the six-month alarm in `COMMENT` text. **A merged migration is never edited, only superseded** ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md)), and a comment does not justify a superseding migration on its own. **It is recorded as a known stale comment** and is corrected by whatever migration next touches that table, which is the same disposition [ADR-053](ADR-053.md) reached about a constraint it could not edit.
- **[`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json) is NOT touched by this ruling, and nothing is transcribed.** `coverage._note` quotes `OQ-SE-02`'s struck reason as history, which stays correct as history. **`coverage` needs no new field**: `evidence_to` already carries the whole of section 3.
- **No `SD-nn`, no migration number, no `CI-06` letter.** `0038` stays free. **A gate for "an alarm threshold exceeds the maximum value its input can take" is a gate worth having and is not claimed here**, because a letter arrives with a seeded violation and there is no alarm code in the tree to seed one against.
- **`OQ-SE-04`'s two blind readers are unaffected in shape and gain one instruction**, which belongs in the calendars README beside the existing `INCLUDES THE FOLLOWING DATES` and Columbus Day warnings: **record, per holiday, whether its hours were inside the finalisation window on the retrieval date.** A reader cannot be asked to judge whether a value is provisional, but both can state a date, and the diff can compare it.
- **Nothing in the engine, the fixtures or the golden loader changes**, and no fixture is written. This ruling is documents and ops.

- **Alternatives considered.**

  **Lower the alarm to thirty days and change nothing else** (rejected in section 5: unsatisfiable at the year-end trough for the same reason six months is unsatisfiable in August, so it relocates `FM-17` rather than removing it, and it leaves one alarm answering two questions).

  **Make the threshold relative, at "coverage reaches the furthest date the source offers"** (rejected: it is the right idea and the wrong place. Merit cannot evaluate it without a retrieval, so as an alarm predicate it is unevaluatable, and as a retrieval trigger it is RULING 4's discovery pass already).

  **Keep one alarm and give it two severities** (rejected: a suppression applies to the key, so muting the noisy severity mutes the quiet one, which is the exact failure section 5 exists to prevent. Two keys is what makes the bounded suppression in RULING 3 expressible at all).

  **Add `calendar.coverage_runway` to the four unsuppressible alarms** (rejected in section 5: it does not meet that set's stated bar, and the bounded `expires_at` is the proportionate control).

  **Rule the coverage number down to one year rather than striking it** (rejected in section 3: one year is not attainable either, section 2's seven passed holidays are unreachable, and a second unattainable number in the same slot is the first defect with a smaller integer).

  **Rule the two-week finalisation window as fact and set the cadence from it** (rejected in section 6: its only source disclaims being evidence. The obligation is ruled structurally and the lead time is a launch candidate, so the ruling holds if the window turns out to be three weeks).

  **Defer the whole thing until the holiday list is retrieved** (rejected: the retrieval is the very act this ruling schedules, and the alarm defect is legible from artifacts already committed. Deferring would leave a merge-blocking-adjacent control specified at a threshold nothing can satisfy, in three merged places, while the transcription it is waiting on is written).

- **Founder approval: PENDING.** **Unsigned by design rather than by omission.** This amends a frozen ADR's ruling, strikes a founder-ruled number and changes what an operational control means, so it is not taken on a session's authority ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md)). **Recommended for acceptance.**

  **The load-bearing claims were each checked against a primary source before the recommendation was made, per the [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) caution that the reconciliation session's worst errors were failures to check a claim against its source.** The five window tables and the four `dcterms:modified` stamps were read out of the `.xlsx` members themselves, not off the filenames and not off the README's summary of them. `OQ-SE-02`'s text, its six-month alarm and its CRON_INVENTORY sentence were quoted from [ADR-042](ADR-042.md). The absence of a calendar row was checked against [CRON_INVENTORY](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md)'s two tables in full rather than by grep. The two-week finalisation note and the eleven-row schedule are the two claims that could NOT be sourced to an artifact, both are marked as such where they are used, and neither carries a ruling that fails if it is wrong.

  **One correction to the brief this ruling was written from, stated because it changes a number somebody might otherwise set.** The brief describes the source as publishing "~2 weeks ahead" and infers the six-month alarm's defect from that. **The artifacts say the DATES reach 138 days, not 14**, and the two-week figure belongs to the finalisation of HOURS, which is a different horizon in a different document. **The brief's conclusion is right and its premise is a merger of two facts.** It matters because a horizon recalibrated to fourteen days would produce an alarm that almost never fires, which is `FM-17`'s twin and one this desk would have shipped.
