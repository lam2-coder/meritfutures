### 2. THE RULING, part one: `reverses_adjustment_id` is not the linkage, on three independent grounds

Any one of the three is sufficient and they are stated separately because they fail in different directions.

**GROUND 1. IT POINTS AT THE WRONG TABLE.** The column references `account_adjustments(id)`. The *"entry"* the reason is about is a `wallet_entries` row: [`0011:49`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) declares that id `bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY`, [ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 3 puts it on the wire as a decimal string, [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s `evidence_refs` names the reference `kind: 'wallet_entry'`, and [`admin-wallet.ts:1650`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) resolves `corrects_entry_id` against **this identity's own wallet rows** and answers `conflict` when it is not one of them. A pointer between two `account_adjustments` rows cannot hold a `wallet_entries` id whatever it is named, and the types agree: `uuid` against `bigint`, which is the same impossibility [ADR-173](ADR-173.md)'s write A hit at the prompt.

**GROUND 2. IT EXISTS EXACTLY ON DEBITS.** [`0038:220-221`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) is a BICONDITIONAL: `(direction = 'debit') = (reverses_adjustment_id IS NOT NULL)`. **A credit adjustment carrying one is not writable**, and the correction that repairs a trader whose balance is short is a CREDIT. So even at the wrong grain the column is unavailable to the majority of the cases the endpoint exists for, and a design that put the corrected entry there would have made the field mandatory on debits and forbidden on credits.

**GROUND 3. ITS SUBJECT IS A DIFFERENT FACT.** The migration says what the column is for in its own words at [`0038:184`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql), *"THE ONLY DEBIT THAT EXISTS. Header item 2."*, and `assert_adjustment_reversal_is_sound` requires the target to be an `account_adjustments` row at the same identity, the same destination and the same cents. That is **Merit undoing its own prior adjustment**. *"Which entry a correction corrects"* is **an operator's claim about a wallet row Merit did not necessarily write**: the entry being repaired may be a `payout` or a `refund_wallet_funded`, neither of which has an adjustment to reverse. The two facts coincide only in the one case where a correction reverses a correction.

**SO OPTION (b) IS REFUSED.** The reason is not stale in the way the row's cheap reading proposed, and `reverses_adjustment_id` is not *"exactly the linkage"*.

**AND OPTION (a) IS REFUSED TOO, ON THE SAME SOURCES.** The row offered that the reason means a LEDGER ENTRY and needs a grain finer than `ledger_transaction_id` gives, in which case the repair would be to make it say `ledger_entries`. **It does not mean a ledger entry.** The four sources in ground 1 all name a wallet entry, and one of them settles it structurally rather than by wording: [`0009:113-116`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) gives `ledger_entries` a `ledger_account_id` and **no identity column at all**, so the `conflict` check the contract specifies (*"does not belong to this identity"*) is not expressible against that table without the join [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) clause 6 already refuses. The grain is not the problem and `ledger_entries` is not the word.

### 7. The entry is rewritten and not deleted, which is `RI-14`'s rule applied to a case `RI-14` cannot see

**THE CHEAP MOVE WAS AVAILABLE AND IS REFUSED.** The clause could have been struck out in one edit. `RI-14`'s own words are that *"a false sentence deleted leaves nothing for the next reader to check"*, and this one is worse than a false sentence for that purpose: **it is a TRUE sentence**, so a reader who meets it again in six months will verify it, find it correct, and conclude the entry is sound.

So the narrowed entry does four things. It **names all three surviving constraints** in the vocabulary of the CHECK that refuses each, so they can be checked against DDL rather than against a mood. It **quotes the old clause verbatim** under `IT READ:`. It **says the clause WAS TRUE WHEN WRITTEN and is still true**, and states what was wrong instead. And it **names `reverses_adjustment_id` and `0038:185` explicitly**, with the three grounds compressed, so the next reader who finds that column arrives at section 2 rather than at option (b).

**`RI-14` DOES NOT READ THIS DEFECT AND CANNOT BE MADE TO WITHOUT CHANGING WHAT IT IS.** That invariant settles whether a claim that a NAMED thing does not exist is right, by asking the tree what it exports. The clause here names no exported thing, its absence claim is about a COLUMN, and the claim is **TRUE**, so `RI-14` was correct to stay green over it on every run since it landed. **This is the class one step past `RI-14`'s: a true absence claim doing a blocker's job after the blocking was ruled away.** No mechanical check in this tree reads it, and section 9 says what one would have to compare.
