## ADR-173: the correction's reference is the adjustment, the corrected entry is in `admin_actions`, and no column is missing  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[ADR-158](ADR-158.md) wrote `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/correct` from [`0011`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) alone across eighteen findings, and [`0038_account_adjustments.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) is the built door for exactly that endpoint.** [Session 298](../sessions/2026-08-27-session-298.md) found it while building the route and stopped the write rather than guessing a column into being. This entry rules the largest of the four disagreements that session recorded: **the contract says `corrects_entry_id` *"Becomes `reference_id`"* and it does not, cannot, and does not need to.**

**THE RULING IS THAT THE CONTRACT IS WRONG AND NO MIGRATION LANDS.** The record of which entry a correction corrects **already exists**, it is durable under the same grant the wallet itself carries, and it holds strictly more than a column would. `0052` is not taken by this entry and no migration number is claimed.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `173`, session **308**, dispatched as `claude/wallet-correction-column`. **MONEY PATH BY CONTENT.** This entry writes no code, opens no transaction and claims no migration number. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is owed on the route this row produces and is not discharged here.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE, AND NEITHER IS A PRIOR ENTRY.** The dispatch stated its premise and said plainly that a premise nobody re-checked is how `ADR-158` got here. Every line of it is re-derived below from the migration, and then a second time from **a running PostgreSQL 16 with all fifty-one migrations applied forward-only under `ON_ERROR_STOP`**, because watching the database refuse the contract's instruction with the constraint name in the error is a different quality of evidence from reading the constraint.

### 2. THE RULING

#### Clause 1. `corrects_entry_id` does NOT become `reference_id`, and the contract row is repaired here

Findings 1 through 6, and write A. The contract sentence is wrong three separate ways and any one of them is sufficient:

1. **By the trigger.** [`0038:559`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) requires `w.reference_id = NEW.id`. A wallet entry whose `reference_id` is anything but its adjustment's id raises `ADJ-C3` at `COMMIT`.
2. **By the type.** `wallet_entries.id` is `bigint` and `wallet_entries.reference_id` is `uuid`. Write A never reached a constraint.
3. **By the migration's own prose.** [`0038:537`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) states the binding in words and calls the adjustment a FOURTH referent, which is `0011`'s comment being **extended** rather than honoured.

`0011:78`'s *"or the corrected entry"* is therefore a third polymorphic case **that was never writable**, on the same type argument, from the day `0011` merged. It is prose in a comment rather than a constraint, and this entry does not touch it: [`0011`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) is merged and sacred under constitution `E2`.

#### Clause 2. The record EXISTS, and it is the `admin_actions` row. This is ADR-158's own clause 7, applied to the finding ADR-158 missed

The dispatch asked whether a correction's provenance is recorded anywhere else and named the third outcome. **It is, and the instrument is one this corpus has already ruled with.**

[ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 7 found that `payout_requests_hold_is_complete` NULLs all five hold columns the instant a hold is released, and ruled that **the durable record is the `admin_actions` row's `before`**. That is the same instrument, and here it is already installed: [`admin-wallet.ts:1504`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) writes, before the append and under the database's own column names,

```
before.corrected_entry = { id, direction, amount_cents, provenance, cause,
                           balance_after_cents, occurred_at }
before.balance_after_cents = the balance the append is computed against
evidence_refs             = [ { kind: 'second_approver', ... },
                              { kind: 'wallet_entry', ref: corrects_entry_id } ]
```

Write D put that row into the live database and read the corrected entry's id back out of it.

**Three things make this a better record than the column would have been, and the third is the one that decides the ruling.**

- **It is as durable as the wallet entry itself.** [`0026:88`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) revokes `UPDATE, DELETE` from `merit_app` and from `PUBLIC` on a list carrying `admin_actions` ([`0026:92`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql)) and `wallet_entries` ([`0026:101`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql)). Confirmed from the live catalog: `information_schema.role_table_grants` gives `merit_app` exactly `INSERT, SELECT` on `admin_actions`, on `wallet_entries` and on `account_adjustments`. **The audit row cannot be edited or removed by the application role any more than the entry it describes can.**
- **It holds strictly more.** A `corrects_entry_id` column would carry an id. The audit row carries the corrected entry's direction, amount, provenance, cause, running balance and timestamp **as they stood before the correction**, plus the balance the append was computed against, plus the second approver. `INV-M6-01`'s `reason` is `NOT NULL` beside all of it.
- **It records the OPERATOR'S CLAIM, which is the fact worth keeping.** Which entry a correction corrects is not a property of the money. It is what the person who wrote it said they were repairing, and `admin_actions` is the table whose entire subject is what an operator did and why.

#### Clause 3. NO MIGRATION LANDS, and the absent column is a decision `0038` recorded rather than an oversight

The tempting reading of finding 8 is that a column is missing. **It is not missing. It was considered and refused, in writing, in the file that would have carried it**, at [`0038:541`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql):

> *"this assertion is what makes the fourth kind real, and it is why the adjustment row carries no `wallet_entry_id` of its own: the reference points one way and the shared transaction id points the other, so a second FK would only be a cycle to insert around."*

That sentence is about the adjustment-to-entry direction and the structural argument carries to the entry-to-entry one: `wallet_entries` is append-only by grant, the adjustment and its entry are inserted in one transaction, and every additional pointer between them is another edge to order the inserts around. Finding 7 is that consequence measured: **nothing in fifty-one migrations points at a wallet entry, and the wallet's own statement is reached through its adjustment or through its transaction.**

**A migration is the expensive answer and the corpus is explicit about when to prefer the cheap one.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s closing caution says to prefer a new CI gate over a bigger model whenever the error is checkable; the same instinct applies to schema. A superseding migration on the money path costs an `E2` line-by-line read, a number that can never be edited afterwards, and a new nullable `uuid` on `account_adjustments` that could not have a foreign key to `wallet_entries` in any case without inverting the insert order `ADJ-C3` already fixes.

#### Clause 4. `corrects_entry_id` becomes OPTIONAL, because two of the three reasons the database admits correct no entry

Write E, and this is the half the dispatch's two directions did not contain.

[`0038:153`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) closes `reason_code` at `goodwill`, `reconciliation_error` and `promotional_credit`. `account_adjustments_reason_picks_destination` is a biconditional, so `promotional_credit` reaches only the promotional class and never the wallet. **Of the two reasons that reach a wallet, exactly one is about an entry.** A `reconciliation_error` repairs a number Merit got wrong. A `goodwill` credit repairs nothing: [`0038`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)'s own example is *"a goodwill credit for a support failure"*, and it names that case to explain why `account_id` is nullable.

Write E confirmed by execution that such a row lands, and that the wallet entry it produces carries `provenance = 'correction'` because [`0011:71`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)'s closed list offers nothing else and `ADJ-C3` requires that value of every `trader_wallet` adjustment. **So under this schema `correction` is the wallet's word for any adjustment, goodwill included, and `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/correct` is the only door to it.**

A REQUIRED `corrects_entry_id` therefore makes a case the database accepts, and that `0038` built the nullable `account_id` for, unreachable through the only endpoint that reaches it. The field is optional.

**This is the safe direction and the entry says so rather than relying on it.** [ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 3: widening a response later is a decision and narrowing one is a break. A required request field becoming optional widens what the endpoint accepts and breaks no caller that was already sending it.

`0038:122`'s header is the general form of the same point and is worth quoting because it settles what an adjustment is FOR: *"THE TABLE IS NAMED FOR THE OCCASION AND NOT FOR THE POSITION."* **An adjustment answers an occasion, and an entry is at most evidence about one.**

#### Clause 5. The `conflict` code survives, conditioned on the field being present

The row's error line reads `conflict` (`corrects_entry_id` does not belong to this identity), and [`admin-wallet.ts:1460`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) performs that check against the identity's own entries under the lock. **It is a real control and clause 4 does not weaken it:** an operator who names an entry gets it validated, and an operator who names none has nothing to validate. The row states the condition rather than leaving a reader to infer it.

#### Clause 6. THE STOPPED APPEND STAYS STOPPED, and this entry unblocks nothing

Session 298 stopped `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/correct` on **four** disagreements. This entry rules the first. **Three stand, and the third of them cannot be ruled from any document in this tree:**

| # | Session 298's finding | After this entry |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `reference_id` is the adjustment's, not the corrected entry's | **RULED.** Clauses 1 to 4 |
| 2 | A correcting DEBIT is unwritable unless it exactly reverses a prior adjustment CREDIT | **STANDS.** Confirmed by write C, with the constraint name in the error |
| 3 | Dual control is a `dual_control_approvals` row plus `dual_control_threshold_cents`, not a `second_approver` string | **STANDS, AND IS THE HARD ONE.** [`0038:191`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) makes the threshold `bigint NOT NULL CHECK (> 0)` and **nothing in this tree supplies a value.** Picking one is a number this repository invented on the cash door |
| 4 | `reason_code` is `NOT NULL` over a closed three-member vocabulary with no wire field | **STANDS.** Clause 4 reads the vocabulary and does not add the field |

So the append is still unwritable and **[`admin-wallet.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) and its suite are not touched by this branch.** The refusal stays exactly where session 298 put it, and its header keeps naming four constraints because four constraints is what stops it. **Repairing one lie in a contract row does not make a handler writable and this entry does not pretend otherwise.**

### 4. Alternatives considered

- **A superseding `0052` adding `corrects_wallet_entry_id bigint NULL` to `account_adjustments`.** This is the direction the dispatch named as legitimate and it was tested seriously before being refused. It costs an `E2` read and a permanent number; it could carry no foreign key without inverting the insert order `ADJ-C3` fixes; it would hold an id where the audit row already holds the whole entry; and [`0038:541`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) refused the same shape of pointer in writing. **The strongest thing that can be said for it is stated in section 6 item 1 rather than buried here**: a column would make the reference enforceable and the audit row does not.
- **A column on `wallet_entries` instead.** Worse. The table is append-only by grant and `0026` revokes `UPDATE`, so the column would have to be written at insert, which is where `reference_id` already is, and it would make `wallet_entries` self-referential on a `bigint` that [ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 3 will not put on the wire as a number.
- **Widening `reference_id` to hold either kind.** Refused twice over: it is a `uuid` column in a merged migration, and `ADJ-C3` would have to stop asserting the binding that makes a `correction` entry legal at all.
- **Removing `corrects_entry_id` from the request entirely.** The dispatch offered this and it is the cheapest of all. Refused: it would delete the `conflict` check at [`admin-wallet.ts:1460`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts), which catches a mistyped entry id before money moves, and it would leave a `reconciliation_error` correction with no statement of which number was wrong. **Optional keeps the control and admits the goodwill case; removal keeps neither.**
- **Ruling findings 2, 3 and 4 in the same entry.** Refused on `ADR-003` strict and on the dispatch's own fence. Finding 3 additionally cannot be ruled here at all: the threshold has no source in this tree and inventing one is [ADR-139](ADR-139.md) clause 3's refusal on the cash door.
- **Amending [`0011:78`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)'s comment so it stops naming a referent that was never writable.** Refused. The file is merged and sacred (`E2`), the comment is prose rather than a constraint, and [`0038:537`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) already reads it correctly one migration later. Named in section 6.
- **Amending [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md).** Refused. `M20` says the endpoint is dual controlled and says nothing about `reference_id`. Nothing in it is wrong.

### 6. Findings reported and not repaired

**1. THERE IS NO DATABASE-LEVEL GUARANTEE THAT A CORRECTION NAMES THE ENTRY IT CORRECTS, AND THAT IS THE COST OF CLAUSE 3.** The `admin_actions` row is written by the handler, not by a trigger. A future writer who composes an `account_adjustments` row and its wallet entry without an audit row gets a correction whose occasion is `reason_note` alone, and nothing refuses it. **A column would have been enforceable and the audit row is not**, which is the strongest argument for the migration this entry declines. It is stated here rather than in section 4 so a founder reading only the findings still meets it.

The repair, if a founder wants one, is [`0048`](../../packages/db/migrations/0048_audited_writes_on_append_only_tables.sql)'s `rewrite_rule_state` shape rather than a column: a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that takes an `admin_actions.id`, asserts the row exists and carries a non-blank reason, and writes the adjustment, its posting and its wallet entry as one act. That makes the audit row a **precondition** of the correction instead of a companion to it, which is the guarantee the column would have given and more of it. **It is a migration, it is `E2`, and it is not this entry's.**

**2. [`0011:78`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) NAMES A REFERENT THAT WAS NEVER WRITABLE.** *"polymorphic: payout_request, purchase, or the corrected entry"* on a `uuid` column, beside a `bigint` primary key declared earlier in the same `CREATE TABLE`. The third case has been unreachable since the file merged and write A is what that looks like at the prompt. The comment is prose, the file is sacred, and [`0038:537`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) already reads it as extended rather than honoured. **A superseding `COMMENT ON COLUMN` would cost a migration number to repair a comment**, so it is named and left.

**3. `ADJ-C3` MAKES `provenance = 'correction'` MEAN "ANY ADJUSTMENT" AND [`0011:64`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) DEFINES IT AS "A COMPENSATING ENTRY, NEVER AN UPDATE".** Write E is a goodwill credit compensating nothing, carrying that provenance because the closed list offers no other value. The two readings coexist today because the wallet has one adjustment door; they diverge the day anyone asks the statement to distinguish a repair from a gift. **[ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 2 found the same table's provenance column mis-describing its debits and left the schema half as a finding.** This is the third member doing it on the credit side, and the repair is the same instrument: a vocabulary, and a migration.

**4. CLAUSE 4 MAKES THE ROUTE STRICTER THAN THE CONTRACT, AND THE ROUTE IS NOT EDITED.** [`admin-wallet.ts:1407`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) reads `corrects_entry_id` as a REQUIRED text field, so a request omitting it answers `validation_failed` where the repaired row now admits it. **The divergence is real, it is observable today** (validation runs before the port, so it is reached even though the append answers 503) **and it is deliberately left**, on the dispatch's fence: the route is touched only if the ruling makes the stopped APPEND writable, and clause 6 is that it does not. Whoever wires the append must edit this validation regardless, because they are adding `reason_code` and the dual-control threshold to the same body. **A stricter handler refuses requests a looser contract permits, which is the fail-closed direction of the two**, and it is named here so the wiring slice does not have to rediscover it. The one-word patch is written out so it costs them nothing:

```
-      const correctsEntryId = textField(ctx.body, 'corrects_entry_id', errors, true);
+      const correctsEntryId = textField(ctx.body, 'corrects_entry_id', errors, false);
```

**5. THE CONTRACT ROW STILL CARRIES THREE STATEMENTS THE DATABASE REFUSES**, listed in clause 6's table. A reader of the repaired row will find `corrects_entry_id` truthful and `direction: "debit"`, `second_approver` and the absent `reason_code` still wrong. **The row is not made honest by this entry, it is made less dishonest by one field**, and saying so is cheaper than a later session rediscovering the other three.

### 8. Approval

**Approval line: UNSIGNED. Two checkable clauses.**

> **1.** [`0038:559`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql) reads `WHERE w.reference_id = NEW.id`; `git diff origin/main -- packages/db/` is EMPTY on this branch; and `API_CONTRACT`'s `WalletCorrectionRequest` no longer contains the string `Becomes \`reference_id\``.
>
> **2.** Against a database with `0001` through `0051` applied: a `wallet_entries` INSERT whose `reference_id` is the decimal string of another entry's `id` fails with `invalid input syntax for type uuid`; the same correction with the adjustment's `id` commits; and an `account_adjustments` row with `direction = 'debit'` and a NULL `reverses_adjustment_id` fails on `account_adjustments_debit_is_a_reversal`.

**Cost if wrong:** an operator corrects a trader's wallet and the record of which entry they were repairing is a jsonb field in an audit table rather than a column, so a reconciliation six months later is a `->>` probe instead of an index scan, and a handler written without an audit row leaves no record at all (section 6 item 1).

**MONEY PATH BY CONTENT.** This entry moves no money and specifies a door that does. The `E2` line-by-line read is owed on the route this row produces and is not discharged by this signature.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Two of the decisions here are judgements rather than derivations, and the first is the entry's whole subject. **That NO MIGRATION LANDS** (clause 3) rests on the claim that `admin_actions` is a sufficient home for the corrected-entry reference. The derivable half is solid: the audit row is append-only under the same grant as the wallet, it holds more than a column would, and `0038` refused a pointer of the same shape in writing. **The judgement is whether "sufficient record" should mean "enforceable record" on the money path**, and section 6 item 1 states plainly that it does not here and prices the function that would make it so. A founder who reads `E2` as requiring enforcement rather than evidence should reject clause 3 and take `0052` for the `rewrite_rule_state`-shaped function, not for a column. **That `corrects_entry_id` becomes OPTIONAL rather than being removed** (clause 4) is the second: the derivation that it cannot be REQUIRED is executed and firm, but keeping it optional preserves a validation the endpoint performs and an operator claim nobody has asked for, and removing it would be defensible on the ground that a field the schema has no home for should not be on the wire at all. The third thing to weigh is not a judgement but a scope call: **three of session 298's four disagreements are left standing** (clause 6), so this entry makes the contract row less wrong without making it right, and a founder may prefer one entry that takes all four over three more.
