## ADR-212: a citation proves the cited line is PART OF WHAT THE SENTENCE NAMES, so `RI-15` gains an anchor, a vacancy rule and the comma list -- and the window it was built on turns out to move nothing at all  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

It matters more than a tooling slice normally would for one reason the reservation states plainly: **`RI-15` and `RI-16` are what nine sessions have leaned on to validate citation repairs, several of them on the money path, so a check that catches drift by luck has been the warrant for work already merged.**

- **Context:** it runs after the entry that built `RI-15` and after the one that built `RI-16` on its grammar, and it changes the ANSWER those two give rather than their scope. It leaves [ADR-157](ADR-157.md), [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) and every scope-class ruling untouched; nothing here decides what a rule IS, only what a pointer at one has to prove.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH, AND THE DISTINCTION IS BY SUBJECT.** No migration is written or edited, no transaction is opened, no ledger account is named, no `SystemReason` and no `SqlExecutorReason` member is added, no `pg` is imported, no cast past a key type is written, and every merged migration is byte for byte unchanged. **What it touches on the money path is a sentence ABOUT the money path**: two citations in [`routes/payouts.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) pointed at the wrong scope rules for `ledgerTransactions` and `ledgerEntries`, and the reason `PayoutTx` carries no `LedgerTx` was resting on them.

What is **judged** is ruling 1's choice of the anchor over the two stronger rules that were measured and refused, and ruling 5's decision to leave `RI-16` where it is.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every premise was re-derived at its file. **Two did not survive and they are section 2.**

### 1. THE RULING

> **1. A CITATION PROVES THE CITED LINE IS PART OF THE DECLARATION THE SENTENCE NAMES.** Not that a word appears near it. Where a citation binds a name, `RI-15` now asserts, in addition to everything it asserted before, that the cited line is **ANCHORED**: either the cited range **DECLARES** the bound name, or the **ENCLOSING DECLARATION** of the cited line is named among the backticked tokens written beside the citation. `class: 'owned'` inside the `payoutRequests` entry is anchored by a sentence that says `payoutRequests`; the same line cited by a sentence about a different table is not. Section 4.
>
> **2. A POINTER THAT LANDS ON NOTHING PROVES NOTHING, AND IT NEEDS NO NAME TO SAY SO.** A cited line that is blank or a bare closing bracket is a finding on its own, asserted over **every** citation rather than only the named ones. It is the shape a pointer takes when what it named moved down, and three of the wrong `scope.ts` pointers on `origin/main` were exactly it: `:1307` and `:909` are both `},`, closing some other entry. Section 5.
>
> **3. A COMMA LIST IS N CITATIONS AND WAS ZERO.** `` `packages/db/src/scope.ts:644,649` `` matched no tail expression, so the whole token fell through as prose and **neither number was ever resolved, including the first one, which on its own would have**. It is now read as one citation per number, each answered separately. Section 6.
>
> **4. A POINTER QUOTED AS HISTORY MUST NOT WEAR CITATION GRAMMAR.** A `file:line` pointer is a claim about THIS tree and every reader follows it as one. Two live source files record pointers that WERE false, spelled as live citations; they are rewritten out of the grammar rather than exempted, because a register entry for them would say "this citation is allowed to be wrong" and the truth is that it is not a citation. Section 7.
>
> **5. THE WINDOW IS NOT THE DEFECT AND IS LEFT WHERE IT IS.** `CITATION_WINDOW` stays at 2. Narrowing it to 0 moves **no site on this tree** and catches **none** of the five, which is derived in section 3 and is the single most surprising thing this session measured. A parameter that changes nothing is not a fix, and tightening it would have read as one.
>
> **6. `RI-16` KEEPS THE OLD ANSWER FOR NOW, ON A MEASUREMENT AND NOT ON A PREFERENCE.** It shares the grammar, so it gains ruling 3 automatically. It does **not** gain rulings 1 and 2, because over `docs/` those two raise **46** findings where 2 stand today, 40-odd of them inside documents whose frontmatter reads `status: approved`, where a change is an ADR rather than a commit. That is a slice, not a line. Section 8.
>
> **7. THIS IS NOT A NEW INVARIANT AND THE NEXT FREE NUMBER IS RETURNED UNSPENT.** `RI-01` through `RI-19` are all taken -- derived by reading `id:` out of the three check files and `RI-17` out of [`api-contract-coverage.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/api-contract-coverage.test.ts) -- so `RI-20` was available and is **NOT TAKEN**. Everything here is the property `RI-15` already claimed, made honest. A second invariant asserting a sharper version of the first one's sentence is the two-copies defect, and this file has refused it before.

### 4. THE ANCHOR, AND WHY THE SUBJECT IS NOT THE NEAREST TOKEN

**THE READER BINDS THE NEAREST BACKTICKED TOKEN, AND IN THIS CORPUS THE NEAREST TOKEN IS THE PREDICATE.** *"`payoutRequests` is `owned` (`scope.ts:1056`)"* binds `owned`. The subject -- `payoutRequests`, the one token in that sentence a runner could actually check the cited line against -- is the token the reader drops, because it is one token further away. `scope.ts` holds 112 entries and 41 lines reading `class: 'owned',`, so a two-line window around any of them is satisfied by all of them.

**THE ANCHOR READS EVERY BACKTICKED TOKEN IN THE SENTENCE RATHER THAN ONE, AND ASKS A DIFFERENT QUESTION OF THEM.** Binding is a claim the check then enforces, so it is narrowed to one name; the anchor is asked whether one particular declaration is named, so a token that names nothing simply answers no. `liability_snapshots` in a sentence and `liabilitySnapshots` in the registry are the same name, compared over letters and digits only, which is how this corpus writes a table and its key.

**THE ENCLOSING DECLARATION IS FOUND BY INDENTATION AND NOT BY A PARSER.** `prettier` and `pg_format` both indent a member under what declares it, so the enclosing declaration of a line is the nearest line above it at a strictly smaller indent that declares a name, continuing upward when that line declares none. Five declaration shapes are read -- a TypeScript binding, an object-literal or interface member, a `CREATE`, a named constraint, and a SQL column -- and **anything else declares nothing, which makes the anchor fail closed**: it reports rather than guesses. A parser would be right about more lines and would be a second compiler inside a check that reads 19 MB in under a second.

**`CITATION_SUBJECT_SPAN` IS 400 AND SITS INSIDE A FLAT REGION RATHER THAN ON A CLIFF.** Over the 56 named citations the repaired tree carries, the count the anchor refuses is 9 at a span of 100 and 7 at 200, 300, 400, 600, 800 and 1600 alike. Every subject in this corpus is written within 200 characters of the pointer citing it, so the parameter buys nothing above that and only loses sentences below it. **It was measured across seven values rather than tuned until a tree went green**, and this entry says so because that distinction is the one the reservation is about.

### 5. THE VACANCY RULE, AND WHY IT IS NOT THE COMMENT RULE

A pointer landing on `  },` or on an empty line gives a reader nothing to compare a claim against, and it cannot even tell them whether the claim is wrong or the pointer is. **Six of the 225 citations on `37d70b3` were this shape and all six are defects**, three of them among the five session 399 measured. It costs nothing and needs no name, which is why it is asserted over every citation.

**THE NEIGHBOURING RULE WAS MEASURED AND REFUSED, AND THE TWO ARE EASY TO CONFLATE.** Extending it from *blank or a closer* to *anything that is not code* turns **57 of 225** red, and they are honest: this corpus argues by quoting a header, and *"`routes/admin-reads.ts:667` states it: WHAT IS MISSING IS NOT AN AUTHORITY, IT IS A SHAPE"* is a citation into a comment doing exactly what a citation is for. **A citation into prose is a citation into something; a citation into `},` is a citation into nothing.** That is the whole of the line between them.

**IT FIRES ONLY WHEN EVERY FILE THE PATH COULD NAME IS VACANT THERE**, on `nearestName`'s own rule that a suffix path is answered by all of them. It is stated as miss (10) and it costs one real finding today: `provisioning/payload.ts:217` cites `ports.ts:98`, which is blank in the `ports.ts` the sentence means and not in the other ten this tree holds.

### 6. THE COMMA LIST

`` `scope.ts:644,649` `` is how this corpus cites two rules of one registry in one breath, and the tail expression was anchored at the end of the token, so it matched nothing and the token was read as prose. **A pointer nobody parses reads as verified exactly as loudly as one that resolves.** Five exist in source and 51 more in `docs/`; every one of the 56 resolves and is in range, so reading them costs no finding by itself and makes two known-wrong pointers reachable by the rest of the check.

**IT DOES NOT FIX MISS (8) AND DOES NOT PRETEND TO.** One name still binds to a whole list. What it stops is the second and later pointers being read as nothing at all -- and the seed in section 9 shows the sharp version: a comma list whose second number is past the end of the file passes the check on `origin/main` and fails this one.

### 10. Approval

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS RULING 1 AGAINST CANDIDATE 2, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY PLACE THIS ENTRY CHOOSES THE WEAKER OF TWO RULES IT MEASURED.** Requiring the cited line to DECLARE the name is simpler, needs no notion of an enclosing declaration and no span parameter, and is unarguable when it fires.   **THE SECOND IS RULING 6, AND IT IS THE one this entry is least comfortable with.** `RI-15` and `RI-16` read one grammar and now answer two different questions about it, which is exactly the divergence the shared reader was built to prevent. The measurement -- 46 findings over `docs/`, most inside approved documents -- is a reason to defer and is not a reason to be content. **A founder should decide whether the follow-on slice is commissioned now or whether the asymmetry is allowed to sit**, because the longer it sits the more likely a later session reads `RI-16`'s green tick as meaning what `RI-15`'s green tick means. It does not.

  **THE THIRD IS RULING 4, WHICH IS A CONVENTION RATHER THAN A CHECK.** Nothing enforces that a pointer quoted as history is written out of citation grammar; two files were rewritten by hand and a third could be written tomorrow.   **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left to be found.** **The enclosing-declaration reader is five regular expressions over indentation, and it fails closed by reporting.** That is the safe direction for a guard and it is the noisy one for a contributor: a file this corpus does not currently cite -- a YAML document, a deeply nested generic, a SQL body `pg_format` indents differently -- would produce an anchor failure that is the READER's fault and reads as the AUTHOR's. **The check that settles whether it matters takes one command: point a citation with a bound name into `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and see what the message says.** If the answer is ugly, the narrowing to write is that the anchor is asked only of the file kinds whose declaration shapes are listed, and it is one condition rather than a redesign.
