## ADR-271: a calendar day has no timezone, so the `date` type parser hands back the wire text and the crossing two libraries were performing has nowhere left to happen  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-268](ADR-268.md) section 7 measured a defect that was live on `main`: the database day `2026-08-28`, read by a process at `TZ=Europe/Berlin`, arrived in Merit code as `'2026-08-27'`.** Not through a Merit line. Through `pg` and Drizzle, on all 52 `date` columns in the estate at once, before anything this repository wrote had run. That is [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 4's forbidden failure performed by two libraries.

**IT IS REPAIRED BY ONE `setTypeParser(1082, ...)` CALL IN [`packages/db/src/client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts), RETURNING THE WIRE TEXT VERBATIM.** ADR-268 named that repair and could not take it, because `client.ts` was outside its fence. This row granted the fence.

**THE REPAIR IS ONE LINE AND THE SESSION IS NOT, BECAUSE `setTypeParser` IS GLOBAL.** Every reader of every `date` column changed at once, so the enumeration is section 3 and it is the substance of this entry. **It came back smaller than the row feared, and the reason is structural rather than lucky: every `.select(` in this tree is in [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), and drizzle's `date` column is typed `string` on both sides of the change.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `271`, session **462**, dispatched as `claude/a-day-that-changes-with-the-timezone`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** The highest on disk is `0074`, and the row expected none: a type parser is a read-direction decision in the driver and no DDL states it. Section 8 finding 1 is the one place a migration is owed, and it is reported rather than taken.

- **MONEY PATH BY BLAST RADIUS. `E2` READ IS OWED AND NOTHING HERE DISCHARGES IT.** Trading days, basis days and cadence anchors are all `date` columns. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** It inherits the `E2` debt on [ADR-268](ADR-268.md), which found this and could not repair it.

- **`RI-25` IS MINTED IN THIS COMMIT**, taking the next free number, and section 7 records the seed that proved its first version wrong.

### 1. THE RULING

> **1. A CALENDAR DAY IS READ AS THE TEXT POSTGRESQL SENT, AND NOTHING BETWEEN THE DATABASE AND THE CONSUMER MAY GIVE IT A TIMEZONE.** `packages/db/src/client.ts` installs `setTypeParser(1082, wire => wire)` at module scope. The property is not "the parser is correct on this machine"; it is that **the same read at `TZ=UTC`, at a positive offset and at a negative offset returns the same day**, which section 5 proves by running rather than by reading.
>
> **2. THE PARSER IS THE ONE PLACE, AND A CORRECTION AT A READER IS REFUSED.** A per-reader fix is the same defect multiplied once per reader, and the next `date` column added would arrive broken with nothing to catch it. **Drizzle already holds the correct path and never took it:** `PgDateString.mapFromDriverValue` returns a `string` argument untouched, and its `toISOString()` branch runs only because `pg` built a `Date` first. Removing the `Date` removes the branch. There is nothing left at a reader to correct.
>
> **3. ONLY OID 1082. `1114` AND `1184` ARE DELIBERATELY UNTOUCHED.** Merit stores instants in UTC on purpose ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)), so an instant HAS a timezone and keeps the coercion a day must not get. **The defect was never that a timezone conversion happened; it was that a calendar day has no timezone and was being given one.**
>
> **4. THE PARSER REFUSES NOTHING, AND THAT IS THE DESIGN RATHER THAN AN OMISSION.** A throw in the driver would be a failure naming no column, no table and no rule, which is the exact complaint [`last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts) already records against the coercion it replaces. The parser's job is to stop INVENTING a value; the doors' own `YYYY-MM-DD` guards are what refuse one, by name. **It is nonetheless strictly better than what it replaced on every malformed input**, which section 6 measures.
>
> **5. THE PROPERTY IS PINNED IN TWO PLACES BECAUSE IT HAS TWO HALVES.** [`date-column-timezone.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/date-column-timezone.test.ts) runs both libraries at five process timezones and asserts the value; `RI-25` asserts across the whole tree that there is exactly one parser, that it names the day OID, and that no other source file installs one. **Neither can do the other's half**, and section 7 is why the tree-wide half is not optional.
>
> **6. THE THREE ASSERTIONS [ADR-268](ADR-268.md) LEFT ARE MOVED AND NONE IS DELETED.** One inverted, two kept their values and lost their claim. Section 6 says which and why, and **the fact that only one of the three could turn red is itself a finding** about what that suite can see.

### 2. The defect, re-derived at source rather than cited

ADR-268 measured this and this entry did not take its word for it. Both libraries were read and then run.

| Step | Source | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `pg` registers a parser for OID 1082 | `pg-types@2.2.0/lib/textParsers.js:174`, `register(1082, parseDate)` | hands `date` to `postgres-date` |
| `postgres-date@1.0.7` on a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` | its `getDate()`: `var date = new Date(year, month, day)` | **the PROCESS'S LOCAL MIDNIGHT** |
| Drizzle renders it | `drizzle-orm@0.45.2/pg-core/columns/date.js`, `PgDateString.mapFromDriverValue` | `value.toISOString().slice(0, -14)`, **which is UTC** |

**AND THE DISPATCHER WAS VERIFIED AT SOURCE: [`client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts) SET NO TYPE PARSER AT ALL**, and `grep -rn setTypeParser` over the tree returned only the three assertions ADR-268 left. **The defect was live in every deployment whose process timezone was not UTC**, and `INFRA` names no timezone.

**MEASURED BEFORE A BYTE CHANGED**, the database day `2026-08-28` through both real libraries:

```
TZ=UTC                 pg -> 2026-08-28T00:00:00.000Z   merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Europe/Berlin       pg -> 2026-08-27T22:00:00.000Z   merit "2026-08-27"   *** DIFFERENT ***
TZ=America/Chicago     pg -> 2026-08-28T05:00:00.000Z   merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Pacific/Kiritimati  pg -> 2026-08-27T10:00:00.000Z   merit "2026-08-27"   *** DIFFERENT ***
TZ=Pacific/Midway      pg -> 2026-08-28T11:00:00.000Z   merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
```

**THE DEFECT WAS ASYMMETRIC AND THAT IS HOW IT SURVIVED, AND ADR-268 DID NOT REPORT THIS.** A local midnight WEST of UTC is the same UTC day a few hours in, so `toISOString()` returned the right answer and **every negative offset was silently correct**. Only offsets east of UTC failed. **`America/Chicago` is the exchange's own zone and it is one of the ones that never failed**, which is precisely how a team reads a green tree and ships this. The row's framing, that the proof must cover a positive and a negative offset, was right for a reason stronger than it stated: the negative side is not a control, it is the side that hid the defect.

### 3. THE ENUMERATION, WHICH IS WHAT MADE THIS A SESSION

The row's trap: `setTypeParser` changes how every `date` column is read, by every caller, at once, and **a repair that fixes the trading calendar and silently reshapes a KYC expiry is not a repair.** So the readers were enumerated before the parser was changed.

**FOUR FACTS BOUND THE BLAST RADIUS, AND THREE OF THEM ARE STRUCTURAL RATHER THAN LUCKY.**

**1. THE TYPE DOES NOT MOVE.** All 52 `date` columns are declared bare, with no `{ mode: 'date' }` anywhere in [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), so every one of them is a `PgDateString`, whose `mapFromDriverValue` returns a **`string` on both branches**. **No reader's type changes.** The change is a value change, never a shape change, which is the difference between "verify 52 columns" and "re-type the estate".

**2. THE CHANGE IS READ-DIRECTION ONLY.** A type parser converts wire text to a JS value. It has no effect on writes, on bound parameters, on `WHERE` or on `ORDER BY`: `coverageSpanningStatement`'s `lte(coverage_start_day, from)` and `calendarSessionStatement`'s `orderBy(day)` are evaluated **inside PostgreSQL** and never saw a parser. **Every comparison and every ordering over a `date` column in this tree is untouched.**

**3. THERE IS NO RAW SQL READ PATH AT ALL.** `.execute(` appears nowhere in `apps/*/src` or `packages/*/src`, and every backticked `.sql` in those directories is a migration filename in prose. **Nothing bypasses drizzle's mapper**, so there is no reader that would have received the raw `Date` and now receives a string.

**4. EVERY `.select(` IN THE TREE IS IN ONE FILE.** All 14 of them are in [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), which is [ADR-102](ADR-102.md)'s and [ADR-172](ADR-172.md)'s architecture doing exactly what it was built to do. The enumeration of `date` readers is therefore the enumeration of doors:

| The door | What it projects | Effect of the change |
|---|---|---|
| `scopedDb(identity).read(key)` | full row, scoped | the stored day instead of a UTC-shifted one |
| `systemDb(reason).read(key)` | full row, unscoped | the same |
| `firmDb().read(key)` | full row | the same |
| `resolutionDb().read(key)` / `publicLookupDb().read(key)` | full row at an instant | the same |
| `selectStatement` / `lockingSelectStatement` | full row in a transaction | the same |
| `insertUnderStatement`'s parent probe | a `via` row, for existence only | **none.** No `date` is inspected |
| `resolvedCounterparty` | a `via` row, to resolve an id | **none.** No `date` is inspected |
| `effectiveFirmParameterStatement` | `firm_parameters`, ordered by `effective_from` | **none.** That column is `timestamp with time zone`, not `date` |
| `calendarSessionStatement` | `trading_calendar`, ordered by `trading_day` | **the repair's own subject.** The ordering is PostgreSQL's; the returned day is now the stored one |
| `coverageSpanningStatement` | `trading_calendar_loads` | the two coverage days, now the stored ones |

**THE ROW'S KYC EXAMPLE IS NOT LIVE, AND THE REASON IT IS NOT IS ITSELF THE ANSWER.** `kyc_verifications.expires_at` is `timestamp with time zone`, not `date`. **[ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2's suffix rule is what makes this blast radius enumerable at all**: an instant is spelled `*_at` and a day is spelled `*_day` or `*_on`, so the two vocabularies do not overlap and a parser aimed at one cannot reach the other. The rule was written as an API-surface refusal and it turns out to have been load-bearing three layers down.

**AND THE SAME READING FOUND THE ONE COLUMN THAT BREAKS IT**, which is section 8 finding 1.

**THE ENUMERATION WAS THEN CONFIRMED EMPIRICALLY RATHER THAN LEFT AS AN ARGUMENT.** With the parser installed, the full suite ran **292 files, 7006 passed, 6 skipped, and exactly one failure** — `expect(clientSrc).not.toContain('setTypeParser')`, one of the three assertions ADR-268 pinned to the defect. **Nothing else in the estate depended on the defective value.**

### 4. Why the parser and not the reader, in the terms the row set

The row's second trap: *do not fix it at the call sites.* This entry agrees and the reason is stronger than "it would be tedious". **Drizzle's mapper reads, in full:**

```js
mapFromDriverValue(value) {
  if (typeof value === "string") return value;
  return value.toISOString().slice(0, -14);
}
```

**THE CORRECT PATH IS ALREADY THERE AND WAS NEVER TAKEN.** A string passes through untouched; the only reason the second line ever ran is that `pg` built a `Date` first. The parser does not add a correction, **it removes the input to the coercion**, and the branch drizzle already had becomes the branch that runs. A per-reader fix would have been a second implementation of a thing the library does correctly, on 52 columns, forever.

**MODULE SCOPE, NOT INSIDE `client()`.** The property must be true before the first query rather than before the first pool. `pg` looks a parser up per field at result-parse time, so the module-scope call covers connections opened later and the `PoolClient` handles `scoped-db.ts` wraps for transactions. **It opens no socket**, which is the one thing that module's import is not allowed to do and which the file states at length.

**AND EVERY CONNECTION IN THE ESTATE COMES FROM THE POOL THAT FILE BUILDS**, verified at source: `client.ts` is the only file that constructs a `Pool`, `scoped-db.ts` imports `Pool` as a **type only** and reaches the live one through `poolFromClient()`, and the ESLint rule `merit/no-raw-db-client` makes that mechanical. There is no path to a query that does not import `client.ts` first.

### 5. THE THREE-OFFSET PROOF, RUN RATHER THAN READ

The row required the property be proved by running. It is proved twice: once in fresh processes at real timezones, and once as a permanent control.

**FRESH PROCESS PER ZONE, IMPORTING THE REAL [`client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts), AFTER THE REPAIR:**

```
TZ=UTC                 offset=+0h    db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Europe/Berlin       offset=+2h    db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Pacific/Kiritimati  offset=+14h   db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=America/Chicago     offset=-5h    db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Pacific/Midway      offset=-11h   db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Asia/Tokyo          offset=+9h    db 2026-08-28 -> merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
```

**Six offsets spanning UTC-11 to UTC+14**, which is the whole range there is.

**AND IT IS A CONTROL RATHER THAN A TRANSCRIPT.** [`packages/db/test/date-column-timezone.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/date-column-timezone.test.ts), **21 cases, no database**, runs the same journey inside the suite: `pg.types.getTypeParser(1082, 'text')` off the live registry, then `mapFromDriverValue` off the **real schema column**, at five zones. It also asserts the property for **all 52 `date` columns** rather than for the one that found the defect, at UTC+14 and at UTC-11.

**THE HARNESS ITSELF IS PROVED LIVE**, because a timezone test that silently stopped moving the clock would pass for a reason that has nothing to do with the parser. One case asserts that `new Date(2026, 7, 28).toISOString()` really does differ across the three zones the cases use.

**WATCHED RED BEFORE GREEN.** Written against the unrepaired tree: **11 of 21 failing.** The red transcript reproduced the asymmetry independently, failing at `Europe/Berlin` and `Pacific/Kiritimati` and passing at `UTC`, `America/Chicago` and `Pacific/Midway`. With the parser installed: **21 of 21.** Removing the parser again turns **12** red across the two `packages/db` files, and the byte-identical restore was confirmed with `sha256sum -c`.

### 6. What the parser does with what is not a day, measured

Clause 4 says the parser refuses nothing. **What it does instead is hand back what PostgreSQL sent, and on every malformed input that is strictly better than what it replaced.** Measured, from the RED run:

| Wire value | Under `parseDate` | Under this parser |
|---|---|---|
| `infinity` | the **number** `Infinity`, which then died in drizzle as *"value.toISOString is not a function"* | `'infinity'`, which meets a door's `YYYY-MM-DD` guard and is refused **by name** |
| `-infinity` | the number `-Infinity`, the same death | `'-infinity'`, the same named refusal |
| `2026-08-28 BC` | **`-002025-08-28T00:00:00.000Z`**, silently shifted | `'2026-08-28 BC'`, refused by name |
| `08/28/2026`, a non-ISO `DateStyle` | **`null`**, then the same death one layer on | `'08/28/2026'`, refused by name |

**THE BC CASE IS THE ONE WORTH READING TWICE.** No Merit column holds a BC date and none ever will, but the old behaviour on one was not a crash and not a refusal: it was a **different year, rendered confidently**. That is the same failure mode as the timezone shift, in a corner nobody would have looked at.

### 7. THE THREE PINNED ASSERTIONS, AND WHICH ONE COULD ACTUALLY FEEL THE REPAIR

[ADR-268](ADR-268.md) pinned three assertions encoding the defect and required that they move with the repair. **They moved. None was deleted. Only one of them turned red, and that is the finding in this section.**

| # | The assertion | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"a `Date` on a calendar row is rendered as a UTC day and the door never sees it"* | **KEPT, VALUE UNCHANGED, CLAIM REWRITTEN.** Retitled to say the mapper is reached with the parser bypassed |
| 2 | *"THE RENDERING IS `toISOString`, so a local-midnight Date one zone east is the DAY BEFORE"* | **KEPT, VALUE UNCHANGED, CLAIM INVERTED IN MEANING.** It was the bug report; it is now the motive |
| 3 | *"nothing in this package installs a `date` type parser, which is why the above holds"* | **INVERTED.** It now asserts the parser IS installed and names 1082. **This is the one that turned red** |

**WHY 1 AND 2 COULD NOT TURN RED, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH RECORDING.** That suite composes its own rows and answers them through `drizzle-orm/pg-proxy`. It exercises drizzle's mapper and **never `pg`'s parser**. So the two cases that describe the defect were never wired to the thing that caused it: **a test that supplies its own driver values cannot see a driver defect.** Their expected values are unchanged because drizzle's coercion is genuinely unchanged; what they stopped being is a bug report. They now record the coercion the parser exists to keep unreachable, and the case asserting it is unreachable lives in the new file.

**ASSERTION 3's SECOND LEG IS KEPT UNCHANGED AND IT IS NOT A LEFTOVER.** `scoped-db.ts` must still install **no** parser: one parser in the one file that constructs the pool is the repair, and a second one anywhere is the per-reader correction clause 2 refuses.

### 9. Reported and NOT repaired, each outside this fence

**1. `simulation_runs.calibration_observed_at` IS A `date` COLUMN WEARING AN `_at` SUFFIX, AND IT IS THE ONLY ONE IN THE ESTATE.** [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2 makes `*_at` an assertion that the value is an RFC 3339 UTC instant and makes a value that is not **a refusal**. `0045_simulation_runs.sql:103` declares `calibration_observed_at date NOT NULL`. **It is a day claiming to be an instant, at the schema level.** Section 3 is why this matters more than tidiness: the suffix rule is what keeps the two vocabularies from overlapping, and this column is the one place they do. **It is currently harmless and that is a fact about today, not a property**: nothing reads it back through a door, and [`packages/harness/src/provenance.ts`](../../packages/harness/src/provenance.ts) writes it as a string. **A rename needs a migration and this row grants none**, which the row anticipated: *"if you think you need one, that is a finding to state."* Stating it.

**2. SIX MORE `date` COLUMNS CARRY NEITHER `_day` NOR `_on`**: `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `negative_balance_since`, `trading_day_vendor`. **These are a weaker case than finding 1 and are not the same defect**: they do not claim to be instants, they simply do not announce themselves as days. Worth a convention ruling, not worth a migration on its own.

**3. THE ASYMMETRY IS NOT WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE AN OPERATOR WOULD SEE IT.** `INFRA` names no timezone, which ADR-268 already reported. This entry makes the answer not matter for `date` columns. **It does not make it not matter**, and the deployment that runs east of UTC still differs from one that does not in every other clock-reading behaviour. A `TZ=UTC` line in the deployment contract is owed and is outside this fence.

**4. CARRIED FORWARD UNCHANGED** from ADR-268: `readLastClosedTradingDay` consults no coverage, `INV-M20-01`'s ledger constraint, the `cancelled_at` constraint, the trader-facing read of a withdrawal, and the approval driver [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) sized.

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**THE PARSER IS GLOBAL TO THE `pg` MODULE AND THIS ESTATE WILL ONE DAY SHARE THAT MODULE WITH `pg-boss`.** [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s central consequence is that enqueue participates in the same transaction as the state change that caused it, so session 147 builds the queue on this pool. **`pg-boss`'s own schema uses `timestamptz` and not `date`**, so nothing it reads is in 1082's path today. **But "today" is the load-bearing word**, and the alternative — passing a `types` object to `new Pool(...)`, which scopes the parser to Merit's own pool — was not taken because the row named the global call and because a per-pool `types` object requires supplying the whole registry. **A reviewer who wants the narrower construction should say so before the queue lands**, because retrofitting it afterwards means proving the queue unaffected rather than never exposing it.

**THE PROPERTY IS PROVED AGAINST WHAT POSTGRESQL SENDS, AND NOTHING HERE OPENS A DATABASE.** Every case takes `'2026-08-28'` as its input on the stated ground that this is `date`'s wire form under the ISO `DateStyle`. **A deployment that sets a different `DateStyle` sends a different string**, and this repair's answer to that is section 6: the value arrives verbatim and a door refuses it by name, rather than the old behaviour of `null` and a crash. **That is a fail-closed answer and not a correct one.** Pinning `DateStyle` in the connection or the deployment contract is the actual remedy and it is outside this fence.

**THE ENUMERATION IS A READ OF THIS TREE AND NOT A PROOF ABOUT FUTURE ONES.** Facts 3 and 4 of section 3 — no raw read path, every `.select(` in one file — are exactly the kind of fact that is true until somebody adds a file. They are load-bearing for the claim that the blast radius is bounded, **and only one of them has a gate**: `merit/no-raw-db-client` keeps the pool in one place, and nothing mechanically keeps `.select(` there. If that changes, this section is where a future reader should be sent.

**AND THE REPAIR IS UNOBSERVED AGAINST A REAL DATABASE.** No integration job runs here, so what is proved is that the registered parser and drizzle's mapper compose correctly on the wire form, at every offset. **That PostgreSQL sends what this entry says it sends is asserted from its documented behaviour and from `0032`'s own DDL, not executed.** It is the same boundary [`last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts) states for itself, and it is the one place a founder read cannot substitute for a database.
