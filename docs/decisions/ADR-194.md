## ADR-194: a search term is a VALUE THE ESTATE HOLDS and never a pattern the operator composed, so the name fragment is removed from the contract rather than expressed (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) made `GET /admin/accounts`'s search term _"account id, platform ref, email, identity id, name fragment, coupon, or payout id"_, and six of those seven are equalities over declared columns while the seventh is a substring predicate.** [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) closed the accessor's predicate vocabulary at a typed equality; [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) opened it by exactly two terms and **refused `LIKE` and every pattern match by name**. This entry rules that the seventh form is **REMOVED FROM THE CONTRACT**, that no vocabulary member is added and `ADR-157`'s refusal stands untouched, and that the reason is not scope and not cost: **the term names data this estate deliberately does not hold**, and a fragment cannot satisfy the invariant the search endpoint exists under.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `194`, session **357**, dispatched as `claude/account-search-predicate`. **The number was RESERVED before dispatch** and the reservation names it **REQUIRED**. The row is amended in place under [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3 rather than answered with a second row. What is delegated is the SHAPE; what is not is the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read.

- **Context:** [session 353](../sessions/2026-08-28-session-353.md) section 2 separated this question from the one beside it and the separation is the whole reason this entry exists. [WAVE-06](../plans/WAVE-06-admin-console-transport.md) reads `readAccount` and `searchAccounts` as one gap; they are two. `readAccount` was blocked by `events` being unregistered and [ADR-191](ADR-191.md) unblocked it this morning. **No registration decision reaches this one**, and this entry takes none.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH BY FILE, and NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN.** No migration is written or edited, no transaction is opened, no ledger account is named, **no `SystemReason` member and no `SqlExecutorReason` member is added**, no `pg` is imported, no cast past a key type is written, and **`packages/db` is not touched at all**. Section 6 is why the last of those is a ruling rather than an economy.

- **THE ENTRY SHIPS `proposed` WITH AN UNSIGNED APPROVAL LINE.** What is **derived** is section 1's measurement of what the estate holds, and it is the load-bearing half. What is **judged** is clause 1, that a contract behaviour is deleted rather than deferred, and clause 5, that no route-side check replaces it. Section 15 is that question.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every claim below was read at its own file before it was relied on. Two of the dispatch's framings did not survive contact with the tree and they are findings 7 and 8.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. A SEARCH TERM IS A VALUE THAT EXISTS IN THE ESTATE AND NEVER A PATTERN THE OPERATOR COMPOSED, AND THE NAME FRAGMENT IS REMOVED FROM THE CONTRACT.** The six that remain each name a row that exists: an account, a platform reference, a login, an identity, a coupon, a payout. A fragment names none, and the accounts it would return share nothing with each other. **This is [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) clause 1's own distinction, applied one layer up**: that entry refused a caller-composed shape at the accessor, and this one refuses it at the contract, which is why no accessor widening is required to satisfy the result.
>
> **2. THE REMOVAL IS ON EVIDENCE AND NOT ON COST, AND THE EVIDENCE IS THAT THE TERM NAMES NOTHING HERE.** Section 1 findings 3 to 7. The only name column in the schema is a nullable leaderboard handle for a leaderboard with no table, the corpus states in its own invariant that such a handle is not a legal name, no legal name is stored anywhere, the one place a name is compared stores a boolean and a score rather than the names, and the response body has no field to render a name into. **A term implemented against `display_name` today would search a column that is empty by construction and report no match to an operator who believes they searched a person's name**, which is worse than the refusal and is the shape [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) names: a thing that reads like a control and is not one.
>
> **3. `INV-M6-10` IS THE SECOND REASON AND IT WOULD BE SUFFICIENT ALONE.** A fragment is by construction not a specific subject, and `FM-M6-10`'s mitigation column already says search requires one. Where a value the estate holds is shared by many identities the query still names a subject, on [`M06:435`](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s ruling that the subject may be the shared thing itself. **The line is between an equality on a stored value and a pattern over stored values, and it is the same line clause 1 draws.**
>
> **4. NO VOCABULARY MEMBER IS ADDED AND `ADR-157`'S REFUSAL STANDS ENTIRELY UNTOUCHED.** No `contains`, no `LIKE`, no `SqlExecutorReason` member, no second door. **`packages/db` is not opened by this entry.** Section 6 prices the widening that was available and section 7 prices the surface that was available, and both are refused on `ADR-157`'s own evidence rather than around it.
>
> **5. THE ROUTE'S TERM LIST MOVES AND THE ROUTE'S BEHAVIOUR DOES NOT.** The validation message is a transcription of the narrowed sentence, and the two are bound by an assertion so neither can be narrowed alone again. **No check of the term's FORM is added, and that is a decision.** Whether a term names a subject is a question only a lookup answers: `jo` is a legitimate exact coupon code. The refusal lives in the ADAPTER, which reads by equality and by nothing else, and a route-side pattern check would be a second answer to a question the accessor already answers, of the kind [ADR-144](ADR-144.md)'s own header refuses about the admin surface.
>
> **6. THIS ENTRY DOES NOT UNBLOCK `searchAccounts` AND SAYS SO RATHER THAN IMPLYING IT.** Section 9. The adapter is still owed, it is owed by no plan, and what remains between it and existing is a fan-out across tables rather than a predicate the accessor cannot express. **What changed is that the remaining work is affordable in the runner, which under the seventh term it was not.**

### 4. Clause 2, which is the part that is a measurement rather than a judgement

**The seventh term does not fail because the accessor is narrow. It fails because there is nothing behind it.** Four facts, each from its own primary source, and each is section 1's row restated in the order that makes the conclusion:

1. **The estate holds no legal name** (finding 5). KYC keeps a vendor pointer and the comment on that column calls it _"the only pointer we keep"_.
2. **Where a name matters most, Merit stores the verdict and not the input** (finding 6). `destination_name_match` is a boolean, `name_match_score` is an integer between 0 and 10000, `name_match_method` is how it was compared. **The two names being compared are held by the KYC vendor and the payout rail, and neither is in this database.** The same paragraph in `0010` says the same thing about bank details in a stronger voice.
3. **The one name-shaped column is a pseudonym for a product that does not exist** (findings 3 and 4). It is nullable because v1 never shows it, and `INV-M11-10` requires it to be something other than the trader's name if it is ever shown.
4. **The response has nowhere to put a name** (finding 7).

**SO AN OPERATOR SEARCHING A NAME FRAGMENT TODAY WOULD SEARCH AN EMPTY COLUMN AND BE TOLD NOTHING MATCHED**, which is the failure this entry is most concerned with: it is indistinguishable from _"that person does not exist here"_, on the screen an incident is run from. **A contract row that promises a search of data the estate does not keep is not a feature waiting for an implementation; it is a wrong sentence**, and the corpus's own rule is that a behaviour in the corpus is a commitment. This entry removes the commitment rather than building the thing that cannot honour it.

### 5. Clause 3, and where the line between a coupon and a fragment actually sits

**The objection to clause 1 is a good one and it is answered rather than avoided: a coupon search can return thousands of accounts and a fragment search can return three, so the row count is not the discriminator.** It is not, and the corpus already said what is.

[`M06:435`](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) ruled that _"Naming a signal and listing the identities that share it **is a specific-subject query**: the subject is the signal"_. Read against the two cases:

|                     | The subject                             | What the returned identities share                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A coupon**        | the coupon, which is a row in `coupons` | **The coupon.** Every returned account really did redeem it, and the relation is the operator's reason for asking                                                                           |
| **A name fragment** | nothing. `jo` is not a row              | **Nothing.** Two identities whose handles both contain `jo` have no relation to each other or to the operator's question, and the set grows monotonically as the operator shortens the term |

**THAT SECOND PROPERTY IS THE ONE THAT MAKES IT A BULK SURFACE RATHER THAN A WIDE ONE.** A term that returns more rows the LESS the operator knows is an enumeration control with a text box in front of it, and `FM-M6-10` names it: _"A bulk PII surface hiding inside a convenience feature"_. The cap and the cursor bound one response; they do not bound a walk, and a single character walked through the alphabet enumerates the estate a page at a time. **A coupon cannot be shortened into a wider coupon. A fragment can always be shortened.**

### 6. SHAPE 2 PRICED: adding a substring term to the accessor, and whether `ADR-157`'s evidence survives

**It survives, both halves of it, and it was re-tested rather than cited.** [`ADR-157.md:59`](ADR-157.md) refused `LIKE` for two reasons:

**REASON ONE, that a pattern is a shape a caller composes.** Unmoved, and this entry is a second instance of it rather than an exception to it. It would also arrive with an escaping obligation `ADR-157`'s three terms do not have: `%` and `_` inside the operator's own value are wildcards, so a term built from a caller's string is a pattern the caller composed **inside a bound parameter, where a reviewer reading the call site cannot see it**. That is [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) section 3's argument against a `SQL` parameter, at a smaller radius and less visible.

**REASON TWO, that the surface that would want one is a JOIN first.** Re-measured at the file and it holds: [`admin-reads.ts:686`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-reads.ts) states _"`AdminAccountSearchItem` joins accounts to identities to flags to reconciliation state"_, and the same header records that there is _"no join and no aggregate to reach for"_. **So a substring term admitted today would not make `searchAccounts` writable.** It would spend a widening, leave the endpoint blocked on the thing it is actually blocked on, and leave the next reader a foreclosure that reads as answered.

**THAT IS `ADR-157` SECTION 5'S OWN SENTENCE ABOUT THE AGGREGATE, ARRIVING FOR THE SECOND TIME**: [`ADR-157.md:91`](ADR-157.md), _"A widening that buys nothing and looks like it bought something is worse than the absence it replaces"_. **An entry that admitted a substring term without answering that sentence would have widened a fence to finish**, and the dispatch was right that this is the same move as weakening a gate to pass it.

**AND THE SUBSTRING TERM IS A WIDER ASK THAN THE ONE THAT ENTRY REFUSED, on three axes it is worth naming separately:**

1. **`ADR-157` clause 1's admission test is that every member REMOVES rows from a read the caller already holds.** A range and an `IS NULL` are both narrowings whose callers are sweeps with a bound. `contains('')` is a narrowing that removes nothing, and its only caller is a human-facing screen governed by `INV-M6-10`. **It passes the reach test and fails the enumeration one**, and no existing term had to.
2. **It has no index and cannot use one** (finding 10). An unanchored `LIKE '%x%'` is a sequential scan of `identities`, taken synchronously on an operator screen, on the exact table whose row count grows with the business.
3. **`ADR-157` clause 2 recognises a term by object identity in a module-private `WeakSet`.** A substring term joins that set and inherits its sharpest edge, and it inherits it on the one term whose value comes from an HTTP query string rather than from a job's own clock.

**COST IF THIS ENTRY IS WRONG ABOUT SHAPE 2:** a later slice that has a real caller for a substring read over a column the estate actually holds writes an entry against `scoped-db.ts` and argues it there, with the escaping question and the index question stated above so they are not discovered at the file. **Nothing this entry does makes that entry harder to write**, which is the property that makes refusing cheap and admitting expensive.

### 7. SHAPE 3 PRICED: moving the search behind a surface that is not the keyed accessor

**Read before proposing, as the dispatch required, and it is the most expensive of the three by a wide margin.**

**THERE IS ONE DOOR OUT OF `scoped-db.ts` AND IT IS CLOSED.** `sqlExecutor`'s vocabulary is one member; [`ADR-157.md:43`](ADR-157.md) clause 7 states it _"DOES NOT MOVE"_ and adds the assertion that watches it; [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 11 rule 10 and [P7](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) section 11 rule 10 each foreclose the reach-around by name; and this session's own standing refusals make a new member a ruling that must overturn that clause **in its own words**. There is no argument here that would overturn it: the widening would be spent on a term section 4 measures as pointing at nothing.

**AND EVERY CONTROL THE ADMIN READ PATH HAS RUNS THROUGH THE ACCESSOR.** [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) made the wrapper the only sanctioned accessor and it is what bounds a BOLA blast radius; [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) built the authority split that makes `systemDb('operator-console')` the admin read's door; [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) built the address and the uniqueness fold; [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) built the term refusals and the lock. **A second surface inherits none of them and would have to restate all of them**, which is the cost the corpus paid once and would be paying twice for one query.

**THE SEARCH-INDEX READING IS THE ONE WORTH NAMING SEPARATELY BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE SOMEBODY WILL REACH FOR.** A trigram index, a `tsvector`, or an external search service all answer the fragment question well. **Every one of them is a SECOND COPY of trader-identifying data, living outside the accessor, with its own access path, its own retention and its own breach surface**, and `INV-M6-10`'s companion control in [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) section 7.9 is that every view is logged as an access to the underlying identities. **A copy nothing logs is the bulk PII surface `FM-M6-10` refuses, built deliberately and called an optimisation.** That is a ruling several sizes larger than this one, it is a schema and a privacy ruling rather than a predicate one, and it would be argued for a term this entry measures as empty.

### 8. What the operator loses, and what already serves the need it was reached for

**The dispatch's argument for the term is the strongest one available and it is answered rather than dismissed: name search is how you find someone whose account id you do not have.** Three things are true about that at once.

1. **The term does not serve it today and would not on the day an adapter shipped**, because the column is empty by construction (section 4). What the operator loses is a promise, not a capability.
2. **The estate's actual answer to "I know who but not which row" is the email**, and it is already a contract term: `users.email` is `citext`, so casing costs nothing, and `email_normalized` is the entity-resolution key with an index over it (section 3). **An operator in a support conversation or an email thread holds an address**; `identities.support_contact_ref` exists so a support conversation resolves to an identity _"without Merit storing transcripts"_.
3. **Where the operator holds a name and nothing else, the name is at the KYC vendor or the payout rail**, which is where finding 6 says Merit deliberately left it. **The remedy is not a predicate. It is a decision to store a name**, and that decision is a privacy ruling with a retention question, a `DATA_MODEL` row, a migration and a disclosure consequence, none of which is this reservation's and none of which this entry takes.

**SO THE HONEST STATEMENT OF THE LOSS IS THIS: an operator who holds only a human's name, and no email, no account, no payout and no coupon, cannot resolve them from the admin console, and could not before this entry either.** Nothing here made that worse and this entry declines to make it look solved.

### 9. What this entry does NOT unblock, stated because "the ruling landed" will be read as "the endpoint works"

**`AdminReadSource.searchAccounts` IS STILL UNIMPLEMENTED AND THIS ENTRY WRITES NO ADAPTER.** `apps/api/src/admin-source/**` is another session's this wave and was not opened. `searchAccounts` is claimed by no plan at all, which [WAVE-06](../plans/WAVE-06-admin-console-transport.md) section 6 measured and this entry does not repair.

**WHAT DID CHANGE IS THE AFFORDABILITY OF THE WORK, AND IT IS THE CONSTRUCTIVE HALF OF THE RULING.** `AdminAccountSearchItem` is a fan-out across accounts, identities, flags and reconciliation state, and the accessor has no join. [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) section 5 already ruled what a caller does about that: pull the rows through `rowsWhere` and do the join in the runner, paying for every row that crosses the boundary. **Under an exact subject that price is bounded by the page limit `INV-M6-10` already caps.** Under a fragment it is bounded by nothing, because the fan-out begins with an unbounded set of identities. **So the term the contract could not express was also the term that made the runner-side assembly unaffordable**, and removing it turns the adapter from blocked into merely unwritten.

**THE ADAPTER STILL HAS ONE QUESTION THIS ENTRY DOES NOT ANSWER**, and it is recorded so it is not discovered at the file: a platform ref is unique among live accounts only (section 3), so that one term may resolve to more than one row and the adapter chooses whether that is a page or an error.

### 10. What this FORECLOSES, which is the part every later slice inherits

1. **THE ADMIN CONSOLE HAS NO FUZZY SEARCH OF ANY KIND, PERMANENTLY, until an entry argues for one.** Not a prefix, not a trigram, not a spelling-tolerant match. Every one of them is a pattern the operator composes and clause 1 refuses the class rather than the instance.
2. **A SEARCH TERM MUST NAME A ROW THAT EXISTS.** A later term added to this endpoint has to point at a stored value, which means a term arrives together with the column that holds it rather than ahead of it.
3. **THE CONTRACT NOW HAS A REMOVED BEHAVIOUR, AND A REMOVAL IS HARDER TO REVERSE THAN AN ABSENCE.** A future session reading section 8 will find six terms and no hint that a seventh was considered unless it reads this entry, which is why the contract carries the removal in its own words rather than only in this file.
4. **THIS ENTRY DECIDES NOTHING ABOUT STORING A NAME.** If Merit ever stores one, the search term is re-arguable and the argument is easier, not foreclosed. **What is foreclosed is reaching a name Merit does not hold by widening a predicate.**
5. **`ADR-157`'S THREE-TERM VOCABULARY IS UNCHANGED AND ITS `LIKE` REFUSAL IS NOW LOAD-BEARING FOR TWO ENTRIES RATHER THAN ONE.** A later entry overturning it now has two refusals to answer.
6. **THE ROUTE STILL CANNOT TELL A SUBJECT FROM A PATTERN** (clause 5), so an operator who types a fragment gets an empty page rather than a validation failure. **That is the ordinary shape of a search that found nothing** and it is named here because it is the kind of thing a later session reads as a missing check.

### 13. Alternatives considered

- **Leaving the contract as it is and writing an adapter that serves six of the seven.** **Refused, and it is the failure mode rather than the fallback.** It narrows a contract behaviour behind a green suite, on the one screen whose purpose is finding a human, and it is the shape every silently-lossy scope member was refused for in [ADR-191](ADR-191.md).

- **Adding a substring term but admitting it at `operator-console` authority only.** Refused: it reads as a narrowing and is not one. - **Recording the removal as an edge case or an open question rather than as a contract amendment.** Refused: the contract is the primary source and a behaviour in the corpus is a commitment, so the commitment is where the removal has to happen. An entry beside it would leave two documents disagreeing and the wrong one load-bearing.
