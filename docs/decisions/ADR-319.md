## ADR-319: an operator close in no session takes the last day that CLOSED, an operator close outside coverage takes no day at all, and three of the constraint's four statuses are not served by either (2026-09-04, status: proposed)

**Row `319` is [ADR-312](ADR-312.md) section 9 item 5 and it asked one question: which trading day an operator close takes when the instant is in no exchange session. `tradingDayAt` answers that question in two ways and this entry rules on both of them differently, which is the point.**

**`not_a_session` TAKES THE TRADING DAY OF THE LATEST SESSION THAT HAS ALREADY CLOSED AT THE CLOSE INSTANT.** Read off a row by comparing an instant with an instant, never nearest, never next, never computed. **`outside_coverage` TAKES NO DAY AND THE CLOSE IS REFUSED**, and section 5 states that cost in full rather than asserting it is small.

**THE TWO ARMS GET DIFFERENT OUTCOMES AND THAT IS HOW [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) `F-4` SURVIVES THIS CONSUMER.** A build that answers both arms with a day, or both with a refusal, is this ruling misapplied and not a shortcut through it.

**AND A THIRD THING IS RULED THAT THE ROW DID NOT ASK FOR AND THE MEASUREMENT FORCED.** On a Saturday the ruling writes Friday's trading day, which is the same value an in-session Friday close writes. **Two different facts about the estate would land as one indistinguishable `date`**, so the BASIS is recorded beside the day (section 6). Without that clause the ruling reintroduces `F-4`'s silent substitution at the audit record while `tradingDayAt` keeps refusing it one package away.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `319`, session **510**, dispatched as `claude/a-close-in-no-session`. **RESERVED, and taken.**

- **MONEY-ADJACENT, `E2` READ OWED.** It decides a value written to a terminal account row that a customer sees. No money value is computed here, no DDL is written, **no migration number, no `RI-nn` and no `SD-nn` were taken.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **`useAdminWriteBackend` IS STILL BLOCKED AND THIS ROW DOES NOT MAKE IT BUILDABLE.** Section 10. It removes one of two obstructions; `principal(request)` is behind the C-08 SSO purchase and is founder-owed.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That `apps/worker` now reads `trading_calendar_loads`, that `admin-writes.ts:1301` is the only `closed_on` write in the tree and `closed_admin` the only terminal status any code sets, and that four one-arm `tradingDay` ports exist against one three-armed resolver, is **MEASURED**, by grep at the moment of writing, each command in section 3. That an instant inside a span has both a closed session behind it and an open session ahead of it is **DERIVED** at [`calendar.ts:544`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/calendar.ts). **The direction of the answer in section 4, the refusal in section 5, the basis field in section 6 and the disposal of the other three statuses in section 8 are JUDGED**, and section 14 says which of them a founder is most likely to overrule.

### 4. THE RULING, part 1: `not_a_session` takes the last day that CLOSED

**`closed_on` is the trading day of the LATEST SESSION WHOSE CLOSE INSTANT IS AT OR BEFORE the close instant**, read off that session's row. Not the nearest session in either direction. Not the next one to open. Not a date computed from a clock.

**FOUR REASONS, AND THE FIRST IS THE CORPUS'S OWN.**

**(1) `R-06` ALREADY FIXES THE DIRECTION.** [`M01:470`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md): *"Every evaluation is against the last closed day and nothing more recent."* An account whose whole rule set is measured backwards from the last closed day does not get a terminal date measured forwards. **This ruling adds no direction to the corpus; it applies the one already there to the one act that had no rule.**

**(2) A TERMINAL DATE MUST NOT BE IN THE FUTURE.** The close takes effect at the instant the operator commits it: the row is stamped `closed_admin` and `phase: 'closed'` in the same statement ([`admin-writes.ts:1328`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)). Writing the next session's day says the account ended on a day it never reached, and the constraint's own comment at [`0007_accounts.sql:133`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql) reads *"A terminal status knows the day it ended."* **A day that has not happened is not a day anything ended on.**

**(3) THE BACKWARD ANSWER IS STABLE AND THE FORWARD ONE IS NOT.** A calendar loaded further forward, a holiday declared, a session halted: every one of those can change which day opens next and none of them can change which day last closed. **A `date` on a terminal row is written once and read for the life of the record**, so the answer that a later load cannot revise is the only one safe to write there.

**(4) IT KEEPS `closed_on` CONSISTENT WITH THE ACCOUNT'S OWN LAST FOLD.** `rule_states.trading_day` is `date NOT NULL` ([`0015_rule_states.sql:44`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql)) with a unique index per account and day ([`0015_rule_states.sql:225`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql)). An account closed on a Saturday has its last state row on Friday. **Stamping Monday puts `closed_on` beyond every `rule_states` row the account will ever have, with nothing in between**, and any reader joining the two has to invent a rule for the hole.

**THE READ IS OVER THE SAME `SessionCalendar` VALUE AND NOT A SECOND ONE.** The instant is compared with `session.closeAtMs`, the greatest such value at or below it is selected, and its `tradingDay` is read off that row. **One value, one coverage definition, one comparison discipline**, which is what stops the operator path and the resolver drifting into two answers about the same estate.

**AND THE ANSWER IS ALWAYS AVAILABLE ON THIS ARM, WHICH IS DERIVED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** A span's bounds are its own first session's open and its own last session's close ([`calendar.ts:544`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/calendar.ts)), so an instant inside a span and inside no session **necessarily has a session of that span behind it**. `not_a_session` therefore never leaves this rule with nothing to read. **If a future change makes that false, the answer is section 5's refusal and never a fallback**, and section 9 makes that a condition on the port's shape rather than a note.

### 5. THE RULING, part 2: `outside_coverage` refuses, and here is what that costs

**THE CLOSE IS REFUSED. No day is written, no row is updated, and the operator is told that the estate has loaded no calendar around this instant.**

**WHY A REFUSAL RATHER THAN A GUESS.** On this arm the estate holds no row that says anything about this instant. Every available day is a day the system knows is outside what anyone loaded, so writing one means claiming a fact from the absence of a fact. **[ADR-042](ADR-042.md) `F-4` exists for exactly this substitution**: *"Coverage has no storage, so an exhausted calendar is indistinguishable from an unbroken holiday: every counter quietly stops advancing, no rule fires, nothing breaches, nothing becomes eligible, and nothing raises"* ([`ADR-042.md:30`](ADR-042.md)). A `closed_on` written from an unloaded calendar is that failure made durable on a customer-visible row.

**THE COST, STATED THE WAY [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) STATED ITS OWN.** An operator who needs to close an account during a calendar outage cannot, through this route, at all. That is not hypothetical: `POST /admin/accounts/:accountId/close` ([`admin-writes.ts:1281`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)) is the only route in this tree that closes an account, and `closed_on` is not optional, because `accounts_terminal_has_close_date` is a merged `CHECK` and migrations are sacred. **There is no partial close: the enforcement and the date are one statement.**

**THREE THINGS BOUND THAT COST AND ALL THREE ARE MEASURED.**

**(a) A FREEZE IS STILL AVAILABLE AND MONEY STILL CANNOT LEAVE.** `POST /admin/accounts/:accountId/freeze` ([`admin-writes.ts:1160`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)) writes `payoutsFrozen: true` with `ctx.backend.now()` ([`admin-writes.ts:1217`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)) and **never calls `tradingDay()`**: the only call site in the file is [`:1301`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts). `payouts_frozen` is a payout veto, read as `identityPayoutsFrozen` and `accountPayoutsFrozen` by the payout backend at [`payout-backend.ts:577`](../../apps/api/src/payout-backend.ts). **THE FALLBACK IS NOT UNCONDITIONAL AND SAYING SO IS THE POINT**: the freeze handler refuses unless the request cites a risk flag that is still open ([`admin-writes.ts:1193`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)), so an operator with no open investigation has neither route.

**(b) AN ESTATE ON THIS ARM HAS ALREADY STOPPED, SO THE REFUSAL IS CONSISTENT RATHER THAN EXCEPTIONAL.** The nightly fold refuses on an exhausted calendar at [`adapter.ts:573`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts) and on an unspanned range at [`adapter.ts:585`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts). No account is being folded, no rule is firing, and nothing is becoming eligible. **The close route holding the line is the same line every other consumer is already holding**, and a close route that answered anyway would be the single place in the estate that wrote a confident day during an outage.

**(c) THE REMEDY IS OPERATIONAL, BOUNDED AND ALREADY OWNED.** Load the exchange calendar. [ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s `F-4` puts the loader in `packages/db/src/seed/` and buys the horizon alarm in the same fact, so the condition raises before it bites rather than being discovered by an operator at a keyboard.

**AND ONE CASE THAT IS NOT `outside_coverage` AND REFUSES ON THE SAME TERMS.** If the arm is `not_a_session` and no session of the span has closed at or before the instant, there is no row to read and the close refuses with the same shape. Section 4 derives that this cannot happen against `buildSessionCalendar` as it stands today; **the clause exists so that a later change to the span bounds produces a refusal rather than a fallback.**

### 6. THE RULING, part 3: the BASIS is recorded, which is how `F-4` survives at this consumer

**THE MEASUREMENT THAT FORCED THIS CLAUSE.** An operator close at 14:00 CT on Friday writes Friday's trading day. An operator close at 09:00 on Saturday writes Friday's trading day. **Same column, same value, two different facts about the estate**, and `closed_on` alone cannot tell them apart afterwards.

**That is `F-4`'s substitution reappearing at the consumer.** `tradingDayAt` keeps the two arms distinguishable and [ADR-251](ADR-251.md)'s seed **B** was seeded and watched failing precisely to keep it that way. **A consumer that discards the discriminator on the way to the database undoes that one layer up, where no test in `packages/rules-engine` can see it.**

**SO THE RULING IS: the close records WHICH ARM ANSWERED, beside the day, in the audit record the handler already writes.** The `after` block at [`admin-writes.ts:1303`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts) is `{ status, phase, closed_on }` and gains one field naming the basis, over a closed two-member vocabulary: the instant was inside a session, or the instant was in no session and the day is the last that closed. **A third member is not available**, because `outside_coverage` never reaches a write.

**THIS IS THE CHEAPEST PLACE IT CAN LIVE AND THE ONLY ONE INSIDE THIS ROW'S REACH.** `accounts` gains no column, so no migration is taken and no merged `CHECK` is touched. The audit trail is the record that already exists for exactly this: `close_reason` is the operator's own sentence and is not this ([`admin-writes.ts:1318`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts) states that in terms), so the basis has nowhere else to go that is not new DDL. **If a founder wants the basis on the row rather than in the audit, that is a column and a migration and it is not this row's to take.**

### 7. THE RULING, part 4: what it governs, and the nightly fold does NOT need it

**THIS RULING GOVERNS THE OPERATOR CLOSE PATH AND NOT EVERY CONSUMER OF `tradingDayAt`.** The row required this to be named either way and the reason is a measurement rather than a preference.

**THE NIGHTLY FOLD IS ALREADY COMPLIANT AND ITS ANSWER WAS RULED SEPARATELY.** [ADR-273](ADR-273.md) ruling 1 is quoted in the fold's own docblock at [`adapter.ts:448`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts): *"a fold may leave the coverage read to its caller only when the caller is handed a value from which the day is unreachable without discriminating on a coverage verdict."* It reaches its day through `latestClosedSession` ([`adapter.ts:485`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)), which is **the same direction section 4 rules for the close**, and it refuses on an uncovered range ([`adapter.ts:585`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)), which is **the same disposal section 5 rules for `outside_coverage`**.

**SO THE FOLD IS NOT OUT OF COMPLIANCE THE DAY THIS IS SIGNED**, which is the answer the row asked for and it is the opposite of what the row expected, because the premise it rested on is stale (section 3). **The two paths agree on both arms and they reached that agreement independently**, which is the strongest evidence available that the direction in section 4 is the estate's own rather than this session's invention.

**WHAT THIS RULING DOES NOT GOVERN, ENUMERATED SO NOBODY INFERS IT DOES.** `apps/worker/src/breaker/ports.ts` `tradingDayOf(at: Date): string` ([`:444`](../../apps/worker/src/breaker/ports.ts)) and `apps/worker/src/digests/ports.ts` `tradingDayOf(at: Date): string` ([`:643`](../../apps/worker/src/digests/ports.ts)) are two more one-arm ports asking the same three-armed question, both unwired, both outside this fence. **Their questions are not this one**: a breaker's `evaluated_on` and a digest's coverage day are not terminal dates and their refusal costs are different. **Each owes its own ruling and this entry does not supply it**, but the direction in section 4 and the refusal in section 5 are the obvious starting points and a session that departs from them should say why.

### 8. THE RULING, part 5: the constraint covers FOUR statuses and this serves ONE

`accounts_terminal_has_close_date` ([`0007_accounts.sql:134`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql)) requires `closed_on` on `breached`, `closed_admin`, `closed_chargeback` and `graduated`. **Only `closed_admin` is an operator close, and this ruling serves only `closed_admin`. The other three are disposed of here rather than left to be assumed.**

**THE MEASUREMENT FIRST, BECAUSE IT CHANGES THE SIZE OF THE QUESTION.** `grep -rn "closedOn" apps/*/src packages/*/src` returns **19** lines. **Exactly one writes a DAY**: [`admin-writes.ts:1330`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts), fed by [`:1301`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts). Two more write the column as `null` on synthetic canary accounts ([`identity.ts:969`](../../apps/worker/src/detectors/identity.ts) and [`:970`](../../apps/worker/src/detectors/identity.ts)), which the terminal constraint does not engage because those rows carry no terminal status; the remaining sixteen are reads, types and projections. `closed_admin` is the only one of the four that any `src/` line sets ([`admin-writes.ts:1328`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)); `breached`, `closed_chargeback` and `graduated` appear in this tree only as enum members and read vocabulary ([`schema.ts:170`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), [`accounts.ts:777`](../../apps/api/src/routes/accounts.ts), [`external-gates.ts:114`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/external-gates.ts), [`types.ts:892`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)). **Nothing writes them. So three of the four arms have no caller to be wrong yet**, which is why this is a ruling about who owes what and not a repair.

| Status | Does section 4 serve it | Who owes the answer |
|---|---|---|
| `closed_admin` | **YES.** It is the subject | This entry |
| `breached` | **NO, AND NOT BY A LOOKUP AT ALL** | The slice that writes the breach |
| `graduated` | **NO, AND NOT BY A LOOKUP AT ALL** | The slice that writes the graduation |
| `closed_chargeback` | **NO. Same SHAPE, different ACTOR, and the refusal does not transfer** | **A ruling that is OWED and is allocated to nobody** |

**`breached` AND `graduated` ARE NOT INSTANT LOOKUPS AND THAT IS THE RULING ON THEM.** Both are engine verdicts. The engine folds a day and writes a `rule_states` row whose `trading_day` is `date NOT NULL` ([`0015_rule_states.sql:44`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql)); [`M01:470`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)'s `R-06` says that day is the last closed one, and the fold obtains it from `TradingDayAnchor` ([`adapter.ts:464`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)) or does not fold. **So the day is an INPUT the verdict already carries, and `tradingDayAt` is the wrong function to reach for**: `closed_on` for these two is the `trading_day` of the fold that produced the verdict, handed across, and no instant is resolved anywhere on that path. **The slice that writes those rows owes the wiring and does not owe a ruling.** `R-49` closes a graduated account ([`replay.ts:169`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/replay.ts)) and that is the edge in question.

**`closed_chargeback` IS THE ONE THAT NEEDS ITS OWN RULING AND THIS ENTRY REFUSES TO SUPPLY IT.** Its instant comes from a PSP dispute, so it is the same shape as the operator close: an external instant that may land in no session. **Section 4's direction transfers. Section 5's refusal DOES NOT**, and that is the whole difference: a webhook has no operator to refuse to. A handler that refuses either retries until the PSP gives up or drops a chargeback on the floor, and both of those are worse than either arm of this ruling. **Naming that as owed is more useful than guessing at it**, and it is exactly the four-arm defect the row exists to remove: an entry that ruled all four here would have ruled the hardest one by inattention.

### 9. THE RULING, part 6: the port's signature changes, and this row does not change it

**`tradingDay(): string` CANNOT CARRY THIS RULING AND MUST BECOME A TWO-ARM VALUE.** Section 5 requires a refusal an operator can act on, and section 6 requires a basis. A `string` carries neither. **Throwing on `outside_coverage` is available and is REFUSED**: the only throw this port has is `AdminWriteUnwired` ([`admin-writes.ts:443`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)), which is answered as 503 and means *no backend is installed*. **An estate whose calendar is unloaded is fully deployed and has a data condition**, and serving that as a deployment failure tells the operator to page the wrong person.

**THE SHAPE IS THE ESTATE'S OWN, TAKEN AND NOT INVENTED.** `TradingDayAnchor` ([`adapter.ts:464`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)) is `{ kind: 'anchored'; tradingDay } | { kind: 'refused'; why }`, and `CalendarAnchor` ([`liability.ts:1587`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/liability.ts)) is the same two-arm value one deployable over inside `apps/api` itself. **The port takes that shape plus section 6's basis on the resolved arm**, over the closed two-member vocabulary section 6 fixes. **The refused arm carries no day, not even as diagnostics**, on [`adapter.ts:459`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)'s own stated reason: a field holding exactly the wrong answer beside a refusal is a field somebody reaches for when the refusal is inconvenient.

**AND THIS ROW CHANGES THE DOCBLOCK AND NOT THE SIGNATURE.** The declaration at [`admin-writes.ts:346`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts) stands. **The reason is a fence and not caution.** Changing the type breaks the unwired default at [`:443`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts) and the caller at [`:1301`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts), both outside the `:317` to `:347` this row was given; the caller's new arm is an HTTP refusal, which needs a problem-document code, and **a code with no [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) row is a behavior not in the corpus**, which the standing rule puts out of scope. That file is not in this fence either. **So the shape is ruled here and built by a slice that holds both files.**

### 14. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.**

This is a **ruling** row and it is **money-adjacent**: it decides the value of a `date` on a terminal account row, which is a customer-visible fact and appears on whatever record that account ends with. What is owed under the approval is a read of the **direction** in section 4, the **refusal and its cost** in section 5, the **basis field** in section 6, and the **disposal of the other three statuses** in section 8.

**WHAT A FOUNDER READ ADDS AND THIS ENTRY CANNOT.**

**THE FIRST AND LARGEST THING TO DECIDE IS SECTION 4's DIRECTION, BECAUSE IT IS A CHOICE ABOUT WHAT A DATE MEANS TO A CUSTOMER AND NOT A MEASUREMENT.** An account closed on a Saturday is stamped with **Friday**, the last day it could have traded, and never with Monday. The four reasons are real and the fourth is the strongest mechanically. **But the customer-facing sentence is the part no grep reaches**: a trader who receives a notice on Saturday and reads a statement dated Friday is reading a date that is not the day they were told. **The case for Monday is that a close notified on Saturday is a Monday event in the trader's calendar and the record should say so.** This entry chooses Friday because `R-06` chooses backwards, because a terminal date in the future is not a date anything ended on, and because the backward answer cannot be revised by a later calendar load. **If the founder's view is that `closed_on` is a NOTIFICATION date rather than a MARKET date, this entry is wrong on its central question** and the remedy is not a tweak: it is a different reading of the column, and it would want the same treatment applied to the fold. Nothing else in the corpus states which of the two `closed_on` is, and that is the gap this ruling fills by choosing.

**THE SECOND IS THE REFUSAL IN SECTION 5, WHICH TRADES AN OPERATOR'S HANDS FOR A ROW'S HONESTY.** A calendar outage means no account can be closed at all. The three bounds are measured and none of them makes the cost zero: **the freeze fallback needs an open risk flag** ([`admin-writes.ts:1193`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-writes.ts)) and freezes payouts rather than closing anything. **A founder who has ever had to close an account in a hurry may weigh this differently**, and the alternative that is honest rather than a guess is an operator-supplied day with the operator named on the row. **This entry did not take it** because a day an operator types is a day nothing validates against a calendar that is not loaded, and because it puts the one durable artifact of an outage into a human's hands under time pressure. **That is a judgement about operators and it is the founder's to overrule.**

**THE THIRD IS SECTION 8's SPLIT, AND THE PART TO ARGUE WITH IS THE `breached` AND `graduated` HALF RATHER THAN THE `closed_chargeback` HALF.** Saying a chargeback owes its own ruling costs nothing and is plainly right. Saying that a breach's `closed_on` is the fold's own `trading_day` and needs no instant lookup **is a ruling about the engine's edge made from outside the engine**, by a row fenced out of `packages/rules-engine/**`. It is derived from `R-06` and from `rule_states.trading_day` being `NOT NULL`, and no code exists yet to contradict it. **If the founder would rather that edge be ruled by the slice that builds it, this section should be read as a recommendation and section 12's list gains a line.**

**AND ONE THING THIS ENTRY IS NOT A JUDGEMENT ABOUT.** Sections 2, 3, 10 and 11 are **findings**: every claim is a path and a line and any of them is refuted by opening the file, not by disagreeing.
