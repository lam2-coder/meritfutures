## ADR-106: a row whose subject is a PAIR of identities belongs to both and is scoped to neither  (2026-08-25, status: proposed)

**[ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 3 named this class and took none of it, on its own rule that a transcription rules nothing. [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) records the refusal in its own words for `identity_links` and `attributions`, [session 194](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-194.md) added `dedupe_matches` on the same ground, and [session 208](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-208.md) found `identity_merges` as a fourth and stopped at wanting a number for it.** Four sessions reached the same place and each stopped correctly: `OwnedRule.column` is ONE column, either choice returns a strict subset of a person's own rows, and on the two canonical-order tables the subset is **selected by UUID ordering**, which is a wrong answer that returns rows rather than an error. **This entry has the number and rules the CLASS, once, rather than three tables one at a time.**

- **Origin:** [P3 wave 3](../plans/P3-wave3-modules.md) section 9's `P3-h` row and section 15's prompt, session **215**. `ADR-106` was **RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH** in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) and not chosen here, which is that file's own remedy for the day three sessions took `103`.

- **Context:** it runs after [ADR-103](ADR-103.md), which is what makes the fourth table reachable at all: `otp_challenges` was refused by [ADR-094](ADR-094.md)'s one-member fold and session 214's report is that *"215 can proceed"*. ### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THERE IS A FIFTH SCOPE CLASS AND IT IS `pair`.** A row carrying TWO columns declared `REFERENCES identities(id)` reaches an identity through EITHER of them, and through both at once. `PairRule` names `columnA` and `columnB`, both asserted against the DDL, both asserted `NOT NULL`, and asserted DISTINCT from each other.
>
> **2. SUCH A ROW IS SCOPED TO NEITHER PARTY. `PairTableKey` IS EXCLUDED FROM `ScopedTableKey`.** The refusal is not that no predicate exists: `columnA = $1 OR columnB = $1` returns precisely the rows that are one person's and no others. It is that every row it returns carries the OTHER party's identity uuid out of a `NOT NULL` column, which is a cross-identity read.
>
> **3. IT IS EXCLUDED FROM `FirmTableKey` TOO, AND `systemDb(reason)` IS THE ONLY DOOR.** [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) clause 5 gives `firmDb()` no reason on the ground that *"no identity is at risk"*; on a pair row two are. A `pair` key is therefore a `TableKey` that is a member of NEITHER narrow type -- **the first key in this registry that is a member of neither** -- and `ScopedTableKey` and `FirmTableKey` no longer partition `TableKey`.
>
> **4. AN `owned` RULE MAY NOT STAND ON A ROW THAT DECLARES MORE THAN ONE IDENTITY COLUMN, and that is an assertion rather than a sentence.** It reads the folded DDL and it is what refuses the wrong answer four sessions declined to write.
>
> **5. A DERIVATION CHAIN DOES NOT TERMINATE AT A `pair` TABLE**, exactly as it does not terminate at a `firm` one. `DerivedRule.via` is `TableKey` and now includes every pair key, so such a rule COMPILES at every call site and throws the first time anybody reads it; the suite refuses it by name.
>
> **6. `otp_challenges` IS `firm`, AND IT IS THE FIRST FIRM ROW WHOSE REASON IS TIMING RATHER THAN OWNERSHIP.** Every other firm table holds a row no identity will EVER own. This one holds a row whose identity does not exist YET.

### 3. Why the vocabulary is five and not four with an exception

**[`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) declared the four classes as *"the four answers"* to one question and asserted the set was closed:** *"The question has exactly these four answers on this schema, which is what makes the vocabulary closed rather than merely short."* **That sentence was false when it was written, and the counter-example predates it by every migration in the tree**: `0002_identity.sql` created `identity_links` with two `NOT NULL` identity columns on the day the schema was written.

**The arithmetic that closes the set at five is over WHERE the identity is, not over what the tables happen to look like.** A row reaches an identity through: the row itself (`root`), one column of this row (`owned`), a column of ANOTHER row (`derived`), **two columns of this row (`pair`)**, or nothing (`firm`). **A sixth would have to be three columns of this row, and it is refused by measurement rather than by argument: zero tables in 111 declare three.** `correlation_groups` is the table that would be it and it reaches its members through `member_account_ids uuid[]`, which is an array of ACCOUNT ids and not identity columns at all -- so the arity-three case exists in this schema and does not arrive as a class.

**Adding a second column to `OwnedRule` instead was considered and is refused in section 11.**

### 4. The disclosure is what decides clause 2, and it is not the same question as clause 1

**Clause 1 is arithmetic and clause 2 is a judgement, and separating them is the point of writing them as two clauses.** The predicate exists, is one line, and is correct. **What it returns is the problem.**

`identity_links` carries `evidence jsonb NOT NULL` written by a detector, `confidence_bp`, `created_by` naming the detector, and the counterparty's uuid. `dedupe_matches` carries `match_strength`, `evidence_snapshot jsonb` from a biometric provider, a `disposition` that starts at `open` before any human has looked, and the counterparty's uuid. **[M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md)'s `INV-M4-06` is that the portal *"never receives detector internals or other identities' ids, so it cannot leak them"***, and its stated enforcement is `scopedDb(identity)` server side.

**THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT IS REAL AND IS ANSWERED RATHER THAN IGNORED.** `risk_flags`, `identity_signals`, `detector_runs`' output and `kyc_verifications` are all registered and all carry detector or provider material, and `risk_flags`' own `why` says the quiet part: *"REGISTERING A TABLE MAKES IT READABLE AND NOTHING ELSE, and this rule answers HOW A ROW REACHES AN IDENTITY rather than who may read it."* **That position is right and it is not disturbed here.** Every one of those tables names exactly ONE person. **What a pair row adds is not sensitivity, it is a SECOND SUBJECT**, and the second subject arrives as the tenancy column itself -- so it cannot be projected away by a reader without projecting away the thing that made the row reachable.

**A scoped read is a FILTER and not a PROJECTION**, which is the sentence the whole clause rests on. `scopedDb(id).rows(key)` is `SELECT *` with a `WHERE`. Registering a pair table under a disjunction would hand the projection the entire job of not disclosing the counterparty, **which is the position [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) scoped the accessor to stop relying on**, in [session 195](../sessions/2026-08-25-session-195.md)'s words about `events`.

### 7. `otp_challenges` is `firm` for a reason no other firm table has

**It carries no column referencing `identities` and there is no correct one.** `POST /auth/otp` is the only endpoint in [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) at required factor `none`, and `0002_identity.sql`'s own comment says the table keys off the normalized address *"rather than a user_id"* because **a user may not exist yet**.

**THE DISTINCTION IS WORTH A SENTENCE BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE A LATER SESSION WILL GET WRONG.** Every other `firm` table holds a row no identity will EVER own -- a published plan version, an exchange calendar day, a vendor file. **This one holds a row whose identity does not exist YET.** Consuming a challenge does not retroactively make the row that person's: `consumed_at` is a timestamp and there is nowhere to write an identity. **A session that wants this table scoped is proposing a MIGRATION and not a re-classification, and should say so.**

**[ADR-102](ADR-102.md) FORECLOSURE 4 IS TOUCHED AND NOT TRIPPED, which is stated here rather than left for a reader to worry about.** That clause says *"the day a `firm` table turns out to hold something identity-shaped, the fix is a per-table re-classification and not a reason"*. `email_normalized citext` is the address a person is reached at and `destination_hash bytea` is an SMS destination hashed: **both are identity-SHAPED and neither addresses a row in this database**, so there is no class to re-classify to. It is recorded as the closest this entry comes to that clause.

**It is [ADR-103](ADR-103.md)'s fold that makes the transcription possible and the first registered table to exercise the fold's second member.** `0029` writes `ALTER COLUMN email_normalized DROP NOT NULL` (SD-M16-05: an SMS challenge has no email address), and until this registration `foldTable(...).relaxed` was empty on every registered table -- session 214's own named landmine, *"the fold's second member runs on no registered table"*. **A new assertion holds that closed as a command rather than as a fact.**

### 8. What this FORECLOSES, which is the part every later slice inherits

1. **A trader-facing read of the link graph, the dedupe queue or an attribution row goes through a projection written by a named module, and never through the accessor. Permanently, until an ADR says otherwise.** Undoing it means giving `scopePredicate` a `pair` case that builds a disjunction, which is ten lines -- **so the control here is the CLASS and not the difficulty**, and a session that wants it should have to say so in an entry rather than in a diff.
2. **`ScopedTableKey ∪ FirmTableKey ≠ TableKey` from here.** Any code that assumed the two-way partition has to say which of THREE it means. The suite asserted that partition and now asserts the three-way one; a fourth door would break it again, which is the point.
3. **`firmDb()` will never serve a pair table**, and giving it one means giving `firmDb()` a reason, which [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) foreclosure 4 already prices as a breaking change at every call site.
4. **An INSERT into a pair table through a scoped handle is a compile error and stays one**, for `derived`'s reason arriving differently: the handle carries ONE identity and the row needs two, so there is nothing to stamp and nothing to guess.
5. **`affiliate_commissions` is not unblocked by this entry and the plan's own prompt says it is.** Section 10 reports the disagreement.
6. **A pair table is unreachable to every `apps/api` request handler**, because a handler is neither `'nightly-batch'` nor `'operator-console'` -- [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 7's standing hole, arriving on a class that has no `firmDb()` to fall back to. **It is not this entry's to close and it now has a second instance**: section 10.
7. **The `pair` class carries no `nullable` field and never will.** [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) clause 3's rule is that a field nothing reads and nothing checks is a second thing asserting itself; both columns are asserted `NOT NULL` against the migrations instead.

### 11. Alternatives considered

**A second column on `OwnedRule`, optional.** `column` plus `columnB?: string`, with `scopePredicate`'s `owned` case branching. **Refused**: it makes the two-identity case a variant of the one-identity case, so every assertion written for `owned` silently spans both, and the assertion in clause 4 -- the one that refuses the wrong answer -- **could not be written at all**, because a rule naming one column of two would be a well-formed `owned` rule. **The class is what makes the refusal expressible.**

**`firm`.** Refused mechanically and correctly by the suite's own *"no firm table carries a column referencing identities"*, which is the `firm` class's only promise. Passing the three under `firm` would have meant relaxing that assertion, which is weakening a gate to pass it.

**Register them `pair` AND serve them to `scopedDb` under the disjunction.** This is the reading the plan's candidate approval clause assumes, and it is the one section 4 and section 5 argue against. **It is one `case` away and stays one `case` away**, which is the honest description of how reversible this ruling is.

**Leave all three unregistered and rule only the class.** [ADR-094](ADR-094.md)'s own precedent, and it was seriously considered. **Refused because the cost was bounded and measurable here and was not there**: ADR-094 could not price its comparison's fallout at all, and this entry's fallout is two assertions in one test file and two cases in one source file, all of which were executed and watched. **A ruling that leaves the tables unreachable through EVERY door also leaves M06's console and M07's detectors with no way to read the link graph**, which is a real cost paid for nothing.
