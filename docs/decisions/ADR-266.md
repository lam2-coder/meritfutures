## ADR-266: the guard ADR-261 named is written, the list port's refusal is moved into the read arm so a deferral cannot decide it, and the second of the two card ports is wired (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-261](ADR-261.md) wired `useCertificateImageSource` and then reported that the port beside it waits on *"a GUARD rather than a VARIABLE"*, that *"the shape of that guard is settled and only the writing is owed"*, and that *"the next session at that port transcribes rather than decides"*. THIS ENTRY IS THAT TRANSCRIPTION, AND THE ROW REQUIRED THE GUARD TO BE RE-DERIVED AT SOURCE BEFORE ANYTHING WAS BUILT ON IT.** It was, in [`routes/certificates.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/certificates.ts), [`certificate-image-source.ts`](../../apps/api/src/certificate-image-source.ts) and [`routes/verify.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/verify.ts) rather than in the summary that named it, and section 1 is what that reading found. `useCertificateBackend` is **WIRED**: the wired count moves from **9** to **10** and the blocked list from **15** to **14**.

**THE REMEDY TRANSFERS, AND IT TRANSFERS WITH ONE DIFFERENCE THE ROW WAS RIGHT TO INSIST BE READ FOR.** [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) shut the image port's version of the trap by reading the whole configuration before the door was opened. `CertificateImageSource` has **one** read method, so "before the door" is one place. `CertificateBackend` has **two** methods and the second is **synchronous and called per rendered row**, so there is no single place: the guard goes in the READ arm, which every caller reaches, and the mint stays a pure function of an origin the guard has already accepted. Section 3 is that reading and section 3.1 is the alternative that was refused.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `266`, session **457**, dispatched as `claude/the-second-of-two-in-order`. The row is **RESERVED, REQUIRED**, and it is taken.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH.** No ledger account, no balance, no payout, no `SystemReason`. `E2` is not owed and none is inherited. **NO MIGRATION IS RESERVED, TAKEN OR SPENT** and `git diff origin/main -- packages/db/` is empty; the next free number is still `0075`, which is [ADR-249](ADR-249.md) section 4's ruling holding rather than being re-derived. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That a deployment which has not set the origin refuses a caller whose certificates are all deferred and the caller beside them whose certificate issued **identically**, and that the scoped door is **never opened** on either; that the same two callers are both **served** once it is set; that five shapes of unusable origin refuse alike and that the refusal names the variable and never its value; that the two minted URLs are `verify.ts`' own path and this file's own image path under one base path; and that a code carrying a URL delimiter is encoded rather than pasted, is **MEASURED**, by executing it. That the two entries expire in ORDER with this one second is **DERIVED** from [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) ruling 13 at the clause it names. What is **JUDGED** is that the image port's remedy transfers (section 3), that the origin is now a gap [ADR-226](ADR-226.md) and [ADR-229](ADR-229.md) permit wiring on (section 4), that a cleartext origin is refused (section 2 clause 5), and that the origin is a new name rather than a borrowed one (section 2 clause 4).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every clause the row quotes was re-read in [ADR-261](ADR-261.md), [ADR-256](ADR-256.md), [ADR-249](ADR-249.md), [ADR-246](ADR-246.md) and [ADR-240](ADR-240.md) themselves, and every line citation was opened before anything was built on it.

### 1. What the guard actually is, re-derived at source rather than from the summary

**THE ROW ASKED FOR THIS FIRST AND IT IS NOT A RESTATEMENT.** [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) section 5 clause 11 says the missing thing is *"a check that reads the origin BEFORE the caller's rows are read, and refuses the whole request"*. That sentence names a **timing** and does not name a **place**, and the place is the whole of the work here.

**THE MECHANISM, READ OFF THE THREE FILES THAT HOLD IT:**

| Fact | Where it is, at source |
|---|---|
| `links` is called PER RENDERED ROW and only for a row that is not deferred | `projectCertificate` returns `{ code: null, verify_url: null, image_url: null }` on `state === 'deferred'` **before** it reaches `const minted = links(row.code)`. There is no other path |
| So a refusal raised in `links` is reachable only by a caller who owns an ISSUED or REVOKED row | [ADR-168](ADR-168.md) foreclosure 4, obtained structurally rather than by a trailing conditional |
| `readCertificates` is called on EVERY request, before any row state is known | `CERTIFICATE_LIST_ENDPOINTS`' handler awaits it, then calls `renderCertificates` with `(code) => wired.links(code)` |
| Both arms are read off ONE capture | `const wired = backend`, so a `useCertificateBackend` call landing mid-request cannot split the two arms across two backends |

**THE GUARD IS THEREFORE: RESOLVE THE ORIGIN INSIDE `readCertificates`, BEFORE `db.scoped` IS CALLED.** That is the only point in this port that every caller reaches and that no row has yet been read at. It is not the factory: a refusal at construction time would abort the process at import, which takes down every other route in the deployable including the wired ones, and no port in this tree does that.

**AND THE SHAPE WAS INDEED SETTLED IN TWO FILES, WHICH THE READING CONFIRMED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** [`routes/verify.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/verify.ts)'s handler resolves `presentation()` before the lookup, in that file's own words *"read before the lookup and validated in full"*; [`certificate-image-source.ts`](../../apps/api/src/certificate-image-source.ts)'s `lookup` calls `readCertificateImageConfig(env)` on its first line, above `db.publicLookup`, with the comment *"BEFORE THE DOOR IS OPENED"*. **What neither of them has is a second arm**, and that is section 3.

### 2. The origin, and the five refusals

> **1. THE NAME IS `MERIT_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, NAMED HERE AND VALUED NOWHERE** ([ADR-012](ADR-012.md)). It is the origin of the public surface **this deployable itself serves**, which is what both link fields address: [ADR-249](ADR-249.md) section 2.4 ruled `image_url` is *"`origin` plus the path this repository already serves, derived from `code` at projection time"*, and [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 6.3 says of `GET /verify/:code` that *"This is the page `verify_url` addresses"*. Two rows, one process, one base path, one origin.
>
> **2. ONE VARIABLE FOR BOTH FIELDS, WHICH IS [ADR-249](ADR-249.md)'s OWN WORDING.** Its owed list rows *"an origin variable for BOTH link fields"*, and `CertificateLinks` has no nullable half: `projectCertificate` runs both through `assertUrl`, so one refusing field refuses the whole response and a second variable would buy a second way to half-configure it.
>
> **3. [ADR-240](ADR-240.md) CLAUSE 10 IS NOT OVERTURNED, ITS CONDITION HAS LIFTED, AND IT SAID SO ITSELF.** That clause held that *"a variable whose only consumer refuses is a name nothing reads"* and put the name with *"the slice that has a card to point it at"*. [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) landed the renderer and [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) installed the composition, so the row `image_url` addresses **answers**, and the sentence [ADR-249](ADR-249.md) section 5 built on it, *"publishing a link to a trader is publishing a promise that bytes are there"*, has expired. **This is the same lift `MERIT_CERTIFICATE_CARD_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` took one entry ago**, under the same clause, for the same reason.
>
> **4. IT IS NOT `PORTAL_ORIGIN` AND THE REUSE WAS CONSIDERED.** [INFRA](../architecture/INFRA.md) section 2.1 puts the `api` service and the `portal` service on the same host, so the two values will usually be equal, and reusing the name would have removed a way for a deployment to set two names for one host and have them disagree. **It is refused because INFRA section 7 scopes environment variables PER SERVICE**: `PORTAL_ORIGIN` is declared in [`apps/admin/src/origin.ts`](../../apps/admin/src/origin.ts) for the admin console's `INV-M6-02` separation check and nothing declares it for this service, so borrowing it would be this file asserting a deployment fact no approved document carries. **The drift risk is real and is named rather than closed**; it is in section 7.
>
> **5. FIVE SHAPES REFUSE AND ONE OF THE FIVE IS A JUDGEMENT.** Absent or blank, not a URL, and carrying a path, a query or a fragment are each [`apps/admin/src/origin.ts`](../../apps/admin/src/origin.ts)' `parseOrigin` transcribed, and the third earns its place here for a reason that file does not have: anything after the host would land **ahead of** `BASE_PATH` and mint an address this deployable does not serve. **THE JUDGEMENT IS THAT CLEARTEXT IS REFUSED.** These two fields are pasted by a trader into a share sheet and opened by a third party who uses them to check a claim about Merit, and `INV-M11-02` makes the row the authority; a cleartext link is one anybody on the path can point at their own page. **THE COST IS NAMED AND IT IS NOT ZERO: a local deployment on a cleartext loopback origin is refused and answers 503 on this row.** The alternative considered was a scheme exception keyed on the hostname, and a rule that admits cleartext for one host is a rule that gets widened for the next one.
>
> **6. THE REFUSAL NAMES THE VARIABLE AND NEVER ITS VALUE**, because this message reaches a log. That is asserted rather than reviewed.
>
> **7. THE ACCEPTED VALUE IS `URL.origin` AND NEVER THE RAW STRING**, so a trailing slash cannot reach the concatenation and produce a double one.

### 3. Whether the image port's remedy transfers, which the row asked to be established by reading

**IT TRANSFERS, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THE PORT'S SHAPE RATHER THAN THE TRAP'S.**

| | `CertificateImageSource` | `CertificateBackend` |
|---|---|---|
| The arm every caller reaches | `lookup(code)` | `readCertificates(session)` |
| The arm a deferral does not reach | the render, inside `lookup` | `links(code)`, called by `projectCertificate` |
| Is the second arm a separate method? | **No.** Both are inside one call | **YES**, and it is synchronous and per row |
| Where the configuration is read | first line of `lookup` | first line of `readCertificates` |
| What the second arm does about it | the render runs `assertCopy` again | `links` resolves the same origin again |

**THE ONE THING THAT DOES NOT TRANSFER IS "READ IT ONCE".** `readCertificateImageConfig` runs once per request because the port has one method to run it in. Here there is no channel from the read arm to the mint: the port's interface passes a `code` and nothing else, by [ADR-249](ADR-249.md) clause 6's deliberate design, *"so no other field of the row can reach the address even by accident"*. **So the origin is resolved TWICE**, and that is safe for a stated reason rather than by convenience: both readings are the same pure function of the same `Environment`, so the second cannot refuse what the first accepted. **It is also precedented in the file [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) wrote**, whose `readCertificateImageConfig` lifts `assertCopy` out of the render and then lets the render run it again, on the ground that *"a check that runs twice is cheaper than the oracle"*.

#### 3.1 The two alternatives, and why each is worse

**(a) MEMOISE THE ORIGIN INSIDE THE ADAPTER, resolved on the first `readCertificates`.** It removes the second read. It also introduces state that outlives a request in an object `start.ts` constructs once, so a deployment whose variable changed would serve the old origin until restart, and a suite would have to reason about which request warmed it. **A per-request pure read has none of that and costs one `new URL`.**

**(b) RESOLVE THE ORIGIN IN THE FACTORY.** It is the strongest possible coupling and it is refused: `databaseCertificateBackend(LIVE_DB)` runs at module scope in [`start.ts`](../../apps/api/src/start.ts), so an unset variable would throw during import and the process would not start at all. **That converts one row's 503 into every row's outage**, including the nine ports wired above it, and no port in this deployable behaves that way. [ADR-226](ADR-226.md)'s rule is that an absent secret is a **refusal**, and a refusal is a response.

### 4. Whether the port is wireable now, which the row asked for explicitly

**YES, AND THE ARGUMENT IS [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) SECTION 5's OWN, WITH ITS ONE REMAINING TERM DISCHARGED.** That section's table listed five things the port waited on and marked four **GONE**, leaving *"an origin"* as a deployment fact and clause 11 adding *"and a guard, and the guard is CODE"*. [ADR-226](ADR-226.md) and [ADR-229](ADR-229.md) permit wiring when the only remaining gap is a thing the deployment sets. **The guard is written, so the only remaining gap is the origin, and an origin is one.**

**THE DISTINCTION [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) RULING 12 DREW IS HELD RATHER THAN ERODED ON THE WAY PAST**, which matters because this entry is the one that benefits from erasing it. That ruling says a port is *"one SLICE from wireable rather than one VARIABLE from wired"* when the gap is code. **This entry did not reclassify the guard as configuration; it wrote the guard.** The `BLOCKED` list has now shrunk twice on this artefact and neither entry closed on a variable, and [`wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts)' header says so in those terms so the next reader tempted to close a remaining entry by naming an environment variable meets that sentence first.

**AND THE ORDER RUNS THE RIGHT WAY ROUND, WHICH IS THE HALF WORTH CHECKING RATHER THAN ASSUMING.** [ADR-246](ADR-246.md) held the two ports expire *"together or not at all"*; [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) ruling 13 narrowed that to *"they expire in ORDER"* with this one downstream. **Wired the other way round, this row would have published `image_url` to every trader with an issued certificate while the row it addresses answered 503**, which is the sentence that kept it shut. That pair is now an assertion rather than prose: [`certificate-image-source.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/certificate-image-source.test.ts) fails if the list backend is installed without the image source.

### 7. Findings, reported rather than repaired

> **8. `verify_url` ADDRESSES A JSON ROW AND THE CONTRACT ALSO CALLS IT A PAGE.** [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 6.3 heads `GET /verify/:code` *"The public verification page's data"* and, in the same subsection, says of that row *"This is the page `verify_url` addresses"*. **There is no HTML page anywhere in this tree**, `routes/verify.ts` is a JSON endpoint under `BASE_PATH`, and it is the only thing in this repository that answers on that path. **So the link this entry mints is the row the contract points it at, and it is the row rather than the page**; inventing an origin for a page that does not exist would have been worse, and building the page is not in this fence. It is reported here and in the pull request.
>
> **9. TWO SPELLINGS OF ONE PATH EXIST AND THEY ARE BOUND BY A TEST RATHER THAN BY AN IMPORT.** `routes/verify.ts` imports `narrowClaims`, `CERTIFICATE_KINDS` and `REVOCATION_CLASSES` from `routes/certificates.ts` at run time, so importing `VERIFY_PATH` back would be a real module cycle rather than an erased type edge. `certificates.ts` therefore writes `/verify/:code` out a second time, and [`certificates.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/certificates.test.ts) asserts the minted link equals `VERIFY_PATH` with the token substituted. **A seed that moved one spelling turned three cases red.** The clean repair is a shared constants module, and that is a file this fence does not include.
>
> **10. NOTHING IN THIS TREE CAN CHECK `MERIT_PUBLIC_ORIGIN` AGAINST `PORTAL_ORIGIN`.** [`apps/admin/src/origin.ts`](../../apps/admin/src/origin.ts) checks its own origin against the public ones **when they are present** and reports the check as NOT RUN when they are not, on this repository's own rule. The equivalent here would compare two variables scoped to two different services, which INFRA section 7's per-service scope makes a thing no single process can observe. **The exposure is a deployment that sets the two to different hosts and publishes links to the wrong one**; nothing here detects it.
>
> **11. THE PORT INTERFACE STILL PERMITS A HALF-WIRING AND THE CASE THAT MEASURES IT IS KEPT.** `CertificateBackend`'s two arms can be composed by hand into a live read beside a refusing signer, and [`certificate-ports.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/certificate-ports.test.ts) still executes that it answers by the state of the caller's rows. **What changed is that `databaseCertificateBackend` cannot BE that backend**; the property is now a fact about the adapter rather than about the type.

### 9. Consequences

- **A trader's certificates page can be served by a real deployment line**, and the two share links on it address rows this same process answers.
- **The `BLOCKED` list has shrunk twice on one artefact, in the order [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) ruling 13 set**, and [ADR-246](ADR-246.md)'s set reading is now fully discharged rather than partly.
- **Neither of the two closed on a variable**, and `wiring.test.ts`' header says so, which is the sentence that protects the remaining fourteen entries from being closed by naming one.
- **A misconfigured deployment refuses every caller of this row identically and never opens the scoped door**, so no response on this surface carries information about which certificates the caller holds.
- **The image row's exposure is now driven by the list row.** The PNG encode [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) put on an unauthenticated path was reachable only by somebody who already held a code; it is now advertised to every trader with an issued certificate, and the rate limit still does not exist.
- **One more approved-contract sentence is reported and unrepaired for the fifth entry running**, which is a repair that keeps not fitting inside anybody's fence.

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST IS THE ORDERING QUESTION [ADR-261](ADR-261.md) ASKED YOU AND THIS ENTRY HAS MADE LOUDER.** That entry wired a PNG encode onto an unauthenticated public path with `INV-M11-05`'s rate limit still unbuilt, called that a degree argument, and offered a one-line revert. **This entry is what makes the degree matter**: until now the image row was reachable only by somebody who already held a code, and from here its address is handed to every trader who opens their certificates page and to everyone they share it with. The reasoning taken is that the two rows were always one artefact and shipping the address without the bytes was the worse order, which [ADR-256](ADR-256.md) ruling 13 settled. **If you would rather this row stayed at 503 until the limit exists, the revert is one line in [`start.ts`](../../apps/api/src/start.ts)** and everything else here stands: the guard, its refusals and its suite are all still there, one line from live.

  **THE SECOND IS THE CLEARTEXT REFUSAL, WHICH IS A JUDGEMENT AND HAS A COST YOU MAY NOT WANT TO PAY.** `certificateOrigin` refuses a non-https origin, so a local or preview deployment on a cleartext loopback host answers 503 on `GET /certificates` and a developer meets a refusal rather than a page. The reasoning is that both fields leave this process in a trader's share sheet and are opened by third parties checking a claim about Merit, and [`apps/admin/src/origin.ts`](../../apps/admin/src/origin.ts) already refuses cleartext for its own stated reason. **The counter-argument is that `preview` and `dev` are real environments in [INFRA](../architecture/INFRA.md) section 3 and this makes one row of the contract unexercisable in them.** It is one condition either way.

  **THE THIRD IS THE VARIABLE'S NAME, WHICH IS A DEPLOYMENT DECISION THIS SESSION MADE FROM A DOCUMENT.** Section 2 clause 4 refuses to reuse `PORTAL_ORIGIN` because INFRA scopes variables per service, and the price is that a deployment now sets two names for one host with nothing able to compare them. **If your platform sets one value for the app origin and injects it everywhere, the reuse is free and this entry has bought you a drift risk for a boundary that does not exist in your vault.** Renaming the constant is a one-line change in one file and one test.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **Section 3's double read of the environment.** The guard runs in `readCertificates` and the mint resolves the origin again, and the argument that the second cannot refuse what the first accepted rests on `Environment` not changing within a request. **Nothing in this tree enforces that**; it is a property of `process.env` being left alone, and a future adapter that recomputed configuration per call would break it silently rather than loudly. The alternatives are in section 3.1 and both are worse for reasons stated there, but if you want the property to be structural rather than argued, the change is to widen `CertificateBackend`'s `links` to receive what the read arm resolved, which is a **port shape** change and therefore yours rather than this session's.
