## ADR-280: the wire pair is correct and the wire pair was undeclared, so nothing is renamed and a validator that promised RFC 3339 and checked `isNaN` becomes the refusal it always said it was (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-276](ADR-276.md) ruled `effective_from`'s nine columns a correct distinction and then named, in its own section 8, the one thing its fence could not reach: _"`effective_from` is a live response field twice, a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` on a PUBLIC page and an RFC 3339 instant on the admin surface, and [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2 reaches neither."_ This row is that fence. It asked whether ONE WIRE NAME CARRYING TWO WIRE TYPES IS A DEFECT, and it forbade answering it from the storage ruling's conclusion.**

**THE WIRE PAIR IS CORRECT AND NEITHER FIELD IS RENAMED. THE DEFECT IS NOT THE NAME AND IT IS NOT THE PAIR: IT IS THAT ONLY ONE OF THE TWO SITES EVER SAID WHICH TYPE IT HELD, AND THAT THE SITE WHICH SAID NOTHING IS ALSO THE ONE THAT PARSES A CALLER'S STRING.** The public site declares `YYYY-MM-DD` in its own field comment. The admin site declared neither shape nor vocabulary in the contract and validated with `Number.isNaN`. **That is [ADR-276](ADR-276.md)'s OWN asymmetry finding, one layer up: six of nine columns declared and three silent, and here one of two wire sites declared and one silent. The repair is the same shape too, a declaration rather than a rename, plus the one thing the schema layer never had, which is an INPUT.**

**AND THE INPUT HALF IS WORSE THAN EITHER PRIOR ENTRY RECORDED. THE ROW NAMED TWO ADMITTED SHAPES AND THERE ARE THREE.** `new Date('2026-02-30T00:00:00Z')` is **not** `Invalid Date`. It is `2026-03-02T00:00:00.000Z`. **A date parser does not refuse a day-of-month past the end of its month, it ROLLS IT OVER**, so an operator naming February 30 wrote a spend limit dated March 2 onto `C-23`'s append-only record and nothing anywhere said so. That was found by writing the test rather than by reading, it is recorded by no prior entry, and it is the sharpest of the three.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `280`, session **471**, branch `claude/a-day-on-the-wire-and-an-instant-on-the-wire`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED AND NO `RI-nn` IS MINTED.** `git diff origin/main -- packages/db/migrations` is empty on this branch and `packages/db/**` is out of fence by the row's own words. No `packages/**` file is touched at all.

- **MONEY-ADJACENT BY BLAST RADIUS AND THE `E2` READ IS OWED RATHER THAN CLAIMED.** No ledger account, no balance and no payout moves, and this endpoint writes no money. What it writes is `wallet_spend_limits`, which is `SECURITY` `C-23`'s control on a compromised session and is the append-only record of what limit was in force when, and `POST /checkout` binds against it ([`checkout.ts:645`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts), _"`effective_from` that has ARRIVED"_). **A limit dated wrong is a limit that binds on the wrong days.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **BOTH OF THE ROW'S TRAPS ARE UPHELD RATHER THAN WORKED AROUND.** No live response field is renamed and no wire vocabulary is tidied, because a wire name is a contract. And nothing here is ruled fine on the strength of [ADR-276](ADR-276.md)'s conclusion: **that entry's ruling is used only as the TEST it offered** (does the type difference track a real difference in what the value means?), applied to what a consumer sees, and the answer at the wire is reached from the two responses rather than from the nine columns.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. NEITHER FIELD IS RENAMED AND THE WIRE PAIR IS CORRECT AS IT STANDS.** Section 3, argued from what a consumer sees. The row offered this as a complete and welcome outcome and it is the one the two surfaces support, but **only for the NAME**: it is not a ruling that the pair was fine, because one half of it was undeclared and unguarded and both are repaired here.
>
> **2. [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) CLAUSE 4 IS LIFTED FROM THE SCHEMA TO THE WIRE, AND IT IS THE RULE THIS ENTRY ADDS.** That clause reads _"a silent name that denotes more than one temporal type must declare which one it holds at every site, in the design record, or be renamed."_ On the wire it reads: **a request or response field whose name carries neither `*_at` nor `*_day`/`*_on` states its temporal type in its own comment, at every site where the field appears, or is renamed.** Written at [API_CONTRACT:37](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md). **The reader's seat at the schema is the DDL; at the wire it is the contract, because a consumer never sees a migration.**
>
> **3. IT BINDS EVERY FIELD ADDED FROM HERE AND THE FIELDS ALREADY ON THE SURFACE ARE BROUGHT TO IT ONE ENTRY AT A TIME.** `effective_from` is declared at both of its sites in this diff. **`period_start` and `period_end` are named as owed and are NOT surveyed**, because reading every unsuffixed temporal field on this surface is a survey this row did not fence and asserting a rule over fields nobody has read is the move [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) section 4 refused in the other direction. Section 9.
>
> **4. `instantField` REFUSES WHAT ITS OWN MESSAGE HAS ALWAYS PROMISED, AND THAT IS NOT A NEW RULE BUT AN ENFORCED ONE.** Its rejection read _"is required and must be an RFC 3339 timestamp"_ while it accepted three strings RFC 3339 does not admit as a `date-time`. [`admin-wallet.ts:1092`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) is the pattern and [`:1128`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) is the parser. Section 5.
>
> **5. A BARE DAY IS REFUSED AND IS NOT READ AS UTC MIDNIGHT, AND THIS IS THE HALF THE ROW CALLED SHARPEST.** It is reachable: the handler reads `asRecord(request.body) ?? {}` at [`admin-wallet.ts:1543`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) and there is no schema before it, so `instantField` IS the validation. **A validator was admitting a value it was written to refuse, and that is a finding independent of how the naming question rules**, which is what the row said it would be.
>
> **6. A DATE-TIME CARRYING NO OFFSET IS REFUSED**, which closes [ADR-274](ADR-274.md) clause 6's reported-and-not-repaired item at the file that entry could not reach. One pattern serves both halves, which its founder-read note predicted in the words _"one guard fixes both."_
>
> **7. A DAY THAT DOES NOT EXIST IS REFUSED, BY A ROUND TRIP AND NOT BY THE PATTERN, BECAUSE A DATE PARSER DOES NOT REFUSE ONE.** Section 5. This is the third shape and no prior entry names it.

### 3. Why the wire pair is NOT renamed, argued from what a consumer sees

**THE ROW FORBADE RULING THIS FROM [ADR-276](ADR-276.md)'s CONCLUSION, SO ITS CONCLUSION IS NOT USED. ITS TEST IS.** That entry ruled the schema pair correct because _"the type difference tracks a real difference in what the row means"_, decidable at each table from its own DDL. **The honest question here is whether a consumer of these two responses gets two shapes under one key with nothing telling it which.** Four things were established before the answer was written, and three of them are measurements.

**FIRST, THERE IS NO CONSUMER OF EITHER FIELD IN THIS REPOSITORY.** `grep -rn "effective_from" apps/admin/src apps/site` returns nothing, and neither does a search for `spend-limit` or `spendLimit` in those trees. **The admin console does not call this endpoint and no page renders the method version's date.** So the "consumer parsing both endpoints" is hypothetical today, which is a fact that must be stated in the direction that weakens this entry's own convenience: **it means the rename question cannot be settled by exhibiting a broken client, and it also means a rename would cost nothing to break.** Neither side gets to win on cost.

**SECOND, THE TWO AUDIENCES ARE DISJOINT BY AUTHORIZATION AND BY ORIGIN, NOT MERELY BY CONVENTION.** The public read is `Auth: none`. The admin write requires `admin_sso`, a role in `{owner, ops}`, the admin origin and an allowlisted IP ([API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). **A caller in a position to send this field cannot have learned the vocabulary by accident**: they hold an operator session, and the endpoint that carries the other spelling is one a member of the public reads.

**THIRD, EACH WIRE TYPE IS FAITHFUL TO THE COLUMN IT RENDERS, AND THAT IS THE PROPERTY A RENAME WOULD DAMAGE RATHER THAN PROTECT.** [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 1 rules that a day is never derived from an instant anywhere on the API surface, and the converse failure is the one available here: **renaming the admin field to `effective_from_at` would satisfy a suffix rule and would not change one byte of what a consumer receives**, while renaming the public field to `effective_from_on` would assert an **exchange trading day** ([API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), _"`*_day` and `*_on` are `YYYY-MM-DD` exchange trading days, never UTC dates"_) about a column [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) clause 5 measured as `**Unit: wall clock**`. **The tidy rename is not neutral. It is a false claim.** [ADR-272](ADR-272.md) clause 3, as [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) records it, is that a name may be SILENT about its temporal type and may not be FALSE about it; `effective_from_on` would be the second.

**FOURTH, A WIRE NAME IS A CONTRACT AND THIS CONTRACT SAYS WHAT CHANGING ONE COSTS.** [API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md): _"Version is in the path; a breaking change means `/api/v2`, never a silent shape change."_ A rename of a live response field is the breaking change that sentence exists to price, taken to buy legibility that a declaration buys for the price of a comment.

**SO THE RULE IS THE SAME ONE [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) REACHED AND THE GROUND IS DIFFERENT, WHICH IS THE POINT.** That entry ruled from the DDL, which a wire consumer cannot see. This one rules from the two responses, the two audiences, and what each rename would assert. **Had the third fact gone the other way, had either field been renderable under the other's suffix without lying, the answer here would have been a rename and a `/v2`.**

### 4. What was actually broken, which is the declaration and not the pair

**THE COLLISION IS NOT THE DEFECT. THE SILENCE IS.** That is [ADR-276](ADR-276.md)'s sentence and it survives being carried one layer up, which is worth noting because it did not have to.

| Layer                                       | Sites that declared                                                                     | Sites that said nothing                                                                  | What enforces the declaration          |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Schema** ([ADR-276](ADR-276.md) clause 5) | the six `date` columns, each carrying `**Unit: wall clock**`                            | the three `timestamptz` columns                                                          | `CI-06m`, which reads `date` rows only |
| **Wire** (this entry, before it)            | `GET /public/methods/:statCode`, at [API_CONTRACT:317](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) | `POST /admin/wallet/:identityId/spend-limit`, at both its request and its response field | **nothing**                            |

**THE WIRE'S VERSION IS WORSE THAN THE SCHEMA'S FOR ONE REASON AND IT IS NOT THE COUNT.** A silent `timestamptz` column is read by a Merit author who can open the migration. **A silent request field is filled in by a caller who cannot**, and the caller's guess is then durable: it lands in `wallet_spend_limits` and in `admin_actions.after`, both append-only, both revoked from `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on `merit_app` and `PUBLIC` by `0026`. **A misdeclared column is a legibility problem. A misparsed input is a record that cannot be corrected.**

**AND [ADR-276](ADR-276.md) SECTION 6'S REFUSAL DOES NOT TRANSFER, WHICH IS WHY THIS ENTRY WRITES A DECLARATION WHERE THAT ONE WROTE PROSE.** There, a `**Unit:**` marker on a `timestamptz` row would have been _"prose wearing a control's clothes"_, because `CI-06m` reads that marker on `date` rows only. **Here the declaration in the contract is backed by an executable refusal in the same commit**, so the comment at [API_CONTRACT:1405](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) is not a claim nothing reads: [`admin-wallet.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/admin-wallet.test.ts) is what reads it, and section 7 is the transcript.

### 5. The validator, its three admitted shapes, and the one no entry had named

**`instantField` IS THE ONLY CALLER-SUPPLIED INSTANT ON THE WHOLE API SURFACE.** That is [ADR-274](ADR-274.md) section 5's census, re-read at this commit rather than carried in: that section's table reads every `new Date(s)` site in the tree and finds one where `s` comes from _"the operator's HTTP request body"_. **Reachability is re-derived here rather than taken from it.** [`admin-wallet.ts:1543`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) is `const body = asRecord(request.body) ?? {};`: the body is raw JSON, no schema runs before the handler, and [`:1875`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) passes it straight to `instantField`. **There is nothing between a caller's string and this function.**

**WHAT IT REFUSED WAS `Number.isNaN` AND WHAT IT PROMISED WAS RFC 3339.** RFC 3339's `date-time` is `full-date "T" full-time` and its `full-time` is `partial-time time-offset`, so a `date-time` requires both a time and an offset. This estate has already transcribed that twice: [`internal.ts:238`](../../apps/api/src/routes/internal.ts) requires `Z` or an explicit offset under the comment _"An ISO 8601 instant with an explicit zone. Refused rather than coerced"_, and [`economic-calendar.ts:177`](../../apps/api/src/routes/economic-calendar.ts) requires `Z`. **Both of those transcriptions require a `T` and a time. Neither of them was on the one site a caller could reach.**

**THE THREE SHAPES, EACH MEASURED AT THIS COMMIT RATHER THAN REASONED.**

| #   | Body value               | `new Date(...)` before this entry                                                                                                                   | Why it is wrong                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | `"2026-09-01"`           | `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z` at **every** zone (run at `UTC`, `America/Chicago`, `Europe/Berlin`, `Asia/Kolkata`, `Asia/Tokyo`, `Pacific/Kiritimati`) | **A DAY PROMOTED TO AN INSTANT.** Not a wrong instant, which is what makes it quiet: a date-only ISO string is UTC by specification, so this is stable and stored and echoed with a time of day the caller never sent. **And a day is precisely the shape the same field name carries on the public methods page** |
| 2   | `"2026-09-01T00:00:00"`  | five instants at five zones, spanning nineteen hours and two calendar days ([ADR-274](ADR-274.md) section 5)                                        | **READ AS LOCAL.** ECMAScript reads a date-time with no offset against the process timezone, which [ADR-274](ADR-274.md) clause 1 established nothing else in this estate consults                                                                                                                                 |
| 3   | `"2026-02-30T00:00:00Z"` | **`2026-03-02T00:00:00.000Z`**                                                                                                                      | **A DAY THAT DOES NOT EXIST, ROLLED OVER RATHER THAN REFUSED.** No prior entry names this                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

**SHAPE 3 IS THE ONE THIS SESSION FOUND AND IT WAS FOUND BY WRITING THE TEST RATHER THAN BY READING THE CODE.** The assertion was written expecting `Number.isNaN` to have covered it, and it went red. **The parser's leniency is not uniform and that is what makes it dangerous**: `2026-13-01T00:00:00Z`, `2026-09-01T25:00:00Z`, `2026-09-01T23:99:00Z` and `2026-09-01T00:00:00+99:00` are all `Invalid Date`, so a reader checking two or three cases concludes the parser is strict. **It is strict about the month, the hour, the minute, the second and the offset, and it ROLLS OVER on the day of the month.** `2026-09-31` becomes October 1 and `2026-02-29` becomes March 1.

**SO THE GUARD IS TWO CHECKS AND NEITHER IS REDUNDANT, WHICH IS STATED IN THE CODE RATHER THAN LEFT TO A READER.** The pattern at [`admin-wallet.ts:1092`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) refuses shapes `Date` accepts (1, 2, and a `24:00:00` that rolls into the next day, which RFC 3339's `time-hour` excludes). The round trip at [`:1158`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) refuses a day `Date` accepts and MOVES (3). **The round trip is on the date part alone at UTC midnight**, because a value carrying a legitimate offset may put its instant on a different UTC day from the one written, and comparing the parsed instant's UTC date against the string would refuse `2026-09-01T05:00:00+05:00` as a calendar error.

**WHAT IT COSTS TO HAVE HAD NONE OF THIS.** `wallet_spend_limits` is keyed `(identity_id, effective_from)` and a limit is an APPEND, which the handler's own refusal says at [`admin-wallet.ts:1912`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts). **The key is the record.** `POST /checkout` binds the limit whose `effective_from` has arrived ([`checkout.ts:645`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts), [`:974`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)), so an instant written two days off the one an operator named is a control that starts or stops binding on the wrong days, during exactly the incident `SECURITY` `C-23` describes.

### 6. Why the offset rule here is not [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 3, and the case for copying it anyway

**THE ONE JUDGEMENT IN THE GUARD IS THAT `+05:00` IS ADMITTED.** [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 3 is _"AN `*_at` IS SPELLED WITH `Z` AND NEVER WITH AN OFFSET, INCLUDING `+00:00`"_, and [`economic-calendar.ts:177`](../../apps/api/src/routes/economic-calendar.ts)'s pattern implements it. Copying that pattern here would have been one character of diff and it is not taken.

**THE REASON IS THE DIRECTION AND IT IS THAT CLAUSE'S OWN REASON READ FORWARDS.** Its stated ground is that _"two spellings of one instant is a normalisation step every consumer has to write and a second place the timezone question gets answered"_, and a consumer is who reads a RESPONSE. **This value is not read by a consumer. It is written by one**, and it is normalised on the way out by the `toISOString()` at [`admin-wallet.ts:1901`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts), whose value is what both the row and the response at [`:1967`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) carry, and which renders `Z` and only `Z`. **So the response side already satisfies clause 3 without the input side refusing anything**, and the assertion that it does is in the suite: the `+05:00` case asserts the response and the inserted row both carry `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

**AND THE FAILURE THAT WAS MEASURED IS AN ABSENT OFFSET, NOT A NON-ZERO ONE.** [ADR-274](ADR-274.md) section 5's five-zone spread is entirely the consequence of a string with no offset at all. `2026-09-01T05:00:00+05:00` is stable at every process timezone and names the instant the operator meant. **Refusing it would refuse a correct value to tidy a spelling, on an endpoint whose whole defect was refusing too little.**

**THE CASE FOR THE OTHER ANSWER, STATED BECAUSE IT IS NOT WEAK.** A `Z`-only input rule is simpler to hold in a head, it makes this pattern identical to the two the estate already carries, and an operator client that can produce an offset can produce `Z`. **A reviewer who prefers it is not making an error**, and the cost of the stricter rule is bounded and visible: the `+05:00` assertion in the suite is the one that would flip. It is section 11's second founder question.

### 8. A THIRD wire site for this name exists, it is empty today, and no guard reaches it

**THIS IS NOT IN THE ROW AND IT IS THE THING MOST LIKELY TO MAKE THIS ENTRY'S RULING STALE, SO IT IS NAMED.** `admin_actions.before` and `admin_actions.after` are opaque JSON and **they reach a response**: [`admin-source/account.ts:589`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/account.ts) projects `before: json(row, 'before', at)` and `after: json(row, 'after', at)` onto the account drill-down, and this handler writes `effective_from` into both ([`admin-wallet.ts:1938`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) and [`:1944`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts)).

**IT IS EMPTY TODAY AND THAT IS MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** The only read of that table in the estate is [`account.ts:715`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/account.ts), `rowsWhere('adminActions', { subjectKind: 'account', subjectId: accountId })`, and this handler audits with `subjectKind: 'identity'`, which [`admin-wallet.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/admin-wallet.test.ts) asserts. **So the spend limit's audit rows do not reach that response**, and no other admin write in the tree records an `effective_from` under an account subject.

**WHAT MAKES IT WORTH WRITING DOWN IS THAT NOTHING WOULD REPORT IT CHANGING.** `before` and `after` are `json`, so every field inside them is outside every projection guard this surface has, clause 2's declaration rule included: **a comment cannot be written on a key that does not appear in any type.** An admin write that audited a `date`-typed `effective_from` under `subject_kind = 'account'` would put a day and an instant under one key on one response, and this entry's rule would not have been violated because the rule cannot reach there. Section 9.
