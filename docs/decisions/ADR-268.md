## ADR-268: the day is a named door, because a catalogue read would hand the payout route R-06 itself and a second transaction would compute a verdict from a calendar the recording transaction never read  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-264](ADR-264.md) section 5 ended with a choice it declined to make.** `R-06` permits a payout evaluation against ONE day, the last closed one, so `PayoutTx.subject()` must select the stored `rule_states` row BY DAY. The calendar that says which day that is is `trading_calendar`, scope class `firm`, and [ADR-233](ADR-233.md)'s `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` is a closed list of five that does not carry it. That entry named two remedies, **[ADR-211](ADR-211.md) clause 2's two-transaction crossing or a sixth catalogued key**, wrote *"BOTH ARE SOMEBODY's RULING AND ADR-264 TOOK NEITHER"*, and put the choice to the next row.

**THE RULING IN ONE SENTENCE: BOTH ARE REFUSED AND THE DAY IS A NAMED DOOR, `ScopedTx.lastClosedTradingDay()`, because a catalogue read would hand the caller `R-06`'s own selection and a second transaction would compute a payout verdict from a calendar snapshot the transaction recording it never read.**

**THE LOSING OPTION IS THE SIXTH KEY AND IT LOST ON WHAT IT WOULD HAVE HANDED OUT RATHER THAN ON WHAT IT WOULD HAVE COST.** It is the shorter diff, `refuseUncatalogued`'s admission test is met by the table, and the argument [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) requires of a member is available. It loses because **the fold a caller holding calendar rows would then perform is `R-06` itself**, that fold is stated **twice in this tree already in two ways that disagree about coverage**, and a third statement of it would be the first on the money path.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `268`, session **459**, branch `claude/a-catalogue-key-or-a-second-transaction`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** `0074` is the highest, the directory was read at the moment of branching, `0075` is still next, and `git diff origin/main -- packages/db/migrations` is empty on this branch.

- **MONEY PATH. This is the last door between the payout port and a verdict, and the value it returns SELECTS the row every gate is read from.** The [**`E2`**](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is **OWED**. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** **And it inherits three `E2` debts**: [ADR-264](ADR-264.md), [ADR-265](ADR-265.md) and [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) are each `proposed` and UNSIGNED, and this entry rests on the first for the question, the second for the shape of the answer and the third for the list it declines to join.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That the door builds three statements on one handle, that neither calendar read carries a clock parameter, that a holiday falls out of the comparison rather than out of a branch, that an empty calendar, an exhausted calendar and a coverage gap each throw, and that `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` is unchanged at five members with neither calendar table among them, are **MEASURED** by [`packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts), watched RED before GREEN and watched firing under seven seeded defects. **That `pg` and Drizzle together render a `date` column as a UTC calendar day is MEASURED, by running both, and it is a defect rather than a control** (section 7). What is **DERIVED** is that the fold is stated twice and that the two disagree (section 4), and that the port does not become wireable (section 8). What is **JUDGED** is the refusal of the catalogue admission (section 4), the refusal of the crossing (section 5), and the exhausted-calendar refusal and what it costs (section 6).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every line row `268` supplied was re-derived at this commit before anything was written. **All of them held.**

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE DOOR IS `ScopedTx.lastClosedTradingDay()`, IT TAKES NO ARGUMENT, AND IT RETURNS ONE DAY.** The selection `R-06` describes is performed INSIDE `packages/db` and the caller receives a `YYYY-MM-DD` string. It takes no argument on `effectiveAccountCap`'s reason one door over: **there is no day a caller could name, because naming one is the question.**
>
> **2. THE SIXTH CATALOGUED KEY IS REFUSED, NOT DEFERRED, AND THE LIST IS STILL FIVE MEMBERS.** `refuseUncatalogued`'s admission test is met by this table, so the objection is not that the argument fails; it is that a catalogue read is the WRONG SHAPE, in three ways that each independently decide it. Section 4.
>
> **3. [ADR-211](ADR-211.md) CLAUSE 2's TWO-TRANSACTION CROSSING IS REFUSED ON THAT ENTRY'S OWN PRECONDITION AND NOT ON A PREFERENCE FOR ONE TRANSACTION.** Its clause 4 made the crossing safe by pinning the readable rows, after which *"nothing readable can move, and the transaction count stops mattering entirely"*. **`trading_calendar` has the opposite property BY DESIGN**: `0026` grants `merit_app` all four verbs, `0032` revoked them only on the two satellites, and CALENDAR-C1 to C3 REQUIRE a correction to leave a prior image rather than PREVENTING one. Section 5.
>
> **4. AN EXHAUSTED CALENDAR IS A REFUSAL AND NOT AN ANSWER, AND THAT IS WHY THE DOOR MAKES THREE READS RATHER THAN ONE.** The maximum row whose session has closed is the last day **Merit knows about**, which is not the same claim as the last day that **closed**. `ADR-042` F-4 is that those differ and that the difference is answerable; `0032`'s header calls the alternative *"the single most silent failure available to this table"*. The door proves the calendar can still see past the day it is about to return. Section 6.
>
> **5. THE RETURN TYPE IS `string` AND THAT IS A CONTROL RATHER THAN A SIGNATURE.** A `string | null` is a value a caller can write `?? new Date().toISOString().slice(0, 10)` against in one expression, **and that expression is the crossing [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 4 forbids, spelled out.** There is no absent value here for anybody to fold.
>
> **6. [ADR-211](ADR-211.md) FORECLOSURE 2 IS HONOURED RATHER THAN OVERTURNED.** *"A `PayoutTx` will not gain a firm method."* It does not. This is a method of `ScopedTx`, the handle a backend implementing `subject()` already holds, exactly as [ADR-233](ADR-233.md)'s catalogue verbs discharged that port's `plan` half without adding a member to the port interface.
>
> **7. NEITHER `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` NOR ANY SCOPE REGISTRATION MOVED, NO MIGRATION WAS WRITTEN, NO PORT WAS WIRED AND NO GATE WAS WEAKENED TO PASS IT.** `usePayoutBackend`'s seventh clause is narrowed and the entry keeps five others; `start.ts` is untouched; the payout routes still answer 503. Section 8.

### 3. Why this is one question and not two

**A NAMED DOOR AND A CATALOGUE KEY ARE NOT TWO WAYS TO SPELL ONE READ, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS WHAT THE CALLER IS LEFT HOLDING.** `scoped-db.ts`'s own header states the argument a door owes: *"it is that ONE handler on ONE path needs this answer inside its own transaction"*. A catalogue key is a different kind of grant: it says a whole table is readable by anybody holding a scoped transaction, forever.

Both options ADR-264 named would have put the calendar within reach of `subject()`. **What separates them is what else they put within reach, and of whom.**

---

### 4. Ruling 2, and why the catalogue read is the wrong shape here

**[ADR-265](ADR-265.md) IS THE PRECEDENT AND THE ROW WAS RIGHT THAT IT CUTS ONE WAY.** It refused [ADR-252](ADR-252.md) section 10's sized admission on the ground that *"a catalogue read hands out ROWS"* and built `effectiveAccountCap` instead. **The row was also right that the calendar is a different shape, and reading the difference is what this section is for. Two of the three differences make the case for the door STRONGER rather than weaker.**

| The difference the row named | What it changes |
|---|---|
| It is `firm` | **Nothing.** So was `firm_parameters`. `firm` is what makes the read legitimate at all, and [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) section 3 settles that the disclosure objection is ABSENT for the class. It is not what decides the shape |
| It is one row per trading day | **AGAINST the admission, and this is the strongest of the three.** The cap was TWO rows on TWO tables and the trap was the caller forgetting the second. Here the rows are UNBOUNDED and the caller's job is not to remember a second read but to **pick one row out of every trading day the exchange has ever had**, by a rule the corpus states as `R-06` |
| `databaseEconomicCalendar` already reads it on another door here | **AGAINST the crossing rather than for it.** That door answers a freshness PANEL and selects no day; this one selects the basis of a payout. Section 5 |

**THE THREE CONSEQUENCES, EACH OF WHICH ALONE SETTLES IT.**

| | What the admission gives the caller | What the named door gives them |
|---|---|---|
| **The selection** | Every calendar row, and `R-06` to apply to them. **The one rule that decides which stored state a payout may be evaluated against becomes a loop in a route** | One day |
| **The second table** | Still nothing about coverage. `trading_calendar_loads` is a separate `firm` table, so the caller either joins the two itself or skips the question. **`scope.ts` says of this table in its own registration that "A READER MUST THEREFORE CONSULT BOTH TABLES"** | Both tables read, three statements, one answer |
| **The scope of the grant** | Two tables, forever, to anybody holding a scoped transaction, so that one route may learn one date | One date |

**AND THE DECISIVE FACT IS THAT THE FOLD ALREADY EXISTS TWICE AND THE TWO DISAGREE.**

| Statement | Where | What it does | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `readLastClosedTradingDay` | [`apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts) | reads every calendar row, skips holidays and unclosed sessions, takes the **maximum `trading_day`** | **NOT CONSULTED.** `calendarCarriesDay` guards only the `MERIT_BATCH_TRADING_DAY` override path, and it reads `trading_calendar` rather than `trading_calendar_loads` |
| `lastClosedDay` | [`apps/api/src/admin-source/liability.ts`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/liability.ts) | folds on the **maximum close INSTANT** with a day tiebreak | **CONSULTED**, by its caller `anchorCalendar`, which can answer `uncovered` |

**Under a well-formed calendar the two selections agree, and that is exactly why the divergence is dangerous rather than obvious**: they differ only on the calendars where the answer matters. **A catalogue admission would produce a THIRD statement, in a route, on the door where money leaves the firm.** That is `FM-16` by name, and [ADR-264](ADR-264.md) section 6 registered the same failure mode one field over on the same port.

**WHAT THIS COSTS, STATED RATHER THAN HIDDEN, AND IT IS [ADR-265](ADR-265.md)'s COST RESTATED.** A named door per question does not scale the way a list does. This is the second such door in a week and the fourth in the file. **A session that finds itself wanting a sixth should reopen the admission with the count as its evidence rather than read this entry as forbidding it forever.** What is refused here is the admission **as the answer to this day**, because this day is a selection rule and a catalogue key can only ever hand over rows.

### 5. Ruling 3, and the second trap answered in the terms the row set

**THE ROW REQUIRED THIS BEFORE THE CHOICE: "a two-transaction remedy on a MONEY path means a payout verdict computed across two snapshots. Establish what that costs before choosing it, and say whether the calendar can move between them."**

**IT CAN MOVE, AND IT IS THE ONE TABLE IN THIS SCHEMA THE CORPUS BUILT A CORRECTION MECHANISM FOR.**

| Fact | Where |
|---|---|
| `merit_app` holds `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` on `trading_calendar` | `0026`'s grants, and `0032` revokes them **only on the two satellites** |
| What stands between a row and a rewrite is three triggers that RECORD a correction | CALENDAR-C1 (an `UPDATE` with no prior image in `trading_calendar_revisions` is refused, `0033`), C2 (a row is corrected, never deleted, `0033`), C3 (a retroactive INSERT must be recorded, `0048`) |
| The table carries an `updated_at` and its satellites do not, **because a correction is an `UPDATE`** | [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts), in its own words |
| A correction to a day anything depends on cannot be written without naming an incident | `trading_calendar_revisions.dependent_row_count`, `0032` F-2 |

**SO THE CROSSING IS UNSAFE HERE FOR THE EXACT REASON [ADR-211](ADR-211.md) SAID ITS OWN CROSSING WAS SAFE.** That entry's clause 4 reported the migration that made clause 2 sound: a published version's size grid immutable, *"with it, clause 2's crossing is safe because nothing readable can move, and the transaction count stops mattering entirely"*. `0028` and `0066` are that pin. **The calendar is designed to be corrected, so no such pin exists or should.** ADR-211's remedy does not transfer, and it does not transfer on its own stated precondition rather than by analogy.

**WHAT THE CROSSING WOULD COST, CONCRETELY.** `db.firm(...)` opens a second transaction on a second connection ([ADR-233](ADR-233.md) measured the mechanism). So the day would be chosen from snapshot A and the `rule_states` row read in snapshot B. A correction landing between them (a half day corrected to a full one, a retroactive session inserted, a `session_close_at` moved) produces a verdict whose **basis day the transaction that recorded it never read**, and the record is not transient: `payout_requests.basis_trading_day` stores it, and `eligibility_snapshot` is append-only under `INV-22`. **`INV-04`'s replay would then compare a stored fold against a calendar that never produced it**, which is the failure `trading_calendar_revisions` exists to make distinguishable and which this crossing would make indistinguishable again.

**AND `databaseEconomicCalendar` IS NOT A COUNTER-EXAMPLE, WHICH IS WHY THE ROW WAS RIGHT TO NAME IT.** It reads the same table on `ApiDb.firm` and it is correct there: it counts sessions still **ahead** for a freshness panel, selects no day, writes nothing, and a calendar correction landing mid-read changes a badge. **The same table on the same door answering a different question is not the same read.**

### 6. Ruling 4, the three reads, and the refusal that costs something

**THE MAXIMUM CLOSED ROW IS NOT BY ITSELF THE LAST CLOSED DAY.** It is the last one inside what Merit has loaded. `ADR-042` F-4 made the difference storable and `0032`'s header item 5 states the failure it closes: *"an exhausted calendar is INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM AN UNBROKEN HOLIDAY: no row means not a trading day, so every counter quietly stops advancing, no rule fires, nothing breaches, nothing becomes eligible, and NOTHING RAISES."*

**ON THIS DOOR THAT FAILURE HAS A SPECIFIC SHAPE AND IT IS WORSE THAN A STALLED COUNTER.** A calendar loaded through June, read in August, would answer `2026-06-30`. A `rule_states` row for that day **exists**, because the fold ran that night. So `ruleStateOn` finds it, every gate reads it, and a trader is paid against a floor two months stale by an endpoint that returns 200. **A wrong answer that returns 200 is worse than an honest 503**, which is this port's own sentence four entries running.

**SO THE DOOR PROVES THE CALENDAR CAN SEE PAST THE DAY, WITH TWO FACTS AND NO DATE ARITHMETIC.**

1. **A NEXT SESSION EXISTS whose `session_close_at` is still ahead of `now()`.** Instant against instant, so `ADR-146` clause 4 has no site to happen at, and it establishes that `now()` falls inside a window the calendar carries.
2. **ONE `trading_calendar_loads` INTERVAL SPANS BOTH DAYS.** That table's DDL is that *"a day inside these bounds with no trading_calendar row is a bug in the load"*, so an interval spanning the two means every day between them is carried, and none of them closed or the first read would have returned a later day.

**WHAT THIS REFUSES THAT A LOOSER DOOR WOULD ANSWER, AND IT IS A REAL COST.** Two ADJACENT loads are two intervals and are deliberately not merged: `apps/api/src/admin-source/liability.ts` states why, that merging them on a date successor is the date arithmetic `R-02` forbids. **So a closed day at the very end of one load whose successor sits in the next load REFUSES**, and a payout request that minute answers 503. The remedy is already this tree's stated convention, in that same file: *"A load that means to extend coverage overlaps by a day, which is a fact its own row states."* **The refusal fails closed on the door where money leaves the firm; the alternative fails open and silently.**

**AND THE DOOR IS STRICTER THAN THE WORKER'S FOLD, WHICH IS REPORTED RATHER THAN RECONCILED.** `readLastClosedTradingDay` consults no coverage, so on an exhausted calendar the nightly job would fold June's day in August while this door refuses. **That divergence is registered in section 9 and `apps/worker` is outside this row's fence.**

### 7. THE FINDING: `pg` and Drizzle render a `date` as a UTC calendar day, before any Merit line runs

**THIS WAS FOUND BY WATCHING TWO CASES PASS THAT WERE WRITTEN TO FAIL**, which is [ADR-265](ADR-265.md) section 7's experience one door over, and here what the passing cases exposed is larger than the door.

**MEASURED, by running both libraries:**

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| `pg`'s type parser for OID 1082 (`date`) | returns a **JS `Date` at the PROCESS'S LOCAL MIDNIGHT**. `grep -rn setTypeParser packages/db/src` returns nothing; nothing in this workspace overrides it |
| Drizzle's `PgDateString.mapFromDriverValue` | renders that `Date` with **`toISOString()`**, which is UTC |
| The database day `2026-08-28` read by a process at `TZ=Europe/Berlin` | arrives in Merit code as **`'2026-08-27'`** |

**THAT IS [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) CLAUSE 4's FORBIDDEN FAILURE PERFORMED BY TWO LIBRARIES**, on every `date` column in the estate rather than only on this door, with nothing between the database and the consumer that raises. `0032`'s own DDL comment says the bound is *"the exchange's CT trading day, never a UTC calendar date derived from a timestamp"*, and the driver stack does exactly the thing the comment forbids.

**IT IS REPORTED AND NOT REPAIRED, AND THE FENCE IS WHY.** The repair is one `setTypeParser(1082, ...)` call in [`packages/db/src/client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts), which row `268` does not fence, and it changes the reading of every `date` column in the estate at once. **Widening a fence to finish is the same move as weakening a gate to pass it.**

**WHAT IS PAID HERE IS THE OBSERVATION.** Three cases in the suite assert the measured behaviour, including one whose comment says in terms that it is a bug report rather than a control, and one asserting that no `setTypeParser` call exists. **The day either library stops coercing, or somebody installs the parser, those cases turn red and the next reader is sent here.** The door's own `YYYY-MM-DD` guard is NOT loosened and NOT deleted: it still catches a malformed string, and a non-string dies in the mapper, so the door fails closed either way.

**HOW EXPOSED THIS DEPLOYMENT ACTUALLY IS.** A process at `TZ=UTC` is unaffected, and `INFRA` names no timezone. **That is the point: the correctness of every trading day in the estate currently rests on an environment variable nobody has written down.**

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**THE ADJACENT-LOAD REFUSAL IN SECTION 6 IS A JUDGEMENT AND IT HAS A COST NOBODY HAS PAID YET.** Nothing has loaded a calendar into any Merit database, so the shape real load rows take is unobserved. **If loads turn out to be written adjacent rather than overlapping as a matter of practice, this door refuses a payout once per load boundary** and the correct repair is the loader's convention rather than a looser door, because the looser door cannot tell a boundary from a hole. A reviewer who disagrees should say so here rather than after the first refusal.

**THE COVERAGE PREDICATE IS ASSERTED BY ITS PARAMETERS AND NOT BY A REJECTION.** Nothing in this suite opens a database, so the recorder answers whatever a case queues regardless of the `WHERE`. **What is proved is that the statement carries both days; that PostgreSQL returns no row for a load covering only one of them is the database's behaviour and is not executed here.**

**THE DOOR IS UNCALLED.** Like `ruleStateOn` before it, it ships with a signature and no caller, because nothing implements `PayoutTx`. **A door that has not met its call site is the one place a design can still be wrong quietly**, and the specific risk is that `subject()` may want the day and the state read in a fixed order this entry has not ruled.

**AND THE STRICTNESS IS ASYMMETRIC BETWEEN TWO CONSUMERS OF ONE RULE.** This door refuses where `apps/worker`'s fold answers. That is deliberate, because the fold's caller closes a night and this one pays a trader, but **two answers to `R-06` in one estate is the condition finding 2 exists to end** and a reviewer may hold that the entry should have stopped and rowed the merge rather than adding the stricter third reading first.
