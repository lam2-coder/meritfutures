**IT IS NOT. ### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE WALLET ENTRY IS ONE OF ELEVEN.** Of the fourteen `BLOCKED` entries, **eleven** leave at least one member of their port's interface unnamed. Three account for every member: `setAdminSessionSource`, `usePayoutBackend` and `setInternalOpsSource`. Section 3 is the table.
>
> **2. THE SAME TWO MEMBERS ARE MISSING FROM FOUR ENTRIES AND THAT IS THE FINDING.** `operator` and `now` are unnamed by `useAdminPayoutBackend`, `useAdminWalletBackend`, `useAdminWriteBackend` and `useCertificateRevokeBackend`. **ADR-366 found this on one of the four and it is on all four**, which is [ADR-358](ADR-358.md)'s mechanism producing the same hole four times rather than four separate oversights. Section 4.
>
> **3. THE MEMBER SET IS DERIVED AND PINNED BY NAME, AND SO IS THE GAP.** A port gaining a member grows the gap and reddens; an entry naming a member shrinks it and reddens. **A bare name is therefore not free**, which is the half a census could not be worth having without. Section 6.
>
> **4. THE REQUIREMENT THAT EVERY ENTRY ACCOUNT FOR EVERY MEMBER IS NOT DERIVED, AND THAT IS A RULING RATHER THAN A SHORTFALL.** It is a policy about what a `BLOCKED` reason owes a reader, nothing in this tree has ruled it, and it is measurably WRONG for at least five entries whose blocker is a property of the PORT rather than of any member. **[ADR-367](ADR-367.md) SECTION 5's REASON APPLIES HERE, TO THIS HALF AND NOT TO THE OTHER TWO.** Section 5.
>
> **5. "ACCOUNTED FOR" IS DEFINED AS NAMED, AND NAMING IS THE NECESSARY HALF RATHER THAN THE SUFFICIENT HALF.** Two stronger definitions were built and MEASURED and both are worse, one counting five of forty-five and one discarding true accountings. **The sufficient half is not derivable without a list of blocker verbs, and a word list is a judgement wearing a derivation's clothes**, which is [ADR-367](ADR-367.md) section 10's own confession adopted rather than re-argued. Section 6.
>
> **6. THE OWED REPAIR FROM [ADR-366](ADR-366.md) SECTION 10 ITEM 1 IS TAKEN AND IT IS BOUND.** The wallet entry enumerates `operator`, `writeCorrection` and `reconcile`, its figure binds to that list, every listed name is asserted to be a real member, and `operator` is pinned by name so a later rewrite cannot inherit its way back. **The retired figure is NAMED rather than quoted** (`RI-14`), and seed 5 watched the collision when it is quoted instead.
>
> **7. `RI-15` HAS A SECOND HOLE AND IT WAS FOUND BY REFUSING THIS ROW'S OWN TRUE CITATION.** Its `DECLARATION_SHAPES` does not recognise a GENERIC method, so `operator<T>(fn: ...)` declares nothing to it while `principal(request: ...)` declares `principal`. **The citation was correct and the invariant was wrong.** Nothing in `packages/tooling/checks/**` was touched: the entry uses `RI-15`'s own designed escape instead. Section 8.
>
> **8. NO NUMBER WAS CHANGED TO MAKE A CHECK PASS.** One figure moved, the wallet entry's, and it moved because the interface disagreed with it. Every other recorded figure this row met was re-derived and found TRUE, and they are listed as such in section 9 rather than left unstated.

### 6. What "accounted for" means, and the two stronger definitions that were measured and rejected

**A CENSUS THAT WENT GREEN ON AN ENTRY MENTIONING A MEMBER'S NAME WITHOUT ACCOUNTING FOR IT WOULD BE WORTHLESS**, and the row was told so. The definition is mechanical, it is asserted by its own fixture before it is used, and each of its clauses has a seed.

> **A member is NAMED when the entry contains a BACKTICK IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWED BY the member's name, ending at a character that neither continues an identifier nor continues a path.**

| clause | why | load-bearing |
|---|---|---|
| **1. the backtick is required** | These members are ordinary English words. `now`, `operator`, `backend`, `provider`, `verify` and `transact` are all member names AND all appear in these entries as prose. Without it, *"a trader can now take that request back"* names `now` | **YES**, seed 9 |
| **2. the backticks are NOT paired into spans** | This file uses a lone backtick as an APOSTROPHE, so `usePayoutBackend` and `useWithdrawalBackend` carry an ODD number of backticks and every span-pairing reader desynchronises at the first one. **This is the same hazard `RI-15` met at `wiring.test.ts:215`** | **YES**, seed 10, which is the obvious implementation producing a DIFFERENT census |
| **3. the trailing character may not continue an identifier or a path** | So that a member name that is a PREFIX of a longer backticked token, as `operator` is of `operator-console`, is not counted | **NO. Defensive and inert on this tree**, and section 10 records how that was found |

**TWO STRONGER DEFINITIONS WERE BUILT AND MEASURED AND BOTH ARE WORSE. THEY ARE REJECTED ON THE MEASUREMENT RATHER THAN ON TASTE.**

- **"NAMED AT ITS OWN DECLARATION LINE"**, meaning the entry carries a `file:line` and the line is where the member is declared, counts **FIVE of the forty-five**. It is not this file's convention, only three entries do it at all, and requiring it would invent a citation discipline nobody ruled.
- **"NAMED WITHIN TWO HUNDRED CHARACTERS OF AN `ADR-nnn` OR A `file:line`"** counts **nineteen where naming counts twenty-three**, and **the four it drops are real accountings**: `useCheckoutAdapters` says `returnUrl` and `cancelUrl` are CONFIGURATION it waits on, and `setAdminReadSource` says `adminReadSourceParts` supplies `exportEvidence`. **A definition that discards a true accounting in order to look stricter is a worse definition.**

**SO NAMING IS THE NECESSARY HALF AND THIS ENTRY SAYS SO RATHER THAN PRETENDING OTHERWISE.** Whether an entry that names a member also states what BLOCKS it cannot be derived without a list of blocker verbs. **WHAT THE PIN BUYS INSTEAD IS THAT A BARE NAME IS NOT FREE**: an entry naming a member it does not account for SHRINKS the pinned gap and turns the case RED with the member's own name and the remedy in the message. **Seed 2 is that, watched.**

### 8. `RI-15` refused a TRUE citation, and the hole is named rather than repaired

**THE INVARIANT WENT RED ON THIS ROW'S OWN DIFF AT 34 OF 35**, on the citation `` `operator` (`routes/admin-wallet.ts:670`) ``. **The citation is correct**: `operator<T>(fn: (tx: AdminWalletTx) => Promise<T>): Promise<T>;` is on `:670`, which is where [ADR-366](ADR-366.md) section 3 put it.

**THE CAUSE IS `DECLARATION_SHAPES` AND IT IS MEASURED RATHER THAN INFERRED.** Its member shape is `/^\s*(?:readonly\s+)?([A-Za-z_$][A-Za-z0-9_$]*)\s*[?!]?\s*[:(]/`, which requires `:` or `(` after the name. **A GENERIC method has `<` there.** Run against the two lines directly, `principal(request: FastifyRequest)` yields `principal` and `operator<T>(fn: ...)` yields **null**.

**THIS IS THE SECOND NAMED HOLE IN `RI-15`**, after the `IDENTIFIER_GLUE` hole [ADR-357](ADR-357.md) section 10 measured and three later rows found live instances of. It is the same class: a grammar that is right about the shapes it was written against and silent about one it was not.

**NOTHING IN `packages/tooling/checks/**` WAS TOUCHED, DELIBERATELY.** Widening a declaration grammar so that this row's own citation passes is indistinguishable from weakening a gate to pass it, whatever its merits, and that file is outside this row's fence besides. **The entry uses `RI-15`'s OWN DESIGNED ESCAPE instead**: the check accepts a citation whose ENCLOSING declaration is named in the citing sentence, so the sentence names `AdminWalletBackend`, which is what `:670` sits inside. **The invariant is back at 35 of 35 with its grammar unchanged**, and the prose is better for naming the interface.

### 12. Open questions, recorded as options because none of them is this row's to answer

1. **Does a `BLOCKED` entry owe an accounting for every member of its port's interface?** This is the question section 5 refuses to answer by deriving. **Options: (a) YES, and the five port-level entries gain a sentence saying their blocker is the port rather than any member, which makes the gap requirable empty and this census a hard gate; (b) NO, and the entry owes an accounting only for members it claims something about, which is roughly today's practice and leaves `useAdminPayoutBackend`'s omission of `operator` a defect while `now` everywhere is not; (c) a middle rule, that an entry which ENUMERATES members must enumerate completely, which catches all four `operator` omissions and demands nothing of the five.** **(c) is the smallest change that catches the defect ADR-366 found**, and it is still a policy rather than a measurement, so it is not taken here.
2. **Are the three unrepaired `operator` omissions worth a row?** `useAdminPayoutBackend`, `useAdminWriteBackend` and `useCertificateRevokeBackend` carry the same hole ADR-366 ruled on the wallet, and `useAdminPayoutBackend` is the one its own entry calls closest to wireable. **Options: (a) one row for the three, which is this row's shape repeated with the ruling already in hand; (b) fold each into the row that next rules its port, which is how the wallet one arrived and took four waves; (c) leave them, on the ground that the census now names them and nothing can quietly grow a fifth.**
3. **Should `RI-15`'s generic-method hole be closed?** Section 8. **Options: (a) widen `DECLARATION_SHAPES` to admit `<` after the name, which is two characters and reaches every generic method in the tree; (b) leave it and let entries name the enclosing declaration, which is what this row did and what the check already prefers; (c) treat it as the `IDENTIFIER_GLUE` hole was treated, named in an ADR and left, which is now four instances deep and has not been closed.** **No option is taken.** It is a `packages/tooling/checks/**` change and outside this fence.

### 13. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **THE DEFINITION OF "NAMED" IS A JUDGEMENT AND SECTION 6 SAYS SO RATHER THAN HIDING IT.** It is defended by measurement against two alternatives and held by four seeds, which is more than the retired counts had, and it is still a rule this row chose. **A member named in an entry that says nothing about it counts as named**, and the pin is what makes that survivable rather than the definition being right.
- **THE CENSUS PROVES A NAME IS PRESENT AND NOT THAT A BLOCKER IS TRUE.** An entry could name every member of its port and be wrong about all of them, and nothing here would notice. **[ADR-366](ADR-366.md) section 9 checked the wallet entry's clauses at their primary sources one by one; this row did not do that for the other thirteen**, and a reader who takes a green bar as evidence the reasons are accurate has taken more than it offers.
