## ADR-262: the affiliate is resolved inside `packages/db` and leaves as a bit, the counterparty stamp is a registry field rather than an assumption, and the two `AffiliateRef` declarations were never one shape (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 2 refused the cross-identity read a second time and named what the port must do instead, and could not build it.** Its own sentence is the fence: *"This entry does not build it, because it needs `AffiliateRef` to stop carrying `identityId` and `packages/affiliate` is outside this fence."* Row `262` grants that fence and this entry builds the remedy. **The refusal is untouched.** Neither `affiliates` nor `affiliate_clicks` became readable, no key joined a key set, `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` still holds five members and still reaches neither table, and no scope rule was reclassified. **What changed is the SHAPE the port asks for**, and the property that buys is structural rather than careful: `apps/api` cannot leak an affiliate identity because there is no field on any shape it holds to put one in.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `262`, session **453**, dispatched as `claude/a-bit-and-not-a-uuid`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** The highest on disk is `0074_firm_parameters.sql`, and section 8 is why nothing here needs a `0075`.

- **MONEY PATH. This is the referral attribution money is paid on.** The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is **OWED** on this diff and nothing here discharges it. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That both read doors project to exactly their declared key sets and hand out no identity, that an `attributions` insert now binds two identities the caller supplied neither of, that a resolution matching no row throws with nothing written, that the click door sends no second statement on a token that named nothing, and that `packages/affiliate` holds no affiliate identity at all is **MEASURED**, by [`packages/db/test/counterparty-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/counterparty-door.test.ts), **16 cases watched RED before GREEN and three seeded defects watched firing** (section 6). That the two `AffiliateRef` declarations are different shapes answering different questions is **DERIVED**, by reading both at their own files. What is **JUDGED** is that a read inside `packages/db` that returns no identity is not the read ADR-238 refused (section 2), that the counterparty's source belongs in the registry rather than in prose (section 4), that the two like-named interfaces must not be unified (section 3), and that this does **not** wire the port (section 7).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every line the row supplied was re-derived at this commit before anything was written. The row's two pointers into `checkout.ts` held as the dispatcher had just repaired them, and its two `AffiliateRef` pointers had **BOTH DRIFTED**: `attribution.ts:135` was the docblock above the declaration rather than the declaration, and `affiliate.ts:375` was thirteen lines above it. Both are repaired under this session's citation-repair rights, along with twenty-five citations this diff's own line movement broke (section 9).

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. THE READ THAT WAS REFUSED TWICE IS STILL REFUSED, AND NOTHING BELOW IS THAT READ.** [ADR-233](ADR-233.md) section 5 and [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 2 refused a GRANT: a buyer's scoped handle reaching rows `affiliates` owns. No such grant exists after this entry. `scopePredicate` still throws for every `pair` key, `ScopedTableKey` is unmoved, `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` is still five members and names neither table, and no rule in [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) changed class. **What this entry adds is a SELECT that runs where every SELECT in this estate already runs and returns a boolean.**
>
> **2. THE OBJECTION WAS NEVER THAT A STATEMENT RUNS. IT WAS THAT A UUID CROSSES.** `client()` is unexported permanently ([ADR-084](ADR-084.md) section 9) and `packages/db` is the boundary itself, so "a query touched another identity's row" describes every scoped read ever written: the predicate is what makes it that person's. **The disclosure ADR-106 and ADR-008 refuse is a value ARRIVING IN A CALLER'S HANDS.** `attributionAffiliate` and `attributionClick` return declared shapes of two and three fields, the uuid is compared and discarded in the same expression, and there is no field to put one in. That is [ADR-230](ADR-230.md)'s "no `RETURNING`" argument applied to a projection: **the ground is ABSENT rather than outweighed.**
>
> **3. THE BIT DISCLOSES NOTHING AND ADR-238 ALREADY RULED SO.** Its own sentence naming this remedy: *"a buyer using their own code knows it is theirs, and a buyer using somebody else's learns only that it is not theirs, which names nobody."* This entry adds the two columns beside it, because the click row DOES belong to the affiliate: `clickId` is a surrogate `bigint` and `clickedAt` is when THIS BUYER followed the link, which is the input `withinLastTouchWindow` folds. `affiliate_clicks_token_uq` makes the token name exactly one row, so no traversal of the affiliate's other clicks is reachable. **The five columns the registry's own rule calls the trap are not projected**: `ip`, `user_agent`, `click_fingerprint`, `referrer_host` and `suspicious_reason`.
>
> **4. WHERE A `pair` ROW'S COUNTERPARTY COMES FROM IS A REGISTRY FIELD NOW, AND ADR-230 WROTE THE ANSWER AS PROSE BECAUSE IT ONLY HAD ONE.** That entry declined to validate the counterparty on the ground that *"the identity it names is one the caller already held"*. **That ground is TRUE of `identity_links` and FALSE of `attributions`**, and ADR-238 ruling 2 is where it broke: the buyer holds a coupon code or a click token, and neither IS an identity. So `PairWriter`'s `party` arm gains a REQUIRED `counterparty`, two arms with a reason each: `by: 'caller'` is ADR-230 unchanged, and `by: 'resolved'` names a NON-IDENTITY column on the row that `pairInsertStatement` follows inside the caller's own transaction.
>
> **5. AN `attributions` INSERT NOW TAKES NEITHER IDENTITY, WHICH IS A STRONGER OUTCOME THAN THE ROW ASKED FOR AND IS NOT A WIDER ONE.** ADR-230 made the writer column unsuppliable; this makes the counterparty unsuppliable on the same table by the same construction. **Both spellings of both columns throw before any statement is built.** So "the handler cannot leak what it never receives" is a property of the signature: there is no parameter through which either identity could be supplied, and no return value through which either could come back.
>
> **6. THE FOLD KEEPS `buyerIdentityId` AND THAT IS DELIBERATE RATHER THAN OVERLOOKED.** `AttributionRow.buyerIdentityId` is now a field the door refuses in both spellings, which makes it a candidate for the same deletion. **It is NOT deleted here.** It is the caller's OWN identity, it discloses nothing, it is not the finding row `262` grants, and `CheckoutTx.insertAttribution`'s docblock already rules what an implementation does with it (*"Assert the two agree if you like, then drop it"*). Deleting it would be widening a diff whose subject is the counterparty. **It is named in section 10 as owed rather than taken.**
>
> **7. THE TWO `AffiliateRef` DECLARATIONS ARE NOT UNIFIED AND MUST NOT BE.** They were never the same shape. Section 3.
>
> **8. THIS DOES NOT WIRE `useCheckoutBackend` AND THE ENTRY IS NARROWED RATHER THAN DELETED.** Two clauses remain, the cap's door and the ledger arm, and either alone refuses every checkout of every payment method. Section 7.

### 3. Ruling 7, and the thing the row told this session to establish first

**THE ROW SAID BOTH DECLARATIONS CARRY THE NAME AND THAT THE DISPATCHER HAD NOT CHECKED WHICH WAY IT GOES. IT GOES THE EASY WAY, AND KNOWING THAT BEFORE MOVING EITHER IS WHY THE INSTRUCTION WAS RIGHT.**

| | [`packages/affiliate/src/attribution.ts:152`](../../packages/affiliate/src/attribution.ts) | [`apps/api/src/routes/affiliate.ts:388`](../../apps/api/src/routes/affiliate.ts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fields, before this entry** | `affiliateId`, `identityId` | `affiliateId`, `code`, `status` |
| **Whose row it describes** | THE COUNTERPARTY of somebody else's purchase | THE CALLER'S OWN, read under `scopePredicate` on the identity the request proved |
| **What produces it** | `resolveAttribution`'s inputs, from a coupon the buyer typed or a token they presented | `AffiliateBackend.affiliate(session)`, whose `null` is the 403 |
| **Did it carry an identity** | Yes, and that is the finding | **No, and it never did** |

**SO EXACTLY ONE OF THEM WAS THE SUBJECT, AND THE OTHER IS A NAMING ACCIDENT.** The overlap is one field of two and one field of three, and the two fields answer different questions about different people. **The finding the row anticipated -- two declarations drifting apart -- is not what is here**: nothing drifted, because they were never a copy of each other.

**AND UNIFYING THEM IS REFUSED RATHER THAN DEFERRED.** A single `AffiliateRef` would put a self-read and a counterparty projection behind one name, and the day somebody added a field for one surface it would arrive on the other -- which on this pair means a field describing the affiliate arriving on the shape a BUYER holds. The collision is recorded in a docblock on the `apps/api` declaration, where the next session to reach for the merge will read it. **NEITHER CARRIES AN IDENTITY NOW, FOR REASONS THAT ARE NOT EACH OTHER'S**: the portal one because the caller's identity is the handle's, the fold's because ADR-238 ruling 2 refuses the buyer the affiliate's.

### 4. Rulings 4 and 5, what was built in `packages/db`

#### 4.1 The registry answers the question, one arm per table, each with its reason

`PairWriter`'s `party` arm gains `counterparty: PairCounterparty` and it is **REQUIRED**, on this file's own totality rule: a `pair` table registered after this entry answers the question or does not compile. `attributions` reads:

```ts
counterparty: {
  by: 'resolved',
  from: 'affiliate_id',
  via: 'affiliates',
  foreignColumn: 'id',
  why: "THE BUYER DOES NOT HOLD THE AFFILIATE'S IDENTITY AND MUST NOT BE HANDED IT ...",
}
```

**THE IDENTITY COLUMN IS NOT RESTATED ON THE RULE AND THAT IS THE POINT OF PUTTING IT HERE.** `via` names a table this registry already classifies, and a resolution is admitted only where that table's rule is `owned` on a NOT NULL column, so the column the stamp reads is `SCOPE_RULES[via].column` and there is exactly one statement of it. A second copy would be the drift `packages/db` exists to keep to one. **The door throws if `affiliates` ever stops being `owned`, or stops being NOT NULL**, rather than stamping whatever it finds.

**`counterparty` SITS INSIDE THE `party` ARM RATHER THAN BESIDE `writer` ON THE RULE.** The question exists only where a party writes: a table nobody may author has no counterparty to fill, and a field two of the three members had to answer with a placeholder is a field a later author answers without reading. This is `by: 'nobody'`'s own reasoning about defaults, applied one level in.

#### 4.2 The insert takes neither identity

`resolvedCounterparty` ([`scoped-db.ts:2496`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) runs before the INSERT is built, in this order:

1. **The counterparty column is refused in BOTH spellings**, which is the writer column's own refusal arriving on the second column. A caller that could name it would be a caller that had already been handed the identity.
2. The column named by `from` is **required and may not be null**, in the Drizzle property spelling: equality against NULL matches no row, so a null address is refused rather than read as an unresolved insert.
3. The `via` rule is read WIDENED and must be `owned` and NOT NULL, which is `insertUnderStatement`'s and `pairInsertStatement`'s stated construction and their stated compiler error.
4. **The resolution is a SELECT on `source`**, which is the caller's open transaction. `insertUnderStatement`'s reason word for word: outside the transaction it is a race, and the value is about to become a column of a row this transaction commits.
5. Zero rows is a **THROW and the row is NOT written**. More than one row is a throw naming the uniqueness the rule asserted and the database disagreed with.
6. The insert binds `{ ...values, [counterparty]: resolved, [writer]: identityId }`, with **no `RETURNING`**.

**SO THE ONE VALUE THE CALLER SUPPLIES IS `affiliate_id`, WHICH IS A ROW OF THE AFFILIATE PROGRAM AND NOT A PERSON**, and it is already the buyer's to hold: `coupons` is a `firm` catalogue row and `catalogRowAt` returns its `affiliate_id` with it.

#### 4.3 ADR-230's `caller` arm has no member left, and it is kept

`attributions` was the arm's only member. **After this entry `by: 'caller'` describes no registered table**, so its three refusals -- the counterparty required, the SQL spelling refused, a null counterparty refused -- are **UNEXERCISED**. That is a real loss of coverage and it is turned into an assertion rather than left as a silence: [`pair-write-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/pair-write-door.test.ts) section 2b asserts the caller arm is empty, asserts the two arms partition `PARTY_WRITABLE`, and its section 2 loops are written over the split, **so the day a table joins either arm they start running without an edit.**

**THE ARM IS KEPT AND DELETING IT IS REFUSED ON ADR-233'S OWN GROUND.** ADR-230 is a landed, unsuperseded ruling with its own argument for that construction, and deleting it would supersede that entry's section 4.3 from inside a fence whose subject is the affiliate -- which is the move ADR-233 refused when it *"declined to invent it from inside a fence that was not its subject"*. It is also the answer a future `pair` table takes when the caller genuinely holds the other party, and a union with one arm would force a table with no address column into `by: 'nobody'` by accident.

#### 4.4 Two named doors and not one generic one

`ScopedTx` gains `attributionAffiliate(affiliateId)` and `attributionClick(clickToken)`. **A general "is this owned row mine" verb is refused**, and the reason is not size: it would be an ORACLE over every `owned` table in the registry, answerable about any row a caller could name. **The argument a door owes here is [ADR-233](ADR-233.md)'s** -- not "the answer is small", but that ONE handler on ONE path needs it inside its own transaction -- and each of the two names the port method it exists for.

**THE CLICK DOOR SENDS TWO STATEMENTS RATHER THAN A JOIN**, on `insertUnderStatement`'s reason for reading its parent separately: the second read is the same read the code path performs, so there is one resolution of an affiliate in the file rather than two that must agree. **A token naming no click is `null` and the affiliate is never asked for. A click whose affiliate row is GONE is a THROW**, because `affiliate_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES affiliates(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT` says that row cannot be gone, and returning `null` there would fold a database contradiction into "this buyer arrived organically" -- which is the exact failure mode ADR-238 ruling 2 refused a read to prevent.

### 5. What changed in `packages/affiliate`, and what it costs

`AffiliateRef` is `{ affiliateId, isBuyer }`. `AttributionRow` drops `affiliateIdentityId`. `attribute` reads `affiliate.isBuyer` where it compared two uuids, and gains a guard on an empty `affiliateId` where it had one on an empty buyer identity -- the same class of refusal, on the value the fold still holds.

**`AttributionRow`'s OPENING RULE IS DELIBERATELY NOT MET ON ONE COLUMN, AND THE EXCEPTION IS WRITTEN INTO THE FIELD RATHER THAN LEFT TO BE NOTICED.** That interface says *"every field the DDL declares NOT NULL is non-nullable here"*, and `attributions.affiliate_identity_id` is `NOT NULL` and absent. It is absent because it is STAMPED, for the same reason `buyer_identity_id` is stamped, and a fold that produced it would first have to be handed it.

**WHAT IS GIVEN UP IS THAT THE FOLD CAN NO LONGER CHECK THE BIT.** Before, `buyerIdentityId === affiliate.identityId` was computed from two values the fold held, so a caller that assembled the input wrongly produced a wrong void. Now `isBuyer` arrives already decided, and the fold takes it. **The two agree by construction at the door -- the bit is computed against the same handle the write is stamped from -- and by nothing at all inside `packages/affiliate`.** That is a real transfer of trust from a pure function to an accessor, and it is the trade ADR-238 ruling 2 chose when it ruled the remedy a stamp: the alternative is the uuid in the handler, which is the thing being refused.

**AND THE PACKAGE NOW HOLDS NO AFFILIATE IDENTITY AT ALL**, which is checkable rather than argued: `counterparty-door.test.ts` reads that file and asserts the declaration is gone, the field access is gone, and `affiliateIdentityId` appears nowhere in it.

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

1. **THE FOLD CAN NO LONGER CHECK THE BIT IT ACTS ON**, and section 5 is the whole of the argument for accepting that. A caller that computed `isBuyer` against a different identity than the one the write stamps would produce a self-deal that is not voided, and nothing in `packages/affiliate` would notice. The construction that prevents it -- both values coming from one handle -- lives in `packages/db` and is asserted there, so **the assertion and the risk are in different packages**, which is exactly the shape this corpus distrusts elsewhere.
2. **`AttributionRow.buyerIdentityId` IS NOW A FIELD THE DOOR REFUSES**, and ruling 6 keeps it on grounds of scope rather than of design. A reader may reasonably conclude it should have gone in the same commit.
3. **THE `caller` ARM'S THREE REFUSALS ARE UNEXERCISED** until some future `pair` table takes that arm. Section 4.3 turns the fact into an assertion, which is the best available answer and is not the same as coverage.
4. **`clickedAt` AND `clickId` COME OFF A ROW THE AFFILIATE OWNS.** Ruling 3 argues both are about the buyer's own act and that the token bounds the read to one row. If that argument is wrong, it is wrong in a direction no assertion here would catch, because the projection is asserted to be exactly those fields rather than to be harmless.
5. **NOTHING HERE HAS TOUCHED A DATABASE.** Every assertion reads the SQL the accessor BUILDS through a driverless handle, which is `pair-write-door.test.ts`'s stated limit and this suite's: the resolution's behaviour against real rows, real nulls and a real `citext` comparison is **stated as unexecuted rather than implied to have run**.

### 11. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.**
- **MONEY PATH.** This is the referral attribution money is paid on, and the diff changes what an `attributions` row's two identity columns are filled from. The `E2` read is owed on the whole diff, and section 4.2's ordering is the part to read against the source rather than against this entry.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS THE TRADE IN SECTION 5, AND IT IS THE ONLY ONE THAT IS NOT REVERSIBLE BY A LATER DIFF.** The literal self-deal check used to be arithmetic over two values a pure, total, synchronous fold held; it is now a boolean the accessor computed. **That is what keeps the affiliate's uuid out of `apps/api` and it moves the check into the layer that is hardest to test without a database.** ADR-238 ruling 2 named a stamp as the remedy and this is the stamp, so a founder who holds that the fold must keep the comparison is holding that the uuid must reach the handler, and the entry would rather be told that plainly than have it discovered later.

  **THE SECOND IS WHETHER `AttributionRow.buyerIdentityId` SHOULD HAVE GONE IN THIS COMMIT.** Ruling 6 keeps it on grounds of scope: it is the caller's own identity, it discloses nothing, and it is not what row `262` grants. It is also, after this entry, a field the door refuses in both spellings, which is a reasonable definition of dead. **A founder who holds it should go gets a small diff in `packages/affiliate` and ruling 6 deleted.**

  **THE THIRD IS ADR-230's `by: 'caller'` ARM, WHICH NOW DESCRIBES NO REGISTERED TABLE.** Section 4.3 keeps it and turns its emptiness into an assertion. **The alternative is a single-shape rule that would force every future `pair` table to have a non-identity address column**, which is arguably the right general ruling and is a bigger one than this row grants.

  **AND ONE THING TO READ RATHER THAN DECIDE.** Section 4.2's ordering is the money-path half: every refusal fires before the INSERT is built, and the one that cannot -- the resolution missing -- sends a SELECT and no INSERT. **That is the property a wrong order would lose silently**, and it is worth reading against `scoped-db.ts` rather than against this entry.
