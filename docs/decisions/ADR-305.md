## ADR-305: the withdrawal driver, sized. Nine slices, five absences and not three, and the installation is blocked on a vendor because the day `LT-06` posts an approved withdrawal has no exit at all  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-270](ADR-270.md) clause 6 named three measured absences standing between a settled authority and a posting, and said none of them needs a ruling and each is a slice's ordinary work.** Row `305` asked for those three sized the way [ADR-287](ADR-287.md) sized the payout backend. **All three were re-derived at source and all three hold.** The sizing is **nine slices**.

**THE THREE ARE REAL AND THEY ARE NOT ALL OF IT.** Two further absences sit between the ruled authority and a composed posting, and neither is in [ADR-270](ADR-270.md)'s list: **`@merit/ledger` exports no `LT-06` builder**, its only door onto that posting being the exported reversal; and **`lt01()` lives in `apps/api` rather than in `packages/ledger`**, which is the deployable `apps/worker` cannot import. **The port whose adapter [ADR-270](ADR-270.md) called ordinary work specifies that adapter in its own docblock as `postTransaction(tx.ledger, ...)`, and `ExpiryTx` declares three members, none of them `ledger`.** So the sentence describing the one-line adapter describes a line that does not compile.

**AND THE SIZING'S CONCLUSION IS NOT THE NUMBER. IT IS THAT THE INSTALLATION CANNOT BE REACHED BY MERIT WORK ALONE, AND THE REASON IS ONE ARROW SET.** `0072`'s `WD-C2` refuses `approved --> cancelled` at the database. `approved --> transferring` is the only other arrow out of `approved`, and `transferring` is reached by enqueueing on a rail with no live adapter and no importer. **So the day `LT-06` posts, an approved withdrawal has no exit in this estate: the trader's wallet claim is extinguished, the cancel they have today is refused, and the payment needs a vendor.** `0072` wrote that consequence down one migration in advance, in its own words, and section 5 quotes it.

**A 503 SAYS MERIT CANNOT DO THIS TODAY. A WIRED DEPLOYMENT WOULD SAY YES AND THEN NEVER PAY.** That sentence is the reason row `305` is a plan and not a build, and section 5 is the measurement under it.

**NOTHING IS WIRED BY THIS ROW AND THE COUNTS DO NOT MOVE: TEN WIRED OF TWENTY-FOUR DECLARED, FOURTEEN BLOCKED** ([`wiring.test.ts:1425`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts)). No line under any `src/` moved, no test changed, no migration was written, no `package.json` was edited and no `RI-nn` was minted.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `305`, session **494**, branch `claude/the-worker-has-no-ledger`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** `0077` is reserved to [ADR-297](ADR-297.md) and is not this row's; `0078` is the first free number and section 7 slice 4 says which row spends it.

- **MONEY PATH. This is the port the whole withdrawal path waits on.** The [**`E2`**](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is **OWED**. Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** It inherits `E2` debt on [ADR-270](ADR-270.md), [ADR-267](ADR-267.md), [ADR-176](ADR-176.md), [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) and [ADR-165](ADR-165.md), every one of which is `proposed` and UNSIGNED, and every one of which this sizing reads as a premise.

- **THIS ROW PLANS AND DOES NOT BUILD.** No adapter is written. [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) and [ADR-176](ADR-176.md) are not reopened, and section 3 says why [ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s remedy is not reached for again.

- **THE RAIL IS NAMED AS OUT OF SCOPE AND THE NAMING STOPS THERE** (section 6). It is vendor work and it is FOUNDER-OWED.

### 2. The two absences this row found, which the three do not name

**BOTH ARE BETWEEN THE RULED AUTHORITY AND A COMPOSED POSTING, AND BOTH ARE INVISIBLE FROM `apps/worker`.** [ADR-270](ADR-270.md) clause 6's list is a list of what the worker lacks. These two are things `packages/ledger` and `apps/api` lack, and a session that took the three at face value would meet both after starting.

#### F1. `@merit/ledger` exports no `LT-06` builder

`walletWithdrawalApprovalPosting` is declared at [`reversal.ts:218`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts) as a **module-scoped `function`**. It is exported from neither `reversal.ts` nor [`index.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/index.ts): the specifier occurs **zero times** in the package's public surface. What the package does export is [`walletWithdrawalFailurePosting`](../../packages/ledger/src/reversal.ts), which is `LT-09`, built from `LT-06` through `reversalPosting`.

**THE PRIVACY IS DELIBERATE AND ITS STATED GROUND IS NOW DISCHARGED.** [`lt06-posting-timing.test.ts:72`](../../apps/api/test/lt06-posting-timing.test.ts) says the builder is private *"deliberately, because nothing in this tree rules `LT-06`'s writer"*. **[ADR-270](ADR-270.md) clause 2 rules it.** So the reason the door is shut is a reason that expired eight entries later, and re-opening it is a slice rather than a reversal of anything.

**THIS IS WHY THE ABSENCE IS INVISIBLE FROM THE WORKER.** Adding `@merit/ledger` to that manifest grants the deployable the package's **exported** surface, and `LT-06` is not in it. A session that took absence 1 as a one-line manifest fix would land the line, import the package, and find no function to call.

#### F2. `lt01()` is in `apps/api`, which `apps/worker` cannot import

The port that absence 2 is about specified its own adapter, at [`sweeps/ports.ts:222`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts). **[ADR-315](ADR-315.md) has since ruled that sentence wrong, and the pointer names the paragraph that replaced it, which records the expression verbatim:**

> THE ADAPTER IS `postTransaction(tx.ledger, await readChart(tx.ledger), lt01(values))` AND NOTHING MORE.

**`lt01` HAS EXACTLY ONE DEFINITION IN THIS REPOSITORY AND IT IS AT [`apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts:933`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts).** `apps/api` declares `@merit/ledger` and composes the posting there; `apps/worker` cannot reach it, on the fact [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) states in its own words, that *"`apps/api` and `apps/worker` cannot import each other"*.

**AND THE OBVIOUS WAY ROUND IS REFUSED BY THE PORT ITSELF, BY NAME.** [`ports.ts:216-220`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts) says this file *"names no ledger account, writes no transfer and contains no ledger arithmetic"*, because a second transcription of `debit trader_withdrawable / credit trader_wallet / credit fees_revenue` is [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 5's two-statements-of-one-fact hazard **arriving on the money path**. So the adapter cannot be written by copying the arithmetic, and it cannot be written by importing it. `lt01` moves, or nothing posts.

#### F3, which is smaller than F1 and F2 and would stop a session just as hard: `tx.ledger` names nothing

`ExpiryTx` ([`ports.ts:157`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts)) declares **three members**: `rowsWhere`, `lockAt`, `updateAt`. **There is no `ledger`.** And the omission is not an oversight to be repaired by adding one: [`ports.ts:76-78`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/ports.ts) states that `ledgerTransactions` and `ledgerEntries` are absent from `EXPIRY_TABLES` **DELIBERATELY**, *"so nothing here can write a ledger row by naming a key."*

**SO THE ADAPTER SENTENCE AND THE PORT DISAGREE, AND THE DISAGREEMENT IS A DESIGN QUESTION RATHER THAN A TYPO.** The posting must commit in the SAME transaction as the state change that caused it ([ADR-006](ADR-006.md), `0057`'s header item 3), so the adapter needs a `LedgerTx` **on that transaction** and not a second handle. Getting one there without putting the two ledger keys back into the narrow union is the question slice 3 answers.

### 3. The authority question, answered here in writing rather than inherited

**ROW `305` REQUIRED THIS ANSWERED RATHER THAN CARRIED, SO IT IS ANSWERED, AND THE ANSWER IS [ADR-270](ADR-270.md)'s WITH ITS GROUNDS RE-DERIVED ON THIS TREE.**

**BOTH REFUSALS STAND AND NEITHER IS SOFTENED BY A WORD.**

- **`SystemReason` is still exactly two members.** [`scoped-db.ts:271`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) is `export type SystemReason = 'nightly-batch' | 'operator-console';`. Nothing in this row widens it and nothing in the sizing needs it widened. [`ledger-posting-authority.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/ledger-posting-authority.test.ts) holds it and [ADR-270](ADR-270.md) section 4 seed 1 watched that case fire on a third member named `'withdrawal-driver'`, **which is the exact member a driver session would be tempted to add**.
- **`ApiDb` still declares no system door.** [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) clause 2's ground is unchanged: a handle generic over the estate handed to the trader surface is authority over every row Merit holds.

**AND THE RULE THAT DECIDES THE DRIVER'S AUTHORITY IS `packages/ledger`'s OWN SENTENCE, WHICH [`tx.ts:48`](../../packages/ledger/src/tx.ts) STATES IN ITS OWN WORDS:** *"the reason is chosen by whoever opens the transaction, which is the caller"*. A job a clock started has no caller to resolve. It is `'nightly-batch'`, on the door `apps/worker` has held since [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 2, through [`workerHandle()`](../../apps/worker/src/db.ts) at [`db.ts:123`](../../apps/worker/src/db.ts), whose `WORKER_REASON` is typed as `SystemReason` rather than inferred **so that a member removal does not compile**.

**SO THE DRIVER'S AUTHORITY IS SETTLED AND NO SLICE BELOW MAY SPEND CONTEXT ON IT.** What every slice below is, is the ordinary work of reaching an authority that already exists.

**AND [ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s REMEDY IS NOT REACHED FOR AGAIN, BECAUSE THE FOUR PINS ARE RUN RATHER THAN RESTATED.** [`lt06-posting-timing.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/lt06-posting-timing.test.ts) holds M05 section 2.1 (`LT-01` CREDITS the wallet and `LT-06` DEBITS it), `INV-M20-01`, M20 section 3.3a and the key `decideWithdrawal` already stores. **What refuses is WHEN the posting happens and not a missing key or a handle**, and clause 4's case is the cleanest evidence: the key half of [ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s price is already paid on this leg and the remedy still fails. **This row reopens neither [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) nor [ADR-176](ADR-176.md).**

### 4. Why the driver is not `driveApprovals` moved, which is the sizing's largest single finding

**THE ENTRY SAYS THE DRIVER PERFORMS THE TRANSITION AND THE POSTING TOGETHER AT A SYSTEM AUTHORITY, AND A READER WOULD REASONABLY SIZE THAT AS A FILE MOVE.** It is not one, and the reason is a sentence in the port's own docblock.

[`wallet-withdrawals.ts:931-936`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts), on `WithdrawalTx`:

> **EVERY METHOD IS SCOPED TO THE CALLER'S IDENTITY BY THE ACCESSOR BEFORE THIS FILE SEES A ROW**, so nothing here is a tenancy control and nothing here can become one by accident. **No method takes an identity.**

**A CLOCK HAS NO CALLER AND THEREFORE NO IDENTITY TO SCOPE BY.** `driveApprovals` ([`:1801`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) opens with `tx.lockScope()` and then reads `tx.identity()` and `tx.approvalCandidates()`, all three of which mean *this one identity's*. A sweep must select candidates **across** identities and then take `INV-M20-01`'s per-identity lock once per identity inside the sweep. **That is a different port, not the same port at a different authority**, and the ordering question it raises (lock, then read, then decide, then write, then post, per identity, in one transaction) is the whole of the concurrency control.

**AND THE PURE HALF HAS A HOME PROBLEM THAT THIS REPOSITORY HAS ALREADY REFUSED TO SOLVE ONCE.** `decideApproval` ([`:1728`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) is total and pure, takes rows and returns a decision, and is exactly the kind of function two deployables should share. **They cannot.** [ADR-286](ADR-286.md) met this on the size-row decoder, found that *"`apps/api` and `apps/worker` cannot import each other"*, enumerated the candidate homes and **refused all of them**, leaving the question as a home rather than a ruling. **The same question arrives here on a different subject, and a driver session that meets it unprepared will either duplicate a money-path decision function or invent a package in an adapter.** Slice 5 exists so that it meets it prepared.

**WHAT DOES NOT MOVE IS THE MEASUREMENT.** `grep -rn driveApprovals apps/api/src` returns **3 lines** today, which are the transition and two docblock references, **and no fourth line is a caller**. `grep -rn driveCancellation apps/api/src` returns **7**, and one of them is a caller. **Both counts were re-derived on this tree and both are what the entry says they are.** The driver landing in `apps/worker` leaves the first count at 3, which is the point: the count is what tells a reader the door is not in this deployable.

### 5. THE TRAP PAST APPROVAL, MEASURED, AND IT IS WHY SLICE 9 IS NOT MERIT'S TO SCHEDULE

**THIS IS THE SECTION THE ROW'S CLOSING SENTENCE IS ABOUT, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS WORSE THAN THE SENTENCE.**

The entry's discharged lockout half ends *"a trader can now take that request back but cannot make it settle."* **That is true before approval and false after it**, and `0072` is where the difference lives.

**THREE ARROWS, AT SOURCE.**

1. **`approved --> cancelled` is REFUSED BY THE DATABASE.** [`0072:211-218`](../../packages/db/migrations/0072_terminal_withdrawal_transitions.sql) raises `WD-C2`: *"`G-TRADER-CANCELS` is drawn from requested and from cooling and from nowhere else, both BEFORE approval."*
2. **`approved --> transferring` is the only other arrow out of `approved`.** [`0072:123-125`](../../packages/db/migrations/0072_terminal_withdrawal_transitions.sql) lists the three non-terminal arrows it does not bind and that is one of them.
3. **`transferring` is unreachable.** `TERMINAL_EDGE_FINDINGS` finding A ([`wallet-withdrawals.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) holds that `packages/rail` opens no socket, that its only `RailAdapter` is `SandboxRail` under `src/fakes/`, and that **a recursive search for the specifier `@merit/rail` outside that package returns zero lines**. `wallet-withdrawals.test.ts` RUNS that finding rather than reading it.

**SO THE DAY `LT-06` POSTS, AN APPROVED WITHDRAWAL HAS NO EXIT AT ALL.** The wallet claim is extinguished, the money sits in `withdrawals_in_flight`, the cancel is refused by a trigger, and the only remaining arrow needs a vendor.

**AND `0072` WROTE THAT DOWN ONE MIGRATION IN ADVANCE, WHICH IS THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE IN THIS ENTRY THAT THE FINDING IS NOT THIS ROW'S INVENTION.** [`0072:27-34`](../../packages/db/migrations/0072_terminal_withdrawal_transitions.sql):

> an `UPDATE wallet_withdrawals SET status = 'cancelled'` from `approved` LANDS. **Today it lands harmlessly, because no door drives the approval edge** and `LT-06` therefore never posts, so `WD-C1`'s sum really is over zero rows. **THE DAY THE POSTING LANDS IT STOPS BEING HARMLESS**: a cancel from `approved` would leave the obligation standing, the trader's wallet claim already extinguished by `LT-06`, and **the money in neither place they could be shown it**.

`0072` closed that arrow so the cancel could not produce the defect. **It did not, and could not, produce a rail.** So the estate is now correct and stuck: the unsafe exit is refused and the safe one does not exist.

**THE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SIZING IS EXACT AND IT IS THE ONLY PLACE THIS ENTRY DEPARTS FROM [ADR-270](ADR-270.md)'s READING.** [ADR-270](ADR-270.md) clause 8 says `useWithdrawalBackend` *"is now waiting on a driver whose authority is settled and whose three absences are named, which is a slice somebody can be dispatched to build."* **Every word of that is true of the DRIVER and none of it is true of the INSTALLATION.** Slices 1 to 8 are dispatchable Merit work. **Slice 9 is not**, because installing the backend in front of a driver that debits wallets into a state with no exit is worse for a trader than the 503 it replaces: today they keep their money, and after it they do not have it and cannot get it back.

**A 503 SAYS MERIT CANNOT DO THIS TODAY. A WIRED DEPLOYMENT WOULD SAY YES AND THEN NEVER PAY.** The measurement above is what that sentence costs, stated as an arrow set rather than as a worry.

### 6. FOUNDER-OWED, named and not sized

**THE RAIL.** Past `approved`, `settled` and `failed` are drawn only out of `transferring`, reached by enqueueing on a rail with **no live adapter and no importer**. `packages/rail` opens no socket and names no vendor SDK; `SandboxRail` is a fake and nothing imports it. **This is vendor work, it is FOUNDER-OWED, and this row does not size it.** No slice below counts it, no slice below assumes a date for it, and the naming stops here.

**IT IS NAMED RATHER THAN OMITTED BECAUSE IT IS SLICE 9's BLOCKER**, and a plan that hides its own longest pole is the thin plan this row refuses. The same shape closed [ADR-287](ADR-287.md): there the critical path ran through a lawyer, here it runs through a payment vendor, and in both cases **no sequence of engineering sessions alone takes the route off 503**.
