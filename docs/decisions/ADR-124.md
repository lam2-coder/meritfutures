## ADR-124: the global ledger halt is a MECHANISM and it is not a `ledger_halts` row  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[EVENTS section 7](../architecture/EVENTS.md) says `ledger.invariant_violated` is *"the one event whose consumer is allowed to change system behavior automatically"* and that what it does is halt payouts. At `scope: "global"` there is no consumer, no row it could write, and no code path that would refuse. [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql) rules in its own comment that the global halt *"is not a row, it is an incident"*, and `assertNoLiveHalt` says the same thing from the other side. So the automatic half of an approved architecture sentence is enforced by nothing, and [RB-07](../ops/runbooks/RB-07-ledger-imbalance.md) B.1 tells an operator to CONFIRM a stop that nothing performs.**

- **Origin:** [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 4, session **236**. The number was RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH and **CONDITIONAL**: [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row 124 reserved it against `M20`'s `INV-M20-06` contradiction with the payout gate. **That contradiction was closed six days before this session opened**, by [ADR-075](ADR-075.md), and [P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 5.1 is the measurement. The condition is met by a different contradiction, found by measuring the tree the reservation asked to be measured.

- **MONEY PATH.** The subject is what refuses a payout. `INV-M5-04` and [ADR-016](ADR-016.md) are the authorities and neither is amended here. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is owed on every line the mechanism lands and is the founder's; this entry lands no code.

- **This entry rules a SHAPE and refuses to design the storage**, which is the boundary a planning session may not cross: the storage is a migration, migrations are sacred, and a plan does not write one.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. The global halt is automatic.** The refusal is performed by code and is confirmed rather than performed by the operator, which is the reading [RB-07](../ops/runbooks/RB-07-ledger-imbalance.md) B.1 already takes and [EVENTS:263](../architecture/EVENTS.md) already states. *"An incident, not a row"* is a statement about STORAGE and is not a statement about who stops the payouts.
>
> **2. It is not a `ledger_halts` row, and enumerating every identity is refused explicitly.** [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql)'s partition stands: a halt row names a subject. A global stop names none, and writing one row per identity would be a set that is stale the moment an identity is created, would need a compensating sweep to lift, and would still not refuse a firm-to-firm posting.
>
> **3. Its subject is the FIRM, so the refusal is a SECOND check and never a widening of the first.** `assertNoLiveHalt`'s early return on an identity-less posting is correct and is not the bug. A global stop must refuse the postings that one cannot see, which are exactly the firm-only ones: `LT-02` and `LT-07` both move `firm_treasury` against the payout wallet position.
>
> **4. It refuses the INTERNAL leg too.** [RB-07](../ops/runbooks/RB-07-ledger-imbalance.md) B.1 names it in its own sentence. Under [ADR-019](ADR-019.md) the internal leg is a ledger posting and nothing else, so a stop that only reaches the rail reaches the leg where no money moves and misses the leg where the liability books.

**Clause 4 is the one worth reading twice.** The pre-wallet reading of "stop payouts" was "stop sending money", and the rail is where sending happens. After [ADR-019](ADR-019.md) the trader is paid by `LT-01` committing, so a global halt that leaves `POST /accounts/:id/payout` serving is a halt that lets Merit go on recognising liability out of a book it has said it does not understand. **That is the failure `INV-M5-04` exists to prevent, arriving through the leg the invariant was written before.**

### 3. What this entry deliberately does NOT rule

> **The storage, the release path, and the authority that may set it.**

**A firm-scoped switch is a `CREATE TABLE` or a column, and it carries at least four decisions this session cannot take from a plan:** whether it is a row in a new table or a state on an existing firm table; whether it has an expiry, and if so whether expiry RELEASES (which every other clock in this corpus does, and which would be catastrophic here); who may lift it and under what dual control; and whether it is one switch or one per branch of the assertion. **Each is a money-path migration decision and [E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) reserves them for a diff the founder reads line by line.**

**The expiry question is flagged rather than left to be discovered.** [ADR-040](ADR-040.md) made every hold in this corpus expire and made expiry RELEASE, on the ground that an unbounded hold is a denial nobody authorized. **The global halt is the one clock where that reasoning inverts**: it is a statement that Merit does not know what it owes, and a halt that releases itself after 48 hours resumes paying out of a book nobody explained. Whoever writes the storage owes an argument, and [`CI-06l`](../testing/STRATEGY.md) will ask for one the moment an expiry column exists.

### 4. Who owns it

**[P5](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md) section 8's `P5-k`**, the slice that builds the three nightly assertions, because that slice is the producer of `ledger.invariant_violated` and a producer whose consumer does not exist is the shape finding 5 measures. **It is named in `P5-k`'s prompt as a REQUIRED ruling with its own ADR number**, allocated in P5's pre-dispatch commit, so the mechanism arrives with an entry rather than as a column somebody added.

**`P5-f` and `P5-h` read it and neither may invent it.** The internal leg and the external leg both refuse under a global halt by clause 4, and a refusal each implements for itself is two spellings of one control, which is this corpus's most repeated defect. **They call what `P5-k` builds, or they are dispatched after it.**

### 5. Alternatives considered

- **Rule that the global halt is procedural and amend [EVENTS:263](../architecture/EVENTS.md).** **Rejected, and it is the cheapest option available.** It would make the corpus consistent by deleting the control: a firm-wide stop that depends on an operator reading a page is exactly the *"a job that reports success is not evidence that the work happened"* class that `INV-M5-18` exists for, applied to a human. It also contradicts [RB-07](../ops/runbooks/RB-07-ledger-imbalance.md) B.1, which would then have to be rewritten from *"confirm it"* to *"do it"*, and B.1 is the step an operator performs at 03:00 during a suspected security incident.
- **Widen `ledger_halts` to admit a `NULL` subject.** **Rejected on `0016`'s own partition and on arithmetic.** The partial unique index is `ON ledger_halts (identity_id) WHERE released_at IS NULL`, and `NULL` never collides, so the table would admit two live global halts. Every read in `halts.ts` filters by subject membership, so a `NULL`-subject row would be invisible to the only code that reads the table.
- **Write one row per identity.** **Rejected in clause 2**, and it fails on the case that matters most: `LT-02` and `LT-07` name no identity, so the leg where cash actually leaves would post straight through a halt covering every trader in the estate.
- **Fold the global stop into the sales circuit breaker.** **Rejected by `INV-M5-12`**, which is structural: *"the breaker pauses sales and can never pause payouts"*, and the asymmetry is described in [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) as the single most important one in the business. Reusing it would put a code path from a liability signal to a payout block, which that invariant exists to make absent.
- **Rule nothing and record the finding in P5 alone.** **Rejected.** [ADR-075](ADR-075.md) section 4 is the precedent and the argument: a finding recorded and owned by nobody is on its way to being treated as handled, and this one has been recorded zero times in four documents that each describe half of it.

### 6. Consequences

- **No document is amended and no code lands.** [EVENTS:263](../architecture/EVENTS.md) stays true as written; this entry says what its consumer is. [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql)'s partition stays; this entry says what falls outside it. `assertNoLiveHalt` is unchanged and gains a sibling rather than a widening.
- **`P5-k` gains a REQUIRED ADR reservation**, and `P5-f` and `P5-h` gain a stated dependency they would otherwise each have discovered alone.
- **`RB-07` branch B step 1 becomes checkable.** *"Confirm it"* names something an operator can look at, which is what a runbook step has to be.
- **The four undecided questions in section 3 are the next session's**, and the expiry one is the one to decide deliberately rather than by copying the sweep beside it.
- **This entry makes a fifth site for one finding and it is the first that owns it.** [EVENTS](../architecture/EVENTS.md), [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql), [`halts.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/halts.ts) and [RB-07](../ops/runbooks/RB-07-ledger-imbalance.md) each state one half correctly.

### 7. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.** **MONEY PATH.** The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is owed on the mechanism `P5-k` lands and is not discharged by this entry's signature.
- **What makes the ruling defensible on a planning session's authority is that it is strictly a REFUSAL of three shapes plus a statement that an approved sentence means what it says.** It adds no state, relaxes no constraint, and admits no identity to anything. The half that decides behaviour, the storage and its clock, is refused in section 3 rather than taken.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Three of the decisions here are judgements rather than derivations. **That the global halt is AUTOMATIC** (clause 1) reads *"an incident, not a row"* as a statement about storage and not about who stops the payouts, and section 5's first alternative is the coherent opposite reading: rule it procedural and amend the approved sentence. That alternative is the cheapest option available and this entry refuses it on the ground that it makes the corpus consistent by deleting the control. **That the halt refuses the INTERNAL leg too** (clause 4) is what makes the ruling bind after ADR-019 rather than before it, and the entry says to read it twice: a stop that reaches only the rail leaves Merit recognising liability out of a book it has said it does not understand. **That the storage, the release path and the authority are REFUSED rather than taken** (section 3) hands the next session four money-path decisions, and the entry flags the one whose usual answer inverts here: every other clock in this corpus releases on expiry, and a global halt that releases itself resumes paying out of a book nobody explained.
