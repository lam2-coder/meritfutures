## ADR-226: the Turnstile token is verified, because a required field that is never checked teaches every caller that any string works  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**`MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md` Appendix D section D2 rules `Turnstile on auth+checkout` among the binding application controls, and this tree took the token and threw it away.** [`routes/auth.ts:281`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts) declared `turnstile_token`, [`:894`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts) refused it as *"must be a non-empty string"*, and those were every mention of it in the file. Re-run before a line changed, `grep -rIn "siteverify\|TURNSTILE_SECRET" apps packages e2e scripts .github` returned **nothing**: **no verification call to Cloudflare existed anywhere in this repository.** This entry rules that the token is **VERIFIED**, and the verification lives in [`apps/api/src/turnstile.ts`](../../apps/api/src/turnstile.ts).

**THIS WAS WORSE THAN AN ABSENT CONTROL AND THAT IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT FOR THE EXPENSIVE HALF OF THE RULING.** An absent control is a gap a reader can see. A REQUIRED field that is never checked is a gap that reads as a control: it **teaches a caller that any string works**, so the first client to be written learns to send `"t"` and every later one copies it, and it makes the endpoint look protected to every reader of the type. [`apps/portal/src/http/client.ts:507`](../../apps/portal/src/http/client.ts) is that reader already, listing CSRF *"among the binding application controls, beside Turnstile and the CSP"*, and **both of its named peers were absent from this tree** when it was written.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `226`, session **416**, dispatched as `claude/turnstile-is-accepted-and-never-verified`. The row is **RESERVED, CONDITIONAL** on a ruling being taken, and the number is spent.

- **MONEY PATH: auth.** `ADR-003` strict, one objective. **The `E2` read is owed on [`apps/api/src/turnstile.ts`](../../apps/api/src/turnstile.ts) and on the `POST /auth/otp` handler in [`routes/auth.ts:1141`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts).** This entry ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** Section 1's greps and section 8's counts are **MEASURED** over the tree at the moment of writing. That `request.ip` is not the address that solved the challenge is **DERIVED** from `server.ts` building Fastify with no `trustProxy` (section 5). What is **JUDGED** is that verifying beats removing the field (section 2), that an absent secret refuses (section 3), that the outage posture is fail-closed (section 4), and that the check belongs in the handler rather than in the backend (section 6).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every claim below was read at its own file. Section 1 records what the row said against what the tree says, **including the one place the row understated the gap.**

### 2. VERIFY, and not remove. There was no third answer and this is why the first one wins

The row admits exactly two: check the token, or delete the field. **Deleting it was the cheaper diff and it is refused on three grounds.**

**It discharges nothing.** The constitution rules Turnstile on auth. Removing the field leaves `D2-7` exactly as absent as it was and additionally removes the one place a later session would have started from.

**It is a contract BREAK where verifying is a contract TIGHTENING.** `turnstile_token` is `required` on `OtpRequest` at [API_CONTRACT `:103`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md). Removing a required request field is a change every caller must be told about; verifying it is a change only a caller sending a fake value notices. **[ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 3's rule points the same way**: widening later is a decision and narrowing later is a break.

**And the field is the cheapest half of the control that exists.** The expensive half is a browser widget nobody has written (section 1's last row). A field with no verifier is one line from being a control; a control with no field is a client change in a package this fence does not hold.

### 3. AN ABSENT SECRET IS A REFUSAL. The deployment-time behaviour is the ruling, not a `TODO`

**`MERIT_TURNSTILE_SECRET` is [named at `turnstile.ts:122`](../../apps/api/src/turnstile.ts) and valued nowhere in this repository** ([ADR-012](ADR-012.md)). When it is unset, the verifier answers `unconfigured` and `POST /auth/otp` serves **`503 service_unavailable`**. It never answers `passed`.

**THE ANSWER THAT WAS AVAILABLE AND IS REFUSED IS "NO SECRET, NO CHECK".** It is the shape almost every anti-bot integration ships with, it keeps every environment working, and **it is the defect this entry exists to remove wearing a different hat**: a control that switches itself off when a variable is missing is a control whose absence is one deploy-config mistake away, and it fails in the direction that serves the attacker while every gate stays green.

**THE REFUSAL IS PER CALL AND NOT AT PROCESS START, on [ADR-197](ADR-197.md)'s stated ground for `MERIT_OTP_MAC_KEY`**: resolving a key in `start.ts` *"would make a missing secret a process that will not start, which turns a config omission on one route into an outage on all of them"* ([`auth-backend.ts:810`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts)). **A second ground is this deployable's own shape and it is stronger here than there.** `apps/api` runs as **two** services off one codebase ([`surface.ts`](../../apps/api/src/surface.ts) `SERVICE_BY_SURFACE`), and `/auth/otp` classifies `public`, so it is not registered on the operator surface at all: a start-time refusal would take **`api-admin`** down over a secret the admin console never uses.

**AND THE SECRET IS READ PER CALL RATHER THAN AT CONSTRUCTION**, for `resolveOtpMacKeys`'s reason: a value captured at import is a value a rotation cannot reach, and the constitution's own *"90-day rotation calendar"* ([`MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md:282`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md)) is a live obligation.

**WHAT THIS COSTS TODAY: NOTHING OBSERVABLE, AND THAT IS WHY IT WAS AFFORDABLE NOW.** `requestOtp` is blocked, so `POST /auth/otp` already answered 503 in every deployment. The strict posture changes which 503 an unconfigured deployment returns and changes nothing a caller can distinguish. **The day delivery lands, this ruling has teeth and the secret must already be set.** Taking the strict answer later, against a live sign-in path, would be a much harder argument to win.

### 4. THE OUTAGE POSTURE. Fail closed, and the cost is stated rather than discovered

**The call is an outbound network dependency on the sign-in path and the ruling is that it refuses when it cannot decide.** A timeout, a reset, a non-2xx, an unreadable body and an answer with no boolean `success` are all `unavailable`, and `unavailable` is **503**.

**SO A CLOUDFLARE `siteverify` OUTAGE BLOCKS NEW SIGN-INS FOR AS LONG AS IT LASTS.** That is the cost and it is not hidden. Four things bound it:

1. **It logs nobody out.** A session is resolved by `sessionByToken` from its own row and reaches no vendor, so every signed-in trader keeps working. The blast radius is new sign-ins only.
2. **The alternative spends real money on the attacker's behalf.** [SECURITY.md](../architecture/SECURITY.md) names **SMS pumping** as this endpoint's own threat, and the SMS branch charges against `otp_send_budget.spend_cents` in integer cents. **Fail-open on an anti-bot control guarding a paid channel is a decision to pay for the flood, taken automatically, at the moment observability is worst.**
3. **Fail-open is the old bug returning under a new name.** A control that admits every token when the vendor is slow is a control an attacker turns off by making it slow, and it is indistinguishable from today's *"any string works"* while it is off.
4. **Cloudflare is already in front of every origin** ([INFRA](../architecture/INFRA.md) section 2.1). A Cloudflare-wide failure plausibly stops the request reaching this code at all, so some of what fail-open would buy is not Merit's to buy.

**THE TIMEOUT IS `TURNSTILE_TIMEOUT_MS = 3000` ([`turnstile.ts:135`](../../apps/api/src/turnstile.ts)) AND THE NUMBER IS ARGUED RATHER THAN PICKED.** Without it there is no deadline this code chooses, and a `siteverify` that accepts a connection and never answers becomes a sign-in request holding a socket, once per attempt, for as many attempts as arrive. **`ENRICHMENT_TIMEOUT_MS` is 800 and is the wrong number here**: that one runs inside checkout's transaction, where the cost of waiting is a held database connection; this one holds no transaction. What this budget must not do is convert ordinary latency variance into a refusal, **because this control fails closed and a budget set too tight is a lockout with no attacker involved**.

**THERE IS NO RETRY.** The outcome refuses either way, so a second attempt doubles the worst-case latency of a sign-in to buy a marginal chance against one dropped packet, and it doubles the traffic Merit sends Cloudflare at exactly the moment Cloudflare is struggling.

**TWO STATUSES OVER THREE REFUSING OUTCOMES, split on what the client should do next.** `failed` is `403 forbidden` and means solve the challenge again; `unconfigured` and `unavailable` are `503 service_unavailable`, section 2's *"dependency down, safe to retry"*, and mean wait. **No new canonical code**, because section 2's table is closed and both codes are in it, on [ADR-221](ADR-221.md)'s precedent. **No `required_factor`**, because [ADR-111](ADR-111.md) clause 4 makes that member the elevation factor a client can offer and a challenge is not one.

### 5. `remoteip` IS NOT SENT, AND THIS IS THE LANDMINE THE FENCE ALMOST WALKED INTO

Cloudflare accepts an optional `remoteip`, and it must be the address that **solved** the challenge. Sending it looks free and is the natural next line.

**`server.ts:155` builds Fastify as `Fastify({ logger: options.logger ?? false })` and `trustProxy` appears in no file under `apps/api/src`.** So `request.ip` is the **immediate peer**, which behind Cloudflare and Railway is an edge address rather than the trader's. Submitting it would send a value that **agrees with itself in every test in this repository and disagrees with the solve on every real request**: a control that passes here and refuses in production, or the reverse, which is precisely [session 412's](../sessions/2026-08-29-session-412.md) `localhost:80` landmine one file over.

The field is optional. **It is omitted until something in this tree can produce the right value**, and the suite asserts the omission rather than leaving it to be read off the source.

### 6. THE CHECK IS IN THE HANDLER AND NOT IN THE BACKEND, and the order of the two lines is ruled

**`AuthBackend.requestOtp` is `blocked('requestOtp', NO_DELIVERY)`.** A verification written inside it would be code no request can reach, no test can drive through the router, and no founder read can watch refuse. **In the handler it is live today**: a token Cloudflare will not vouch for is refused before the 503 the blocked backend produces, and the suite asserts that ordering.

**VALIDATION FIRST, CHALLENGE SECOND, BACKEND THIRD.** A malformed body is `400` without spending a call on Cloudflare, so a flood of garbage costs Merit nothing outbound. **The challenge runs ahead of `requestOtp`, so a caller that cannot pass it never reaches the send budget** and never causes an SMS to be paid for. Anti-bot before the thing the bot came for is the point of the placement.

**THE ONE COST OF THAT ORDER, STATED.** The rate limit lives in the backend, so a caller can drive `siteverify` calls without passing a rate limit. Today that is theoretical, because the budget has no reader either ([session 411](../sessions/2026-08-29-session-411.md)'s `D2-6`). **The alternative order is worse**: it spends the SMS budget before deciding whether the caller is a bot, which is the one resource here denominated in cents.

**THE PORT'S DEFAULT IS THE REAL VERIFIER AND NOT AN UNWIRED SENTINEL.** That is the one place this differs from `AuthBackend` and it is deliberate: `start.ts` is outside this fence, and a control that is live only when a wiring slice remembers it is the same class of defect as a field nothing checks. `turnstile` is initialised at [`auth.ts:634`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts) with `cloudflareTurnstileVerifier()`, which reads the secret per call. **The port therefore has no unwired state: it verifies, or it refuses.** `wiring.test.ts` records exactly that as its fourth no-op entry rather than as a seventeenth liability.

### 7. WHAT A GREEN SUITE PROVES HERE, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

**Said plainly, because the interaction is easy to miss and a reader will otherwise assume coverage.** `requestOtp` is blocked on `NO_DELIVERY`, so **the deployed arm that would spend this token does not run**. Every assertion in `auth.test.ts` goes through the real router and the real handler and reaches a **fixture** backend.

**WHAT IS PROVED:** the handler's ordering, its four answers, that the caller's own token is the one submitted, that the request Cloudflare receives carries the secret and the token and no `remoteip`, that the call carries a deadline, and that **no failure mode of any kind returns `passed`**.

**WHAT IS NOT PROVED:** that a real `requestOtp` is reached only by a passing challenge, because there is no real `requestOtp`. **That is a smaller gap than it sounds** -- the ordering is asserted against the budget the fixture keeps -- and it is stated rather than left for the day delivery lands.

**AND THE THING THAT MADE THE FIX CHEAP IS THE SAME FACT.** No live traffic could be broken by this ruling today, which is why the strict posture in sections 3 and 4 was affordable to take now.

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS THE OUTAGE POSTURE, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY CHOICE HERE THAT CAN COST REVENUE.** Section 4 rules that a `siteverify` outage blocks every new sign-in. **The counter-position is respectable**: sign-in is the top of the funnel, an outage of a third party is not the trader's fault, and most of the industry fails open on exactly this call. **The answer this entry gives is that the paid channel is what makes Merit's case different** -- SMS costs cents per send and the threat row on this endpoint is SMS pumping -- **but a founder who weighs an hour of lost sign-ups above an hour of unmetered SMS should say so now.** The change is one arm of one `switch`. **It is much cheaper to decide before delivery lands than after.**

  **THE SECOND IS `3000`, which is the one number in this entry that is a judgement rather than a derivation.** It is not a rate limit and it is not config; it is a constant, argued in section 4 against `ENRICHMENT_TIMEOUT_MS`'s 800 on the ground that this call holds no transaction and that a fail-closed control must not be trigger-happy. **If it is wrong it is wrong in the direction of lockouts**, and the two-minute check is to look at what `siteverify` actually costs from Railway, which nothing in this repository can measure.

  **THE THIRD IS WHETHER `unconfigured` SHOULD BE LOUDER THAN A 503.** Today an unconfigured deployment is indistinguishable to a caller from a Cloudflare outage, and both are indistinguishable from the blocked backend's 503, so **the only place the difference is visible is a log line**. That is enough while `requestOtp` is blocked and it will not be enough afterwards. **The candidates are a startup log line naming the missing variable, or a health-check leg**, and both are `start.ts` or `server.ts`, which are other sessions' files.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left to be found.** **Section 5's `remoteip` reasoning is correct about the tree and could age badly.** It rests on `trustProxy` being unset today. **If a later session sets `trustProxy` for a rate limiter or for logging, `request.ip` becomes the client address and the argument for omitting `remoteip` evaporates** -- and nothing here will notice, because the suite asserts the omission rather than the reason for it. The repair on that day is one parameter and one test, and the sentence to look for is this one.

  **AND ONE OBJECTION WORTH TWO MINUTES, because it is the strongest thing on the other side.** A reader could say that this entry ships a control that nothing can currently exercise end to end: the widget does not exist, delivery is blocked, and the only traffic that will ever meet it is traffic written after this lands. **The honest answer is that this is true and it is the argument FOR shipping it now rather than against.** The field existed and lied for as long as it did precisely because nothing exercised it. **A control written before the first real caller is a control the first real caller is built against**; one written afterwards is a migration of every client that learned `"t"` works.
