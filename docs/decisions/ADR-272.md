## ADR-272: a suffix that lies inside the temporal vocabulary is the defect, and a name that says nothing is not  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-271](ADR-271.md) section 3 bounded a live type parser's blast radius with this sentence: *"an instant is spelled `*_at` and a day is spelled `*_day` or `*_on`, so the two vocabularies do not overlap and a parser aimed at one cannot reach the other. The rule was written as an API-surface refusal and it turns out to have been load bearing three layers down."* That entry then found the one column that breaks it and could not repair it. This row asks the prior question: whether [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 2's suffix convention is a RULE the schema must satisfy or a CONVENTION the schema mostly follows.**

**IT IS NEITHER, AS WRITTEN, AND THAT IS THE RULING.** Clause 2 governs **response fields at the point of projection** and reaches no column name at all. What makes it a schema rule anyway is that a repair has already been reasoned from it: ADR-271 argued a global driver change safe because `*_at` columns are instants. **A property a repair has been argued from is a rule, whatever document it was written in.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `272`, session **463**, dispatched as `claude/a-column-that-lies-about-its-type`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** The highest on disk is `0074`. Section 5 specifies the rename that is owed and states why this session is not the one that lands it.

- **NOT MONEY BY CONTENT, AND NOT MONEY BY BLAST RADIUS EITHER.** No DDL moves, no reader moves, no value moves. The only executable thing this entry lands is a check. `E2` is not claimed and not discharged.

- **`RI-26` IS MINTED IN THIS COMMIT** with its row in the same commit, and section 7 records the four seeds it was watched red under before it was claimed to work.

- **`RI-25`'s ROW WAS MISSING FROM THE `RI-nn` TABLE AND IS WRITTEN HERE, LATE.** Section 9. [ADR-271](ADR-271.md) section 8 states that check was *"minted here with its row in the same commit"*; the table still read *"`RI-25` IS THE NEXT FREE NUMBER"* and carried no such row. Citation-repair rights were granted up front.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) CLAUSE 2 IS NOT AMENDED, WIDENED, OR RESTATED HERE.** It remains a refusal about response fields at the point of projection. This entry adds nothing to the API surface and takes nothing off it.
>
> **2. THE SCHEMA-LEVEL PROPERTY IS A RULE, AND IT IS A RULE BECAUSE A LANDED REPAIR WAS ARGUED FROM IT.** Finding 3. [ADR-271](ADR-271.md) shipped a global `setTypeParser` on the ground that the two vocabularies do not overlap. That is not a convention somebody preferred; it is a premise a live change depends on, and a premise nothing checks is a premise that stops being true without anybody noticing.
>
> **3. THE RULE IS THIS, AND IT IS NARROWER THAN THE CONVENTION IT COMES FROM. A COLUMN NAME MAY BE SILENT ABOUT ITS TEMPORAL TYPE AND MAY NOT BE FALSE ABOUT IT.** A name is FALSE when it names a member of the temporal vocabulary that the column does not hold. `{instant, day}` is that vocabulary. `calibration_observed_at` says instant and holds day, so it is false. `period_start` says nothing, so it is silent. **The defect is not the missing suffix. The defect is the wrong one.**
>
> **4. THE GROUND FOR CLAUSE 3 IS DETECTABILITY AND NOT TASTE, AND IT IS WHY THE FOUR COLUMNS IN FINDING 8 ARE NOT DEFECTS.** A name that lies INSIDE the temporal vocabulary hands a reader a wrong answer of the right shape: `date` and `timestamptz` are both strings to every reader in this tree, both order lexicographically, and nothing downstream refuses either. A name that lies ACROSS type families, `win_day boolean`, is refused by the first type the compiler, the driver or the planner gives it. **Section 3 is the argument. Only the undetectable one needs a rule, because the detectable one already has one.**
>
> **5. A NAME THAT IS SILENT MUST STILL DENOTE ONE TYPE ACROSS THE ESTATE.** Finding 7. `effective_from` is a day in six tables and an instant in three, and no reader can tell which without opening the migration. **This is clause 3's harm arriving through a name that makes no claim at all**, so silence buys immunity from clause 3 and not from this one.
>
> **6. THEREFORE, AND THIS IS THE DISPOSITION THE ROW ASKED FOR: `simulation_runs.calibration_observed_at` IS A DEFECT. `effective_from` IS A DEFECT OF A DIFFERENT AND MEASURED KIND. THE OTHER SIX SILENT NAMES ARE NOT DEFECTS AND THE ROW'S SECOND TRAP IS UPHELD FOR THEM.** `effective_to`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `payable_after`, `negative_balance_since` and `trading_day_vendor` each denote exactly one type everywhere they appear. **The burden the row placed on the rule is not discharged for those six and this entry does not churn them.**
>
> **7. NEITHER DEFECT IS REPAIRED IN THIS COMMIT, AND THE REASON IS A FENCE RATHER THAN A JUDGEMENT.** Section 5. Both repairs are specified precisely enough for the session that holds the fence to execute without re-deriving anything.
>
> **8. THE PROPERTY IS PINNED BY `RI-26` WITH BOTH DEFECTS AS RULED EXCEPTIONS, ASSERTED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** `0027`'s float-column idiom, which [data-model/README](../architecture/data-model/README.md):492 states as this estate's own shape: *"an unlisted column fails, and so does a stale entry naming a column that no longer exists."* **A new violator fails, and the day either repair lands the check names the entry to retire.** Section 7.
>
> **9. `_day` AND `_on` ARE NOT RULED SYNONYMS AND ARE NOT RULED DISTINCT HERE.** Finding 9 found the contract and the schema disagreeing about what `_on` promises. That is a real drift, it is on an `approved` document this row does not fence, and section 6 records it as owed. **`RI-26` treats them as one vocabulary member and says so in its own `covers` line**, because a check that guessed would be encoding a ruling nobody made.

### 3. Why a suffix that lies across type families is not this defect, which is the clause a reviewer should attack

**The tempting rule is the tidy one: every column's suffix matches its type.** It is one sentence, it is mechanical, and it would have turned five columns red instead of two. It is also wrong, and the reason is worth stating because the tidy rule is what a future session will propose.

Take the two on the money path. `daily_marks.win_day` is `boolean NOT NULL`, sitting in a table whose second column is `trading_day date NOT NULL`. Ask what goes wrong if a reader trusts the suffix:

| | `win_day boolean`, read as a day | `calibration_observed_at date`, read as an instant |
|---|---|---|
| What the reader gets | `true` | `'2026-08-14'` |
| What refuses it | `tsc`, on `boolean` against `string`. The driver, on the wire. Any comparison against `trading_day`. Any renderer expecting `yyyy-mm-dd` | **Nothing.** Both are `string` in drizzle, both are `string` in the response shape, both order lexicographically, and `'2026-08-14'` is a legal prefix of an RFC 3339 instant |
| When it is noticed | The first time it runs | Possibly never |

**That is the whole argument.** A type system is a checker that already exists, and a rule whose only job is to duplicate it buys nothing while charging four renames on a merged money-path table. The rule earns its keep exactly where the type system is blind, and it is blind between a day and an instant because Merit stores both as text and reads both as `string` ([ADR-271](ADR-271.md) section 3 fact 1: *"every one of them is a `PgDateString`, whose `mapFromDriverValue` returns a `string` on both branches"*).

**The honest cost of this clause, stated rather than buried.** `traded_day` and `win_day` are predicates wearing a noun's suffix, and a person skim-reading `daily_marks` may briefly read them as dates. This entry accepts that cost. What it will not accept is a migration against a merged money-path table to buy a clarity the compiler already enforces. **`RI-26`'s `covers` line names all four columns as ruled rather than missed**, so the next reader who notices them finds a decision instead of a gap.

### 4. `effective_from`, and why the row's second trap protects six names and not the seventh

**The row was right to set the trap and right about six of the seven.** Its words: *"`effective_from` and `period_start` are ordinary English, and the burden is on the rule to justify churning them, not on them to justify existing."* A convention argument does not discharge that burden. **A measurement does, and only for `effective_from`.**

`period_start` is a `date` in `affiliate_statements` and nowhere else. Reading the name tells you nothing, and then you open one file and know. Cost: one lookup, once.

`effective_from` is a `date` in six tables and a `timestamptz` in three. Reading the name tells you nothing, **and then you open one file and know something that is false about the other eight.** The lookup does not converge, and the failure it invites is precise: a helper written against `contract_specs.effective_from` that treats the value as a day, reused against `firm_parameters.effective_from`, silently takes the UTC calendar date of an instant. **That is [ADR-146](ADR-146.md) clause 4's forbidden derivation and [ADR-271](ADR-271.md)'s repaired defect, reached through a name rather than through a driver.**

**Nothing in the tree does this today and that is not the claim.** [ADR-271](ADR-271.md) section 3 checked the one door that reads `firm_parameters` and recorded it explicitly: *"`effectiveFirmParameterStatement` | `firm_parameters`, ordered by `effective_from` | **none.** That column is `timestamp with time zone`, not `date`."* **A reader had to check, and the name did not help.** The claim is that the ambiguity is real, measured, load-bearing on a column ordered by a live door, and until now written down nowhere.

**It is still not a rename this session takes**, and section 5 says why: three of the nine tables are `wallet_spend_limits`, `price_floors` and `firm_parameters`, and `firm_parameters` is `0074`, the newest migration in the estate.

### 7. `RI-26`, and the four seeds it was watched red under

**The property deserves an `RI-nn`** and the row's condition is met, so `RI-26` is minted with its row in the `RI-nn` table in the same commit. **It reads DDL, not a database, not `schema.ts`**, because the migration set is where a column is declared and `schema.ts` is a mirror that can drift from it.

**Two legs, each in both directions, and the reverse direction is the one that matters most.** An exception set nobody can prove stale is a suppression list. So each entry carries the migrations that WITNESS it, and on a tree carrying those migrations the check fails if the entry stops being a violator: either the repair landed and the entry is stale, or the reader stopped reading. **A broken reader turns this check RED rather than green**, which is the failure mode `RI-24` and `RI-25` each needed a separate sentinel to reach.

**WATCHED RED BEFORE GREEN, ON THE REAL TREE.** The check was written with **empty** exception sets and run first. It reported exactly two findings, `calibration_observed_at` and `effective_from`, and **the second was not in the dispatch**: the check found finding 7 before this entry knew to look for it, which is the whole case for preferring a mechanical assertion to a careful reading.

| Seed, on the real tree | Result |
|---|---|

Adding a member to `CHECKS` enrols it in that suite's clean-tree case automatically, and shipping the gate without the seeded red-watch its 25 siblings carry would read the *never weaken a gate* limit backwards. **A reviewer is invited to disagree.**

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**Clause 4 is the load-bearing judgement and it is a judgement.** The claim that a `boolean` wearing `_day` is caught by a type system is true of every reader in this tree today; it is not true of a human reading `daily_marks` in a review, and `daily_marks` is the payout basis. **A reviewer who thinks the money path should carry the tidy rule regardless of detectability is disagreeing with clause 4 and not with a fact**, and clause 4 is where to push.

**Clause 5 may be over-reach.** Requiring a silent name to denote one type across the estate is a rule this entry invented, on one measured instance, and its cost is a future rename of a word as ordinary as `effective_from`. The narrower alternative, ruling only `calibration_observed_at` and leaving `effective_from` reported, was available and the row explicitly said it would be a complete outcome. **This entry took the wider one because finding 7 is measured rather than aesthetic, and a reviewer may hold that a measurement of an ambiguity nothing has yet tripped over does not justify a nine-table debt.**

**And the exception set is the thing to distrust on principle.** Two grandfathered entries in a check's first commit is two more than none. The defence is that both are named, both carry their ground, both are asserted in both directions, and neither can outlive its repair silently. **The alternative was a gate this tree cannot ship green without editing files this row does not fence**, and the constitution's limit runs the other way.
