## ADR-067: An adjustment posts to the wallet and never to withdrawable, a debit may only reverse a credit this table itself posted, and the eligibility pair the fold plan offered is false because the corpus has two exits  (2026-08-20, status: proposed)

**[FOLD-03](../plans/FOLD-03-vendor-parity-gap-fill.md) section 6 specifies an audited admin credit or debit against an account or a wallet, and hands the money session three things to settle rather than inherit: the eligibility trade, the debit direction, and `GS-298`'s state. All three are settled here. The sharpest content is that the first of them is not a trade in the shape it was posed. The fold plan offers "excluded from eligibility and therefore unwithdrawable" against "admitted and therefore an obligation under zero denial", and BOTH HORNS ASSUME ONE BALANCE. [M20:41](../plans/M20-wallet.md) states in terms that the wallet is "the only balance in the system with two exits", so a credit can be excluded from the payout computation and still be money the trader can take. The ruling takes that third path, states what it costs, and says which registry rows it contradicts.**

- **Context:** [FOLD-03](../plans/FOLD-03-vendor-parity-gap-fill.md) section 6 is the specification and [ADR-066](ADR-066.md) section 7 deliberately did not rule it, because it is the one money-path item among the six referred by the vendor baseline check. It amends [ADR-010](ADR-010.md), whose sensitive set is a closed list of five that does not contain an adjustment ([M06:160](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)). It amends nothing else, and section 2 is the reason: the mechanism it needs already exists in [`0009`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) and [`0011`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) and has never been reachable. `0038` is the migration, `SD-M6-09` is the delta, and `GS-296` to `GS-299` were registered ahead of this ruling by [session 86](../sessions/2026-08-20-session-86.md), which said at the time that a reader finding them first "will find scenarios describing a feature the corpus has not ruled on".

### 0. What was checked, and the three corrections that shape the ruling

**Every claim below was read at `file:line` in this session rather than inherited from the fold plan**, per the [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) caution that this corpus's three worst errors were a ledger class that did not exist, a debit account that broke ruled recognition timing, and a sign written backwards inside the ADR recording the first two. **All three of those are live risks in this exact file**, so section 2 names the destination class, section 2.3 names the debit leg against the ruled recognition moments, and section 2.4 writes the sign convention out in full.

### 2. The destination, which is the whole of why the other two questions get easier

#### 2.1 `trader_withdrawable` is not a position anybody may credit

**It is the ledger's mirror of an engine computation, and the engine's copy is the one every decision reads.** [`gates.ts:78`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/gates.ts) is four lines:

```
if (state.phase !== 'funded') return 0n;
const surplus = state.balanceCents - plan.sizeCents - plan.funded.bufferCents;
return surplus > 0n ? surplus : 0n;
```

**`balanceCents` is the trading balance.** It arrives from marks and fills through the vendor bridge, `clampPayout` reads `state.withdrawableCents`, and `R-43`'s entire product claim is that the number the trader is **shown** and the number they are **paid** come out of one function. **A ledger entry cannot reach any of that.** So a credit to the `trader_withdrawable` class would raise a ledger position that no gate, no clamp and no screen reads, while the engine's number stayed exactly where it was. The result is not a manufactured obligation. It is a ledger that disagrees with the engine about what a trader may draw, which is the failure [ADR-027](ADR-027.md) was reversed over, arriving from the other end.

**The only way an admin action could manufacture payout eligibility is by writing a trading balance or by writing `rule_states` directly, and both are already forbidden**: [M06:35](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) puts "computing eligibility, liability inputs, or any rule" outside this module in terms, and `rule_states` is engine output. **Nothing in this ADR creates a path to either**, and no new invariant is needed to say so.

#### 2.2 `trader_wallet` is where money Merit owes already lives, and `correction` is already a legal provenance for it

**Three facts, none of them added here.**

1. [M20:41](../plans/M20-wallet.md) defines a wallet balance as **"money the trader has already earned, which has already cleared every gate, which Merit owes them unconditionally"**, and calls it the most certain liability on the book.
2. [`0011:74`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) already carries **`correction`** in `wallet_entries.provenance`'s closed list, described at [`0011:64`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) as *"a compensating entry, never an update"*. **`0038` widens no closed list**, adds no provenance value, and therefore does not go anywhere near `INV-WALLET-NO-DEPOSITS`.
3. [`0011:90`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) makes `balance_after_cents >= 0` a `CHECK`. **A debit that would overdraw a wallet is already unwritable**, four migrations before this one, which is half of section 4's answer arriving for free.

**A goodwill credit is therefore not a new kind of object. It is the kind of object the wallet was built to hold, posted through a door nobody had built.**

#### 2.3 The debit leg, named against the ruled recognition moments rather than chosen

**The chart of accounts is seven codes and they are declared "the whole permitted set"** ([`0009:49`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql)), enforced twice: the `CHECK` on `ledger_accounts.code` and `LEDGER-C2` in [`0027`](../../packages/db/migrations/0027_triggers_invariants.sql). **There is no expense class**, and the seven contain no natural home for goodwill.

**The debit leg is `fees_revenue`, and no eighth class is created.** An adjustment credit posts, in the ledger's signed convention:

| Leg | Account | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| **credit** | `trader_wallet`, the identity's | `-amount_cents` |
| **debit** | `fees_revenue`, firm scope | `+amount_cents` |

**This is checked against the ruled recognition timing rather than asserted to be compatible with it.** The batch 1 gate ruled three moments ([M05:560](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) `OQ-M5-04`): **payout liability books at approval, cash derecognizes at settlement, evaluation fees recognize at purchase.** An adjustment is none of the three. It books a liability and reduces revenue **in one moment, its own**, and it moves no cash: cash still moves at `LT-02` and `LT-07` and nowhere else. **`firm_treasury` as the debit leg was the available mistake and is refused for the same reason [M05:140](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) refused it on `LT-01`**: it books a cash movement at a moment when no cash moved.

**What the choice costs, stated rather than discovered later.** `fees_revenue` now nets goodwill given against fees earned, so **a revenue figure quoted without joining `account_adjustments` understates fees and understates goodwill by the same number**. That is a reporting join, and it is cheaper than an eighth ledger class: the row already carries reason, direction and amount, so "goodwill given this month" is one query. **An eighth class was considered and refused in the Alternatives**, on the ground that this entry already amends [ADR-010](ADR-010.md) and a second amendment, to the file this corpus was reversed over, should be argued on its own merits and not carried in as a convenience.

#### 2.4 The sign convention, written out because getting it backwards is this corpus's named repeat error

[`0009:118`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) states it and says why: **`amount_cents` is signed, positive is DEBIT, negative is CREDIT**, and the convention "is stated in three places because getting it backwards is the error that landed four times in one day on `LT-01`". **`wallet_entries` deliberately does NOT share it** ([`0011:58`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)): there, magnitude is always positive and `direction` carries the sign, because "reusing one convention for two different questions is the shape of error [ADR-027](ADR-027.md) was reversed over".

**`account_adjustments` follows `wallet_entries` and not `ledger_entries`**: `amount_cents > 0` always, `direction` carries the sign. It is a wallet-facing record and it sits beside `wallet_entries` in every query that matters. **Restated once as a table, because the trigger in `0038` asserts exactly this mapping and a reader must be able to check it without holding two conventions in their head:**

| Adjustment `direction` | `wallet_entries.direction` | `ledger_entries` on `trader_wallet` | `ledger_entries` on `fees_revenue` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `credit` | `credit` | `-amount_cents` | `+amount_cents` |
| `debit` | `debit` | `+amount_cents` | `-amount_cents` |

### 3. Eligibility: the trade is stated, and then the pair is refused

**The fold plan's framing, quoted so the refusal is visible.** *"Zero denial means an eligible request is paid, so an adjustment that creates eligibility creates an obligation. Excluding adjustments makes a goodwill credit unwithdrawable, which is a different broken promise."*

**Both horns are real and the trade between them is real. The pair is not exhaustive.**

| | What it would mean | Why it is not the ruling |
|---|---|---|
| **Admit into eligibility** | The credit raises `withdrawableCents` and the next request pays it out | **Unreachable.** Section 2.1: no ledger posting is an input to that function, so "admit" would require writing a trading balance or `rule_states`, both of which [M06:35](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) already forbids. The horn describes a door that does not exist |
| **Exclude, and accept an unwithdrawable credit** | Goodwill that the trader can see and never take | **The premise is false.** [M20:41](../plans/M20-wallet.md): two exits. Wallet value is withdrawable through `wallet_withdrawals` and spendable inside Merit, neither of which is the payout computation |
| **Exclude from the payout computation, land in the wallet** | The credit never touches a gate, and the trader takes it on the rail built for money already owed | **The ruling** |

**What the ruling costs, in full, because a third path with no cost would be a sign that something was not read.**

1. **The wallet rail's own gates apply.** [M20:137](../plans/M20-wallet.md): KYC verified, destination outside cooling, name matched, and **at least 10,000 cents**. **So a goodwill credit of less than 10,000 cents cannot be withdrawn on its own.** It is spendable inside Merit and it aggregates with later wallet value, but a trader offered 5,000 cents of goodwill cannot take it as cash that day. **This is the honest residue of the eligibility question and it is not zero.**
2. **The obligation is created, deliberately and visibly.** The half of the fold plan's first horn that survives is that a credit **is** an obligation. It is one immediately, in the most certain liability class on the book, on the Open Liability page, rather than one that appears later when a gate happens to clear. **That is the better failure**: an obligation Merit can see the moment it takes it on.
3. **`P-1` may hold a withdrawal that contains adjustment value.** [M20:176](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s provenance rule composes withdrawals FIFO and holds for review any composition including payout credits from accounts purchased with promotional credit. An adjustment credit carries provenance `correction` and is not itself in `P-1`'s predicate, **but it sits in the same FIFO queue**, so a withdrawal that spans it and a `P-1` credit is held on the other credit's account. Nothing here changes that and nothing here should.

**`GS-296` is written against this and its registry row does not contain it.** Section 9.

### 4. The debit: permitted, and only as the reversal of a credit this table posted

**The question, from the fold plan: a debit takes money from a trader's balance and nothing in the corpus permits that today. `INV-M6-03` says no admin action can deny a payout. Is a debit permitted at all, and if it is, what stops it becoming the denial mechanism `INV-M6-03` forbids?**

**Ruled: permitted in exactly one shape. A debit row must name a credit row this table itself posted, must match it in identity, destination and amount, and there may be at most one debit per credit.** In DDL that is a `CHECK` (`(direction = 'debit') = (reverses_adjustment_id IS NOT NULL)`), a partial unique index on `reverses_adjustment_id`, and a trigger asserting the four-way match and refusing a reversal of a reversal.

**What that buys, stated as the property rather than as the intention: a debit can only remove money Merit itself put there by adjustment. It can never reach a cent the trader earned.** A clawback, a penalty, an offset against an alleged loss, a recovery of an overpayment that was not an adjustment: none of them can be written, because none of them names a prior credit from this table. **That is not a rule an operator follows. It is a row the database refuses.**

**Why this is the line and not a wider one.**

- **[M20:65](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-09`: a wallet balance is "payable on demand forever", and dormancy never forfeits it.** A debit available on judgment would make that clause conditional on Merit's opinion, which is the same sentence as a forfeiture clause with a slower delivery.
- **`INV-M6-03`'s adjacency is the fold plan's real worry and it is right to be worried.** A debit against a balance is not literally a payout denial: `withdrawableCents` is untouched and the request still evaluates and still pays. **But the money a trader would have withdrawn and the money a debit removes are the same money**, so a general debit power is a denial mechanism that arrives one table over from the invariant forbidding it. The reversal-only rule is what keeps the two apart, and it keeps them apart structurally.
- **A genuine overpayment that was not an adjustment already has its own machine.** An erroneous payout reverses through `LT-03` ([M05:131](../plans/M05-payout-system.md)); a refund of a wallet-funded purchase is `M03`'s; a reconciliation difference on a trading balance is absorbed and reported through `absorbed_corrections_cents` ([`0009`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql), the `OQ-10` ruling). **Every one of those is a path that exists.** An adjustment debit that reached into them would be a second expression of a mechanism the corpus already has, which is this repository's most repeated defect.
- **Partial reversal is refused and full reversal plus a corrected credit is the remedy.** A 50,000 cent credit that should have been 20,000 cents is reversed in full and re-posted at 20,000, which leaves three visible postings instead of a row that quietly shrank. That is [`0009:96`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql)'s `SD-M5-05` reasoning applied one table up: **corrections are compensating entries, never updates**, and reconstructing which reversal answered which original must be instant rather than archaeology.

**A reversal is dual-controlled on its own amount**, by the same `CHECK` as any other row, so reversing an above-threshold credit needs the second key too.

**And the debit cannot overdraw.** [`0011:90`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)'s `balance_after_cents >= 0`. If the trader has already withdrawn the credit, **the reversal fails and the recovery is a dispute rather than a debit**, which is [M20:165](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s own position on a settled halt: "the money is gone; recovery is a dispute, not a reversal."

### 5. Dual control, and the amendment to [ADR-010](ADR-010.md)

**[M06:160](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) states the sensitive set as a closed list of five: payout cap, split, cadence gap, treasury credentials, and rail credentials. An adjustment is not on it. THIS ADR ADDS IT, AND THAT IS AN AMENDMENT TO [ADR-010](ADR-010.md) RATHER THAN AN APPLICATION OF IT.** Stated in those words because [ADR-066](ADR-066.md) section 0 row 4 is the finding that a fold treating it as an application would have written a false citation into a frozen document.

**The set is six. The sixth is: an account or wallet adjustment at or above the configured threshold.**

**Why an adjustment is the most dangerous member of the set, which is a stronger claim than "it belongs in it".** [ADR-010](ADR-010.md)'s honest framing is carried verbatim into the UI: at launch scale this is **compromise resistance, not insider resistance**, because both credentials are the founder's on separate hardware keys. **For the other five, a compromised session that moves a parameter must then wait for traders to act before any money leaves.** For an adjustment it does not wait: a credit to a wallet the attacker controls, then a withdrawal on the wallet rail. **The threshold is therefore not a convenience dial. It is the size of the loss one compromised owner session can cause without a second key**, and it should be set low and read as that number.

**The threshold in force is a column on the row.** `dual_control_threshold_cents`, `NOT NULL`, recorded at request time on [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql) `plan_breaker_state`'s precedent, which stores `threshold_bp` and `min_sample` beside the ratio for exactly this reason. **Without it, "was this adjustment above the line" is answered by today's configuration against a row written last quarter**, and a threshold that was raised after the fact would retroactively make an uncontrolled adjustment look compliant. The `CHECK` reads `amount_cents < dual_control_threshold_cents OR dual_control_approval_id IS NOT NULL`, so **the control cannot be written around: a row that needed a second key and does not name one does not exist.**

**No new dual-control machinery is built.** `dual_control_approvals` ([`0016:224`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql)) already carries the payload hash, the distinct-approver `CHECK`, the window and the status. `subject_kind` is `text` and takes `account_adjustment` without a schema change.

**One residual is not ruled here and is named rather than left.** The `CHECK` is per row, so **a compromised session can post repeated sub-threshold credits** and no constraint sums them. The corpus has the right shape for the answer already, in [M20:63](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-07`'s per-identity wallet spend velocity limit, but a per-actor rolling limit and its alarm live in velocity policy and [CRON_INVENTORY](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md), **both outside this session's fence**. `OQ-F6-02` carries it, the launch mitigation is a low threshold plus the `admin_actions` trail `INV-M6-01` already guarantees, and **this entry says so rather than letting the per-row `CHECK` read as the whole control.**

### 6. The reason: a closed vocabulary and a free-text note, and the vocabulary decides the destination

**Three values, closed in the migration, and the closure is the point: a reason column that accepts free text is a reason column nobody can aggregate, and "goodwill" spelled four ways is four categories.**

| `reason_code` | Destination | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `goodwill` | `trader_wallet` | Merit chooses to give value. A service failure, a gesture, a dispute settled in the trader's favour |
| `reconciliation_error` | `trader_wallet` | Merit got a number wrong in a way that costs the trader, and the correction is not one the existing paths carry |
| `promotional_credit` | `promotional_credit` | A marketing grant, **never wallet value** |

**`reason_note` is `NOT NULL` and non-empty by `CHECK`.** Both are required, as the fold plan specifies. **The emptiness `CHECK` is not decoration**: `INV-M6-01` already requires a non-empty reason on `admin_actions` and describes `NOT NULL` as "the whole control", and a column that accepts a single space satisfies `NOT NULL` while satisfying nothing else.

**The third row is the correction from section 0.** [M20:59](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-03` says promotional credit can never become wallet balance **and that no chain of transactions may convert it into one**. A single table with one destination and three reasons would be that chain, in one hop, on its first row. **So the pairing is a `CHECK` and not a convention**: `(reason_code = 'promotional_credit') = (destination = 'promotional_credit')`. **`INV-M20-03` becomes unbreakable through this surface rather than defended on it.**

**And a promotional adjustment must name a grant.** `promotional_credit_grant_id` is `NOT NULL` exactly when the destination is `promotional_credit`. [`0024:204`](../../packages/db/migrations/0024_offers.sql) makes `promotional_credit_grants.expires_at` `NOT NULL` on the stated ground that expiry "is what distinguishes it from a payable" and that "an unexpiring promotional balance is a liability wearing a marketing label". **An admin route that posted to the `promotional_credit` ledger class without a grant row would create exactly that**, so this ADR refuses to open one.

### 7. `restricted` and `closed`

**`GS-298` says `restricted` and the fold plan is right that this matters.** [`0001:27`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) declares `identity_status AS ENUM ('active', 'restricted', 'closed')` and [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) refused a fourth value in terms, because "`suspended` would be `restricted` under a second name, and two expressions of one concept is this repository's most repeated defect". **There are three states and this ruling addresses all three.**

**Ruled: an adjustment is permitted against `active`, `restricted` and `closed` alike, in both directions, and the restriction gates extraction rather than the record.**

- **`restricted` already blocks both exits and needs nothing from this table.** [M20:62](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-06` enumerates the blocking set and names `identities.status = 'restricted'` in it, for spend **and** withdrawal, on the stated ground that "a freeze that stops the cash door and leaves the product door open is not a freeze". **So a credit posted to a restricted identity is an obligation Merit acknowledges and the trader cannot extract until restore.** The control is already there; a second one here would be a second expression of it.
- **Refusing the credit would make Merit unable to correct its own error against a person under investigation**, and a restriction is reversible by design ([ADR-041](ADR-041.md)). A reconciliation error does not stop being an error because its subject is being looked at, and holding the correction until an investigation closes is a decision about money made by the absence of a mechanism.
- **`closed` is the separate case the fold plan asks for and the answer is the same, for a different reason.** `INV-M20-09` says a wallet balance is payable on demand **forever** and that dormancy never forfeits it. **Merit owes what it owes after closure**, so a correction against a closed identity is permitted and posts normally. **It does not reopen the identity**, and the balance follows the dormancy and escheat path in [M20:182](../plans/M20-wallet.md) like any other. Refusing here would mean the one state in which a trader can least argue is the state in which Merit's error becomes permanent.
- **The debit is not treated differently by state**, because section 4 already made it the reversal of Merit's own credit. Restriction is not a reason to leave a mistaken credit standing.

**What is owed instead of a gate is a display.** An operator adjusting a restricted or closed identity must see the state on the screen they are acting from, so that "permitted" does not become "invisible". **That requirement lands in `SD-M6-09`** rather than as a new `INV-M6-nn`, and section 10 says why the invariant number was not claimed.

**`GS-298`'s registry row reads "Refused or held, per the ruling" and this ruling is neither.** Section 9.

### 8. Open Liability, and the one residual on the live figure

**The immediate-reflection requirement is satisfied by construction and needs no new arithmetic.** [M06:85](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) `P-M6-01` defines Open Liability as `sum(withdrawable_cents)` across funded accounts **plus `sum(wallet_balance_cents)` across identities**, and `INV-M6-11` requires every liability figure to include wallet balances. **An adjustment credit is a wallet balance. It is therefore in Open Liability by being what it is**, and `P-M6-01` already shows the two components separately, which is the presentation this ruling would otherwise have had to ask for.

**The residual, found by reading `P-M6-01` rather than assuming it.** `P-M6-01` is "as of the last closed day", and [M06:166](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s live Open Liability is **last closed snapshot plus intraday movement implied by the indicative feed**. **An adjustment posted at midday is in neither term**, so the live figure understates the book by exactly the adjustments posted since the last close.

**Ruled: the live Open Liability figure carries a third term, same-day adjustment postings at par.** It does not weaken `INV-M6-12`, and the reason is precisely why the term is allowed at all: `INV-M6-12` forbids an indicative figure being presented as an as-of-last-closed figure, and **an adjustment is neither indicative nor stale. It is an exact, Merit-originated, already-posted integer.** The second term is a guess about the market; this one is a fact about a row. **Adding it makes the live figure more authoritative rather than less**, and the third term is labeled as what it is on the same principle `INV-M6-12` already applies to the second.

**The prose home for that term is [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) section 3.5 and this session did not write it there.** Section 10.
