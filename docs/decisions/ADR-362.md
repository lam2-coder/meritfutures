## ADR-362: the two ways money comes IN hold defaults of different KINDS, and the one whose name says production does not refuse, it takes the payment (2026-09-06, status: proposed)

**ADR-357 section 3 listed `useCheckoutBackend` and `useCheckoutAdapters` side by side under one word, `refuses`. THE FIRST EARNS IT AND THE SECOND DOES NOT.** `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS` ([`checkout.ts:1203`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)) refuses ONE of the four members' paths, is UNREACHABLE on two, and SILENTLY DEGRADES on the fourth, and **a funded wallet checkout against it returns 200, records a purchase and quotes a figure.**

**RULED: `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS` DOES NOT REFUSE. IT FAILS ON ONE ARM AND SERVES ON THE OTHER.** The deployment is safe today because of the OTHER port and not because of this one.

- **MONEY PATH, `E2` READ OWED.** This is the checkout route and the ledger arm behind it. **Ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line. Merging is not signing.**

### 2. THE RULING

**RULED: THE TWO DEFAULTS ARE DIFFERENT KINDS OF THING AND ONLY ONE OF THEM REFUSES.**

1. **`UNWIRED_CHECKOUT_BACKEND` refuses, completely and in the ordinary way.** One member, rejecting with a distinctly named class the handler catches and serves as 503.
2. **`PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS` does not refuse.** Of its four members: **one refuses** (`adapterFor`), **two are unreachable** (`returnUrl`, `cancelUrl`), and **one degrades silently** (`enrichment`). There is no member whose default refusal covers the wallet arm, and the wallet arm is a path that takes money.
3. **A checkout CAN succeed against `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS`, and it does.** Measured through the real router: **200**, one purchase written at status `paid`, `amount_cents` and `wallet_debit_cents` both quoted.
4. **What refuses on the tree as it stands is the BACKEND, not the adapters.** `start.ts` calls neither setter, so `UNWIRED_CHECKOUT_BACKEND` rejects before the transaction body runs and no adapter member is read at all. **The adapter default's behaviour is therefore INVISIBLE today and becomes live the moment a wiring slice installs the backend alone.**
5. **`refusing: 13` / `live: 1` is wrong by one in each direction.** It is not this row's file to repair; section 7 states what is owed and to whom.

### 4. QUESTION 2: yes, a checkout can succeed, and this is the serious finding

**RULED: AGAINST `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS`, A FUNDED WALLET CHECKOUT TAKES THE PAYMENT.**

Measured through the real router, backend installed and adapters left at their declared default:

```
POST /checkout  payment_method=wallet   ->  200
{"purchase_id":"...","plan_version_id":"...","amount_cents":34900,
 "discount_cents":0,"payment_method":"wallet","wallet_debit_cents":34900}
purchases: 1   status: paid   ledger transactions: 1
```

**A PURCHASE IS RECORDED. MONEY MOVES IN THE LEDGER. A FIGURE IS QUOTED.** All three, against the value whose name is `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS` and whose docblock says *"this resolves nothing and checkout answers 503"*.

**THE REASON IS AN ORDERING AND IT IS ONE LINE.** The wallet arm of `completePurchase` returns before the adapter guard further down the same function. It touches exactly ONE adapter member on its way out, `enrichment`, whose `null` is guarded and skipped. **So the member that refuses is the member the money path never consults.**

**AND THE DOCBLOCK IS NOT WRONG SO MUCH AS PARTIAL.** *"Checkout answers 503"* is true of the card arm and false of the wallet arm, and the sentence does not say which. It is **not edited by this row**: it is a claim about what `packages/psp` ships, which is accurate, and repairing it is a `src/` change this row has no other reason to make. It is recorded so a reader grepping for `503` does not predict the wrong outcome for a wallet purchase.

**WHAT KEEPS THE DEPLOYMENT SAFE TODAY IS THE OTHER PORT.** Under the full default pair the backend rejects first and the adapter members are never read: measured at **503 with an EMPTY call trace and zero purchases**. **That is a safety that rests on a SECOND port staying unwired, which is not the same as this port refusing.** `useCheckoutBackend` is an active objective with its own entry and its own blockers, and the day a slice installs it without installing adapters, the request above is what the deployment serves.

### 5. QUESTION 3: one default cannot quote a number and the other one does

**RULED: `UNWIRED_CHECKOUT_BACKEND` CANNOT QUOTE A FIGURE, AND `PRODUCTION_CHECKOUT_ADAPTERS` DOES.**

- **The backend default: NO, and it is asserted rather than assumed.** Its 503 document carries exactly the five members `handlerProblem` writes, `type`, `title`, `status`, `code` and `instance`, and no sixth. A seeded `detail` reading *"Your balance is 34900 cents."* was watched RED.
- **The adapter default: YES.** `amount_cents: 34900` and `wallet_debit_cents: 34900`, on the success above. Both are integer cents through `centsToJson` and **no float is introduced anywhere on this path**, so the defect is that a figure is quoted at all and not how it is spelled.

**THIS IS THE SHARPEST FORM OF THE RULING.** ADR-358 recorded of `useAffiliateDeps` that *"there is no configuration under which this port quotes a number"*, and that is the property a refusing default has. **`useCheckoutAdapters` does not have it.**

### 9. Open questions, recorded as OPTIONS because none of them is this row's to answer

1. **Is a default that serves one arm and refuses another `live`, `refusing`, or a third thing?** This row rules on the BEHAVIOUR and declines to name the category, because the name lands in a file it does not own. **Options: (a) `live`, on the ground that any served request is service, which makes `{14, 12, 2}`; (b) a third term such as `partial`, which makes the triple a quadruple and is the most informative and the most disruptive; (c) `refusing`, kept deliberately with the arm-level fact recorded beside it, which is the smallest change and the one that preserves the reading this row has just falsified.** **(c) is recorded as available and is NOT recommended.**
2. **What are `returnUrl` and `cancelUrl`?** **NO VALUE IS INVENTED HERE.** They decide where a buyer's browser lands after paying, which is a product and brand decision and, in the cancel case, a decision about what a buyer who abandoned a purchase is shown. The module's own docblock refuses to default them for exactly this reason. **Options: (a) two configured absolute URLs supplied by the wiring slice; (b) one base URL with two derived paths; (c) provider-hosted pages, which is a contract question with the vendor that does not exist yet.** No timeout, retry count, currency or price is invented anywhere in this row.
3. **Should the wallet arm consult the adapter set at all?** It has no need of a PSP adapter and reads only `enrichment`. **The finding above exists because the two arms share one port.** **Options: (a) leave it, and let a wiring slice's ORDER be the control, which is what stands today; (b) refuse a checkout at the top of `completePurchase` when the adapter set is the empty one, which makes the port refuse uniformly and would have prevented this finding; (c) split the port, so the wallet arm depends on an enrichment port and the card arm on a PSP port.** **(b) is a `src/` behaviour change on the money path and this row did not take it**, because a refusal added to a path that currently succeeds is a decision about whether wallet-funded purchases may run in a deployment with no card processor, and that is a business ruling rather than a wiring one.

### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **THE FINDING IS CONDITIONAL AND THE CONDITION IS NAMED EVERYWHERE ABOVE.** The wallet success requires the checkout BACKEND to be installed. `start.ts` installs neither port, so **no request served by this deployment today reaches it.** A reader who takes section 4 as "checkout is taking payments right now" has read it wrong. **What is ruled is that the adapter default does not refuse, and that the safety is the other port's.**
- **The wallet arm's success is measured against the SUITE's `CheckoutBackend` fake**, because that is the only `CheckoutBackend` in the tree. It is a faithful transactional fake and the case drives the real router, but **a real backend could refuse earlier for reasons no fake models**, and this entry cannot see one.
- **`LIVE_DEFAULT` is a ruling and section 8 hands a ruling to another row.** If that sibling's mechanical derivation contradicts section 2, **the derivation is the thing to believe and this entry is the thing to correct.**
- **Section 6's claim that `contracts`, `now` and `report` wait on nothing is a claim about TYPES being satisfiable**, not a claim that an enrichment wiring is one commit away. The `integration_contracts` row `enrichment` needs is a separate matter that ADR-115 clause 4 governs.

### 12. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.**

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS SECTION 9 QUESTION 1, AND IT IS NOT A NAMING PREFERENCE.** A port that serves one arm and refuses another is a shape the wiring vocabulary has no word for, and ADR-357 minted `refusing` and `live` one wave ago without one. **Choosing `live` for this port makes the triple honest and makes two ports called `live` that differ enormously in how much of their contract they serve. Choosing a third term is more truthful and moves a structure a sibling row is mechanising this very wave.**

  **THE SECOND IS SECTION 9 QUESTION 3, AND IT IS A BUSINESS RULING WEARING A WIRING QUESTION'S CLOTHES.** May a deployment with no card processor take wallet-funded purchases? **If yes, what stands today is correct and section 4 is a documentation finding.** If no, the checkout route needs a refusal it does not have, and the ordering that produced this finding is a latent defect rather than a curiosity. **This entry does not answer it, because the answer decides whether real traders can spend a balance Merit already owes them.**

  **THE THIRD IS THE ONE THIS ENTRY IS LEAST COMFORTABLE LEAVING.** The safety of the money-in path today rests on `useCheckoutBackend` staying unwired, and that port is under active work with its own entry and its own blockers. **There is nothing in this tree that fails if a slice installs the backend and forgets the adapters.** Section 9 question 3 option (b) would be that guard. **Whether one is wanted is a decision, and the absence of one is now a measured fact rather than an oversight.**
