## ADR-167: the pass-rate CUSUM is RECOMPUTED and never stored, `plan_breaker_state` keeps its one-row-per-plan-day key, and the migration number reserved for a table returns to the pool  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[P7 section 5.3](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) found that [`0049`](../../packages/db/migrations/0049_reserve_coverage_snapshots.sql) dispositioned `API_CONTRACT`'s `per_plan` as needing nothing, checked four of its five fields, and left the fifth -- the `cusum` object -- with no column anywhere that fits it.** The plan enumerated three readings and took none, and [P7 section 10 item 3](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) held the choice for the founder. This entry takes the third: **`S_t` is folded at read time from a series the schema already carries, and no column, table or row holds it.** It rules that on three grounds, and **the plan's own stated ground is not one of them and is corrected here** rather than repeated. It forecloses widening `plan_breaker_state`'s primary key, forecloses any CUSUM value reaching the loss-ratio breaker's columns, forecloses a CUSUM alarm ever spelling `state = 'paused'`, and forecloses a float or a division entering the recurrence. **`0051` is NOT SPENT and returns to the pool.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `167`, session **281**, dispatched as `claude/p7c-cusum-home` against [P7](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) section 8's `P7-c` row. - **Context:** [`0016`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql) built `plan_breaker_state` for `SD-M6-02`, the loss-ratio breaker that pauses a plan's new sales, and it built it keyed one row per plan per evaluation day. ### 2. The three readings, and what the two that lose actually cost

**P7 section 5.3 enumerates them and takes none. Each is stated here at its strongest before its cost.**

#### Reading 1: `metric` joins the primary key

At its strongest: the column is already there, `NOT NULL`, distinguishing nothing. Widening the key to `(plan_id, evaluated_on, metric)` costs one `ALTER TABLE` and lets one plan-day carry a loss-ratio row and a CUSUM row side by side, which is what a `metric` column looks like it was for.

**Cost A. It changes the IDENTITY of a merged table.** `plan_breaker_state_current_idx (plan_id, evaluated_on DESC)` at [`0016:168`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql) is *"the breaker's state for this plan, most recent first"*, and after the widening every reader of it silently gets two rows where it got one. Nothing in this repository reads the table yet, which makes this cheap **today** and makes it a defect that lands fully-formed on the day `P7-k` and the console arrive together.

**Cost B. The columns become polymorphic, and one of them cannot be satisfied even dishonestly.** A CUSUM row must supply `numerator_cents` and `denominator_cents` (`bigint NOT NULL`, cents a CUSUM does not have), `ratio_bp` and `threshold_bp` (a loss ratio's basis points), and `sample_size`. And it must supply `min_sample integer NOT NULL CHECK (min_sample > 0)` -- **the check forbids even the honest zero**, so a CUSUM row is required to invent a minimum sample it does not have in order to be insertable at all. **A column whose meaning depends on the value of a sibling `text` column is precisely what `ratio_bp` having a name prevents.**

**Cost C, and it is the one that decides it: reading 1 gives an ADVISORY statistic a column that spells a REVENUE PAUSE.** After the widening, `state text NOT NULL CHECK (state IN ('armed', 'paused', 'insufficient_data', 'manually_overridden'))` governs CUSUM rows too, and `per_plan.sales_paused` is `state = 'paused'`. Finding 5 says the contract carries `sales_paused` and `cusum.alarm` as separate fields; finding 6 says the panel's own gloss is *inspect*. **Reading 1 puts a plan's sales one `UPDATE` away from being paused by a statistic that was never meant to pause anything**, on the table [`INV-M5-12`](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) is written about -- *"the circuit breaker pauses sales and can never pause payouts"* -- and one rail in from it. That invariant's own evidence column is *"Structural: the breaker's only effect is a flag read by M3's checkout"*, and reading 1 makes the flag settable by a second, weaker signal. **A primary-key change is not worth a money-path defect.**

#### Reading 2: a second table

**This is the reading that looks right, and its cost is not the migration.** A table keyed `(plan_id, evaluated_on)` carrying `statistic_bp`, the reference mean, the slack, the threshold and `alarm` types every field honestly, needs no borrowed column, and answers *"what did the CUSUM say on the day"* by lookup forever. The steelman is real: `detector_runs`' own header at [`0008:58`](../../packages/db/migrations/0008_risk.sql) is *"Provenance for every flag, so 'why did this not fire in March' is answerable"*, and a breaker is at least as consequential as a detector.

**Cost A. It stores a derived value beside its own inputs with no way to police the two agreeing -- which is the objection `0049` ITSELF raised, on this same rail, one migration ago.** [`0049`](../../packages/db/migrations/0049_reserve_coverage_snapshots.sql) founder-read item 1: *"a ratio stored beside its own numerator invites recomputation drift, where the stored `rcr_bp` and the stored `bounded_near_term_cents` disagree with each other in the same row. A ratio the database computes cannot disagree with its own inputs."* `0049` bought its way out with a `GENERATED` column. **`S_t` cannot be `GENERATED`: a generated column is a row-local expression and `S_t` is a recurrence over the row before it.** So reading 2 reintroduces exactly the drift `0049` named, with the remedy `0049` used structurally unavailable. **A stored `S_t` that disagrees with a replay of the same series is worse than no stored `S_t`, because it yields two answers and no rule for which is right.**

**Cost B. The parameter columns have no values and no source, and both dispositions of that are wrong.** `mu_0` and `sigma` are `DEP-M6-05`'s and `DEP-M6-05` is Wave 4 (finding 7). Declared `NOT NULL`, the table cannot be written at all and `P7-k` is blocked on a column rather than unblocked by a ruling. Declared nullable, a row can record a statistic **without recording what it ran under**, which is `INV-M7-04`'s failure -- *a run that cannot record the parameters it ran under cannot answer why it did not fire in March* -- reproduced on the control that pauses revenue.

**Cost C. A new table moves the grant surface the wrong way.** [`0026`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql)'s `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES` grants `merit_app` the whole of every future table, so separation gets **further away** when a table is added rather than closer. That is the sentence [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s `164` row starts from, one number up, and it is a cost every candidate table in this wave pays.

**Cost D. A migration is forever, and reading 2 asks this session to fix a parameter set's shape at the moment of maximum ignorance about it.** The counter-argument is good and is recorded rather than dismissed: the recurrence fixes the shape at three numbers -- a reference mean, a slack and a threshold -- whatever the harness emits, so the columns are knowable even when the values are not. **What that argument does not supply is the effective-dating**, and a parameter that can move needs a versioned home with a date range rather than a column on a daily row. Section 6 is where that lands, and it is not `0051`.

### 3. The ruling, and why the plan's own reason is NOT the reason

**P7 section 5.3's stated ground for reading 3 is that recomputation is *"arithmetically available because `S_t` is a recurrence over a series the engine already keeps and which nothing about storing it makes more reproducible."* The first half is true (finding 8). THE SECOND HALF IS TOO WEAK TO CARRY THE RULING AND IT IS CORRECTED HERE RATHER THAN REPEATED.**

Storing `S_t` **does** make the value retrievable. What it does not do is make it **checkable**, and reproducibility is the second property and not the first. A statistic is reproducible when the series and the parameters it ran under are both recorded; caching the output supplies neither. **So the honest form of the plan's sentence is not "storing it adds nothing" but "storing it adds the half that is already derivable and leaves the half that is not."** That is a stronger claim and it is the one this entry rests on.

The ruling rests on three grounds:

**(a) `S_t` is a pure function of a series that IS stored and parameters that are NOT.** Finding 8 supplies the series. Finding 7 supplies the absence of the parameters. Storing the output of that function today stores the derivable half.

**(b) `0049`'s own objection applies here and `0049`'s own remedy does not.** Cost A above. **The migration that got `per_plan` wrong reasoned correctly about exactly this hazard four items earlier in its own header**, and applying its reasoning to the field it mis-dispositioned refuses the table.

**(c) The CUSUM has no inputs today, and the console already renders that absence rather than a number.** Finding 7. `FM-M6-07` reads that an uncalibrated CUSUM is *"either constant alarms or none, which is the same as no chart"*, and `apps/admin` acts on it: `P-M6-06` is **listed as pending rather than omitted**, naming its blocker. **A ruling that landed storage for an uncalibrated statistic would put a column behind a panel that this repository already, correctly, refuses to draw.**

**What is ruled:**

1. **`S_t` is computed at read time**, by folding the pass-rate series in `evaluated_on` order with the floor at zero, and **no column, table or row anywhere holds it.**
2. **`plan_breaker_state`'s primary key stays `(plan_id, evaluated_on)`.** `metric` stays out of it. One plan-day is one row.
3. **The CUSUM never writes `plan_breaker_state.state`.** That column is the loss-ratio breaker's and `'paused'` is a sales pause. `cusum.alarm` and `sales_paused` are separate facts here because finding 5 makes them separate fields on the wire.
4. **The statistic is INTEGER BASIS POINTS and no float and no division enters the recurrence.** Section 4.
5. **`P7-k` renders `per_plan[].cusum` as ABSENT until `DEP-M6-05` lands**, on `apps/admin`'s own existing disposition of `P-M6-06`, and does not manufacture one. **Absence is the answer the console already gives and the wire shape is `P7-b`'s to carry**, not this entry's to invent.
6. **`0051` is not spent.** Section 7.

### 4. THE ARITHMETIC, ARGUED RATHER THAN REACHED FOR

**[P7 rule 17](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) says `numerator_cents` and `denominator_cents` are `bigint`, `ratio_bp` is basis points, and *"the CUSUM statistic is neither."* The first clause is right, the second is WRONG, and getting it right is what removes the float.**

Read the recurrence's operands rather than the table's columns. `S_t = max(0, S_(t-1) + (x_t - mu_0 - 0.5*sigma))`:

- **`x_t` is a PASS RATE** -- passes over resolutions on the plan for the day. Dimensionless, in `[0, 1]`.
- **`mu_0` is the in-control mean of that same rate.** Dimensionless.
- **`sigma` is that rate's standard deviation.** Dimensionless.
- **The alarm threshold is "4 to 5 sigma"**, so it too is in that rate's units.

**Every operand is a rate, so `S_t` is a rate, and a rate has an exact integer representation in basis points.** The statistic is therefore **commensurable with a basis point and NOT commensurable with `ratio_bp`**, which is a LOSS ratio -- a different quantity carried in the same unit. **That distinction is the one `0049` collapsed: it read the unit and not the quantity, and "there is a `_bp` column" became "the CUSUM fits."** It is also the distinction P7 rule 17 misses in the other direction, by inferring from *not `ratio_bp`* to *not basis points*.

So `x_t`, `mu_0`, the slack and the threshold are integers in basis points, and `S_t` is their running sum floored at zero: **integer addition, subtraction and comparison only. No division, no float, nothing that rounds.**

**One term would have introduced a rounding, and it is removed by storing the term the recurrence actually uses.** `0.5*sigma` is **never computed on the evaluation path**. The slack `k` is configured directly as an integer `k_bp`, and `sigma` is halved once by whoever calibrates rather than every day by the evaluator. Halving an odd basis-point value truncates; the truncation is signed the same way every single day; **and it accumulates, because this is a running sum rather than a per-row ratio.** Over a thirty-day window a half-basis-point bias is up to 15bp of drift against a threshold of a few hundred. **A recurrence is exactly the shape where a per-step rounding stops being negligible**, and the fix is not a wider type -- it is not doing the division.

**`integer` is sufficient, and the bound is arithmetic rather than assumed.** The per-day increment is at most `x_t - mu_0 - k`, and `x_t` is at most `10000`, so `S_t` after `n` days is at most `10000n`. `int4`'s `2147483647` is not reached until `n = 214748` days, which is **588 years**, and the `max(0, ...)` floor plus any real `mu_0 > 0` puts every actual series far below that line. **No `bigint`, no `numeric`, no `double precision`, and none of them argued away by "it is probably fine" -- the bound is a multiplication.**

**AND THE NUMERIC-TRANSPORT RULING IS NOT REACHED, WHICH IS STATED DELIBERATELY RATHER THAN PASSED OVER.** [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) finding 8 measured in a container that `SUM` over a `bigint` column returns **`numeric`**, that `pg` hands a `numeric` back as a **string**, and that `Number('9007199254740993')` is `9007199254740992`; it declined to rule numeric transport in passing and said so: *"a money aggregate is a ruling about numeric transport before it is a ruling about a predicate."* **Nothing in this ruling sends a number across that boundary.** The fold is over ROWS, in TypeScript, and the columns it reads are `date`, `uuid` and `integer`; `int4` reaches the driver as a JavaScript number exactly, with no string and no widening in between. **What WOULD reach that ruling is named here so the next session does not walk into it: the moment `x_t` is computed with a SQL `COUNT` or `SUM` rather than by counting rows, the aggregate returns `bigint` or `numeric`, `pg` hands back a string, and THAT is `ADR-157`'s ruling and must be taken there rather than incidentally here.**

**And one thing this entry does NOT claim to have settled for `P7-k`.** Whether the pass-rate series is read as rows or as an aggregate is [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)'s question, that file is [`P5-a`](../plans/P5-payouts-and-wallet.md)'s, and [ADR-157](ADR-157.md) section 5 refused the aggregate on evidence. **That question is IDENTICAL under all three readings** -- every one of them has to compute `x_t` from the same series before it can store, borrow or fold anything -- **so it is not a cost that separates them and it is not resolved by choosing among them.** `P7-k` inherits it exactly as [P7 section 10 item 1](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) leaves it.

### 5. `min_sample` IS ONE SCALAR AND `OQ-M6-02`'s PROPOSAL IS TWO COUNTS, AND ONLY THE SCHEMA HALF IS RULED HERE

**[P7 section 5.4](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md) separates a judgment from a schema question and this entry keeps them separated.** The judgment -- *how much evidence is enough to pause revenue* -- is [`OQ-M6-02`](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) and it is the founder's, per [M06:538](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md). **Nothing below chooses a number.**

The schema question is that `min_sample integer NOT NULL CHECK (min_sample > 0)` is **one scalar**, `plan_breaker_state_respects_min_sample` is **one comparison against one `sample_size`**, and [M06:313](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s proposal is a **conjunction of two counts over two different populations**: *"20 purchases and 3 settled payouts on the plan in the window."* **The row can express one of the two terms.**

**Ruled: `sample_size` is the DENOMINATOR's count, and the source is `0016`'s own header rather than a preference.** [`0016:116`](../../packages/db/migrations/0016_treasury_controls.sql) states the delta's content as *"A loss-ratio breaker with no minimum sample fires on **a two-transaction denominator**"*, and [M06:305](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s `AS-M6-02` says the same in its own words: *"In the first weeks of a plan's life, **the denominator is tiny**."* The loss ratio is settled payouts over fees, so the denominator's count is the purchase count, and **that is the term the constraint has to carry for the constraint to be structural**: a floor on the numerator's count would leave a denominator of one permitted, which is the exact outage `AS-M6-02` exists to prevent.

**So the second term lives in the evaluator or it does not exist, and `P7-k` may not quietly put the other one in the column.** A row whose `sample_size` held the settled-payout count would satisfy every `CHECK` in `0016` and describe the wrong population, and no gate in this repository can see which count an integer is.

### 6. WHAT NEEDS A HOME IS THE PARAMETER SET, IT IS NOT THE STATISTIC, AND IT IS NOT THIS ENTRY'S TO BUILD

**This is the part of section 5.3's question that survives the ruling, and it is stated so it is not rediscovered.**

`S_t` is reproducible from the series **only with the parameters in effect at each step**. If `mu_0` or `k` ever moves mid-series, a replay must replay the change, which means the parameter set needs a **versioned home with an effective date range** -- not a column on a daily row. **That obligation is identical under readings 2 and 3**; reading 2 merely hides it behind a cached output that would then be able to disagree with the replay.

**It is not built here, and the reason is that the thing that produces the values does not exist.** `DEP-M6-05` is Wave 4 (finding 7). **When it lands, the parameter set gets its versioned home and THAT is a migration** -- and it will be a **PARAMETER** table and not a **STATISTIC** table, which is this entry's whole content compressed to one sentence.

**[`detector_definitions`](../../packages/db/migrations/0008_risk.sql) is the shape that already exists for exactly that job** -- `PRIMARY KEY (detector, version)`, `parameters jsonb NOT NULL`, `effective_from date NOT NULL`, `effective_to date NULL`, `is_sensitive boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true` -- **and this entry deliberately does NOT put the breaker in it.** That table is [`P7-d`](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md)'s file and M07's population, its `detector` column is free text so nothing would refuse the row, and **`is_sensitive` is [`P7-j`](../plans/P7-risk-and-abuse.md)'s evidence-pack strip list** -- so a breaker parameter landing there would join a redaction policy by accident, which is the class of change `INV-M7-10` computes rather than hand-lists precisely so that it cannot be joined by accident. **Naming the shape is not taking it.**

### 8. What this FORECLOSES, which is the part every later slice inherits

1. **`plan_breaker_state`'s primary key does not widen.** A later session that wants `metric` in the key is **superseding this entry**, not extending the table, and it inherits section 2's cost C as the thing it must answer.
2. **No CUSUM value is ever written into `ratio_bp`, `threshold_bp`, `numerator_cents`, `denominator_cents` or `sample_size`.** Those columns are the loss-ratio breaker's and their names are load-bearing.
3. **There is no automatic path from a CUSUM alarm to `state = 'paused'`, and there is no path at all.** The CUSUM does not pause sales; only the loss-ratio breaker does. [`INV-M5-12`](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) says the breaker can never pause payouts, and **this is that asymmetry read one rail in: an advisory statistic can never pause sales either.**
4. **No float and no division on the evaluation path.** `sigma` is halved at calibration and never at evaluation, and the configured term is `k_bp`.
5. **`0051` is not spent, no table is created, and no `SqlExecutorReason` or `SystemReason` member is added by this entry or is needed by it.**

**What it does NOT foreclose, stated so the exit is visible rather than argued for again:** a table, once `DEP-M6-05` lands. Section 6 states what it will be for and what it will not be for.
