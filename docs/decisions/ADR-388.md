## ADR-388: a pointer in a dated record is repairable exactly where the record names its subject and the tree once agreed, which is 289 of 8,390; the other 8,101 are not protected, they are undecidable; and `ADR-352:30` was exact on the day it was written (2026-09-06, status: proposed)

**[ADR-384](ADR-384.md) SECTION 14 QUESTION 3 ASKED WHETHER A CITATION INTO A DOCUMENT THAT HAS GROWN BY INSERTION MAY BE REPAIRED AT ALL, AND RECORDED IT AS OPTIONS BECAUSE IT WAS NOT THAT ROW'S TO ANSWER.** It is this row's. The collision is real and both halves are defensible: `RI-14` keeps what was corrected beside its correction, a dated record records what was true on its own day, and `RI-15` and `RI-16` bind a citation to a line number that every later insertion moves. So the corpus accumulates false pointers by design.

**RULED: THE QUESTION HAS BEEN ASKED AT THE WRONG GRAIN. "MAY A DATED POINTER BE REPAIRED" HAS NO ANSWER UNTIL YOU KNOW WHETHER IT WAS TRUE ON THE DAY IT WAS WRITTEN, AND THAT IS RECOVERABLE FROM `git` AND FROM NOWHERE ELSE.** Two gates decide it, both mechanical. **Where both hold, repair is permitted and is not a rewrite of history. Where the record was already wrong, repair is FORBIDDEN, and not out of reverence: moving the number converts a check that did not check into a check that did.** And where the citation names no subject, which is 96.6 percent of the population, nobody can tell the two apart, so nobody can repair one honestly.

- **CONTROL OVER THE CORPUS'S HONESTY. Ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line. Merging is not signing. `E2` read owed.**
- **THE DELIVERABLE IS A RULING, A CORPUS-WIDE CENSUS, ONE REPAIR AND NINE SEEDS.** No invariant number was minted, no gate was added or widened, no register grew, and no file outside this row's fence was written.

### 2. THE RULING

**A CITATION IN A DATED RECORD MAY BE REPAIRED WHEN, AND ONLY WHEN, TWO GATES BOTH HOLD. BOTH ARE PROPERTIES OF THE RECORD AND OF `git`, AND NEITHER IS A JUDGEMENT ABOUT TASTE.**

1. **G1, THE SUBJECT GATE.** The citing text names what the pointer is about, in a token a later reader can search for. **Without it a repair is a guess about somebody else's sentence**, and [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) section 8 is the case: the most careful row in this corpus repaired a pointer by tracking where the old line's TEXT had gone, produced a second wrong pointer, and left an instruction that would have propagated it.
2. **G2, THE HONESTY GATE.** At the commit that last wrote the citing line, the cited line held that subject. **Without it the record is not stale, it is wrong.**

**THE THREE OUTCOMES, AND ONLY THE FIRST IS A REPAIR.**

- **G1 and G2 both hold, and the pointer is wrong today: REPAIR IS PERMITTED.** The record's claim (*"I read this subject at this place"*) is preserved and only the coordinate moves. Nothing the record asserted becomes untrue. **The retired coordinate is NAMED beside the new one and never re-cited**, section 4. **AMENDED BY [ADR-397](ADR-397.md), WHICH KEEPS THIS SENTENCE WHOLE AND ADDS THE PRECONDITION IT NEVER STATED: *only the coordinate moves* PRESUMES A COORDINATE TO MOVE TO.** Where the named subject is no longer anywhere in the cited file there is none, the permission is EMPTY, and the pointer is LEFT with the finding written beside it, as a record that was TRUE about its own day rather than as a check that did not check. **THAT IS NOT A FOURTH OUTCOME AND NO CLASS IS MINTED FOR IT**: the two gates partition this population into the three stated here and admit no fourth cell, and priced over every pointer this corpus can decide, the precondition fails ONLY where G2 has already failed, so not one verdict in section 4 moves.
- **G1 holds and G2 fails: REPAIR IS FORBIDDEN, and the reason is stronger than the rule it comes from.** [ADR-352](ADR-352.md)'s table cell reads **YES**, meaning the claim was checked at that line on that day. If the line never held the subject, then repairing the number turns a record of a check that did not check into a record of a check that did. **That is not preserving history. It is manufacturing evidence, and it is worse than the false pointer it removes.** The pointer stays and the finding is written beside it.
- **G1 fails: UNDECIDABLE, and therefore not repairable by anybody.** The record does not say what the pointer was for, so no reader can tell the second case from the first. **G1 fails on 8,100 of the 8,390 pointers in dated records, and on one more of the 290 that pass it the write commit is not recoverable, so 8,101 are undecidable and 289 are not.**

**SO OPTION (a) IS NOT A CHOICE, IT IS A CONSEQUENCE, FOR 96.6 PERCENT OF THE POPULATION.** The standing rule looks like a decision to protect the record. It is mostly the corpus being unable to derive what a repair would even say.

**AND [ADR-384](ADR-384.md)'s OPTION (b) IS ADOPTED FOR NEW POINTERS AND REFUSED AS A MIGRATION.** A citation into a document names the ROW, SECTION or SYMBOL it is about, beside the pointer, in the token or in front of it. **It is NOT proposed as a check and this row does not reopen [ADR-377](ADR-377.md)**, which measured that the form cannot be made checkable and that even the named form binds unreliably by position. **The name is asked to do a different job here: it is the condition of REPAIRABILITY, and repairability is decided by a person with `git blame`, not by a runner.** A name that binds unreliably is a poor control and a perfectly good record of intent.

**FOUR THINGS THIS RULING DOES NOT DO.** It mints no invariant, because the decision it governs is taken by a person and G2 needs history that `RI-15` says of itself it never reads. It repairs nothing outside this row's fence. It does not touch `RECORD_HEADING`, and section 8 explains why it does not need to. And it does not move a line in [`CRON_INVENTORY.md`](../ops/runbooks/CRON_INVENTORY.md), section 10.

### 4. The three classes, separated, and the number that decides the ruling

**DRIFT ALONE DOES NOT DECIDE ANYTHING. THE QUESTION IS WHETHER THE POINTER WAS EVER TRUE, AND THAT IS ANSWERABLE ONLY WHERE G1 HOLDS.** For every named pointer whose write commit is recoverable, `nearestName` was run at the commit that wrote the citing line and again on this tree, through the same window `RI-15` and `RI-16` use.

| Verdict, over 289 named pointers in dated records | Count |
|---|---|
| True then, true now. Nothing owed | 169 |
| **DRIFTED after being true. G1 and G2 both hold: REPAIRABLE** | **76** |
| **FALSE ON THE DAY IT WAS WRITTEN and still false: NOT REPAIRABLE** | **42** |
| False then, true now, by coincidence | 2 |

**OF THE 118 WRONG NAMED POINTERS IN DATED RECORDS, 42 WERE NEVER RIGHT. THAT IS 36 PERCENT, AND IT IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT.** A rule that says "repair a stale pointer in a dated record" would, applied to this corpus's own measurable population, fabricate a verification more than a third of the time. The same shape holds in live documents, where 9 are drifted and 9 were born wrong.

**AND THE RETIRED COORDINATE HAS A FORM, WHICH IS `RI-14`'s COLLISION ARRIVING AT A CITATION, MEASURED IN BOTH SCOPES.** [ADR-367](ADR-367.md) section 7 measured that a correction which QUOTES its false sentence hands a prose-matching case two answers, and ruled that a correction NAMES its retired figure rather than reproducing the sentence that carried it. **A repaired pointer must survive that, and a coordinate is not a sentence, so the rule has to be re-derived rather than inherited.** It was, at the reader:

| Where the correction is written | What the checkers' reader does with `` `:822` `` beside the new pointer |
|---|---|
| A document | **Nothing.** `RI-16` calls `citationsIn` with inheritance OFF, so a bare coordinate with no path and no link of its own is not a citation, in either order, on the same line or a later one |
| A source file, within 6 lines of the pointer above it | **It becomes a live citation into that file**, because `RI-15` inherits a path across `CITATION_INHERIT_LINES`, and the check then range-checks a number the correction itself says is retired |
| A source file, 7 lines or more away | Nothing |

**SO THE RULE IS: IN A DOCUMENT, KEEP THE RETIRED COORDINATE IN BACKTICKS BESIDE THE NEW ONE. IN A SOURCE COMMENT, SPELL IT IN WORDS OR PUT IT OUTSIDE THE WINDOW.** Seeds D, E and F watched all three. **A correction that re-cites the retired coordinate WITH ITS PATH is refused in both scopes**, because the checks cannot tell a retired pointer from a live one and would read the correction as two claims.

### 7. Why this is not the form `ADR-377` refused, stated before it can be read as a re-litigation

**[ADR-377](ADR-377.md) RULED THAT A CITATION CARRYING ONLY A PATH AND A LINE CANNOT BE MADE CHECKABLE ON THIS CORPUS, AND THIS ROW DOES NOT DISTURB ONE WORD OF IT.** Its three measurements were re-read rather than re-run: a form change is 8,250 sites and cannot be enforced on new citations only; outcome (b)'s second datum does not exist, because the quotes this corpus writes beside its pointers are quotations of prose and exactly one of 1,558 lands near the line cited; and widening `IDENTIFIER_GLUE` reaches none of the population it would be widened for and turns 33 honest citations red.

**THE DIFFERENCE IS THE JOB THE NAME IS ASKED TO DO, AND IT IS A DIFFERENT JOB IN A PRECISE SENSE.** A check must decide, mechanically and now, whether a citation is true. A repair must decide what a citation was FOR, and it is made by a person who can open `git`. [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) section 6 measured that the named form binds or fails to bind depending on where in a file it sits, and concluded that a migration to the named form buys less than it appears to. **That measurement is about BINDING. It says nothing about a human reader, and this row reproduced the binding instability rather than taking it on trust:**

**SEED G IS THE SAME WRONG POINTER THREE TIMES, IN ONE LIVE DOCUMENT, AT ONE WRONG LINE.** The token `mintCertificateCode:10`, written inside a markdown link naming that file, turns `RI-16` **RED** and the finding names the 136-line gap. The token `certificate-code.ts:10` in the same link, which is the same defect with the name removed, is **GREEN**. And the name written in FRONT of that token rather than inside it is **GREEN** too, which is [ADR-357](ADR-357.md) section 10's `IDENTIFIER_GLUE` hole reproduced live rather than cited.

**ALL THREE ARE EQUALLY REPAIRABLE BY A PERSON AND ONLY ONE IS CHECKABLE.** That is the whole of the distinction, and it is why this ruling adds a convention and not a gate.

### 8. The seam between "dated" and "old", and why the ruling deliberately does not stand on it

**THE DISPATCH ASKED WHETHER A DISTINCTION BETWEEN RECORDS THAT DATE A MEASUREMENT AND RECORDS THAT MERELY HAPPEN TO BE OLD IS AVAILABLE. IT IS, THE CORPUS ALREADY HAS TWO OF THEM, AND THEY DISAGREE ABOUT 248 POINTERS.**

`RECORD_HEADING` fires on a level 1 or level 2 heading naming a date or a session, and a deeper heading inherits it. **A modern entry writes its title as `##` and its sections as `###`, so the date survives the whole file. Five older ADRs and ten reviews write their sections as `##` too, and the first section heading cancels the date the title carried.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Files under `docs/` whose own first heading or filename dates them, and whose citations the shape rule nonetheless calls LIVE | **15** |
| Citations inside them that `RI-16` therefore reads as live | **248** (66 in five ADRs, 182 in ten reviews) |
| The largest single one | [`2026-08-20-plan-config-audit.md`](../reviews/2026-08-20-plan-config-audit.md), 80 of its 81 |

**THE RULING IN SECTION 2 DOES NOT USE THIS AXIS AT ALL, AND THAT IS THE POINT RATHER THAN AN OVERSIGHT.** G1 is a property of the citing sentence and G2 is a property of `git`. Neither asks what heading the pointer sits under. **So the 248 stop mattering for repairability**, and what is left is a scoping question about a CHECK, which belongs to whoever owns `RECORD_HEADING` and is section 15 item 3.

**A RULING THAT HAD STOOD ON THE SHAPE RULE WOULD HAVE INHERITED ITS SEAM**, and a repairability rule that changes its answer when somebody re-levels a heading is a rule that decides nothing.

### 10. The scheduled table's long cells: ruled, refused here, and specified

### 17. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **G1 IS MEASURED WITH THE CHECKERS' BINDER AND G1 IS NOT THE BINDER.** The gate asks whether the record names its subject; the measurement asks whether `citedIdentifier` or `citedInToken` binds one. **Seed G3 is this entry's own proof that the two differ**: a name written in front of the token rather than inside it is a name a person reads and a name the binder misses. **So 289 is a FLOOR on the decidable set, the true figure is higher, and it cannot be derived without reading 8,390 sentences.** The ruling's shape does not move; the population it reaches does.
- **"DRIFTED" MEANS THE CITED LINE'S TEXT CHANGED, WHICH IS NOT THE SAME AS "THE POINTER IS WRONG".** A variable renamed on the line the pointer names counts as drift while the pointer stays right, and a line rewritten into something unrelated counts exactly once, the same as a line deleted. **The 42.1 percent is a proxy and the direction of its error is not known.** Section 4's three-way split is the honest number and it exists only for the 4.7 percent that carry a name.
- **`git blame -w` NAMES THE COMMIT THAT LAST TOUCHED THE CITING LINE, NOT THE ONE THAT FIRST WROTE THE POINTER.** Where a later commit reflowed a sentence around an unchanged pointer, G2 is tested against a tree later than the one its author saw, and a born-wrong pointer then looks intact. **The 42 is a floor for the same reason the 289 is.** It is not a floor for [ADR-352](ADR-352.md) line 30, where `git log` names exactly one commit over that path and there is nothing to interpret.
- **SECTION 9's BASE RATE SAMPLES THE LAST COMMIT OF EACH DAY.** A ragged row introduced and repaired inside one day is invisible to it, so **13 occurrences is a floor** and the warrant for the leg is if anything understated.
- **SECTION 10 RESTS ON A CHARACTER COUNT PLUS A READING OF WHAT A RUNBOOK IS FOR.** The 91.2 percent is derived; the claim that an operator scans columns 1 to 4 and reads column 5 only for the row that fired is a judgement about a person under pressure, and nobody has watched one. **A founder who holds that the severity column is the first thing an operator needs would reverse the ruling and keep the table as it stands.**
