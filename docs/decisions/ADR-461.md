### 2. How the population was derived, and why the dispatch's five are not it

The dispatch's definition has three arms: an entry that says a repair **costs some specific thing**, that **names a forcing constraint**, or that says a shape is **required "because"** of a property of the tree. Two derivations were run, and both are reported because they answer different questions.

**DERIVATION A, THE LABELLED PRICES. This is the census.** The corpus has an idiom it invented for exactly this purpose: a bolded `Price` opening a clause, inside a numbered item of a section naming work the entry did not take. Enumerated over all **444** ADR files:

```
grep -ohE '\*\*?Price[^*]{0,40}?:' docs/decisions/ADR-*.md
```

**39 read `Price:`, one reads `Price of the step:` and one `Price of the widening:`, for 41 clauses over 7 entries.** The idiom appears nowhere before [ADR-445](ADR-445.md) and in every entry of this wave that names owed work, which is why it is a population and not a keyword: it was minted by this wave, it is used consistently inside it, and it marks precisely the claim the dispatch describes.
