## ADR-078: `replay` joins the rules engine's public surface, and the site count is not what decides it  (2026-08-22, status: accepted)

**[M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) contradicts itself about whether `replay` is exported, in the same shape it contradicts itself about `clampPayout`, and [`index.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/index.ts) already ruled that one the other way.** This entry rules this one, and **the two rulings are opposite because the reason points opposite ways**, not because the contradictions are shaped differently. They are shaped identically.

- **Context:** three artifacts wait behind the answer and all three were measurable before this entry was written. **`PT-06`'s arrival-order permutation** was skipped by derivation off the engine's own surface, with a body that threw the day the export landed. **`GS-071`** is section 39's single `writable` row. And **M01 section 3.7's *"there is no second code path"*** was already false: [`apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts)`:253` folds its own loop with its own break at `:262`.

  **The brief that commissioned this session, and the [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row it was written from, both stated the balance wrongly**, and the correction is section 1. It is recorded rather than absorbed because **applied as stated it would have refused the export**.

- **Decision:** `replay` is exported. `ReplayAssertionError` is exported with it. `evaluate`, the scaffold's identity stub, is deleted. **The public surface goes from thirteen names to fourteen, gaining exactly one function.**

---

## 1. The site count cannot decide this, and the count that was offered was wrong

The brief and the [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row both read: *"two of the three sites say export and one does not, which is the opposite balance."* **Section 1.3 does not say that.**

Its layout lists `replay.ts`. **It also lists [`payout/clamp.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout)**, and `day/breach.ts`, `day/floor.ts`, `day/counters.ts`, `payout/gates.ts` and `hash.ts`, **none of whose functions are exported**. A file in a layout says the file exists. It is not a vote for export, or `hash.ts` would be one too.

| | layout lists a file | a section-3 `export function` | 1.3's prose lists it among the six |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clampPayout` | yes, `payout/clamp.ts` | yes, section 3.6 | no |
| `replay` | yes, `replay.ts` | yes, section 3.7 | no |

**The balance is identical. Applied mechanically, the `clampPayout` precedent refuses this export.**

## 2. The rationale decides it, and that is why the answers differ

Section 1.3 gives its own reason for the limit: *"every additional export is a way for a caller to reimplement a rule slightly differently."* **Ask what withholding buys, per function.**

**For `clampPayout`, withholding SERVES the rationale at no cost.** `evaluatePayout` still reaches the clamp, and M01 section 4 names that one function for both the eligibility read and the payout write, which is what makes *"the identical function with the identical inputs"* true rather than aspirational. Nothing a caller needs becomes unreachable, so nothing gets reimplemented. [`index.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/index.ts) says exactly this and it is correct.

**For `replay`, withholding DEFEATS the rationale.** No exported function reaches the fold over a whole account life. `advanceDay` folds one day. So a caller that needs a life writes the loop, the sort, the break and the assertion handling itself, **and that is not hypothetical**: `apps/worker` wrote one. Withholding `replay` does not prevent a second implementation of the rule. **It compels one.**

> **The limit exists to stop reimplementation. Where withholding causes reimplementation, the limit is being applied against its own purpose.**

## 3. Four places where section 3.7's pseudocode does not typecheck, and what each cost

3.7 is the specification and it is transcribed rather than reinterpreted: total order, `advanceDay` per mark, throw on any assertion, break at `closed` or `graduated`. **Four of its lines do not compile against the types that exist today.** Each is reconciled in [`replay.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/replay.ts) with its reason beside it, so a later reader does not conclude the pseudocode was transcribed loosely.

**1. `openedOn` is a WIDENING of 3.7's signature, and it is stated as one.** [`types.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)`:780` carries `openedOn` on `DayInput` as non-optional, and [ADR-051](ADR-051.md) rules it *"REQUIRED, NEVER OPTIONAL"*, on the ground that an optional anchor makes `R-32` silently not fire. 3.7 predates that and passes no such field. **An account's first tradeable day is an account fact and is not derivable from its marks**: the earliest mark is the first day it TRADED. Defaulting to it would anchor `R-32` on the wrong day and expire nobody, which is the exact failure ADR-051 rejected. So the signature takes a sixth parameter.

**2. `calendar.get(mark.tradingDay)` is impossible, not merely awkward.** [ADR-049](ADR-049.md) made the slice plain data and [`types.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)`:277`'s `CalendarSliceIsData` asserts **at compile time** that no property of it is function-valued. The whole slice is passed and `advanceDay` resolves the day, which it must: `R-37` counts a cadence gap by sequence subtraction from an anchor that may sit months outside any single day.

**3. `byTradingDayThenId` names a field `DailyMark` does not have.** There is no `id` on a mark. The tiebreaker is **`sourceHash`**, the only deterministic identity the row carries, and the pair is a total order needing no stable sort, which is what 3.7's own comment on the line asks for. Two marks sharing a day and a hash are byte-identical, so their order cannot move the fold.

**4. `ReplayAssertionError` did not exist, and it is deliberately NOT an `EngineInvariantError`.** [`errors.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/errors.ts) draws one line and it is load bearing: the engine **throws** when its own arithmetic broke an invariant, and **refuses** through the assertion channel when the input contradicts something it was entitled to assume, *"because the day's data being wrong is not the batch's crash"*. A refused day is the second kind, so filing it under `EngineInvariantError` would put vendor data into the class that means a future edit broke a rule.

**Why replay throws at all, when `advanceDay` deliberately does not.** Replay cannot represent a refusal. No state is written for a refused day, so there is no `prior` for the next mark, and **a `RuleState[]` that silently skipped a day is indistinguishable from a complete history**. The batch decides what a refusal costs; replay's contract is that the array is contiguous or there is no array.

## 4. What running it found that reading it did not

**`replay` refused the demo's own corpus, correctly.** `PT-06`'s permutation property was first written over 30 sessions of the demo population and stopped at `2026-12-08` with `opening_mismatch`, `INV-18`, *"opening balance is not the prior balance plus the adjustment"*.

**The demo folds in SEGMENTS and `replay` does not.** [`scripts/demo/fold.ts`](../../scripts/demo/fold.ts) restarts the simulator when an account passes its evaluation, because the platform account is re-provisioned at `size_cents`. That is a second life. A re-provisioned opening balance is not the prior closing plus an adjustment, and refusing it is the correct behaviour of a fold whose subject is **one** account life.

So `PT-06`'s corpus is the contiguous prefix, **taken by asking `replay` where it stops** rather than by hard-coding a day the simulator could move. This is recorded because it is the first live demonstration that the two folds have different subjects, and a later session may reasonably ask whether `foldAccount` should call `replay` per segment. **This entry does not rule that**: `scripts/demo/fold.ts` is a demo and its segmentation is not the money path.

- **`evaluate` is deleted.** It was the scaffold's identity stub, was never among 1.3's six, and nothing folds it: `engineIsIdentityStub` in [`golden-loader/src/run.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/run.ts)`:244` folds `advanceDay`, as does `runFixture`. The two `eslint-plugin-merit` hits are `RuleTester` code strings and are never resolved.
- **`PT-06`'s arrival-order permutation is LIVE and passing**, with an anti-vacuity sibling asserting some generated permutation actually reordered something. A shuffle that silently stopped shuffling would otherwise make the invariance property pass for the wrong reason.

- **`apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts` is NOT migrated onto the export.** Sessions 138 and 140 hold that file. **The second code path still exists on `main` after this entry**, and closing it is what makes 3.7's sentence true. Stated as owed rather than left implied.
- **[P2 build plan](../plans/P2-rules-engine-build.md)`:296` carries the miscount corrected in section 1** and is outside this fence. Named as owed.
- **No `SD-nn`, no migration number, no CI gate letter.** Nothing here is schema and nothing here is a check.
- **`adr_count` moves by one.** [`gates.mjs generate`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) is re-run and its output committed, per [ADR-034](ADR-034.md).

  **It names the list rather than the count on purpose.** The brief's candidate clause was *"the engine's public surface gains exactly one name"*, and that was false arithmetic: deleting `evaluate` while adding `replay` is net zero, and adding `ReplayAssertionError` makes it plus two minus one. **A clause that names the list cannot drift the way a count can**, and the list is machine-checked by a test that already fails when an export is added without it.

  **What it costs if it is wrong.** A seventh, eighth or ninth name on the surface widens exactly what section 1.3 narrowed on purpose, and every widening is a route by which a caller reimplements a rule slightly differently. **The specific risk this entry accepts is `ReplayAssertionError`**: it is a class rather than a function, so it computes nothing and has no second implementation to drift from, which is the same reasoning `index.ts` already applied to the `EngineEvent` union. **If that reasoning is rejected, the remedy is one line** -- throw `EngineInvariantError` instead and drop the name -- **at the cost of collapsing the distinction `errors.ts` spends its header establishing**, which section 3 argues is worth more than the name.
