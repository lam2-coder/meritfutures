### 2. The universal rule, quoted, and what it requires

[`STATE_MACHINES:11-14`](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) opens *"Universal rules for every machine below"* and rule 1 is the one this row is about:

> **1. A transition happens in one database transaction with its event write. No event without the fact, no fact without the event.**

**THREE THINGS IT REQUIRES, EACH SEPARATELY UNMET TODAY.**

1. **An event write exists for every transition.** Nothing writes one, so this fails everywhere rather than in places.
2. **It is in the SAME TRANSACTION.** `EventSink.emit` takes the open transaction as its first argument and has no other way to reach a database ([`events.ts:927`](../../apps/api/src/events.ts)); there is no `flush`, no queue and no buffer. **That design is correct and it is what makes the gap total rather than partial**: a producer that could hold an event past the commit would at least be installable somewhere, and this one can only be installed inside the transaction that writes the fact.
3. **The biconditional runs both ways.** *"No event without the fact"* is enforced by construction. *"No fact without the event"* is enforced by nothing, and that is the half this estate is failing.

**THE FAILURE MODE IS NOT A MISSING FEED. IT IS A RECORD THAT CANNOT BE RECONSTRUCTED.** [`EVENTS:9`](../architecture/EVENTS.md) rows the same table as the source of the admin feed, analytics, lifecycle messaging, **audit** and the evidence pack. `events` is append-only by grant ([`0026:88-107`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON ... events ... FROM merit_app, PUBLIC`), so a transition that commits without its row cannot be given one afterwards by any means the application holds. **Every day this stands is a day of history that no later session can write.**

### 3. THE PLACEMENT QUESTION, WHICH IS THE ROW'S ACTUAL SUBJECT

The dispatch asked whether the writer can live anywhere, given [`RI-04`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs)'s rule that no deployable depends on a deployable, and `.npmrc`'s `node-linker=isolated`. **The answer is that the two halves of a working sink are one deployable apart, that neither deployable can be given the other half, and that the third address which would work is a relocation rather than a wiring.**

**HALF ONE: THE HANDLE. `apps/api` HAS NONE AND `apps/worker` HAS THE ONLY ONE.** `events` is scope class `either` ([ADR-191](ADR-191.md), [`scope.ts:1578`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)), and the three transaction handles `packages/db` mints declare their `insert` over three different key sets, read out of the accessor rather than restated: `ScopedTx.insert<K extends OwnedTableKey>` ([`scoped-db.ts:3607`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)), `SystemTx.insert<K extends TableKey>` ([`:3773`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)), `FirmTx.insert<K extends FirmTableKey>` ([`:3808`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)). **Only `SystemTx` reaches this table**, and `TRANSACTION_EVENT_WRITER` refuses every other handle **by brand** rather than by shape, because all three carry `insert(key, values)` at run time.

[ADR-165](ADR-165.md) section 6 is a closed list and it decides the rest: `@merit/api` gets *"Two doors, `scoped` and `firm`, and a `system` door refused"*; `@merit/worker` gets *"ONE door, `batch`"*, which is `transaction(systemDb('nightly-batch'), fn)` at [`apps/worker/src/db.ts:141`](../../apps/worker/src/db.ts). **So the deployable holding the producer holds no handle that can carry the write, and the deployable holding the handle holds no producer.**

**AND THAT SENTENCE IN THE PRODUCER'S OWN HEADER RESTED ON A COUNT THAT HAD DRIFTED BY THREE, WHICH IS THIS ROW'S SECOND FINDING.** It read *"`apps/api`'s own two doors are `scoped` and `firm`"*. Two was ADR-165's figure on 2026-08-27; `src/db.ts` declares **five** doors today and [`db.test.ts:172`](../../apps/api/test/db.test.ts) has been named for five since [ADR-231](ADR-231.md). The three that arrived after ADR-165 are `resolution` ([ADR-126](ADR-126.md)), `establishment` ([ADR-196](ADR-196.md)) and `publicLookup` ([ADR-231](ADR-231.md)). **NOT ONE OF THEM MOVES THE CONCLUSION, WHICH IS EXACTLY WHY IT WENT UNCAUGHT**: `ResolutionDb` and `PublicLookupDb` are not transactional at all and `EstablishmentTx` carries one verb and no `insert`. The sentence is repaired in place beside its original under `RI-14`, and section 6 is the case that makes the wider claim mechanical.

**HALF TWO: THE PRODUCER. `apps/worker` CANNOT REACH IT IN EITHER SPELLING.** A `@merit/api` line in `apps/worker/package.json` is refused by `RI-04`, whose `DEPLOYABLES` literal holds `['site', 'portal', 'admin', 'api', 'worker']` and whose second loop demands no dependency field naming another app. A relative `../../api/src/events.ts` is refused by nothing in the module system and by [`apps/worker/test/event-sink.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/event-sink.test.ts) section 3, which session 379 wrote for exactly that reason. `node-linker=isolated` is why the manifest line is load-bearing rather than decorative: `.npmrc` says of it that *"under a hoisted layout an undeclared import resolves anyway"*.

**HALF THREE: THE ADDRESS THAT WOULD WORK, AND WHY THIS ROW DOES NOT TAKE IT.** A `packages/*` library reaches both deployables and violates nothing. **That is not a novel idea, it is a decided precedent**: [`OVERVIEW:84`](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md) rows `packages/ledger` as *"A LIBRARY BECAUSE TWO DEPLOYABLES POST AND `RI-04` FORBIDS AN APP DEPENDING ON AN APP"* ([ADR-104](ADR-104.md)), and that package *"declares no dependency and takes the caller's OPEN transaction as its first argument"*, which is `events.ts`'s disposition already. **The producer is shaped for the move and the move is not a wiring.** Measured before it was refused: `grep -rn 'apps/api/src/events.ts'` reaches **88 lines across 31 files**, of which **15 are session logs and 9 are ADRs**. Session logs are a dated record and a relocation would falsify them wholesale; this session holds citation-repair rights in `apps/**` and holds none in `docs/**`. **So it is founder-owed, it is section 8's first named decision, and forcing it here would have been a 31-file structural change taken by a row dispatched to wire a sink.**

### 5. C-09: the write path can never update and never delete, checked rather than assumed

[`SECURITY:27`](../architecture/SECURITY.md) rows `C-09` as *"Append-only tables enforced by database grants (no UPDATE, no DELETE)"* with `events` named first among its examples, and [`0026:88-107`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql) is the grant: `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON ledger_entries, ledger_transactions, events, ... FROM merit_app, PUBLIC`, *"against `merit_app` AND against PUBLIC, because a grant that only binds the application role is a grant that a second connection string bypasses"*.

**THREE THINGS WERE CHECKED ABOUT WHAT THIS ROW WRITES, AND ALL THREE HOLD BECAUSE OF SHAPE RATHER THAN BECAUSE OF DISCIPLINE.**

1. **`EventInsertTx` declares ONE METHOD.** [`events.ts:1053`](../../apps/api/src/events.ts) is `insert(key, values): Promise<unknown[]>` and nothing else, so the writer holds no `updateAt` and no `deleteAt` to call **even if a handle carrying them is passed in**. The narrowing is structural and a wider handle does not widen it.
2. **`TRANSACTION_EVENT_WRITER` calls `tx.insert` and nothing else.** One statement, one table, `EVENT_WRITE_TABLE` spelled as a value the suite binds to a real `TableKey`.
3. **A FAILED EMISSION CANNOT SILENTLY DROP A TRANSITION**, which is the harder half and is the one this row went looking for. Three refusals are in the path and every one of them **throws**: `buildEvent` throws `EventError` on an unknown name, on a bad subject, on a payload rule and on an untenanted row; `assertEventInsertTx` throws on the wrong brand; and a write that returns other than exactly one row throws with the message *"the fact is about to commit without the event that records it"*. **There is no `catch`, no log-and-continue and no boolean return anywhere in the path.** Because `emit` runs inside the caller's transaction ([ADR-006](ADR-006.md)), every one of those throws rolls the state change back with the event, which is the correct direction: rule 1 refuses a fact without an event, and a refusal that aborts is the only way to honour it. **`UNWIRED_EVENT_SINK` is the same argument at the top of the file**: it rejects rather than resolving, so a deployment missing its writer fails loudly on its first emit instead of committing a transition nothing recorded.

**ONE THING IS NOT PROVED HERE AND IS NAMED RATHER THAN IMPLIED.** `apps/worker/src/sweeps/expiry.ts` catches per-item failures and reports them as `failed` outcomes rather than dying, which session 379 watched happen to `payout.freeze_expiring` against a live database. **That is `FM-M5-13`'s design and it is correct at the SWEEP level**, because the transaction for that item is rolled back and the item is reported; it is recorded here because a reader checking "can an emission fail quietly" will meet it, and the answer is that the item's own transaction aborts and the sweep's report is the record.
