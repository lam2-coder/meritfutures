### 10. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **THE PAYOUT DERIVATION RESTS ON A HABIT RATHER THAN ON A RULE.** It works because `payout-backend.ts` spells the member name into every refusal, *"so a 503 names the member a reader can go and look at"*. **Nothing requires that habit to continue.** The capture guard catches a refusal spelled some other way and turns it into a RED bar, which is the right direction, but it turns red with no defect present, and whoever writes the next refusal pays for it.
- **THE DOTTED-NAME RULE IS A JUDGEMENT WEARING A DERIVATION'S CLOTHES.** `idempotency.find` is read as a store member and `insertPayoutRequest.hold` as a method branch, and the thing that tells them apart is whether the head is declared as a `readonly` property of an interface. **A future member declared as a property whose type happens to have a member named like a branch would be classified wrongly**, and nothing here would notice.
- **SECTION 6 RULES ON AN INFERENCE AND NOT ON A BEHAVIOUR, AND THE BEHAVIOUR IT LEANS ON IS ANOTHER ROW'S.** The 200 is [ADR-362](ADR-362.md) section 4's measurement against the suite's `CheckoutBackend` fake, re-read here and not re-run. **If that measurement is wrong, section 6 is wrong**, and the thing to attack is that entry's case rather than this one's pin.
- **THE `EXACTLY ONCE` RULE IS BLUNT.** It cannot tell a retired quotation from a legitimate second use of the same sentence shape, so an entry that genuinely needs to say the phrase twice cannot. **That is the intended direction of the error** and it is still a cost, and the cost lands on prose rather than on code.
- **THIS ROW READ FOUR ADRs AND ONE FILE, AND THE OWED ITEMS IT ENUMERATED CAME FROM THOSE FOUR.** An item owed by a row outside the wave, or recorded only in a session log, would not have been found.
