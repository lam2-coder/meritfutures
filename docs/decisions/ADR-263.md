## ADR-263: the edge had a guard, a clock and a database behind it and no door, and what moves is one paragraph of a frozen contract rather than a route this session invented  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-234](ADR-234.md) built `requested --> cancelled` and `cooling --> cancelled` under `G-TRADER-CANCELS`, guarded, with [`0072`](../../packages/db/migrations/0072_terminal_withdrawal_transitions.sql) binding the arrow set at the database, and left it called by nothing.** `cancelled` is one of the three statuses that release an identity and it is the only one a tree with no rail can reach, so it was the whole of the exit. **Every status a created withdrawal can hold is inside `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx`'s predicate**, which means a wired backend gave a trader a withdrawal they could open and no way to close, and `gateNoInFlight` then refused that identity's every later withdrawal, permanently, behind a screen saying one was in flight.

**THE DOOR NOW EXISTS AND IT IS A TRANSCRIPTION RATHER THAN AN INVENTION.** [API_CONTRACT section 6.2](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) stated in terms that *"There is no endpoint that cancels a withdrawal"* and named `G-TRADER-CANCELS` *"as owed rather than invented"*. That paragraph is what this entry moves: it is replaced by the endpoint row it was holding a place for, quoted in full inside its replacement, and **nothing else in that document changes**.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `263`, session **454**, dispatched as `claude/a-withdrawal-a-trader-cannot-close`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** The highest on disk is `0074` and this ruling needs none: `0072` already added `cancelled_at` and already binds the arrow set, and section 3 is the one place the database is short, where the repair is a constraint on a merged money-path table and is larger than the door.

- **MONEY PATH: this is a trader's own money and a lockout they cannot escape. `E2` READ IS OWED AND NOTHING HERE DISCHARGES IT.** This entry ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** It also inherits the `E2` debt on [ADR-232](ADR-232.md) and [ADR-234](ADR-234.md), both `proposed` and both UNSIGNED, because the door it opens is onto their edge.

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That the six database cases in section 3 refuse and permit what they are said to is **MEASURED**, against PostgreSQL 16.13 with all 68 migrations applied forward-only from empty under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1`. That no route in this deployable reached a terminal status before this one, and that `grep -rn driveApprovals apps/api/src` still finds no caller, is **DERIVED** by search at the scope each claim names, and both are assertions rather than sentences. What is **JUDGED** is the required factor (section 4), the absence of an idempotency key (section 4), that one refusal document answers three situations (section 5), and that the port stays blocked (section 6).

### 2. The amendment: the exact line that moves, and why it is not an invention

**ONE PARAGRAPH OF ONE `approved` DOCUMENT IS REPLACED**, at what was [API_CONTRACT:732](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md), inside section 6.2 and immediately below the `POST /wallet/withdrawals` row it belongs to. It read:

**IT IS QUOTED INSIDE ITS REPLACEMENT RATHER THAN DELETED**, which is this estate's own rule about a sentence that stops being true: a false sentence removed leaves nothing for the next reader to check, and the paragraph above is the reason the route did not exist for two entries.

**WHAT THE PARAGRAPH ACTUALLY WITHHELD WAS A ROUTE, A BODY AND AN ERROR SET, AND ALL THREE ARE NOW STATED**: `POST /wallet/withdrawals/:withdrawalId/cancel`, no request body, a three-field response, and `conflict`, `forbidden`, `unauthenticated`. **The document is the specification and the module transcribes it**, which is the order this corpus keeps: [ADR-232](ADR-232.md) section 9 and [ADR-234](ADR-234.md) section 7 each refused to mint this row, and each refused it because the document was outside the session's fence rather than because the endpoint was wrong. **Row `263` puts that one row inside a fence and rules the amendment here**, which is the difference between an ADR and a commit.

**WHY THE PATH IS SHAPED THIS WAY AND NOT ANOTHER.** `POST /phone/change/:id/cancel` and `POST /sessions/:id/revoke` are the two trader-surface rows that already end a resource's life through a named sub-action, and both take `POST` on a segment under the resource. **`DELETE /wallet/withdrawals/:withdrawalId` was refused**: a cancellation is a transition into a terminal status that the row keeps forever, with a clock recorded on it, and a verb whose ordinary meaning is that the resource stops existing is the wrong word for a record that must survive. **`DELETE` IS AVAILABLE AND WAS STILL REFUSED**: [`registry.ts`](../../apps/api/src/registry.ts)'s `HTTP_METHODS` carries it, so this is a choice about meaning rather than about what the registry admits. **The segment is spelled `:withdrawalId` and the document writes both spellings**: its headings use `:accountId`, `:identityId` and `:flagId` in some places and a bare `:id` in `POST /phone/change/:id/cancel` and `POST /sessions/:id/revoke`. The qualified form is taken because this path already carries two nouns and a bare `:id` under `/wallet/withdrawals/` reads as a wallet's, and because the reader's own header names section 1 spelling `:id` where the heading spells `:accountId` as one of the mismatches that made prose unparseable.

### 3. THE FINDING: the status is immutable at the database and the terminal CLOCK is not

**`0072`'s two triggers are the reason a cancel handler does not have to be trusted, and there is exactly one write they do not see.** `wallet_withdrawals_terminal_transition_is_drawn` fires `WHEN (OLD.status IS DISTINCT FROM NEW.status AND ...)`, so **an UPDATE writing `cancelled` over `cancelled` never reaches it**, and the pair of CHECKs `0072` added is satisfied by the row such an UPDATE produces: `wallet_withdrawals_cancelled_has_timestamp` wants a non-null clock and gets one, and `wallet_withdrawals_uncancelled_records_no_cancellation` wants the status to be `cancelled` and it is.

**EXECUTED, PostgreSQL 16.13, all 68 migrations applied forward-only from empty under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1`, 118 base tables:**

| Case | What was written | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The edge this door drives** | `requested --> cancelled`, `cancelled_at` at `10:00:00Z` | **PERMITTED** |
| **B. THE FINDING** | the same row cancelled AGAIN, `cancelled_at` at `23:59:59Z` | **PERMITTED, and the clock MOVED** |
| **C. The clock alone** | `cancelled_at` set to `2020-01-01`, status untouched | **PERMITTED, and the clock moved BACKWARDS by six years** |
| **D. The race this door must not win** | `approved --> cancelled` | **REFUSED**, `WD-C2`, naming `G-TRADER-CANCELS` |
| **E. Past the rail** | `transferring --> cancelled` | **REFUSED**, `WD-C2` |
| **F. A release revoked** | `cancelled --> requested` | **REFUSED**, `WD-C2` assertion 1 |

**CASES D, E AND F ARE WHY THIS HANDLER IS NOT THE ONLY CONTROL ON ITS OWN CORRECTNESS**, which is what the row asked be established before the handler was written. A cancellation that raced an approval past `lockScope()` is refused by the trigger and not merely by `decideCancellation`, and the arrow set the door may walk is the drawn one whatever the application believes.

**CASES B AND C ARE THE HALF NOTHING BACKS UP.** `wallet_withdrawals` carries **15 CHECK constraints and 4 constraint triggers**, read from `pg_constraint` and `pg_trigger` on the installed database rather than from a grep, and **not one of them binds `cancelled_at` against being rewritten on a row that is already `cancelled`**. So the `not_cancellable` hold in `decideCancellation` is the entire control on a retried cancellation, and a door that answered a retry by writing again would move the record of when a trader took their own money back, with every gate in this repository green.

> **RULED: the retry is refused in the application, the gap is reported rather than closed, and the door is written so that the gap cannot be reached from outside.** The handler holds every status but `requested` and `cooling`, so no HTTP request can produce the write case `B` describes. **The repair that would make it unrepresentable is a CHECK or a third trigger arm on a merged money-path table**, which is a migration whose subject is `0072`'s own, and this row reserves no number for it: writing it here would put two files in charge of one transition, which is the reason [ADR-234](ADR-234.md) section 7 gave for leaving `0070`'s neighbourhood alone.

**AND A LIVE SENTENCE IN THE SUITE SAID THE OPPOSITE, WHICH IS HOW THIS WAS FOUND.** [`wallet-withdrawals.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wallet-withdrawals.test.ts)'s `holds every status that is not one of the two tails` carried the comment *"`0072` refuses leaving a terminal status, so a driver that re-cancelled a cancelled row would be refused at the statement rather than here"*. **It is false, and it is false about the exact write a door makes possible.** `0072` refuses `cancelled --> requested` (case `F`) and says nothing about `cancelled --> cancelled`, and the two are one word apart in a comment nothing executes. **It is repaired at its own line under this row's citation-repair rights**, with the measurement rather than with a softer sentence.

### 4. The factor, and the key that is not required

**THE ROW DECLARES `passkey or dual_channel` AND CARRIES NO `C-27:` TAG, AND THE TWO HALVES ARE DECIDED SEPARATELY.**

**THE TAG IS WITHHELD BECAUSE C-27's ACTION LIST IS CLOSED AT THREE.** [`auth.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts)'s `C27Action` says it in terms: *"a fourth sensitive action is a ruling and not a value"*, and section 12's matrix names payout destination change, contact change and external withdrawal. **A cancellation is money staying**, so tagging this row `external withdrawal` would record that this door performs the act C-27 guards, which is false, and `CI-06k` reads section 12's matrix rather than the field either way. **A fourth action is available to a founder or a later ADR and is not taken here.**

**THE FACTOR IS TAKEN ANYWAY, ON THE RESOURCE RATHER THAN ON THE ACT.** A single-factor session may not alter a `wallet_withdrawals` row, because a single-factor session could not have created one. **AND THE OBVIOUS OBJECTION IS ANSWERED BY A PROPERTY RATHER THAN BY A PREFERENCE.** The objection is that a guard on this arrow is the lockout arriving through a second door, which is precisely what [ADR-234](ADR-234.md) section 3 refuses: *"a guard that refused it would trap the row open and the identity behind it"*. **It does not, and the reason is that the two doors take the same factor.** A withdrawal exists only because an elevated session opened it; a trader who cannot elevate cannot open the next one either; so elevation on the cancel holds the release for exactly as long as it holds the request, and never one moment longer. **A guard that expires with the thing it guards is not a lockout.**

**WHAT THE COUNTERFACTUAL COSTS, so a founder is not asked to weigh one side.** With `session` instead, a stolen single-factor session could cancel a trader's open withdrawal. That is a denial and not a theft -- the same session cannot open a withdrawal, cannot register a destination and cannot outrun `payout_destinations`' immutable `first_seen_at` -- **so the choice is between a delay an attacker can impose and a ceremony the trader has already performed once.** This entry takes the second and section 9 records it as the first thing a founder read may overturn: it is one field.

**NO IDEMPOTENCY KEY, AND THE SAFETY IS A PROPERTY RATHER THAN A PROMISE.** [Section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s required set is four endpoints and the creation's membership in it is *"required by the schema rather than by this sentence"*, because `wallet_withdrawals.idempotency_key` is `text NOT NULL`. **This door writes no row.** A retry finds the row already `cancelled`, `decideCancellation` holds, and nothing is written a second time, so a key would be protecting a write a retry does not perform. **The cost is stated and it is real**: a client whose first response was lost reads `409` on the retry rather than the original `200`, and has to re-read the withdrawal to learn that it succeeded. **The alternative was answering `200` on an already-cancelled row**, which would need `cancelled_at` carried on `WithdrawalApprovalCandidate` and would make one response mean two different things; it is named as the cheaper of the two repairs if a client surface asks for it.

### 5. One refusal document for three situations, and the one hold that may name itself

**`not_cancellable` ANSWERS AN ID THAT NAMES NO ROW, AN ID THAT NAMES ANOTHER TRADER'S ROW, AND THE CALLER'S OWN ROW PAST `approved`, WITH THE SAME BYTES.** That is not an imprecision, it is the control: [section 13](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s ruling 1 is that existence is not confirmed to a stranger, `driveCancellation` reads through an accessor that has already scoped every row to the caller, and a detail naming the row's current status would answer the first two apart from the third. **The sentence is written about the machine and never about the row**, and the equality is asserted rather than described: the two responses are compared field for field in the suite, with only `instance` allowed to differ.

**THE CODE IS `conflict` AND IT IS NOT THIS MODULE'S TO CHOOSE.** API_CONTRACT section 2's table is CLOSED, which `payouts.ts` records and which `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`' docblock in this same module already pays for once. A `not_cancellable` code would be a closed vocabulary widened to improve a message.

**THE HALT IS THE ONE HOLD THAT NAMES ITSELF**, and the ground is that it is already the trader's to see: section 6.2 renders the halt to the trader as `{ halted_at, resolves_by } | null` on a subsequent read, so a refusal saying an investigation is open discloses nothing this surface withholds. **A trader told only that a cancellation is impossible retries; a trader told an investigation is running knows what they are waiting for.** The hold itself is [ADR-234](ADR-234.md) section 3's ruling, unchanged and not re-opened here: the database permits a halted `requested` row to be cancelled and this module refuses it, and that remains a founder question on that entry rather than on this one.
