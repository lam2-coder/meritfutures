## ADR-254: `R-38` is ACCOUNT grained, the index that already enforces it is right and is M01's own delta, and the identity rule it was confused with is a different rule on a different table that is already served  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**[ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `254` sent this session to rule the grain of `R-38`, which [ADR-248](ADR-248.md) section 5 found stated two ways inside one FROZEN plan and deliberately refused to resolve. It is ruled.**

> **`R-38` is ACCOUNT grained: `hasPayoutInFlight` is true when an `approved`, `frozen` or `held_pending_review` `payout_requests` row exists FOR THE SUBJECT ACCOUNT, which is `payout_requests_no_in_flight_uq`'s predicate, and it is never the identity's.**

**THE LOSING READING IS THE IDENTITY, AND IT LOST FOR A REASON THAT IS NOT "THE INDEX SAID SO".** Section 3 names it and quotes every source that supported it, so the next reader can see why it lost rather than re-deriving the argument.

**AND THE TWO READINGS TURN OUT NOT TO BE TWO READINGS OF ONE RULE. THEY ARE TWO RULES.** `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT` spans both payout legs and the corpus already says so at [ADR-158](ADR-158.md) finding 8: it is *"a UNIQUE index on the internal leg and a plain index on the external one"*. The internal leg's is account grained on `payout_requests` and is `hasPayoutInFlight`. The external leg's is identity grained on `wallet_withdrawals`, is [ADR-019](ADR-019.md)'s, is enforced in the handler because that leg's index is not unique, and **is already built and shipping**. Neither rule is withdrawn by this entry. What is ruled is which of the two the engine's field denotes.

### 1. Establishing the tiebreak first, because the row required it: the index DOES take a side

**IT TAKES THE ACCOUNT SIDE, AND IT IS UNIQUE, WHICH IS THE HALF THAT MAKES IT A COMMITMENT RATHER THAN A HINT.**

CREATE UNIQUE INDEX payout_requests_no_in_flight_uq

**THE ROW ASKED WHETHER THE PLAN OR THE INDEX IS WRONG, AND WARNED THAT THE ANSWER IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY THE INDEX. **THE GRAIN HAS NEVER MOVED IN THE DATABASE.** [`0010`](../../packages/db/migrations/0010_payouts.sql) created the index and `0031` dropped and re-created it under the same name to widen the **predicate**; both creations key off `account_id`. **AND IT IS A REAL RULING RATHER THAN A SLIP, WHICH IS WHY THIS ENTRY DOES NOT OVERTURN IT.** `B` is founder-ruled and accepted. What is refused is the claim that `hasPayoutInFlight` denotes it.

**GROUND 1. `ADR-019`'s PREMISE WAS REMOVED BY `ADR-040`, AND `ADR-040` RE-CREATED THE ACCOUNT-GRAINED OBJECT RATHER THAN DROPPING IT.** `ADR-019` migrated the guard *"to the external leg only"* on the stated ground that *"there is no window in which an internal payout is in flight"*. [`ADR-040`](ADR-040.md) then introduced `held_pending_review`, *"a pre-approval state entered when an unresolved high-severity flag exists at request time"*, and ruled in its own words: **"A held request is outstanding."** So an internal payout can be in flight for up to 48 hours with nothing posted against it. `ADR-040` did not treat the guard as migrated away: it dropped and re-created **both** `SD-09` objects on `payout_requests (account_id)`, widened to include the new state, and its `0031` header states the reason at [`0031:96-98`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql): *"Under ADR-019 the internal leg settles instantly to the wallet; under ADR-040 a hold sits BEFORE approval. The outstanding states are therefore exactly 'approved', 'frozen' and 'held_pending_review'."*

**GROUND 2. `ADR-040` NAMED M01'S TWO SITES AS UNSWEPT, AND IT NAMED THEM AS THE `hasPayoutInFlight` COMMENT AND `SD-09`, WHICH ARE THE ACCOUNT-GRAINED PAIR.** Its cross-cutting correction reads: *"[M01] `SD-09` and its `hasPayoutInFlight` comment carry the pre-ADR-028 predicate."* **The last accepted entry to touch this predicate treated the account-grained sites as the live ones needing a vocabulary repair, and did not treat the identity sentence as a competing rule at all.**

**GROUND 3. THE LOSING SENTENCE IS SATISFIABLE ON NEITHER TABLE, WHICH MAKES IT A FOLD ARTEFACT RATHER THAN AN INDEPENDENT READING.** It names *"a wallet-to-rail withdrawal ... in `approved`, `transferring`, or `frozen`"*. [`0001:95`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) declares `wallet_withdrawal_status` with **no `frozen`**; `payout_status` has **no `transferring`**. The set `{approved, transferring, frozen}` is `payout_requests`' own pre-`ADR-028` vocabulary, and it is the SAME set `M01:207` carries. **So the sentence took its status set from the internal leg and only its grain word from `ADR-019`.** A sentence whose predicate belongs to one table and whose subject belongs to another is a fold that half-landed, and it enforced nothing on either.

**GROUND 4, AND IT IS THE ROW'S OWN TRAP INVERTED. THE CONSERVATIVE READING WOULD SILENTLY WITHDRAW A CAPABILITY THE CORPUS GRANTS.** `AS-09` is ruled at the gate: *"no identity-level extraction ceiling in v1"*, and it states the consequence deliberately rather than by omission: *"Merit accepts that a single identity can legitimately extract 1,500,000c in one day, and the control is that the money is reserved for before it is requested."* An identity-grained `hasPayoutInFlight` **is** an identity-level extraction ceiling, of one, imposed by a boolean nobody would read as a policy. It would refuse nine of a copy trader's ten accounts. **Fewer simultaneous payouts is not safer when the corpus has already priced and reserved for the larger number; it is a denial nobody authorized, which is the shape `ADR-040` exists to make impossible.**

**GROUND 5. THE ENGINE IS PER ACCOUNT AND `AS-09` SAYS SO ON PURPOSE.** *"The engine stays per-account, because cross-account state inside a pure fold would destroy replay."* `evaluatePayout` folds one account's day. An identity-grained gate is cross-account state, so the identity reading is not merely wrong about the population: it is unavailable to the evaluator that consumes it without breaking the property `INV-23` and `SD-06` exist to protect.

### 4. What is JUDGED here, stated as a judgement

Grounds 1 to 3 are derivations. **The judgement is that `ADR-019`'s per-identity rule and `hasPayoutInFlight` are DIFFERENT RULES rather than one rule at a contested grain**, and the alternative reading is worth stating because a reader may prefer it: that `ADR-019` moved `R-38` wholesale, that `ADR-040` restored an account-grained INDEX without restoring the GATE, and that `hasPayoutInFlight` should therefore read `wallet_withdrawals` at the identity.
