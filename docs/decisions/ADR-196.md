## ADR-196: an identity comes into existence at the first VERIFIED contact, in one unit of work with its `users` row, and the row it writes carries nothing  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**Nothing in `apps/` or `packages/` inserts into `identities`, so no trader can exist, and every per-identity posting this corpus has built is unreachable.** This entry rules **where the row appears**, **what it carries when it does**, and **what it deliberately does not**. It does not build the route. The reason it does not is section 7, and it is structural rather than a shortage of session.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `196`, session **365**, dispatched as `claude/identity-creation`. **Migration `0060` was reserved CONDITIONALLY and the condition is NOT met: the number RETURNS TO THE POOL UNSPENT.** Section 8 is why, and it refuses a guard that was written and executed rather than one that was imagined.

- **MONEY PATH. [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) READ IS OWED** on this ruling, and nothing here discharges it. **Merging is not signing.** The entry ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line.

- **NO MERGED MIGRATION IS EDITED.** [`0002`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql), [`0029`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql) and [`0054`](../../packages/db/migrations/0054_identity_ledger_accounts.sql) are byte for byte unchanged, and `0054`'s `AFTER INSERT ON identities` trigger is the thing that makes this question load bearing rather than a thing this entry moves.

- **WHAT IS DERIVED AND WHAT IS JUDGED, separated before the argument starts.** That nothing creates an identity is DERIVED, re-measured in section 1. That the frozen [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) already places the moment is DERIVED, in section 2, and it is the finding that makes this entry smaller than its dispatch expected. That the `sms` channel cannot register anybody is DERIVED, from a `NOT NULL` in a merged migration. **What is JUDGED is the shape of the write** (one unit of work, identity and login together), **what the row carries** (section 4), and **that no migration should carry the guard yet** (section 8).

- **[ADR-126](ADR-126.md) SECTION 7 ASKED FOR THIS ENTRY BY NAME.** It reported the third construction, declined to build it, and said in its own words that it is *"a harder ruling than either of these, which is why it is worth a number rather than a line"*. This is that number.

### 1. The finding, re-derived at source

**Re-measured at the moment of writing, over every non-test `.ts` file in `apps/` and `packages/`: ZERO of them insert into `identities`.** The search is not a grep for the table name, which appears in 48 such files; it is a grep for a write to it, in every spelling this tree uses -- raw `INSERT INTO identities`, Drizzle's `insert(identities)`, and the accessor's `insert('identities', ...)`. **No spelling has a hit.**

**The absence is not an oversight, it is a TYPE.** [`scope.ts:591-594`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) registers `identities` as `class: 'root'`, and [`scoped-db.ts:365-368`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)'s `OwnedTableKey` admits only the `owned` class, so the scoped writer cannot name the table at all. The reason is written at the type, [`scoped-db.ts:354-355`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts): *"`root` is `identities` and inserting one CREATES an identity. A handle already bound to an identity has no correct row to write there."*

**What the absence costs, executed rather than asserted.** All 58 migrations were applied in order, forward-only from empty, to a real PostgreSQL 16 under `ON_ERROR_STOP`. Against that schema:

| Statement | Result |
|---|---|
| `INSERT INTO identities DEFAULT VALUES` | one row, and **three `ledger_accounts` rows appear** -- `promotional_credit`, `trader_wallet`, `trader_withdrawable` -- from [`0054:144-146`](../../packages/db/migrations/0054_identity_ledger_accounts.sql)'s trigger |
| the same, with no identity ever inserted | `SELECT count(*) FROM ledger_accounts WHERE scope = 'identity'` is **0**, and [`payouts.ts:785`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts)'s `lt01` and [`checkout.ts:1713`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)'s `LT-08` both throw at `chart.ts`'s `resolve` |

So `0054` works and has never fired outside a test, because the statement that fires it is the one statement this tree cannot write.

### 2. The corpus already answers the TIMING, and the answer is in a frozen document

**THE DISPATCH SAID THIS QUESTION HAS NO OBVIOUSLY RIGHT ANSWER. HALF OF IT DOES, AND THE HALF THAT DOES IS ALREADY WRITTEN DOWN.** [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) is frozen, and its section 3 states the moment in three places that agree with each other.

| Where | What it says | What it forecloses |
|---|---|---|
| [`API_CONTRACT:104`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) | `type OtpResponse = { sent: true; expires_in_seconds: number }` | **`POST /auth/otp` returns no identity.** A creation there would produce a row the caller is never told about |
| [`API_CONTRACT:91`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) | `POST /auth/otp` *"Deliberately does not reveal whether the destination exists"* | An identity created at OTP request is created for an address **nobody has proven they hold**, and the endpoint's own privacy property is what says the request proves nothing |
| [`API_CONTRACT:117-121`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) | `VerifyResponse` carries `identity_id`, `user_id` and **`is_new: boolean`** | `POST /auth/verify` is where an identity is FIRST NAMED to a caller, and `is_new` is the field that reports **whether this call is the one that made it** |

**`is_new` is the whole ruling in one field, and it has been in the contract since FREEZE.** A boolean saying "this call created the row" is meaningless at an endpoint that does not create the row. It is transcribed into this tree at [`auth.ts:277`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts) and it has exactly one other occurrence in the workspace, `is_new: false` in the suite's fixture: **nothing has ever been able to answer it `true`.**

**A SECOND FROZEN DOCUMENT AGREES, AND IT SETTLES THE ORDERING AGAINST PHONE.** [`SECURITY:297`](../architecture/SECURITY.md) prices the tripped cost breaker and says the residual in its own words: *"during a degraded window, **identities exist** that have not proven they hold their number"*. So the identity row **precedes** phone verification rather than following it, and [ADR-039](ADR-039.md)'s *"Phone verification is mandatory at registration"* is a gate INSIDE the registered state and not a precondition of entering it. Any reading that waits for a verified phone before writing `identities` contradicts a frozen invariant's stated residual.

**AND THE SCHEMA'S OWN COMMENT REFUSES THE EARLIER MOMENT DIRECTLY.** [`0002:305-307`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) explains why `otp_challenges` keys off the normalized address: *"Issued before a user may exist, so this keys off the normalized email rather than a user_id."* A creation at OTP request makes that sentence false in a merged migration.

**SO THE TIMING IS DERIVED AND NOT CHOSEN.** What this entry adds is the shape of the write, what the row carries, and the refusals in section 3 stated as refusals rather than left as silence.

### 3. The four candidates, and the failure mode of each

#### (a) At `POST /auth/otp`, the first contact. REFUSED.

**Failure mode, priced in rows.** The endpoint is the only one in the contract at required factor `none`. An identity written there is **four rows** -- one `identities` and three `ledger_accounts` from `0054`'s trigger -- **written by anyone who can type an email address, with no authentication and no proof of control.** That makes `POST /auth/otp` an unauthenticated row-writer against the money schema, and each row is addressable: [ADR-191](ADR-191.md) made `identities` the root the sixth scope class hops to, and every `owned`, `derived` and `pair` rule in [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) resolves through it.

**And the rows cannot be cleaned up.** `identity_signals`, `users`, `orders`, `accounts` and `ledger_accounts` all declare `REFERENCES identities(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`, and [`0002:82-85`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) sets the table's retention to *"forever (financial counterparty record)"*. **An identity minted by a stranger's typo is permanent.** That is the decisive half: the cost of the early row is not storage, it is that the estate has no eraser for it and was designed not to have one.

**It also contradicts two merged sentences**, `0002:305-307` above and the contract's own enumeration defence: creating a row per requested address builds exactly the list of addresses `API_CONTRACT:91` refuses to disclose.

#### (b) At the first VERIFIED contact, `POST /auth/verify`. **TAKEN.**

Section 2 derives it. What makes it right rather than merely contractual is that it is **the first moment in the whole flow at which Merit knows something true about a person**: they hold the address they typed. Everything earlier is an assertion by a stranger; everything later is too late for the reason (c) gives.

#### (c) At the first purchase. REFUSED, and it is not late, it is UNREACHABLE.

**This is the candidate the dispatch worried about most, and the tree already forecloses it.** Both checkout endpoints declare `required: 'session'` ([`checkout.ts:2437`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) and [`:2501`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)). A session is a `sessions` row, which `REFERENCES users(id)`, which `REFERENCES identities(id)` ([`0002:337-338`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql), [`:243-245`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql)). **By the time checkout runs, the identity must already exist, or the caller could not have reached the endpoint.** Creation at first purchase is not a later instant on the same path; it is an instant the path cannot contain.

**Even with the authority removed it would be wrong.** [`0006:97`](../../packages/db/migrations/0006_commerce.sql) declares `orders.identity_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES identities(id)`, so the identity would be created **inside the checkout transaction**, which means `0054`'s ledger provisioning trigger fires inside a unit of work that rolls back for PSP reasons. Provisioning a person's ledger positions would then be contingent on a payment processor's latency.

**[ADR-183](ADR-183.md) NAMED THIS ALTERNATIVE AND COULD NOT RULE IT, AND THIS ENTRY DISCHARGES THAT.** Its "where this entry is most likely wrong" paragraph says: *"If the right trigger is the first purchase rather than the first sighting, this entry has put provisioning one step too early."* **It did not.** The instant it chose is the only instant reachable, and `0054` is correct as merged.

#### (d) An explicit registration route. REFUSED.

[ADR-039](ADR-039.md) removed the form, and a `POST /auth/register` would take an address and create a row for it **before any code was verified**, which is candidate (a) at a different URL with (a)'s failure mode intact. It would also be a second door to the same row, and the two would drift on which one is entitled to answer `is_new`. **The dispatch says do not build a registration form and this ruling does not imply one:** the sign-up is a branch inside a handler that already exists in the contract, not a surface of its own.

### 4. What the row carries at creation, and what it deliberately does not

**THE ANSWER IS THAT IT CARRIES NOTHING, AND THAT IS A PROPERTY OF THE MERGED DDL RATHER THAN A CHOICE THIS ENTRY MAKES.** Every column of `identities` at [`0002:32-76`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) is either defaulted or nullable. The creating statement is therefore, in full:

```sql
INSERT INTO identities DEFAULT VALUES
```

Executed, and it is the exact statement measured in section 1. **The root of every scope rule in this corpus holds no personal data at all.**

| Column | At creation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `gen_random_uuid()` | the tenancy value everything else stamps |
| `status` | `'active'` | and `status_reason` stays NULL, which `identities_status_is_explained` ([`0002:73-75`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql)) permits only for `active` |
| `payouts_frozen` | `false` | the freeze columns stay NULL, held by `identities_freeze_is_explained` |
| `leaderboard_opt_in` | `false` | **opt-in is opt-in.** A default of `true` would publish a person by their arrival |
| `first_seen_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at` | `now()` | and `first_seen_at` is now a true statement rather than an approximation, because under this ruling the first sighting and the row are the same instant |
| `display_name` | **NULL, deliberately** | [`0002:35-37`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) calls it *"Reserved per the Wave 1 schema list. Nullable because v1 never shows it"*. **A registration form would fill this and there is no registration form**, which is `ADR-039` reaching the schema |
| `max_accounts_override` | **NULL, deliberately** | a per-entity cap override exists for grandfathered merges. Writing one at creation would make an exception the default |
| `support_contact_ref` | **NULL, deliberately** | the Chatwoot pointer is written when support first meets the person, and a row that has one before anybody has contacted support is a pointer to nothing |

**WHAT IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT CARRY IS EVERY IDENTIFYING THING, AND EACH ONE LIVES EXACTLY ONE HOP OUT.**

- **The email is on `users`**, not here ([`0002:248`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql)).
- **The phone is a HASH on `identity_phones`** ([`0029:101-110`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql)), never here and never in the clear.
- **Every resolution signal is a digest on `identity_signals`** ([`0002:94-132`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql)), whose own comment bounds what a breach yields to *"these two accounts shared something"*.
- **No IP, no country, no user agent.** `country_code` is on `users` ([`0002:257`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql)) and is nullable; the request's IP reaches `otp_challenges.request_ip` and `sessions.created_ip` and stops there.

**THAT IS THE PRIVACY HALF OF THE RULING AND IT IS STATED AS A COMMITMENT RATHER THAN OBSERVED AS AN ACCIDENT: a column added to `identities` is a column added to the row every scope rule hops through, so the table's emptiness is a control and a later migration that fills it is spending that control rather than extending a table.** The corollary a founder should read twice: **an `identities` row on its own identifies nobody**, so the erasure surface is `users`, `identity_phones` and `identity_signals`, and the counterparty record survives an erasure exactly as `0002:82-85` intends.

**THE ONE THING THE RULING ADDS TO THE WRITE IS THAT IT IS NOT ALONE.**

> **An identity and its first `users` row are written in ONE unit of work. A committed `identities` row with no `users` row is not a state this system creates.**

That is [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) section 7's own description of the act -- *"A sign-up writes `identities` AND `users` in one unit of work"* -- ruled here rather than described. The reason is the RESTRICT-forever retention above: an identity with no login is a permanent row nobody can reach, delete or explain.

### 5. The ruling

1. **An identity comes into existence at `POST /auth/verify`, on a code that verifies, when the address resolves to no existing `users` row.** Nowhere else.
2. **The identity row and its `users` row are one unit of work.** Either both commit or neither does.
3. **The identity row is written with defaults only.** Every column above stays as `0002` set it.
4. **`is_new` is `true` on exactly the call that performed clause 1**, and `false` on every other verification, including every later one by the same person.
5. **`is_new` is ALWAYS `false` on the `sms` channel.** This is derived, not chosen: see section 6.
6. **`0054`'s trigger is the whole of ledger provisioning and no handler duplicates it.** A handler that also inserted `ledger_accounts` would be a second opinion about a set the database already writes.
7. **`POST /auth/otp` creates nothing.** Its only write is the `otp_challenges` row `0002` designed for a caller with no identity.

### 6. The `sms` channel cannot register anybody, and nothing in the corpus says so

**This was not in the dispatch and it is a consequence of clause 1 meeting a merged `NOT NULL`.**

[`0002:248`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) declares `email citext NOT NULL UNIQUE` on `users`. `POST /auth/verify` accepts `channel: "sms"` with a `phone` and no `email` ([`API_CONTRACT:116`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)). **So a verification arriving on the `sms` channel for an unknown number has no value to write into `users.email` and cannot create the login clause 2 requires.** Executed against the merged schema: the insert fails on the `NOT NULL`, and there is no default.

**The `sms` channel is therefore a LOGIN channel and never a registration one**, and it resolves through `identity_phones.identity_id` ([`0029:102-103`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql)) to an identity that must already exist. That is coherent with everything else -- [ADR-039](ADR-039.md) makes a verified phone an identity SIGNAL rather than a contact field, and a signal about nobody is not a signal -- but **it is written down in no document in this corpus**, and an implementer reading `VerifyRequest`'s symmetric two-channel shape would reasonably build a symmetric handler and discover the asymmetry from a constraint violation on a money path.

**Recorded here as a clause rather than deferred, because the alternative is a `NOT NULL` teaching it to somebody at run time.**

### 7. The build is larger than one session, and here is the price

**THIS ENTRY RULES AND DOES NOT BUILD, and the reason is that the door does not exist and building it is `packages/db`'s, which this session's fence excludes.**

**[ADR-120](ADR-120.md) NAMED TWO BLOCKERS AND [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) DISCHARGED BOTH.** B1, no pre-identity read, is served by `resolutionDb` at [`scoped-db.ts:1467`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts), whose one-member table vocabulary `ResolvableTableKey = 'users'` exists, in its own docblock's words, *"because `POST /auth/verify` must turn the address a person typed into the identity that owns it"*. B2, no `derived` insert, is served by `insertUnder`.

**THE THIRD IS THIS ONE AND IT IS OPEN.** [`scoped-db.ts:1448-1455`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) states it exactly: *"ESTABLISHING AN IDENTITY AT ALL HAS NO DOOR AND NO CALLER... the endpoint that would sign somebody up is `POST /auth/verify`, which is blocked on the OTP digest whatever this file does."*

**So the build is four rulings, in this order, and none of them is transcription:**

| # | What | Where it lands | Why it is not this session's |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The OTP digest.** `otp_challenges.code_hash` has no specified digest. A plain hash of a short human-typed code is a one-million-candidate offline break for anybody holding the table; a keyed MAC is right and **there is nowhere to keep the key**, because the table carries no salt column and no key id | a deployment secret **or a migration**, and [`auth-backend.ts:311-317`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts) says so in those words | it is a config-surface ruling with a migration possibly behind it, and it blocks `requestOtp` and `verifyOtp` alike |
| 2 | **The establishment door.** A `packages/db` construction that writes `identities` at an authority holding no identity, in one unit of work with `users` | `packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts` | **`packages/**` beyond a migration is nobody's this wave.** And [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) section 7 already declined it once, correctly, for want of a caller |
| 3 | **Delivery.** Nothing in this deployable sends a code, and the SMS branch needs a per-send price to charge against `otp_send_budget.spend_cents` | `apps/api` wiring plus [M10](../plans/M10-integrations.md) config | a handler answering `sent: true` having sent nothing is worse than the 503 it replaces |
| 4 | **The handler.** `verifyOtp`: resolve, branch on `is_new`, establish, mint | [`apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts) and `auth-backend.ts` | it is the smallest of the four and it is last |

**Item 2 is the one this entry unblocks, and it unblocks it by supplying what ADR-126 said was missing.** That section refused to build the door because *"this one has no caller at all"*. **A ruling that names the caller, the moment, the unit of work and the row's contents IS the caller specification**, and `job-queue.ts`'s rule is satisfied the way [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) satisfied it: a primitive built for a named, ruled caller is removable, because the ruling says what it was for.

**Honest count: 1 of 4, and the 1 is the ruling rather than a file.**

### 8. `0060` is returned to the pool, and the guard was BUILT and MEASURED before it was refused

**The condition on `0060` is *"only if the ruling needs a guard the DDL does not carry"*, and the candidate guard is obvious: clause 2 of section 5 is a cross-row assertion, which a row-local `CHECK` cannot make and a `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED` constraint trigger can.** It was written and executed rather than reasoned about:

```sql
CREATE CONSTRAINT TRIGGER identities_have_a_login
  AFTER INSERT ON identities DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION identity_has_a_login();   -- raises unless a users row exists
```

**IT WORKS, AND IT REFUSES EXACTLY WHAT THE RULING REFUSES.** Measured on the 58-file schema:

| Statement | Result |
|---|---|
| `BEGIN; INSERT INTO identities; INSERT INTO users (...); COMMIT` -- clause 2's shape | **ACCEPTED** |
| `BEGIN; INSERT INTO identities; COMMIT` -- candidate (a)'s shape | **REFUSED at COMMIT** |

**AND IT IS REFUSED ANYWAY, ON THREE GROUNDS, THE FIRST OF WHICH IS A MEASUREMENT.**

**(i) It breaks a merged probe, and it is the ledger's own.** All 15 `probe_*.sql` scripts in [`scripts/db/`](../../scripts/db/) were run against a clean schema with the guard absent and again with it installed. **Without it: 15 of 15 pass. With it: `probe_ledger_constraints.sql` fails and the other 14 pass.** That probe is the acceptance script for `0054`'s trigger, and it creates identities with no logins because a probe is not a registration. **A migration whose first effect is to break the probe proving the previous migration is a migration that costs a repair in `scripts/db/`, which is outside this fence.**

**(ii) It has no caller, which is the rule [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) section 7 applied to this exact construction.** Nothing writes `identities`, so the guard's only live effect today is on probes and fixtures. A constraint admitted before the write it guards is a constraint nobody can remove, and its first real test would be against the handler that item 4 above has not written.

**(iii) It is narrower than its name, and the gap is `identity_merges`.** A merge *"REPOINTS OWNERSHIP into the surviving `identities` row"* ([`scope.ts:527`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)). An `AFTER INSERT` trigger never sees the `UPDATE` that moves the last login off an identity, so the invariant it advertises holds at creation and silently stops holding afterwards. **Making it whole means a second trigger on `users` at `UPDATE` and `DELETE`, on a table `0002` froze, which is a much larger object than the one this ruling needs.**

**SO THE RULING'S TIMING IS ENFORCED BY WHAT IS ALREADY MERGED, AND THAT IS THE real answer to the condition.** Candidate (c) is refused by `checkout.ts:2437`'s `required: 'session'` plus `sessions -> users -> identities`; the `sms` registration is refused by `0002:248`'s `NOT NULL`; candidate (a) is refused by a contract clause rather than by a constraint. **What is left unenforced is a handler's discipline about a handler that does not exist, and a constraint cannot make a handler exist.**

**`0060` RETURNS TO THE POOL UNSPENT.** The `0060` row names [ADR-167](ADR-167.md), [ADR-181](ADR-181.md) and [ADR-191](ADR-191.md) as the fourth, fifth and sixth times the pool absorbed a number, so **this is the seventh**. The dispatch said sixth; the allocation row it was drawn from says otherwise, and the row is the record.

**The path from a person typing an email to `LT-01` posting is therefore blocked at exactly the four items in section 7 and at nothing else in the money layer.**

### 10. Findings not in the dispatch

1. **The frozen contract already carried the timing.** `is_new` at `API_CONTRACT:120` is the ruling in one field and predates FREEZE. The dispatch's premise that the question "has no obviously right answer" is **half refuted**: the timing is derived, the shape and the contents are the judgement. **That makes this the fifteenth dispatcher premise a session has refuted, and the refutation is in the dispatcher's favour rather than against it**, because a contract that already agrees is a stronger place to rule from.
2. **`auth-backend.ts`'s `NO_PRE_IDENTITY_READ` is STALE.** [`auth-backend.ts:291-297`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts) tells an operator, in a live 503's log line, that *"a pre-identity read has no door"* and that the repair would be widening `SystemReason`. [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) built the door as `resolutionDb` and ruled explicitly that *"the vocabulary that moves is the TABLE and never the REASON"*. **The reason string cites a refusal its own repair superseded**, on `verifyOtp` and `passkeyLoginVerify` both. `auth-backend.ts` is not in this fence; the repair is one string and is owed by whoever next holds that file.
3. **`resolutionDb` has no caller anywhere in `apps/`.** Measured: its only references in the workspace are inside `packages/db` and its own suite. A door built for a named endpoint, which that endpoint does not open, is the shape that decays into a door nobody remembers the reason for.
4. **`0054`'s backfill has now been exercised and is still a no-op for the right reason.** Its header predicts *"on a fresh install this statement writes nothing"*, and that is confirmed: the identity partition of `ledger_accounts` is empty until the first `identities` insert, whoever writes it.
5. **The `sms` registration asymmetry of section 6 is in no document.** It is the largest gap between the contract's shape and the schema's that this session found, and it is one clause rather than a slice.
