### 2. THE RULING

**RULED: MONEY CANNOT LEAVE, AND THE TWO DOORS ARE SHUT BY DIFFERENT KINDS OF THING. NEITHER IS A MISSING DOOR.**

1. **The payout pair carries EIGHT members and SIX of them are BUILT.** `postgresPayoutBackend` serves `transact`, `lockScope`, `identityStatus`, `subject`, `listPayouts` and `insertPayoutRequest`'s approval branch. **What refuses is `holdFlag` and the hold branch beside it, and their supplier is COUNSEL**: `HoldFlag.tosClause` has no value space in this repository. `idempotency` is the eighth and it is **constructible today and deliberately not installed**, which is a ruling rather than an absence.

### 6. THE FINDING: the withdrawal default is not a whole refusal, and the member that serves is the one every request touches first

**`UNWIRED_WITHDRAWAL_BACKEND`'s docblock opens *"A backend that refuses every call"* and `now: () => new Date()` does not refuse.** It is read at `wallet-withdrawals.ts:2468` on the create row and `:2604` on the cancel row, in both cases as the FIRST member of the port any request touches.

**THE SPLIT ACROSS THE DEPLOYABLE IS CLEAN AND IS MEASURED RATHER THAN SAMPLED.** Six `UNWIRED_*` defaults in `apps/api/src` declare a `now`. **FOUR throw a named `*Unwired`** (`UNWIRED_ADMIN_PAYOUT_BACKEND`, `UNWIRED_ADMIN_CERTIFICATE_BACKEND`, `UNWIRED_ADMIN_WRITE_BACKEND`, `UNWIRED_ADMIN_WALLET_BACKEND`). **TWO return the wall clock**, and they are `UNWIRED_WALLET_BACKEND` and `UNWIRED_WITHDRAWAL_BACKEND`, which is to say both of the wallet surfaces and both on the money path. **`start.ts` calls `useWalletBackend` and does not call `useWithdrawalBackend`, so among the ports `start.ts` does not call, EXACTLY ONE holds a clock that serves, and it is this one.**

**THE VALUE IS RIGHT AND THE SENTENCE IS WRONG, AND THAT IS MEASURED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS RATHER THAN ARGUED.** `const at = active.now()` sits OUTSIDE the only `try` either handler in the file opens. A `now` written the way the other four write theirs throws past `unwiredOrThrow`, past `endpointHandler`, which rethrows anything that is not an `AuthBackendUnwired` (`auth.ts:1058`), and into `server.ts`'s error handler, **which answers `internal_error` at 500 ON THE DOOR WHERE CASH LEAVES MERIT.** That is [ADR-285](ADR-285.md)'s finding arriving from the other side, on the other extraction door.

- **Seeded and watched:** replacing the clock with the four-fold shape turns the money-out refusal into a **500**, and the case that asserts it is in the file.
- **Seeded the other way and watched:** moving the clock read INSIDE the `try` and refusing the clock restores the **503**, and the case goes RED. **So this ruling fails on good news**, which is [ADR-330](ADR-330.md)'s test.

**SO THE SERVING CLOCK IS LOAD BEARING RATHER THAN A LAPSE, AND WHAT IS OWED IS A DECISION AND NOT AN EDIT.** No ruling in the corpus says which of the two conventions governs a clock on a fail-closed default. Making this one refuse WITHOUT moving the read would be a money-path regression from a retryable status to an internal error; moving the read is a money-path handler change, and **this row did not make one on the same day it re-derived the port's blocker inventory**, which is [ADR-358](ADR-358.md) section 6's discipline applied to itself. Section 10 records the options.

### 11. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **Section 6 rules that the serving clock must stand, and that rests on the handler as it is written rather than on a principle.** If a reviewer holds that a fail-closed default must refuse on every member unconditionally, then the correct reading is that the HANDLER is defective and has been since the row was written, and the repair is `OQ-361-01`'s option (b) taken now rather than recorded. **The measurement is the same either way; what differs is whether the 500 is a latent defect or a correct consequence.**
- **Section 3 says neither port lacks a door, and that claim is only as good as the member list.** It was taken by matching braces over a comment-stripped body and is executed, so a member arriving turns it red. **It says nothing about whether the fourteen members `databaseWithdrawalBackend` serves are CORRECT**, which is that adapter's own suite's question and not this row's.
- **The `holdFlag` narrowing in section 3 could be read as a route to option (b) of `OQ-361-03` and it is not one.** An episode exists only where an operator opened a restriction, and the hold predicate ranges over unresolved detector flags. **Most flags that would hold a payout have no episode**, so citing the episode's clause serves the minority case and leaves the majority exactly where it is.
- **The 503 on `POST /accounts/:accountId/payout` is measured against `UNWIRED_PAYOUT_BACKEND` and not against `postgresPayoutBackend`.** With that adapter installed, `GET /payouts` would serve real history while the create row still answered 503 from the same member. That is a partial serve and it is a consequence of `OQ-361-02` rather than an independent finding.
