## ADR-282: a generated line is never written twice, and the check that says so cannot live inside this row's fence  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**Row `282` said the ceiling is 8 and it does not catch doubling, it catches the eighth doubling. That is right, the eight duplicates it measured reproduce here, all eight are defects, and the sharper discriminator it named survives every test this entry could put to it. THE ROW'S FENCE DOES NOT.** The discriminator the row proposes is grounded in `gates.mjs generate`, and that generator writes into every tracked `.md` in this repository rather than into `docs/`. Measured at this commit, the live population of the defect is fifteen lines over five files, and the fifth file is [`packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md`](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md), which carries **seven** of them and which the fence does not name.

**So `RI-31` IS SPECIFIED HERE AND IS NOT MINTED.** A check scoped to `docs/` would land green over 8 of the 15 and would be a gate shaped to fit a fence, which is the move [ADR-279](ADR-279.md) was written to end. A check scoped to the generator would land RED on seven findings in a file this session may not edit. Neither is shippable, and widening the fence is not this session's to do. The number stays reserved, the specification below is complete enough to transcribe, and the answer needed is one line.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `282`, session **473**, branch `claude/a-ceiling-that-catches-the-eighth-doubling`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED** and `packages/db/migrations/**` is untouched. **NOT the money path**: no `src/` line moves, no schema, no ledger, no engine.

- **`RI-12` IS UNCHANGED, AND THIS ENTRY ARGUES THAT ITS NUMBER IS CORRECT.** Section 4 shows what breaks at each candidate value and finds a legitimate line sitting at exactly 8. The ceiling is not loose. It is saturated.

### 2. THE RULING

**RULING 1. A GENERATED SPAN MAY LEGITIMATELY APPEAR MANY TIMES IN ONE FILE. A LINE CARRYING ONE MAY NOT APPEAR TWICE.** The row asked this question first and it is the right one to ask first, because getting it wrong in either direction ruins the check. `gate_count` alone occurs **192** times across `docs/`, twice on a single line wherever a document writes "N of N", and every one of those is correct. The property is therefore about the LINE and never about the span name.

**RULING 2. ON THIS CORPUS, NO LINE CARRYING A GENERATED SPAN IS LEGITIMATELY WRITTEN TWICE, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS STRONGER THAN THE ARGUMENT.** There are **175** lines carrying a `<!--gen:` span under `docs/`, spread over **34** files. The number of those lines that appear byte-identically in **two different files** is **zero**. Not one sentence in this corpus that carries a derived number is written the same way twice anywhere, let alone twice in one document. The row's hypothesis, that one query rendered in two sections would make some of the eight correct, is a real possibility and it has no instance. A second rendering of the same query is written as a different sentence, because it is being said for a different reason.

**RULING 3. ALL FIFTEEN ARE DEFECTS, RULED PER SITE RATHER THAN PER CLASS.** The row required that the ruling come before the repair. It does, and the evidence is per site:

| Site | Copies | Why it is a defect and not a rendering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `docs/architecture/data-model/README.md:474-492` | 7 and 4 and 2 | One paragraph and one `###` heading interleaved with each other. **A document does not render a heading four times.** The section reads `paragraph, heading, paragraph, heading, paragraph, heading` where it should read `paragraph, heading` |
| `docs/architecture/data-model/README.md:542-546` | 5 | Five adjacent identical lines under one `###` |
| `docs/STATE.md:1798,1800` | 2 | Adjacent identical pair, one blank line apart |

| `docs/STATE.md:2527,2560,2585,2608,2629` | 5 | **The interesting one.** One sentence in five different session sections, and **four of those five sections already carry their own, different "The position:" line.** Section 2.1 |
| `packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md`, seven lines | 7, 7, 4, 5, 5, 4, 4 | Adjacent identical runs, the first two interleaved with each other exactly as the `data-model` case is. **Outside this fence.** Section 6 |

**RULING 4. THE DISCRIMINATOR IS THE SPAN, THE SCOPE IS ONE FILE, AND THE CEILING IS ONE.** Not a length floor, not a ceiling above one, and not a span name. Sections 3 and 4 measure each of those alternatives rather than asserting against them.

**RULING 5. `RI-12` STAYS EXACTLY AS IT IS.** Its ceiling is not a compromise that a sharper check now makes unnecessary. Section 4 finds a **legitimate** line sitting at 8, so the ceiling could not be lowered by even one without a false positive, and `RI-12`'s scope contains 29 further repeated lines that are not this defect and must not become findings.

### 3. The discriminator, measured rather than taken

The row named the discriminator and told this session to measure it rather than take it. Three measurements, each of which could have refuted it.

**FIRST: DOES IT SEPARATE THE DEFECTS FROM THE LEGITIMATE REPETITION?** Inside `RI-12`'s own scope, lines of at least 80 characters repeating within one file, this tree carries **37** distinct such lines. Partitioned by whether the line carries a `<!--gen:` span: **8 carry one and all 8 are defects; 29 do not and the largest of them are legitimate.** The partition is clean in both directions on the whole population, which is the strongest form this claim could take.

**SECOND: WOULD IT HAVE CAUGHT WHAT WAS ACTUALLY MISSED?** The row says the dispatcher hand-collapsed this defect at three merges with the gate green. Three such commits are in the visible history, `ca2f86f`, `ce026dd` and `c3e7293`, and **every line removed by every one of them carries a `<!--gen:` span**. All three collapsed the same STATE.md headline, the one whose first span is named `adr_count`. `RI-12` saw none of the three because each stood at two or three copies. The proposed check is three for three on the only real trials available.

**THIRD: IS THE DEFECT CLASS ALREADY ON THE RECORD AS NEEDING THIS EXACT GATE?** Yes, and it predates `RI-12`. [`docs/STATE.md`](../STATE.md), in session 174's section, records _"A PRE-EXISTING DEFECT IS REPORTED AND NOT REPAIRED, BECAUSE IT IS OUTSIDE THE FENCE AND NEEDS A GATE CHANGE. The `gen:adr_count` line is DUPLICATED VERBATIM in both STATE and INDEX ... `CI-06g` cannot see it: the gate rewrites every span it finds and compares each to its own query, so two identical copies both pass."_ That was written on 2026-08-24. `RI-12` landed on 2026-08-27 and does not close it. **This is the fourth wave to name this gate and the first to specify it.**

**A FOURTH MEASUREMENT THAT CAME BACK NEGATIVE AND IS REPORTED ANYWAY.** Masking fenced code blocks the way `gates.mjs` masks them, so that a documented example of the form is not read as a span, changes **nothing** on this tree: fifteen duplicates either way. The masking belongs in the check because the generator has it and because [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s own `CI-06g` section shows the form inside a fence, but it is a correctness choice for a future document rather than a thing that moves today's number, and saying so is cheaper than letting a later reader assume it was load bearing.

That is worth knowing and is reported in section 8; it is not this row's fence and nothing here changes it.

**QUESTION TWO: WHAT CEILING DOES THE NEW CHECK TAKE?** Its scope is different, so the answer is different, and it is derived rather than borrowed.

**THE LENGTH FLOOR GOES TOO, AND FOR THE SAME REASON.** `RI-12` needs its 80-character floor because `---` and `\|---\|---\|` and a bare fence are lines. None of those carries a generated span. ### 6. THE FENCE, AND WHY `RI-31` IS SPECIFIED AND NOT MINTED

**THE ROW'S FENCE FOLLOWS FROM A `docs/`-ONLY MEASUREMENT WHILE THE ROW'S OWN DISCRIMINATOR FOLLOWS FROM THE GENERATOR.** The row grounds the discriminator in one sentence: _"a line carrying a `<!--gen:` span is written by `gates.mjs generate`"_. That generator's file set is `markdownFiles()`, which is `allFiles()` filtered by extension, and `allFiles()` walks the repository rather than `docs/`. So the row's reasoning reaches every tracked `.md`, and the row's fence reaches four documents under `docs/`.

**THAT GAP IS NOT HYPOTHETICAL. IT IS SEVEN LIVE DEFECTS.** [`packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md`](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md) carries seven duplicated generated lines, of the same family as the four repaired here, sharing the `manifest_changes` and `migration_files` spans with them and standing at up to seven copies. That file is `status: review`, it is not frozen, it declares `docs/architecture/data-model/README.md` in its own `depends_on`, and the same merges produced both. It is not in the fence.

**BOTH AVAILABLE MOVES ARE REFUSED, AND THE REFUSALS ARE THE SAME RULE TWICE.**

1. **Scope the check to `docs/`.** It lands green, it reports PASS, and it is silent over 7 of the 15 live instances of the very defect it is named for. That is an absence check over a scope built to make the tree pass, which is what [ADR-279](ADR-279.md) found `RI-28` doing one wave ago. **Weakening a gate to pass it.**
2. **Scope the check to the generator and repair `DELTA_MANIFEST` too.** Correct, and it edits a file the row did not name. **Widening a fence to finish.**

The row's own standing rule says these are the same move, and this session may take neither. **The number `RI-31` stays reserved and unspent, and no register row is written**, because `RI-29`'s second leg requires a row to name a check that exists and a row for a check nobody wrote would fail it.

**WHAT IS BEING ASKED FOR IS ONE LINE.** Either add `packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md` to row `282`'s fence, in which case section 7's specification is transcription and the check lands green over every tracked `.md`; or rule that the check is `docs/`-scoped on `RI-12`'s precedent and row the `DELTA_MANIFEST` half separately, in which case the check must say in its own `covers` that it reads a subset of what the generator writes and name the file it does not read. **This entry recommends the first.** A gate whose stated subject is the generator and whose scope is not the generator's is a gate that will be quoted later as covering something it does not.

**AND IT IS THE SECOND WAVE RUNNING THAT `RI-31` IS RETURNED.** [ADR-281](ADR-281.md) section 6 returned it for a different reason. [ADR-277](ADR-277.md) section 8 already named this pattern, that the durable repair for a tooling defect keeps being deferred to a row nobody has written. **This entry is not deferring the repair to a later row; it is handing over a finished specification and one blocking question.** The distinction matters and this entry would rather be judged on it than hide it.

### 7. The check, specified to the line

Written so that landing it is transcription rather than design. It goes in [`packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs) beside `RI-12`, on `RI-13`'s stated file-boundary precedent: a property of a document's structure that needs no registry.

- **`id`**: `RI-31`.
- **`title`**: `No document repeats a whole line that carries a generated span`.
- **Scope**: every tracked `.md`, matching `gates.mjs`'s `markdownFiles()` rather than `RI-12`'s `docs/`, subject to section 6.
- **Masking**: fenced code blocks are masked with the generator's own expression, character for character. `gates.mjs`'s `spansIn` replaces each fenced block with a copy of itself whose every `<` has become a NUL, so a documented example of the form is not read as a span. Copy that expression rather than writing a second one. Measured to change nothing today, section 3.
- **Predicate**: trim each line; skip any line not containing `<!--gen:`; count identical survivors per file; **any count above one is a finding**. No length floor and no ceiling, each justified in section 4.
- **Finding text** names the file, every line number the duplicate sits on, the count, and the first 90 characters, so a reader can act without opening the file. It also names `RI-12` and says why that gate is silent, because the first question anyone asks of this finding is why nothing caught it.
- **SENTINEL ONE**: no `.md` file walked at all is an ERROR, not a pass.
- **SENTINEL TWO, and it is the one that matters**: **zero lines carrying a `<!--gen:` span anywhere is an ERROR.** This check is an absence check and its scope is defined by a comment syntax. If the span form is renamed, or the masking expression is edited into swallowing the whole document, the scope empties and the check reports PASS forever over a corpus it never read. That is [ADR-274](ADR-274.md)'s and [ADR-279](ADR-279.md)'s rule, and `RI-29`'s own sentinel at [`repo-invariants.mjs:7060`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs) is the idiom to copy.
- **Test obligations**: the synthetic fixture in [`packages/tooling/test/repo-invariants.test.ts`](../../packages/tooling/test/repo-invariants.test.ts) must gain at least one markdown file carrying one span, because the clean-tree case runs every member of `CHECKS` against that fixture and sentinel two is an unconditional throw. Then one seeded case per direction: a doubled gen line is a finding, a span name appearing twice on one line is not, a span appearing in two different files is not, a doubled line carrying no span is not, and each sentinel throws.
- **Register row**: in the same commit as the check, with the title copied out of the live `CHECKS` array, per `RI-29`'s third leg.

### 8. Reported rather than repaired

1. **`packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md` carries seven duplicated generated lines.** Section 6. Outside the fence, reported, not touched.
2. **`RI-12` IS SATURATED AT ITS OWN CEILING BY A LEGITIMATE LINE.** [`docs/reviews/2026-08-20-admin-parity-audit.md`](../reviews/2026-08-20-admin-parity-audit.md) heads eight tables with one identical 94-character row. A ninth parity table turns `RI-12` red on a document doing nothing wrong. **`RI-12` should not be lowered and this is not an argument to raise it**; it is a fact about the gate that nobody had measured, and the remedy when it fires will be to shorten the header rather than to move the number.
3. **Twenty-nine repeated long lines under `docs/` carry no generated span and are untouched.** They are out of this row's subject. Whether any of them is also merge splatter is a separate question this entry did not open, and the honest statement is that it looked at their counts and not at their content.
4. **`CI-06g` remains blind to this by construction and that is correct.** It rewrites every span it finds and compares each against its query, so N identical copies are N passes. It is the count gate and this is not a count defect. Nothing about `CI-06g` should change.

### 9. Where this entry is most likely wrong

**RULING 2 IS AN EMPIRICAL CLAIM ABOUT A CORPUS AND NOT A THEOREM.** "No line carrying a generated span is legitimately written twice" is true of 175 lines over 34 files today. A document could be written tomorrow that renders one query in two places in identically worded sentences, and the check would call it a defect. The cost of being wrong is one reworded sentence and the cost of the alternative is the check, so the trade is heavily one-sided, but the claim is a measurement rather than a proof and it is written down here as one.

**AND THE SECOND IS THAT SCOPE MIGHT BE A SMALLER QUESTION THAN THIS ENTRY MAKES IT.** A founder may hold that `docs/` is the right scope on `RI-12`'s precedent, that `DELTA_MANIFEST` is a different row's business, and that returning the number over it costs more than the hole. Section 6 states the counter and the recommendation; the judgement is not this session's.

### 10. Approval

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

**THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS SECTION 6, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING BLOCKING THE CHECK.** One line either way. Add `packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md` to row `282`'s fence and the check lands over the generator's own scope, green, in one short session; or rule it `docs/`-scoped and the check lands with a stated hole and a second row to close it. **This entry recommends the first and refuses to take it unilaterally**, because a fence a session widens for itself is not a fence.
