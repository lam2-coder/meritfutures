## ADR-082: the closed unit vocabulary widens its DEFINITION and never its token list, and `rail clock` names its class after its commonest member  (2026-08-23, status: proposed)

**This amends a frozen document, which is why it is an ADR and not a commit.** [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) is `accepted` and closed the unit vocabulary at three tokens; [`CI-06m`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) check 3 is that ruling's enforcement. **The third token's DEFINITION moves here. The token list does not.** Non-money: no migration, no engine change, no `SD-nn`, and no new `CI-06` letter.

- **Origin:** `OI-28`, opened by [session 120](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-120.md) while writing [`0045_simulation_runs.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0045_simulation_runs.sql), and recorded in [DELTA_MANIFEST](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md) section 16. That session declared a token, said in the same entry that the noun was wrong, and named the ADR as owed rather than taking it from a money-path fence.

- **Context:** `CI-06m` requires every `date` column in the migration set to carry a design-record row declaring `**Unit: <token>**` from a closed vocabulary of three. [`simulation_runs.calibration_observed_at`](../architecture/data-model/simulation_runs.md) is the day an external calibration source **observed** the figures a simulation was run against. Two of the three tokens are refuted by their own definitions and the third fits by its operative half while its noun does not fit at all.

---

### 1. The finding, restated against the primary source rather than against the report of it

`UNIT_TOKENS` in [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) carried three entries, and each carries the definition the gate is enforcing:

| Token | Definition as it stood |
|---|---|
| `trading day` | the exchange CT trading day, answered only by `TradingCalendar` |
| `wall clock` | Merit's own clock, answered only by `now()` |
| `rail clock` | the rail's own clock, quoted and never computed by Merit |

**`wall clock` is refused by its own definition.** `calibration_observed_at` is never `now()`. Writing `now()` into it is precisely the defect the column exists to prevent, which is `AS-M21-01` in [ADR-071](ADR-071.md) section 4: stale calibration figures are exactly as plausible as fresh ones at the moment of the decision, so the observation day has to be the vendor's and not Merit's.

**`trading day` is refused harder.** A calibration vendor does not observe on the exchange session boundary, and `TradingCalendar` cannot answer for a day it did not define.

**`rail clock` fits by its operative half.** *"Quoted and never computed by Merit"* is exactly true: `observedAt` is validated against `yyyy-mm-dd` in [`provenance.ts`](../../packages/harness/src/provenance.ts) and stored as given. [`affiliate_commissions.chargeback_window_ends_on`](../architecture/data-model/affiliate_commissions.md) already carries the token for the same reason, the card networks' window being theirs rather than Merit's. **And the noun is wrong. A calibration vendor is not a rail.**

**Session 120 was right on both counts and that is why this is an ADR.** It declared the token that was true and it refused to pretend the name was. Its own entry states the standing risk: picking the nearest-looking token quietly is how three rows passed this check by accident before the marker was tightened, and the accident is recorded in `gates.mjs` beside `UNIT_MARKER`.

---

### 2. The ruling

> **The vocabulary stays at three tokens. `rail clock`'s definition widens to the class it has always enforced: a third party's own clock, quoted and never computed by Merit, whether that party is a payment rail, a calibration vendor or any other counterparty whose day Merit reads and never derives. `rail` names the class after its commonest member and is not a claim about the counterparty.**
>
> **`simulation_runs.calibration_observed_at` is `rail clock` and was correctly declared on the day it landed.**

This is shape **(b)** of the three the objective put, taken with shape **(c)**'s conclusion about the column and against shape **(c)**'s disposition of the definition. Section 5 says why.

---

### 3. Why a fourth token is refused, which is the argument the runner's own comment demands

The comment above `UNIT_TOKENS` argues against widening by implication and its sentence is the one to answer:

> An open vocabulary would defeat the check within a release: every cell would declare its own phrasing, no two would agree, and "the unit is named" would become "some words about time are present".

A fourth token is not an open vocabulary. **The sentence still decides the question, because it names a direction and a fourth token is a step in it.** The answer is not that four is a small number. It is that **a vocabulary is closed only when it partitions on a question with finitely many answers**, and the two candidate questions have very different answer sets.

**The question these three tokens partition on is: who answers this date, and may Merit compute it?** There are exactly three answers. Merit's exchange calendar. Merit's own clock. Neither, so somebody else's, quoted. **There is no fourth answer to that question**, and that is what makes the set closed rather than merely short.

**A fourth token partitions on a different question: who is the counterparty?** That question has no finite answer set. `vendor clock` today; the next `date` from a KYC provider has as good a claim to `kyc clock`, a market-data feed to `feed clock`, a card processor to `processor clock`, and each one would be defended exactly as `vendor clock` would be defended here. **Every one of them means "not Merit's, quoted", which is one answer wearing four names**, and that is the state the closed vocabulary exists to prevent: two rows declaring the same unit in two spellings and agreeing only by accident, which is the sentence `CI-06m` already prints when it refuses an unknown token.

**And the distinction a fourth token would draw is enforced nowhere.** ADR-042 wired three mechanisms against computing a date Merit does not own: the `packages/eslint-plugin-merit` import ban, the SQL shape check over the migrations, and this gate. **Not one of them treats a payment rail differently from a data vendor**, because there is nothing to treat differently. Both are forbidden identically and for the same reason. A token whose only content is a distinction the corpus never acts on is a token that will be chosen by feel, and choosing by feel is the failure `UNIT_MARKER`'s own comment records.

**So this widening does not slide, and the reason is structural rather than a promise to be careful.** The definition moved along the axis the token already partitioned; the token list did not move at all. The next session reading the widened sentence and reaching for `vendor clock` is refused by the same closed set, and section 7 is where that refusal is watched happening rather than asserted here.

---

### 4. Why the definition moves rather than the name

**A rename is out of reach and would be the wrong repair even if it were not.** `rail clock` is declared by rows in two design records, and this session's fence holds neither of the files a rename would have to edit. More to the point, a rename moves every existing declaration, and **a vocabulary change that silently re-classifies an existing column is the failure this ruling must not cause.** Widening a definition while leaving every token string alone is the only one of the three shapes that cannot re-classify anything, and section 6 measures that rather than asserting it.

**The noun survives as a wart and it is a recorded one.** `rail` is the class's first and commonest member. That is a real cost and it is the price of not moving 54 resolutions to buy a better name.

---

### 5. Why shape (c) is refused, having agreed with its conclusion

Shape (c) rules that the column is correctly `rail clock` and that the noun is a naming wart the corpus accepts. **Its conclusion about the column is this ADR's conclusion.** Its disposition of the definition is what is refused, and the reason is measurable rather than aesthetic.

Under (c) the definition still reads *"the rail's own clock"*, so a careful reader arriving at a vendor column reads it as refuted, exactly as session 120 did. That session then had to argue the token in the open inside a design record and open an item to get the argument ruled. **The next vendor `date` column repeats that in full**: same refutation, same argument, same item under a new number. **A definition that has to be re-argued each time it is applied is a defect in the definition and not a wart in the noun**, and (c) leaves the defect exactly where it was found.

---

### 6. What changed in the runner, and the measurement that says it re-classified nothing

Three changes, all inside `UNIT_TOKENS` and `CI-06m`.

**One.** `rail clock`'s `why` states the class. No token string moves, so no declaration on disk changes and none needs to.

**Two.** **`CI-06m` prints a note**, which it did not before. The gate could report `PASS` while the population under it moved, and the figure this ruling turns on could only be got by reading 57 markers by hand. The note now prints the number of `date` columns in scope, the per-token tally and the unresolved count on every run, so a vocabulary change that re-classifies a column shows up as a moved tally instead of as nothing.

**Three.** **The definitions are printed with the finding.** `why` was carried beside every token and rendered nowhere: the refusal offered `trading day | wall clock | rail clock` and no definition of any of them, so a session standing at a failing column had to open `gates.mjs` to learn what it was choosing between. Session 120 did exactly that. **That is the widening's delivery mechanism**, and without it the widened sentence would reach the next session only if that session went looking for it, which ADR-042 already ruled is what prose does rather than what a control does.

**The measurement, run on this tree with the gate's own parser, before the definition moved and after.** Both runs are `node scripts/corpus/gates.mjs check`:

| | `date` columns in scope | `trading day` | `wall clock` | `rail clock` | unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Before** (definition unwidened, note added) | 54 | 34 | 18 | 2 | 0 |
| **After** (definition widened) | 54 | 34 | 18 | 2 | 0 |

**Identical, and identical is the whole claim.** The tokens are what the gate matches on and none of them moved, so every one of the 54 resolutions resolves to the token it resolved to before. The two rows the widening speaks to are the two counted under `rail clock`, and they are the same two rows.

**The count is 54 and not 49.** `falsify.mjs`'s `real` field for `CI-06m` says *"the schema holds 49 `date` columns"*, which was true when it was written and is five short of this tree. It is outside this session's fence and is reported rather than repaired, in the session log's own **Reported, not touched** list. **The note line exists so that the next hand-maintained count of this population is the last one.**

### 7. The falsify case, because a token nobody has watched being rejected is not a closed vocabulary

`CI-06m/widened-definition-is-not-a-fourth-token` seeds `**Unit: vendor clock**` onto a design-record row that declares `rail clock`, and the gate refuses it:

```
in scope, failed      CI-06m/widened-definition-is-not-a-fourth-token
    affiliate_commissions.chargeback_window_ends_on: its row declares
    `**Unit: vendor clock**`, which is not one of trading day | wall clock | rail clock.
```

**`vendor clock` is the seed and not an arbitrary bad token.** It is the nearest correct-sounding guess available to a session that has read the widened sentence and taken *"a calibration vendor's"* for a licence rather than for a definition. Section 3's argument says that guess must be refused; this is the refusal being watched.

**The seed targets a `rail clock` row on purpose.** Any declaring row would produce the same finding and would prove less. The rows this ruling touched are the ones a slide would move first, and seeding one of those says the widening reached the definition and stopped there.

**It is derived rather than literal**, on the lesson the case above it in the file already carries: a seed naming `simulation_runs.calibration_observed_at` by literal would go stale the day that column is superseded, and it would go stale **silently**, planting nothing while still reporting a run. The anchor throws if no row declares `rail clock`, which is [session 139](../sessions/2026-08-22-session-139.md)'s finding that an empty anchor starves a seed.

Scope cases go 55 to 56. `node scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs` reports all 28 gates passing clean and failing dirty.

### 8. Consequences

- **[`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) carries the widened `rail clock` definition**, a `CI-06m` note line, and the definitions printed with both unit findings. `UNIT_TOKENS` still holds exactly three members and `unitDeclared` is unchanged.
- **[`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) carries `CI-06m/widened-definition-is-not-a-fourth-token`**, in the same commit as the gate change.
- **[`simulation_runs`](../architecture/data-model/simulation_runs.md)'s `calibration_observed_at` row keeps the token it declared and cites this ADR** in place of session 120's open argument. The declaration does not move; the reason it is correct stops being a question.
- **`OI-28` is closed by this ruling and its row is not edited here.** That row lives in [DELTA_MANIFEST](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md) section 16, which is outside this session's fence, so it still reads `open` until a session holding that file amends it. **Stated rather than left to be discovered**, because one item with a closing ruling in one file and an `open` row in another is [ADR-074](ADR-074.md)'s subject in miniature.
- **No `SD-nn`, no migration, no new `CI-06` letter and no engine change.** `0047` stays free and `x` stays unclaimed.
- **ADR-042 is not superseded and its two-unit ruling is untouched.** That ruling governs **an obligation Merit binds itself to**, and it still says trading days or wall-clock hours and nothing else. `rail clock` was never one of those two: it is the token for a date that is not Merit's obligation at all, and widening it does not reach the ruling above it.

- **Founder approval: PENDING.** **Unsigned by design rather than by omission.** This amends the definition inside a frozen `accepted` ADR, which is not taken on a session's authority ([E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md), [ADR-003](ADR-003.md)).

  **One checkable clause, carrying the direction the widening could fail in rather than the direction it was written for:** *the vocabulary is still exactly three tokens after the widening, `vendor clock` is refused by `CI-06m` on a row that declares `rail clock`, and the same 54 `date` columns resolve to the same 34, 18 and 2 before and after.* **Cost if wrong: check 3 degrades from "the unit is declared" to "some words about time are present" across all 54 `date` columns, including the money-path ones ADR-042 wired this gate for, and it degrades quietly, because a gate that has stopped discriminating reports exactly like one that found nothing wrong.**

  **Every load-bearing claim above was executed or read at its source in this session.** The two token counts are two runs of `gates.mjs check` on this tree rather than one run and an inference. The refusal in section 7 is quoted from the harness output rather than predicted from the message template. `ADR-082` was verified free against [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) before it was claimed, `081` being the last claimed row and no file under `docs/`, `scripts/` or `packages/` citing `082`. **The three mechanisms named in section 3 were read in ADR-042 itself** rather than recalled, which is the [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) caution that the reconciliation session's three worst errors were each a failure to check a claim against its primary source.
