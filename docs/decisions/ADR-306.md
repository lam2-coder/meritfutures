## ADR-306: `subject()` has three legs, and the two that cannot answer refuse by name instead of the member refusing wholesale (2026-09-03, status: proposed)

**[ADR-287](ADR-287.md) slice 4 is built.** [`PayoutTx.subject(accountId)`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) refused on ONE LINE, `subject: () => Promise.reject(new PayoutBackendUnwired('subject'))`, under a comment naming ADR-287 slices **4 AND 5 together**. Three of its four legs are buildable on this tree with no ruling owed and no vendor, and this row builds them: **the `null` arm, `gates`, and `plan` up to but not including the size decode.**

**THE MEMBER STILL CANNOT ANSWER, AND WHAT CHANGED IS WHICH WORD IT REFUSES WITH.** A blanket rejection made a member nobody had started indistinguishable from a member three quarters built, and that cost a session: [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `303` and [STATE](../STATE.md)'s last `Next` both named **slice 5** as what follows while **slice 4 had never been built**, so a session dispatched to slice 5 would have been asked to COMPLETE legs that did not exist. The refusal now names `subject.state` and `subject.plan.size`, which is the remainder ADR-287 section 7 sizes as slice 5, and it names **both** rather than whichever one the body reaches first.

**NOTHING IS STUBBED AND THE RULE IS NOW STRUCTURAL RATHER THAN STATED.** The comment this diff replaced said why: *"a synthesised `RuleState` is a payout basis nobody computed."* `SubjectLeg<T>` has no arm that carries a partial value, so a later session cannot fill one in without changing the type, and the member's `return` reads every leg's `value` or none of them.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `306`, branch `claude/subject-has-three-legs`. **MONEY PATH. `ADR-003` STRICT. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) read is OWED.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** **NO MIGRATION IS WRITTEN AND NO NUMBER IS RESERVED**; `packages/db/migrations/**` is untouched.

- **NOTHING IS WIRED AND THE COUNTS DO NOT MOVE: TEN WIRED OF TWENTY-FOUR DECLARED, FOURTEEN BLOCKED.** [`start.ts`](../../apps/api/src/start.ts) still does not call `usePayoutBackend`, asserted at [`rule-state-producibility.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/rule-state-producibility.test.ts) before and after this diff. Installation is ADR-287 slice 9 behind a founder read and is not this row's.

- **NO ROUTE BEHAVIOUR CHANGED.** [`routes/payouts.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) is untouched. `decidePayout` still calls `tx.subject(accountId)`, and what it receives changed from a 503 naming the port to a 503 naming two legs, or to `null`, or to the 500 an unreadable row has always earned.

### 1. The four legs, and which three this row built

`PayoutSubject` ([`payouts.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts)) carries three fields and the method's `null` arm is a fourth thing to build. Every verdict below was re-derived at its primary source in this session rather than carried from ADR-287.

| Leg | Verdict | Source it is built out of |
|---|---|---|
| The `null` arm | **BUILT** | `handle.rowAt('accounts', { id: accountId })`. `accounts` is scope class `owned` on `identity_id`, so an empty read is a foreign account or an absent one and section 1's 404 is the answer to both |
| `gates: ExternalGates` | **BUILT** | `resolveExternalGates` ([`external-gates.ts:374`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/external-gates.ts)) over five raw columns and two whole chains |
| `plan: ResolvedPlan` | **BUILT UP TO THE SIZE DECODE, THEN REFUSED** | `accounts.plan_version_id` read on the catalogue door, `rules` decoded by `decodePlanRules` ([`rules-codec.ts:472`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/rules-codec.ts)). `resolvePlan`'s second argument has no decoder anywhere |
| `state: RuleState` | **REFUSED BY NAME** | Nothing. Section 3 |

**THE GATES READS ARE `eligible-next-7d.ts`'s READS, DELIBERATELY.** `gatesOf` ([`eligible-next-7d.ts:657`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/eligible-next-7d.ts)) already makes this call in this deployable, and this body is its shape: every value handed over is the **raw column**, because narrowing `accounts.status` here would be a second place the seven-versus-six question is answered and `external-gates.ts` exists to hold it in one. The kyc read is the **whole chain** and not the head, because `SD-M19-01` makes a re-verification a NEW ROW pointing at the one it supersedes. The payout read is **every row** with no status filter, because a filter here would be another copy of `payout_requests_no_in_flight_uq`'s predicate with nothing comparing the two.

**NEITHER READ NAMES A TENANCY COLUMN AND NEITHER NEEDS TO.** `kycVerifications` and `payoutRequests` are both `owned` on `identity_id` ([`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)), so the handle's own predicate is the identity conjunct; `rowsWhere('payoutRequests', { accountId })` narrows to the SUBJECT ACCOUNT and nothing else, which is `R-38` at the account on [ADR-254](ADR-254.md). `identities` is `root` on `id`, so `rows('identities')` is the caller's own row.

**THE IDENTITY ROW IS READ AGAIN RATHER THAN CARRIED FROM `identityStatus()`, AND THAT IS ADR-140's ORDERING KEPT.** That member's docblock forbids memoisation because a value cached across two members would make the order an accident of which one ran first, which is `INV-M5-23`'s placement argument. One transaction that calls both therefore reads `identities` twice, and a case asserts the count is exactly two.

### 2. `plan`, and why the cut is AT the decode and not one read earlier

**THE HALF THAT LANDED IS [ADR-283](ADR-283.md)'s.** `plan_versions.rules` is `jsonb NOT NULL` ([`schema.ts:400`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts)), the row is reachable because `CATALOG_TABLE_KEYS` ([`scoped-db.ts:3392`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)) carries `planVersions`, and `catalogRowAt` now returns the row `schema.ts` declares rather than `unknown` ([ADR-303](ADR-303.md), [`scoped-db.ts:3666`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)). So the pinned version is read on the payout transaction and its blob is decoded by the ENGINE's decoder. **`PayoutSubject.plan`'s own docblock forbids a fourth transcription of that blob in this deployable** (`FM-16` on the money path, and [ADR-269](ADR-269.md) refused exactly that for `readLiability` one port over), so `decodePlanRules` is imported and a case asserts this file contains no rules-document key at all.

**THE HALF THAT DID NOT IS THE SIZE ROW'S DECODING, AND IT IS SLICE 5's.** `resolvePlan(rules, size)` takes a decoded `PlanVersionSizeRow`, `plan_version_sizes.payout_cap_schedule_cents` is itself `jsonb` holding cents, and a census finds no decoder for it anywhere. ADR-283 section 5 measured it and declined to take it, because the two readers that exist read two DIFFERENT sources under two different key spellings, so merging them is a ruling rather than a transcription. **NO CAST STANDS IN FOR IT**: a `PlanVersionSizeRow` asserted onto an untyped row is a payout basis nobody checked, and it is worse than a refusal because it looks like a decode.

**AND THE SIZE ROW IS NOT READ EITHER, WHICH IS THIS ROW'S ONE DISCRETIONARY CALL.** The row's wording is *"up to but not including the size decode"*, and the read sits inside that boundary on a literal reading. It is left out, on this argument: `catalogRowAt` answers a row **or `undefined`**, and what an absent `plan_version_sizes` row MEANS on this door -- an account pinned to a size its own plan version does not publish -- is a refusal rule nobody has written. Writing one here would settle half of slice 5's question inside an adapter, which is this port's entire history. **The rules decode is different in kind and that is why it runs:** its decoder EXISTS and is ruled, so calling it applies a control that this tree already has.

**THE DECODED VALUE IS DISCARDED AND THE DECODE IS STILL THE POINT.** Nothing consumes `PlanRulesJson` until `resolvePlan` can be called, so what the line buys is the **refusal**: a `rules` document this build cannot read stops on the account's own transaction rather than on the day slice 5 installs a fold over it for the first time. A case seeds `schema_version: 2` and watches `PlanRulesCodecError` escape, so a session that deletes the call as dead code goes red.

### 3. `state`, and why refusing it is ruled rather than preferred

**BOTH DOORS `state` NEEDS EXIST, AND IT IS STILL NOT THIS ROW's.** `PayoutSubject.state`'s docblock requires a backend implementing `subject()` to **call `ruleStateOn`** ([`rule-state-reader.ts`](../../apps/api/src/rule-state-reader.ts)) and **not to fold a state in the request path**, on `INV-M5-02` and [ADR-239](ADR-239.md): the API reads the state the WORKER wrote, and a request-path fold is the divergence [ADR-026](ADR-026.md) `C-07`'s `state_hash` exists to make detectable, computed on the one path no replay audit reads. The day is `ScopedTx.lastClosedTradingDay()` on [ADR-268](ADR-268.md) and never a calendar folded here.

**WHAT IS ACTUALLY MISSING IS AN ORDERING PLUS THE READER'S OWN ARGUMENTS, AND THAT IS A ROW.** `ruleStateOn` raises `RuleStateAbsent` for a day the nightly fold has not closed, and [ADR-285](ADR-285.md) ruling 4 constrains where that refusal may escape from: the ownership answer is FIRST, so `RuleStateAbsent` may only leave this member for an account the handle can already see. Building the leg means placing that read against that rule and against [ADR-285](ADR-285.md)'s `stateNotFolded` arm, which is slice 5's stop condition and not a line this row can add honestly.

**THE REFUSAL IS ASSERTED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** A case reads the table keys this member touched and requires that `ruleStates`, `tradingCalendar` and `accountDays` are **not among them**, so a session that builds `state` by folding either goes red here and not only on review.

### 4. Why both unbuilt legs refuse in ONE throw

**THE SHAPE IS `resolveExternalGates`'s AND IT IS THE SAME SHAPE FOR THE SAME REASON.** That function evaluates every leg before any of them refuses, and its own comment says the order is the claim: `R-41` conjoins all five, so the useful report is the whole set at once rather than whichever column was read first. `subject()` has the identical problem one level up. **A member that stopped at the first unbuilt leg would tell the next session the remainder is one item when it is two**, which is precisely the failure row `306` was dispatched to repair.

So the body resolves what it can, collects the members that cannot answer, and throws once:

```
PayoutBackend.subject.state and subject.plan.size is not wired. ...
```

**IT IS A `PayoutBackendUnwired`, SO THE ROUTE ANSWERS 503 AND NOT 500.** `unwiredOrThrow` catches that class and rethrows every other, and an unbuilt member is the contract's *"dependency down, safe to retry"*. **The two other refusal paths keep their own statuses**: a `PayoutRowError` and an `ExternalGatesRefusal` are both **uncaught**, so a column outside its own enum stays a 500, which is `RuleStateUnreadable`'s ruling applied one door over. No retry fixes Merit's records disagreeing with Merit's schema.

**AND EVERY BUILT LEG RUNS BEFORE EITHER REFUSAL, WHICH IS A PROPERTY AND NOT AN ARRANGEMENT.** A 503 thrown before the gates resolved would MASK an `ExternalGatesRefusal` or a `PlanRulesCodecError` on that account, and slice 5 would then be installing two readers that had never once run against a real row.

### 5. The stop condition, and how the read order proves it

**THE THREE LEGS ANSWER, AND THE PROOF IS THE RECORDED SEQUENCE OF READS RATHER THAN A RETURNED VALUE.** The member cannot return a `PayoutSubject` while two legs are unbuilt, so no case can assert the resolved `ExternalGates` record directly. What a case CAN assert is that the whole sequence ran, in order, and the ordering carries the proof:

| # | Read | Reachable only if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `rowAt('accounts', { id })` | -- |
| 2 | `rows('identities')` | the account resolved |
| 3 | `rows('kycVerifications')` | -- |
| 4 | `rowsWhere('payoutRequests', { accountId })` | -- |
| 5 | `catalogRowAt('planVersions', { id })` | **`resolveExternalGates` RETURNED a record rather than refusing** |
| -- | the refusal naming both legs | **`decodePlanRules` returned too** |

**AND THE RECORD'S OWN FIVE MEMBERS ARE NOT RE-ASSERTED HERE, DELIBERATELY.** That is `external-gates.ts`'s property and is asserted in its own package's suite; a case here that restated it would be agreeing with its own fixture, which is [ADR-084](ADR-084.md) section 7's defect and the thing [`db-recorder.ts`](../../apps/api/test/db-recorder.ts) names in its own header as what it must never become.

**FOUR SEEDED DEFECTS WERE WATCHED RED AND RESTORED.** Replacing the gates call with a literal record reds **9** cases; deleting the rules decode reds **2**; collapsing the member back to the one-line blanket refusal reds **16**; moving the `identities` read ahead of the account read reds **4**. Each was restored and the pair of suites is green at **79** cases.

### 6. The one file outside the stated fence, and the one this row refused to touch

Row `306`'s fence is [`payout-backend.ts`](../../apps/api/src/payout-backend.ts) and its suite, this entry, [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md), [STATE](../STATE.md), [INDEX](../INDEX.md), the registry READMEs and the session log. **Two files outside it came up. One moved and one did not, and both are reported rather than left for a reader to find.**

**THE FENCE WAS EXTENDED TO COVER THE MOVED FILE, AND THE EXTENSION WAS THE DISPATCHER'S TO GRANT BECAUSE THE FENCE WAS THE DISPATCHER'S ERROR.** This session reported the conflict and was answered: `apps/api/test/rule-state-producibility.test.ts` is inside row `306`'s fence for that one assertion, and it should have been there from the start, because **ADR-287's own remainder is asserted in that file** and no row that shrinks the remainder can leave it untouched. A stop condition is a property of the tree. A fence is only a guess about which files reaching it needs. When the two contradict each other, the fence is the careless artifact.

**THE GRANT WAS MADE AFTER READING THE PRIMARY SOURCES, NOT AFTER READING THIS SESSION'S ACCOUNT OF THEM.** The dispatcher confirmed at source that the retired assertion required `new PayoutBackendUnwired('subject')` to be PRESENT while the stop condition requires it ABSENT, leaving no third option; and confirmed that the replacement transcribes the `insertPayoutRequest` precedent ten lines below it in the same file, `not.toContain` the plain refusal beside `toContain` the narrower one. One assertion became three and the property strengthened. **A ruling that had taken this entry's word for either fact would have been a ruling about prose rather than about the tree.**

**MOVED: [`apps/api/test/rule-state-producibility.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/rule-state-producibility.test.ts), ONE ASSERTION.** It looped over `['subject', 'holdFlag', 'listPayouts']` requiring `payout-backend.ts` to contain `new PayoutBackendUnwired('<member>')` for each. **That assertion and this row's stop condition contradict each other directly**: the stop condition is that `state` and the size decode refuse BY NAME *and not through the member's blanket rejection*, and the assertion requires the blanket rejection's exact text to be present. One of the two had to move, and ADR-287 section 7 and row `306` both say which.

**THE EDIT IS A TRANSCRIPTION OF A PRECEDENT SITTING TEN LINES BELOW IT RATHER THAN A NEW JUDGEMENT.** `insertPayoutRequest` left the same list when [ADR-295](ADR-295.md) built its approval branch, and the case records the move in its own words: the plain refusal is asserted **absent** and a NARROWER one asserted **present**. `subject` now does the same, with the two leg names as the needles. **The property did not weaken.** Before, one assertion said the member refuses; after, three say it refuses, that it no longer refuses wholesale, and that the two names it refuses with are ADR-287's slice 5. A session that collapses them back goes red on the first.

**REFUSED: [`apps/api/test/db-recorder.ts`](../../apps/api/test/db-recorder.ts), AND THE SUITE CARRIES A SECOND FIXTURE BECAUSE OF IT.** That recorder cannot drive this member, in two ways that are its design rather than an oversight:

1. **`Replies` carries ONE `rows` array for every table.** `subject()` reads `identities` and `kycVerifications` through `rows`, so one seed would have to be a valid answer to both, and a case seeding a kyc chain would be seeding the identity row with it.
2. **There is no catalogue verb at all.** `DbCall.verb` is a closed union of nine and the handle offers `rows`, `rowsWhere`, `rowAt`, four writes and `lockScope`. `catalogRowAt` is absent, so `subject()`'s `plan_versions` read is a `TypeError` against it.

**EXTENDING IT WOULD HAVE BEEN A DIFF ON FIVE SUITES' SHARED FIXTURE, SO THE PAYOUT SUITE CARRIES ITS OWN INSTEAD.** The local fixture answers per table, records the same four facts `db-recorder.ts` says a recorder proves -- which door, whose identity, which table, in what order -- and **throws rather than answering empty for a table it does not seed**, which is `NO_PRE_IDENTITY_DOORS`' argument one verb down. **This is a debt and this entry names it as one:** consolidating the two, per-table replies and a catalogue verb on the shared recorder with the local fixture deleted, is worth doing and is owed to a row whose fence reaches that file.

### 8. Approval

**MONEY PATH. `E2` READ OWED.** What is owed is a read of the member's body against sections 2 and 3: that the two refusals are the ruled ones and not convenient ones, that no leg returns a value it did not read, and that the read order in section 5 is the one [ADR-285](ADR-285.md) ruling 4 requires. It inherits `E2` debt on [ADR-303](ADR-303.md), [ADR-301](ADR-301.md), [ADR-295](ADR-295.md), [ADR-291](ADR-291.md), [ADR-287](ADR-287.md), [ADR-285](ADR-285.md), [ADR-283](ADR-283.md) and [ADR-268](ADR-268.md), every one of which is `proposed` and UNSIGNED and every one of which this build reads as a premise.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

**THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS NARROWER THAN THIS ENTRY FIRST STATED IT, AND SECTION 6 RECORDS WHY.** An earlier draft of this block called the out-of-fence edit *"the one place this row went past its instructions"* and a call *"not a session's to make"*. **That framing was withdrawn, and it is withdrawn here rather than deleted, because it was wrong in a direction that would have taught the next session the wrong lesson.** The session stopped and reported, which is what it was told to do; the dispatcher then read both primary sources and granted the extension, on the ground that the fence had omitted a file the stop condition could not be reached without. **Whether these three lines were allowed is therefore settled and is not a founder's question.**

**WHAT IS STILL A FOUNDER'S QUESTION IS WHETHER THAT ASSERTION SHOULD KEEP TRACKING THE REMAINDER AT ALL.** Its needles are, by construction, whatever `subject()` cannot yet answer. Slice 5 retires `subject.state` and `subject.plan.size`, and **by slice 9 every needle it asserts is gone**, so a case written to prove a member incomplete becomes a case with nothing left to say. It can be retired then, or rewritten now as an assertion about the refusal SET rather than about two names in it. **That is a design call about how the corpus tracks partial work, and this entry declines to make it.** The practical consequence is recorded in the session log as a landmine: **slices 5 and 9 must carry this file inside their fences up front**, so that no future session is put in the position this one was.

**THE SECOND IS WHETHER SLICES 4 AND 5 SHOULD HAVE COLLAPSED INTO ONE ROW, WHICH ROW `306` RAISED AND DID NOT TAKE.** [ADR-287](ADR-287.md) split them on a precondition -- the size-row decoder -- that this row has now read at source and found **still absent**, so the split is still real and the collapse is not free. But the cost of the split is now visible and measurable: this member reads four tables and cannot answer, and that state ships. **Whether a payout adapter should sit in the tree in that state, or whether slice 5 should be dispatched immediately so the two land together behind one read, is a founder's sequencing call** and this entry declines to make it.

**THE THIRD IS SECTION 2's DISCRETIONARY CUT.** The row's words admit reading the `plan_version_sizes` row inside slice 4, and this row left it out because an absent size row's meaning is a refusal rule nobody has written. That is a defensible line and it is a line: a founder who wants the read here gets the absence question answered one row earlier, and a founder who wants it in slice 5 gets the read and its refusal in one commit.

**NOTHING ELSE HERE IS A JUDGEMENT.** The two refusals are transcribed from the port's own docblocks and the ADRs they cite; the three built legs are transcribed from `resolveExternalGates`, `decodePlanRules` and an existing caller of the first in this same deployable; and every count in the session log was derived from a runner at reporting time.
