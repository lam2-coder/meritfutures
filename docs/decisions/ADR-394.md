### 2. THE RULING

**THE RETIRED COORDINATE IS NAMED IN BACKTICKS BESIDE THE NEW REFERENCE AND IS NEVER RE-CITED WITH ITS PATH**, which is [ADR-388](ADR-388.md) section 4's rule for the document scope, itself [ADR-367](ADR-367.md)'s remedy carried onto a coordinate.

**AND NO INVARIANT IS MINTED FOR ANY OF IT.** [ADR-388](ADR-388.md) already ruled that repairability is decided by a person with `git blame` and that `G2` needs history the checks say of themselves they never read. ### 11. Where this entry is most likely wrong

- **THE NAME-INSTEAD-OF-NUMBER REPAIR IS A JUDGEMENT AND IT COSTS SOMETHING REAL.** A coordinate takes a reader to a line; a row name makes them search. [ADR-377](ADR-377.md) measured that the named form binds unreliably by position, and [ADR-388](ADR-388.md) adopted it for new pointers anyway while explicitly refusing it as a migration. **This row treats a repair as a new pointer. That inference is stated rather than smuggled, and a founder who reads the repair as a migration in disguise would be reading it against ADR-388's own refusal.**
- **THE `G2` READS ARE SINGLE-COMMIT READS.** Each was taken at the commit `git blame` names as the last writer of the citing line, which is the ruling's test. **A line rewritten for unrelated reasons after the pointer was written would move that commit forward**, and the read would then be at a commit that did not author the claim. Nothing in the three cases here shows that shape, but the method cannot rule it out and does not claim to.
- **THE COUPLING WAS WATCHED ONCE, ON ONE ROW.** The generator copies every session file's index line, so the mechanism is general, but this row observed it on a single line of a single file.
- **SECTION 4's REPLACEMENT IS UNTESTED PROSE.** It was not applied, so it has not been read back at the file, and the row that takes it should re-derive the two clauses rather than paste it.

### 13. Approval

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST QUESTION IS WHETHER A REPAIRED POINTER SHOULD EVER BE A NUMBER AGAIN.** This row found two cases where the correct number was knowably unstable at the moment of writing, one of them unstable because of this row's own diff. **If that is typical rather than incidental, then repairing a coordinate to a coordinate is a ritual that mostly re-arms the defect**, and the corpus has been paying rows to perform it. If it is incidental, this row has made two pointers harder to follow for no gain. **The measurement that would settle it is not this row's to take** and it is a bigger question than the four items dispatched here.

  **THE SECOND IS WHAT A CONFIRMATION IS WORTH WHEN IT LEAVES NO TRACE.** Three rows have now derived that `ADR-337`'s pointer must stay wrong, and until this diff the site recorded none of them. **A verdict that has to be rediscovered is indistinguishable from a verdict nobody has reached**, and rediscovery is the cost [ADR-381](ADR-381.md) already measured on defects. Whether every class-two confirmation should be written beside its pointer as a standing rule, rather than left to a row that happens to own the file, is a convention the founder can set and a row cannot.

  **AND THE THIRD IS THAT THE OWED LIST IS NOW A SOURCE OF ERRORS.** [ADR-391](ADR-391.md)'s figure is wrong, and [ADR-385](ADR-385.md)'s published repair was wrong for three waves, and both were written to be picked up without re-derivation. **The convention is buying speed with accuracy at a rate nobody has priced.** This row re-derived everything and lost time doing it; the previous row re-derived everything and caught a false repair. **Whether owed items should carry findings only, and never repairs or counts, is the question those two data points raise.**
