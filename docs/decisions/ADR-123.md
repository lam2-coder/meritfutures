## ADR-123: the refusal on `accountsAudited === 0` moves into `runReplayAudit`, and an empty book now REFUSES instead of reporting clean  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 5 closed `CI-09`'s replay leg with an obligation rather than a build: *"when the replay leg is built, it refuses on `accountsAudited === 0`. A leg whose activation condition is a seeded world must assert that the world is there."* [ADR-119](ADR-119.md) clause 7 records where that refusal actually landed, and why it landed in the wrong place on purpose: `apps/worker` was outside session 231's fence, so the guard went into the CALLER, and the entry rowed it as owed in one sentence.** This entry moves it, and it is written rather than skipped because the move is not a transcription: **it changes what `runReplayAudit` promises**, and the tree carried a green assertion of the old promise.

- **Origin:** [ADR-119](ADR-119.md) section 6 owed item 3, session **235**. The number was **RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH and CONDITIONAL** ([ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row 123): *"no entry is expected ... reserved because moving a refusal from a caller into an audit changes what the audit PROMISES."* **The condition is met and section 1 finding 4 is the proof**, which is a passing test asserting the opposite of what this entry rules.

- **MONEY PATH BY POSITION and it decides nothing about money.** `runReplayAudit` is `INV-04`'s right-hand side and `INV-04` is what a payout eventually rests on ([B.1](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md): *"any difference halts payout eligibility for that account and pages"*). Nothing here reads or writes a money field. The `E2` line-by-line read is owed on every line this lands and is the founder's.

- **The session's other half rules nothing and is deliberately absent from this entry.** Wiring [`packages/enrichment`](../../packages/enrichment/src/index.ts) into [`checkout.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) transcribes [ADR-115](ADR-115.md) clause by clause; its findings are session findings and are in [session 235](../sessions/2026-08-27-session-235.md).

### 2. THE RULING

#### Clause 1. `runReplayAudit` refuses on `accountsAudited === 0`, and the caller stops carrying a guard for the audit it calls

`ADR-119` clause 7's sentence, executed: **"A refusal in the caller is weaker than a refusal in the audit, because the next caller inherits nothing."** The guard leaves `auditDemoWorld` and enters `runReplayAudit`. The demo runner, the `replay-self-audit` nightly job and every future caller now get the same answer from the same place, and a caller that forgets to ask gets it anyway.

**The class is reused and no new error type is minted** (finding 5). Three guards, one class, three messages, and the message is what says which one fired.

#### Clause 2. WHAT THE AUDIT NOW PROMISES, stated as a promise rather than as a diff

> **`runReplayAudit` returns a report only when at least one account held stored state. A run that had nothing to compare REFUSES.**

That is strictly stronger than what it promised yesterday, and the strengthening is the whole entry. Yesterday's promise was *"a run that compared nothing while rows exist refuses"*, which is `OI-14`, and it left the case where **no rows exist at all** returning a clean-looking report: `accountsAudited: 0, diverged: 0`. **`FM-17` is that an audit which has stopped looking reports exactly like one that found nothing**, and a book with nothing in it is the widest way to stop looking.

#### Clause 3. THE COST IS A REAL ONE AND IT IS ACCEPTED RATHER THAN ARGUED AWAY

**On a genuinely empty production book the nightly self-audit now fails.** Before the batch has ever written a `rule_states` row, `accountsWithStoredState()` returns nothing and this function throws.

**That is the intended behaviour and not a side effect.** An audit is a control, and a control with no subject must say so. The alternative is the sentence [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 5 refused to ship a leg on: green, every night, over nothing. **A nightly that is red on day one because there is nothing to audit is a nightly somebody reads on day one**, and the message says exactly that: *"If this run is over a book that genuinely holds no `rule_states` row yet, then INV-04 has no subject and the answer is that the batch has not written one, not that the replay agreed with storage."*

**Nobody pays this cost today** (finding 6). Nothing schedules the audit in production, so the first caller to meet it is the one that also brings a book.

#### Clause 4. The two guards are ORDERED, and the order is decided rather than incidental

The empty-book guard runs FIRST. **Over an empty book the `OI-14` conjunct is false anyway, so the order changes no outcome on any input.** It is fixed so that a reader is handed the diagnosis that is TRUE, *"there was nothing to audit"*, rather than the narrower *"nothing was in scope for this engine version"*, which would send an operator looking for an engine upgrade or a calendar correction that did not happen.

**This clause exists because the ordering is invisible in the diff and a later reader would reasonably move it.**

#### Clause 5. `OI-14`'s row is NOT closed here, and neither is `STRATEGY`'s sentence

Both are documentation edits in directories this session's fence does not hold. Section 6 rows them.

### 3. The alternative, argued: leave it in the caller and let every caller carry its own

**It is what the tree already did, it cost nothing to leave alone, and it is worth arguing rather than asserting because "the caller knows its own world" is a real principle.**

The demo world genuinely does know more than the audit does. It counted six accounts and ninety-four rows BEFORE the audit ran, and [`checkAgainstExpectation`](../../scripts/demo/world.ts) compares seven counters against that count. A caller with an expectation can assert far more than "at least one".

**The argument fails on the word EVERY.** `checkAgainstExpectation` is the demo's, and the `replay-self-audit` nightly job runs the demo, so today the two are the same caller. **The production nightly that ADR-119 and `replay.ts` both record as unwritten will have no expectation to check against**: it reads a database, it does not know how many accounts should be there, and the only figure it can compare "0" to is "more than zero". So the caller-side guard protects exactly the caller that needed it least, and the caller that needs it most is the one that inherits nothing.

**And the second half is worse, because the weakness is invisible.** A caller that omits the guard does not fail to compile and does not fail a test. It reports a clean audit, which is `FM-17` in its purest form: the defect is the ABSENCE of a line nobody will notice is missing. **A refusal that has to be remembered is not a control.**

### 7. Alternatives considered

- **Leave the guard in the caller.** Section 3, at length. **Rejected on the word EVERY**: the caller it protects is the one with an expectation, and the caller that will need it has none.
- **Duplicate it: keep the caller's guard AND add the audit's.** **Rejected.** The caller's would become unreachable the moment the audit's landed, which is measured rather than predicted: the empty-world case in [`scripts/demo/test/world.test.ts`](../../scripts/demo/test/world.test.ts) went red with `ReplayAuditRefusal` where it expected `DemoWorldRefusal`, because the audit throws first. A dead guard that reads like a live one is worse than no guard.
- **Refuse on `storedRows === 0` instead of `accountsAudited === 0`.** **Rejected because ADR-073 section 5 names the counter**, and because the two differ on one real input: a port that returns an account id and no rows for it. On that input `accountsAudited` is 1 and the audit has something to say about the account, which is that its stored set is empty; `storedRows` is 0 and would refuse over a book that does have an account in it.
- **Return a report carrying a `refused` flag rather than throwing.** **Rejected on `OI-14`'s own words**, quoted in `replay.ts`: *"a refusal that returns a report can be read like a clean one, which is the exact failure the row names."* A flag is a field a caller can forget, which is the caller-side weakness in a different shape.
- **Widen `replay`'s or `BatchPorts`' contract so the audit can tell "no accounts" from "no adapter".** **Not reached.** Both are outside this fence, and the message already distinguishes them for a reader.
- **Skip the entry, on the ground that ADR-073 section 5 already ruled the refusal.** **Rejected by finding 4.** ADR-073 ruled that the LEG refuses; it did not rule that the AUDIT does, and the tree held a green assertion that the audit does not. An entry that had not been written would have left that inversion in a diff.

### 8. Consequences

- **`runReplayAudit` has one more promise and it is the widest one it makes.** A report from this function now means at least one account was looked at. That is the sentence `CI-09`'s replay leg needed and did not have, and `nightly.yml`'s step comment becomes true of the audit rather than only of the runner.
- **[`scripts/demo/world.ts`](../../scripts/demo/world.ts) shrinks by a guard and gains nothing.** `auditDemoWorld` calls the audit and inherits the refusal. Its `checkAgainstExpectation` comparison is untouched and is still what makes a green run mean the rows were read.
- **One green assertion is INVERTED, in a money-path suite, and it is named here so the `E2` read finds it rather than discovers it.** `apps/worker/test/replay.test.ts`'s empty-book case asserted a returned report and now asserts a refusal.
- **A third refusal message joins `ReplayAuditRefusal`**, so the class now covers the empty book, the empty in-scope set and the history that is not one account life. Any caller catching the class catches all three, which is what makes the message load bearing.
- **[STRATEGY section 4.1](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s `CI-09` row is now stale in one sentence** and section 6 item 1 rows it. **It is stale in the direction that under-states the tree**, which is the safe direction: the row says something is owed that has been delivered.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Clause 3 is the judgement and this entry states its cost in its own words: **on a genuinely empty production book the nightly self-audit now fails**, on day one, before the batch has written a row. The entry argues that a control with no subject must say so, and that the alternative is the sentence ADR-073 refused to ship a leg on: green, every night, over nothing. The other reading is that a red nightly on day one is a red nobody triages twice, and section 3 is where the argument for this one lives. **The second thing a read adds is that one green assertion in a money-path suite is INVERTED by this entry** rather than added to: the empty-book case asserted a returned report and now asserts a refusal. That inversion is named in section 8 precisely so an `E2` read finds it rather than discovers it, and whether a promise may be strengthened under a green test that asserted the old one is a judgement about what a passing suite is evidence of.
