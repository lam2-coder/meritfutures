## ADR-074: A definition site is a row or a heading inside a DECLARED register, and `CI-06/identifier-series` reads a written scope rather than a discovered one  (2026-08-20, status: proposed)

**[WAVE-04 section 1](../plans/WAVE-04-fixture-backlog-and-gate-inventory.md) says a gate cannot read a property nobody has stated. This ADR states the property.** It is ruled on [the identifier-series survey](../reviews/2026-08-21-identifier-series-survey.md), which measured all 215 series under `docs/` before any of this was drafted, and every number below is that survey's.

> **AMENDED IN PLACE, 2026-08-21, session 125**, on the review desk's reading of the four disagreements [session 115](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-115.md) reported rather than re-scoped away. **The ruling is `proposed` and its approval line is unsigned, so it is amended and not superseded** ([ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3), and section 8 records every change with the measurement behind it.
>
> **Two of the four resolutions change what this ADR DECIDES and not only what it counts**, and they are named here rather than folded quietly into an arithmetic correction: **section 1's admissible-register class widens**, and **section 5 gains a third disposition** for a member a recorded decision declined to mint. **Neither is taken on a session's authority, because there is no signature to bypass**: the approval line at the foot of this document is still unsigned and it now carries the amended text. **A resolution that had belonged to a different subject would have taken its own ADR instead**, and none of these four does: all four are about what this ruling's own scope, register class and counts are.
>
> **What it costs the gate is stated and NOT implemented here.** `CI-06/identifier-series` is correct against the ruling as it stood; the amendment moves the target, and moving the runner belongs to a session that holds `scripts/corpus/`. Section 8 lists the five edits and the measurement that says the amended scope lands clean.

- **Context:** `CI-06/identifier-series` was briefed as cheap and slug-shaped. **It is the widest-scoped gate this repository has proposed**, and three findings from the survey are why.

  **One. There is no such thing as "the" definition site, because four shapes are in live use.** A filename (`ADR-070.md`), a table row (`R-nn` in M01), a heading (`### AS-M14-01: ...`), and a bold lead (`**OQ-M5-01 (RULED, ...)**`). Taking the union of all four and asserting one site per identifier fails on **364 members**, and most of those failures are correct: every `EC-nnn` and every `ADR-nnn` has both a document and a register row, and [`CI-06n`](../testing/STRATEGY.md) requires exactly that pairing in both directions. **A rule that cannot tell a register row from the document it points at calls the corpus's own discipline a violation.**

  **Two. Taking the narrow shape instead fails in the other direction**, on **349 members** across the 74 series that have no first-cell row anywhere. Those series are not undisciplined: `AS-*` is defined by heading, consistently, in the module plan that owns it.

  **Three. The scope cannot come from the pattern.** The census matches `SHA-256`, the external citation `NFA I-26-12`, and `PRE-ADR-014`, which is prose meaning "before ADR-014". It also cannot see `D0-1`, because its digit floor is two. **A gate whose scope is a regular expression is asserting that a hash algorithm needs an allocation table, and is silent about a series whose members are single-digit.**

  **The `SD` case is the one that decides the search space, and it was run rather than reasoned.** [ADR-026](ADR-026.md) requires a [`DELTA_MANIFEST`](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md) row for every `SD-` identifier appearing under `docs/`. An unallocated `SD` number was appended to a document, `ADR-026` failed with *"cited in `docs/` but has no DELTA_MANIFEST row"*, and the file was reverted. **So an `SD` allocation cannot live in a document: writing the number to reserve it creates the obligation the reservation cannot discharge.** The survey cannot even quote its own probe token without failing the gate it is describing.

### 1. What a definition site is

> **A definition site is a table row whose first cell LEADS with the identifier, or a markdown heading whose text LEADS with the identifier, occurring inside the series' DECLARED REGISTER.**
>
> **Every member of an in-scope series has exactly one, unless it is a DECLARED WITHHELD member (section 5.1). Every other occurrence anywhere in the repository is a citation and is unconstrained.**

**The clauses that carry that sentence are below and each is a measurement rather than a preference.** They are not counted here: this document has already been wrong once about a count it stated in passing, and section 3.1 is what that cost.

**"Leads with", not "equals".** [STATE](../STATE.md) writes its finding rows as `| **`OI-06`. The 48 hour payout-destination cooling window has no storage.** | ...`: identifier, full stop, then the finding, all in the first cell. **An equality rule reports the six rows that exist as six rows that do not**, which is the direction that gets a gate switched off. Stripping is [`CI-06u`](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s `firstCellKey` and not a second copy of it.

**A row OR a heading, and NOT a bold lead.** The heading shape carries 42 series that have no rows at all and is unambiguous. The bold lead is rejected: **a bold span opening a line is this corpus's ordinary emphasis idiom**, present thousands of times, and admitting it makes the rule match prose. A series defined only by bold lead is therefore not in scope, and its remedy is stated in section 5 rather than left implicit.

**"Inside the declared register", which is the whole ruling.** A first cell is not always an identity. `ADR-014` has four rows leading with it: its register row, and three rows in [ADR-052](ADR-052.md) and [ADR-057](ADR-057.md) whose first column is **the source being quoted**. That is `CI-06u`'s dimension problem in a new place, and scoping the search to a named register removes the class instead of enumerating it.

**A register is a file, a file and one `##` section, or an [ADR-043](ADR-043.md) REGISTRY DIRECTORY.** Section granularity is not a convenience: it is what separates `INV`'s definition table from M01's coverage table, and it moves six series from failing to clean. **It is ADR-043's own reading** -- a registry may be split, and a row is not a document. **The directory shape is stated here rather than left to the runner to invent**: `FOLD`'s register is the golden-scenario registry directory, and [the survey's section 6](../reviews/2026-08-21-identifier-series-survey.md) records that `EC`'s and `ADR`'s are directories too. **This clause as first written admitted none of the three while the gate read all three**, which is a ruling one shape narrower than the tree it governs, and it is corrected rather than left for the runner to carry.

**A TABLE ROW IS A ROW WHEREVER IT SITS ON THE LINE.** [ADR-067](ADR-067.md)'s open-question table is **indented two spaces**, inside the `- **Open questions for the founder:**` list item, which is this corpus's ordinary shape for a decision block's own tables. **A parse anchored to column zero reads those three rows as prose** and reports a series with a perfectly good register as having none. Leading whitespace is therefore stripped before the row test, exactly as emphasis and code ticks are stripped before the "leads with" test. **Measured, because a widening that is only argued is a widening nobody has to defend: it adds no site anywhere in the declared scope except the three it was written for**, over 1,084 members. It cannot create a double it does not fix.

**A register must BIND and must be REPAIRABLE.** A corpus document, a package README, **or an ADR-043 registry ENTRY that binds** -- an `ADR`, an `EC`, a `GS` -- or a directory of them. **Never a session log and never a review record**, and the two exclusions have one reason between them rather than two. **A [review record](../reviews/2026-08-21-identifier-series-survey.md) is expressly outside the corpus and binds nothing by existing.** **A session log is the record of what one session did and is never afterwards amended**: a defect inside one cannot be repaired, only contradicted by a later log, so a register that lives there can never be fixed and the pending entry naming it could never close. **An ADR is the opposite of both**: it binds by existing, it is amendable while `proposed` and supersedable once signed, and [`CI-06n`](../testing/STRATEGY.md) already guarantees in both directions that every entry has a registry README row and every row resolves, so an ADR entry is reachable from INDEX transitively. `OQ-P` is the one series the review-record exclusion costs today.

**The search space is the repository, not `docs/`.** Registers already live under `packages/`: `L-nn`'s is the fixtures README, `RI-nn`'s is the tooling README, and `OI-nn`'s is `DELTA_MANIFEST`. **A gate that looked only under `docs/` would report three series as unregistered and would be wrong about all three.**

### 2. The scope is WRITTEN, never discovered

> **`CI-06/identifier-series` reads a hardcoded list of declared series and their registers. It must NOT compute its scope from the tree.**

**This is the assertion the gate is worth nothing without.** A gate whose scope is "every series that currently satisfies the rule" passes forever by construction: the day a series breaks, it leaves scope, and the gate reports 117 of 117. **A control that cannot fail is [STRATEGY section 4](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s advisory gate wearing a blocking gate's name.**

The declaration is a table in [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs), not in a document, for [ADR-065](ADR-065.md)'s reason and `CI-06w`'s: **the registration and the implementation are then the same line and cannot drift apart.** It is the idiom `CI-06u` already uses for its dimension headers -- a closed list, one argued entry each, and an entry matching nothing is itself a finding.

### 5. What is pending, and a pending register that cannot become furniture

**36 series are neither in scope nor exempt, and calling them exempt would be the dishonest move this section exists to avoid.** They are **pending**: a real defect, named, with the artifact it waits for.

| Pending class | Series | Members | The artifact it waits for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Has a register with holes or doubles** | 10 | 156 | A repair to the register. `R`'s 13 doubles and `ST`'s 7 are a coverage table sharing a section with a definition table; `RE-U`, `L`, `RI`, `HO`, `M6-N`, `INV-M6` and `PW` are members with no row; `OI` is section 7 |
| **`OQ-M*`, no register at all** | 20 | 105 | A ruling naming which end of an open question's lifecycle owns it: the module plan that raises it, or the gate-closure document that disposes of it. **Not this ADR's to make**, because it is a question about how rulings are recorded and not about identifiers |

**The first class was written as 18 series over 190 members and NAMES TEN OF THEM.** The other eight are identified nowhere in this ruling and nowhere in the survey, and the tree offers twelve candidates that could be read into the description, so **reaching eighteen means choosing eight of twelve on a session's authority. The class is narrowed to the ten it actually names**, and the choice is deliberate: **an arbitrary eight would be a worse artifact than an honest ten**, because a pending entry's entire value is that it names one real defect and the repair that ends it, and eight entries picked to satisfy a total name neither. **Nothing is exempted by this and nothing is repaired by it.** Any of the twelve that a later session argues into the class joins it with its own argument, which is the same standard the ten met.

**The two member figures the same paragraph carried are corrected against the survey rather than adjusted to fit**: the ten named hold **156** and not 190, the six in the third class hold **20** and not 29, and the `OQ-M*` class's *"~100"* is exactly **105**. The approximation is removed because a tilde in a count that section 3 asks a gate to reproduce is a target nothing can hit.

**The pending list is a register in `gates.mjs` with `CI06U_REGISTER`'s defining property**: *a register entry that no longer names a real defect is a finding.* **So it shrinks as repairs land and cannot become furniture**, and its size is **printed on every run** rather than stated in prose, because a repair moves the number and this document does not.

**Undeclared series -- anything in neither list -- are claimed as nothing and counted on every run.** That count is the honest measure of what the gate does not cover, and printing it is what stops "not in the table" from becoming a hole nobody can see. **Section 3.1's partition names those twelve**, so the figure is now checkable against a list rather than only reported.

#### 5.1 A member a decision declined to mint is WITHHELD, and that is not a defect

**A gap in a series has two possible causes and this ruling had vocabulary for only one of them.** A member with no definition site is normally a register failing to do its job. **`P-M6-11` is the other kind**: [session 112](../sessions/2026-08-21-session-112.md) records, in terms, that it was **deliberately not minted** -- *"a `P-M6-nn` written in application code is a claim on a series with no allocation table, made from a fence that cannot add the row to the document that owns it, which is `ADR-034`'s condition exactly"* -- and the code cites `AS-M6-04`, an identifier that already exists, while its roster carries the eleventh number with the reason beside it. **A series is not defective for a number somebody decided, on the record, not to allocate.**

> **A WITHHELD MEMBER is an identifier of an in-scope series that a recorded decision declined to mint. It is excluded from its series' membership and its series stays IN SCOPE.**

**It is DECLARED, never inferred, and that is the whole difference between this and a hole.** A third table sits beside the declared scope and the pending register, in the same idiom section 2 requires of both: **a closed list, one argued entry each**, naming the identifier, the record of the decision, and what would end it. **The entry carries the pending register's property in both directions, so it cannot become furniture either:**

- **An identifier in the table that has ACQUIRED a definition site is a finding.** The decision not to mint it has been reversed and the table has not been told.
- **An identifier in the table that no longer appears anywhere is a finding.** The reason to withhold it has gone with it.

**One entry today.** `P-M6-11`, on session 112's record, ending when whoever holds [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md) next decides whether the panel table gains a row or the third number is declared to live inside `P-M6-01`'s block. **That disposition is not taken here**, because it is a question about what M06's read surface says and this is a ruling about identifiers.

**A withheld member is the narrowest thing that could have worked, and the alternatives are in the list at the foot of this document.** In particular it is **not** a rule that the census stops looking at session logs: that rule would have taken `P-M6-11` out of scope by accident rather than by decision, and it would have taken `OQ-F6`'s three members with it.

### 6. Does the gate pass the tree as it stands

> **YES. `CI-06/identifier-series` passes: 117 series, 1,083 members, zero findings.** [`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) makes a gate that cannot pass an ERROR, and this one is not that.

**That is now a measurement rather than a forecast, which it was not when this section was first written.** The scope this amendment rules was run over the tree before the amendment was committed: **117 declared series, 1,084 census members, and exactly one member without a definition site**, that one being the withheld `P-M6-11`. Section 9 gives the command and the result. **The indented-row widening in section 1 was measured in the same pass and adds no site anywhere in the declared scope except the three it was written for**, so it cannot create a double it does not also fix.

**`W8` must not have to decide this at implementation time, so the argument is given here in full, including the part that is uncomfortable.**

**The two options WAVE-04 named were "narrow the scope" or "arrive with a registered exemption set". The ruling takes both, and the distinction between them is what makes it defensible rather than convenient.**

**What would have been narrowing to pass, and is refused.** Dropping a series **because it fails** is weakening a gate to pass it, which [the constitution](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) forbids in the same words the working agreements use. **Not one of the 36 pending series is exempt.** Each is named, counted, and carries the artifact it waits for, and the register that holds them fails the day one of them is quietly repaired without being promoted.

**What is not narrowing, and is what section 4 does.** The four exempt classes are excluded **by argument about what they are**, not by whether they pass. X1 is not identifiers. X2 would be a second copy of an existing rule. X3 is a name that exists once. **Every one of those exclusions would stand even if the series passed**, which is the test for whether an exemption is a reason or an excuse.

**Why an all-series gate was rejected outright.** It would report **349 findings** under the narrow rule or **364** under the broad one, on arrival, most of them false. `CI-06a` once reported 109 phantom anchors and the corpus was right and the gate was wrong; **a gate that arrives with hundreds of false findings is a gate somebody switches off**, and `CI-06q` is already declared-and-not-written in this repository for exactly that reason at a scale of 28.

**The one thing this ruling cannot claim.** A gate over 1,083 members that passes on the day it is written has proven nothing yet. **Its value is entirely in what it refuses tomorrow**, and the two mechanisms that make that real are section 2's written scope, so a breaking series fails rather than leaving, and section 5's pending register, so a repair cannot land without the series being promoted into scope.

### 7. Whether `OI` gets an allocation table

**`OI` already has one, and correcting that premise is the whole of this section.** The brief states `OI` has no registry table at all. It has [`DELTA_MANIFEST`](../../packages/db/DELTA_MANIFEST.md) **section 16**, headed *"Allocation: `OI-nn` identifiers and section numbers"*, written under [ADR-034](ADR-034.md) after two collisions in one day, and **deliberately** placed there rather than in [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) on a stated argument: *"`OI-nn` and the section numbers are this document's own namespace."*

> **The ruling: that premise has expired, and `OI`'s allocation moves to [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) as a fourth table, superseding section 16.**

**The argument is the manifest's own.** `OI-16` onward were opened by sessions about engine purity, gate coverage, lint scope and the fixture inventory. **None of that is the manifest's namespace**, so the reasoning that put the table in `packages/db/` is the same reasoning that keeps ten of the twenty-five members out of it -- `OI-16` to `OI-23`, and `OI-25` and `OI-26`, which WAVE-04 allocates in a plan document and says so about itself. **A register whose declared subject excludes 40 percent of its series is not that series' register**, and filing those ten into a schema-delta manifest to satisfy a gate would be filing a finding by where the check can see it.

**ALLOCATION is where it belongs and the reason is mechanical.** Its three tables are in one document *"because each is read AS A TABLE: `CI-06f`, `CI-06h` and `CI-06p` call one shared parser over the first cell of every row."* **A fourth table there is read by that parser on the day it lands.**

**`OI-06` is allocated twice and the manifest records it against itself** -- *"CLAIMED TWICE, 2026-08-16, and left that way"* -- and the repair is not ruled here. **It is a disposition question about two live findings and it belongs to the session that moves the table**, with the manifest in its fence.

**The move is NOT in this session's fence and the gate does not wait on it.** `OI` is a pending entry naming the artifact: an `OI` allocation table in `ALLOCATION.md`. **The day that table lands, the pending entry stops naming a real defect and the gate fails until `OI` is promoted into scope in the same commit** -- which is [WAVE-03](../plans/WAVE-03-duplicate-registry-keys.md)'s rule that a register entry goes with its repair, in the same commit.
