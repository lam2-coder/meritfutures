## ADR-070: plan configuration is first-class versioned config, and the constraint question is not the versioning question  (2026-08-20, status: accepted)

**The referral asked whether every plan parameter is "first-class versioned config, not hardcoded". The answer is that none of them is hardcoded, and that is not the interesting half.** [FOLD-05](../plans/FOLD-05-plan-config-and-designer.md) sections 2 and 3 checked the tree rather than recalling it, and found three states rather than two: first-class, **versioned but unconstrained**, and **absent**. This ADR rules the four gaps, and it rules the two questions the gap list does not contain.

- **Context:** **The per-size scalars need nothing and the audit says so first**, because an audit that proposes work everywhere it looks is an audit nobody can act on. `plan_version_sizes` carries `size_cents`, `price_cents`, `reset_price_cents`, `drawdown_cents`, `profit_target_cents`, `buffer_cents` and `win_day_floor_cents`, **every one a `bigint` with a `CHECK`**. Account size, price, buffer, win-day floor and per-size scaling are first-class already.

  **The policy parameters live in `plan_versions.rules`, a `jsonb` blob**: drawdown width and type, the daily loss limit, the consistency percentage and its evaluation point, minimum days, win-day count, payout cap, cadence gap, split and ladder. **They are versioned** -- the blob sits on the version row, a published version is immutable, and every account keeps the version it was sold under. **The property the referral is really asking about holds.** What does not hold is database-level constraint.

  **And one parameter class is absent rather than unconstrained**, which is section 4 and is the most consequential finding here.

---

### 1. `OQ-F5-01`: the blob stays, and the constraint moves to the write path rather than to the shape

**This is the question the gap list does not contain and it is the one that decides the other four**, so it is ruled first rather than last.

**The framing "columns or blob" is a false pair, and taking it at face value costs the thing being built.** A `jsonb` rule set is what lets a plan shape change without a migration. `M21` exists to make plan and pricing design an in-product experiment; **an experiment that requires a migration per shape is not one.** Constraining `rules` into columns would buy database-level validation at the price of the flexibility the next module is being written to exploit.

**But the defect is real and is stated precisely rather than dismissed.** [`validate.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/validate.ts) checks the blob at the application layer and `PW-01` to `PW-04` warn at publish. **A malformed `rules` blob written by anything other than that path is accepted by the database.** That is a live hole, not a theoretical one: it needs no attacker, only a second write path.

> **RULED: `plan_versions.rules` stays an unconstrained `jsonb` blob, and the constraint attaches to the STATUS TRANSITION instead.** A `plan_versions` row may not reach `published` without a validation receipt. The blob's *shape* is unconstrained; the blob's *publication* is not.

**AND THE CORPUS ALREADY DOES THIS, FOR ELEVEN PARAMETERS, WITH ITS OWN REASONING WRITTEN DOWN.** The taxonomy in the Context above is three states and the tree holds a fourth: **MATERIALIZED** -- declared in `plan_versions.rules` as a ratio or a flag, projected **at publish** onto a constrained column, with a `CV-nn` asserting the two agree. [`0004_catalog.sql:183`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) states why, and it is this ruling's argument reached independently:

> "a CHECK constraint cannot read another table. Rather than push the guarantee into a trigger (which is a weaker control: it can be disabled, and it fires per row rather than per constraint), the flag is MATERIALIZED here alongside every other value this table materializes **at publish**. ... The publish path writes both, and CV-publish validation asserts the materialized flag matches the parent's jsonb."

**So section 1 generalizes a pattern this schema already runs rather than inventing one.** The floor-lock flag is constrained at publication, not in the blob and not by a trigger, for exactly the reason given above. This ruling says the same thing about the blob's *publication* as a whole.

**Why this is the right seam and not a compromise.** It is also the arrangement [ADR-014](ADR-014.md) chose one layer down, in its own words: `INV-21` derives from publish-time validation "rather than from a compensating recompute ... the stronger arrangement because it fails at publish time instead of at settlement time". `CV-11` and `CV-17` are publish-time checks for exactly that reason. **This ADR applies the same rule to the document those checks read.**

**What it costs, stated because it is not free.** A `draft` row may hold anything, so a reader of `plan_versions` cannot assume a well-formed blob without checking `status`. That is a smaller surprise than a migration per experiment, and it is checkable in one place.

**What it does not decide.** Whether the receipt is a trigger, a generated column, or a separate `plan_version_validations` row is a schema question and belongs to the migration that implements it, under [E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md). **This ADR rules the property, not the mechanism.**

### 2. Gap 1, fee-back credit: the cheapest of the four, and the audit should say so

**It is a configuration and a trigger, not a new ledger concept, and the guard rail the referral asks for is already structural.**

`promotional_credit` is an **existing ledger class** ([M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md), [ADR-019](ADR-019.md)), deliberately **outside the withdrawable set**, posted by **`LT-08` only**, and separated from earned balance by [M14](../plans/M14-loyalty-retention.md)'s `INV-M14-10`.

> **So "withdrawable until earned" cannot be violated here BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by rule.** No invariant has to be written to forbid it and no gate has to watch for it: the credit posts into a class the withdrawable calculation does not read. **A constraint that holds by construction is worth more than the same constraint holding by discipline**, and this fold gets one for free.

**What is actually new is three configuration fields**: the trigger ordinal `N`, the amount (fixed cents, or "the evaluation fee actually paid"), and whether it repeats. All three sit on the plan version and are therefore covered by section 1.

**`GS-306` is not padding and the ADR says so explicitly.** A credit rule that fires at `N` on a plan configured to zero and posts a zero-value ledger row is a real defect in a double-entry system -- it is a journal entry asserting that nothing happened -- and **the off case is the one nobody writes**. `GS-305` pins the on case: the credit posts as `promotional_credit` and not as withdrawable balance.

### 3. Gap 2, ladder progression, and the tier an unlock reads

**Completing a ladder optionally unlocks a larger runway or size tier for the same identity**, per plan version, **with the unlock rule explicit rather than implied by ordering**. It ties into [M18](../plans/M18-graduation-track.md)'s `payouts_to_graduate` and `G-LADDER-COMPLETE`.

**`OQ-F5-03` is answered here rather than deferred, because leaving it open ships a cap-aggregation bug wearing a loyalty feature's clothes.** [M07](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md) is already careful: only a **hard merge** changes what a trader may buy.

> **RULED: an unlock reads the HARD-MERGED identity and nothing weaker.** A soft-linked pair does not share an unlock.

**The reasoning is the same one M07 already made about purchase entitlement**, and the failure it prevents is concrete: two soft-linked accounts each completing a ladder and each unlocking a larger tier is one identity acquiring two larger tiers, which is cap aggregation reached through a feature rather than through an attack. `GS-307` pins the unlock; `GS-308` pins the configured-no-unlock case, which is the off case again.

### 4. Gap 3, display-name decoupling, and why this is a disclosure question

**Plan display name, marketed size label and internal size are three independent fields.** `plans.name` exists, the marketed label is new, and `plan_version_sizes.size_cents` is the internal truth and does not move. [M09](../plans/M09-marketing-site.md) renders whatever the config says, which it already does for plans and rules.

**`OQ-F5-04` is answered, and the answer is the one that costs more.**

> **RULED: the marketed size label is versioned WITH the plan version.** It is not a mutable presentation field on `plans`.

**Because a label that drifts from `size_cents` is a disclosure problem rather than a display problem.** An unversioned label means a plan sold under one description can be described differently later, to the same trader, about the same account, with no record that it changed. **M09's whole point is that the site renders the config**; a field the site renders that is not versioned with what it describes defeats that. `GS-309` pins the label rendering while every computation uses `size_cents`; `GS-310` pins the absent-label fallback to a **stated default rather than an empty string**.

### 5. Gap 4, contract limits: absent, and this ADR picks one

**A grep for `contract limit`, `max_contract`, `contract_limit`, `position limit` and `max_position` across `docs/` and `packages/` returns hits, and EVERY ONE OF THEM IS A DESCRIPTION OF THE ABSENCE** -- [FOLD-05](../plans/FOLD-05-plan-config-and-designer.md)'s own sentence declaring them absent, plus the registries and the golden-scenario section it spawned. **No schema, no config key, no plan document and no engine type defines a contract or position limit.** The count is recorded rather than a bare zero, because "the grep returns nothing" stops being true the moment somebody writes down that it did. **This is a specification gap, not a configuration gap**, and it is the gap the referral did not know it had.

**And `set_risk` does not carry them.** Every reference in [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) is the auto-liquidation setpoint at the account's floor. The plan says it in one sentence, at `M02:326`: **"Merit's entire intraday risk posture is one number pushed to the vendor."**

**The three live options were Merit config through `set_risk`, a platform setting Merit documents and does not own, or out of scope with a reason. This ADR picks the first, and separates ownership from transport.**

> **RULED: a contract limit is MERIT-OWNED CONFIGURATION on the plan version, pushed at provisioning and on restore, alongside the setpoint.** **Which vendor call carries it is a vendor-call question and is explicitly NOT ruled here.**

**The argument is about whose liability it bounds, and it is decisive.** The auto-liquidation setpoint bounds the account's loss **at the floor**. It does not bound the loss **below** the floor: a large position gapping or slipping through the setpoint closes beneath it, and the corpus already contemplates exactly that -- `GS-087` is *"day low below the floor with no liquidation record"*. **The trader's downside is capped by the setpoint; the firm's is not.** A contract limit is the second number that bounds how far a single position can carry an account past the one number Merit currently pushes.

> **A parameter whose risk lands on Merit but whose value is set by the platform is a control Merit cannot price.** That is what rules out option two. Option three is ruled out because "out of scope" for a prop firm's position limit is not a scope decision, it is an unpriced liability.

**What this deliberately leaves open, and why that is not a dodge.** [M02](../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) holds at `review` under [ADR-005](ADR-005.md) pending the Rithmic vendor call, and whether `set_risk` accepts a contract limit, whether a second call carries it, or whether the platform exposes no such control at all is a **fact about the vendor that no amount of ruling can establish**. **Ownership is rulable now and transport is not**, and conflating them is how this parameter stayed absent. `GS-311` pins whichever transport the vendor call establishes; the ownership it pins is ruled here.

### 6. What needs no work

**Stated so this audit is not mistaken for a to-do list.** Account size, price, reset price, drawdown cents, profit target, buffer, win-day floor and per-size scaling are **first-class already**, with `CHECK` constraints.

**ONE COLUMN OF THAT TABLE IS THE EXCEPTION AND THIS SECTION OVERSTATED UNTIL IT WAS PARSED RATHER THAN READ.** [`0004_catalog.sql:168`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) declares `payout_cap_schedule_cents jsonb NOT NULL`, and a grep across `packages/db/migrations/` returns **exactly one line for it, its own declaration**. It carries no `CHECK`, no domain and no trigger. **So "the size table proves constraint is achievable" is true of the other columns and not of this one**, and the exception is a money parameter: `R-42` resolves the cap per ordinal out of this schedule. **It is named here as an open item and is deliberately NOT ruled**, because it is a fifth gap rather than one of the four this ADR was scoped to, and inventing scope for it inside the ruling that found it is how an audit becomes a to-do list. Drawdown type, DLL, consistency percentage and evaluation point, minimum days, win-day count, payout cap, cadence gap, split and ladder are **versioned already**. **None of them is hardcoded.** The open question was constraint, and section 1 rules it.

- **Alternatives considered.**

  - **Constrain `plan_versions.rules` into columns.** Rejected in section 1: it buys database validation at the price of the flexibility `M21` is being written to exploit, and the same validation is available at the publication boundary without that cost.
  - **Leave the blob and the write path both unconstrained**, on the reasoning that `validate.ts` is the only path anyone uses. Rejected: "the only path anyone uses" is a description of today's callers, not a property of the schema, and it is the exact shape of every control this corpus has had to replace with a gate.
  - **Contract limits as a platform-side setting Merit documents.** Rejected in section 5: the sub-floor loss is Merit's, and a control whose risk Merit bears but whose value the platform sets cannot be priced into a plan.
  - **Contract limits out of scope pending the vendor call.** Rejected: the vendor call decides **transport**, and deferring **ownership** to it is how this parameter reached a FREEZE with no home at all.
  - **An unlock keyed to the soft-linked identity**, which is the more generous reading. Rejected in section 3 on M07's own rule: it is cap aggregation reached through a feature.
  - **The marketed label as a mutable field on `plans`.** Rejected in section 4: it makes a disclosure mutable without a record.

- **Consequences:**

  - **Nothing in P1 or P2 changes.** No migration is written by this ADR, no engine behaviour moves, and no gate runs today because of it. It rules four gaps, one open question the gap list did not contain, and one that it did.
  - **The publication constraint in section 1 is owed a migration and does not have one.** It is a money-path schema change under [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) and belongs to its own session; **this ADR rules the property and explicitly not the mechanism.**
  - **`M02` gains a vendor-call question it did not carry**, and it is the useful kind: ownership is settled, so the call has to answer only whether the platform can carry the number.
  - **Seven golden scenarios are named and none is written.** `GS-305` to `GS-311` are in the registry and each pins one half of a ruling above; four of the seven are **off cases** (`GS-306`, `GS-308`, `GS-310`, and `GS-311` under whichever transport is ruled), which is deliberate.
  - **`ADR-071` and `M21` are not decided here.** The blob ruling in section 1 is what makes `M21`'s experiment loop possible, and admitting `M21` as a module after FREEZE is a separate ruling with its own approval.

- **Founder approval: GRANTED 2026-08-21.** Given by the founder in session 95 as an **explicit delegated approval**, not as a line-by-line read. **[E2](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) reserves that read for the money path and this entry is not on it**, though it rules the shape of the document every plan is sold under.

  **The load-bearing ruling is section 1, and it is the one that reads as a compromise and is not.** The `rules` blob **stays** and its **publication** is constrained instead, which keeps a plan shape changeable without a migration. That is the property [ADR-071](ADR-071.md)'s `M21` exists to exploit, so the two rulings stand or fall together and were signed together. It also generalises a pattern [`0004:183`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql) already runs for eleven parameters rather than inventing one.

  **What the grant does not settle**: the publication constraint is **owed a migration and does not have one**, and the entry rules the property while explicitly not ruling the mechanism. Contract-limit transport remains a vendor-call question.
