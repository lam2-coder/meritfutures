## ADR-211: one transaction does not make two reads consistent, so the crossing is two transactions keyed by the scoped result -- and the payout port stays blocked anyway, because nothing in this database pins the numbers `ResolvedPlan` is built from  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**The reservation asks whether one transaction may serve a scoped read and a firm read. The answer is that the question does not decide anything, and measuring it is what showed that.** [`transaction()`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) issues a bare `BEGIN` ([`scoped-db.ts:2540`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)), a bare `BEGIN` runs at `read committed`, and **two reads of one row inside one such transaction returned `100000` and then `999999` across a concurrent commit, executed on PostgreSQL 16 against the merged migrations.** So a composed handle over one `BEGIN` buys write atomicity, which a READ does not need, and buys no consistent snapshot at all. The alternative it was proposed against costs exactly the same.

**What actually decides the crossing is whether the catalogue rows can move, and that is where the finding is.** [`0028`](../../packages/db/migrations/0028_supersede_plan_version_immutability.sql) pins a published `plan_versions` row, and **`plan_version_sizes` carries ZERO triggers**: measured from `pg_trigger`, `buffer_cents` moved from `100000` to `777777` on a PUBLISHED version, and that version's whole size grid `DELETE`s. [`ResolvedPlan`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) reads **every cents value** from that table. So `PayoutTx.subject()` cannot return a plan the approval it feeds can be held to, at one transaction or at two, and **`usePayoutBackend` stays unwired on [ADR-190](ADR-190.md)'s standard.**

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. NO. ONE TRANSACTION MAY NOT SERVE A SCOPED READ AND A FIRM READ, AND THE COMPOSED HANDLE IS REFUSED ON A MEASUREMENT RATHER THAN ON AUTHORITY.** Measurement 8: two reads inside one `BEGIN` straddled a concurrent commit, because a bare `BEGIN` is `read committed` (measurement 7) and every statement in one takes its own snapshot. **One `BEGIN` buys ATOMICITY OF THE WRITE, which a read does not need, and buys no consistent view of what was read.** A fifth door in [`db.ts`](../../apps/api/src/db.ts) handing out two authorities at once would therefore cost [ADR-008](ADR-008.md)'s *"reach the database and choose a scope are the SAME ACT"* and pay for nothing. **The number of transactions is not what makes this crossing safe, and an entry that ruled "one" would have installed a door whose only product was the appearance of consistency.**
>
> **2. THE HANDLE IS TWO TRANSACTIONS, SCOPED FIRST, AND THE FIRM READ TAKES `(plan_version_id, size_cents)` OFF THE `accounts` ROW AS ITS ARGUMENT.** That is [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) clause 1's direction rule applied unchanged, it is what the estate already does twice (measurement 17), and measurement 16 makes it exact here rather than approximate: the scoped row carries both halves of `plan_version_sizes`' unique key, so the firm read has no shape in which to widen. **THE CATALOGUE READ IS A SECOND PORT BESIDE `PayoutTx` AND NEVER A METHOD ON IT**, on [`CheckoutEnrichment.contracts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)' precedent at `checkout.ts:938` -- *"the `firm` reader for `integration_contracts`. `firmDb()` satisfies it"* -- and for [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) clause 2's reason: a handler holding the transaction then has no method on it that reaches the catalogue, and a handler holding the catalogue reader has none that reaches a payout.
>
> **3. AND `usePayoutBackend` STAYS UNWIRED, BECAUSE CLAUSE 2 NEEDS [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) CLAUSE 3's IMMUTABILITY AND `plan_version_sizes` DOES NOT CARRY IT.** That clause rules the crossing safe only where the fields crossed on cannot move, and says in its own words that *"a later slice crossing on a MOVABLE catalogue field does not inherit this argument and owes its own"*. **This is that slice and the field set is the whole of `plan_version_sizes`**: zero triggers (measurement 12), `merit_app` holds UPDATE and DELETE (13), a published version's `buffer_cents` moved and its grid deleted (14). Those are the numbers that decide `approvedCents`, `traderCents`, `firmCents`, the cap and the clamp reason. **[ADR-190](ADR-190.md)'s distinction decides it: a partial backend in front of the arm that approves payouts is a worse outcome than the 503 standing today.** The 503 stands.
>
> **4. WHAT UNBLOCKS IT IS A MIGRATION, AND IT IS REPORTED RATHER THAN MINTED.** [`0028`](../../packages/db/migrations/0028_supersede_plan_version_immutability.sql)'s shape one table out: a published version's size grid immutable, a retired version's terminal, the pinned set DERIVED from `to_jsonb(NEW)` rather than listed so a column added later is pinned automatically. **With it, clause 2's crossing is safe because nothing readable can move, and the transaction count stops mattering entirely.** **NO NUMBER IS TAKEN BY THIS ENTRY.** Section 6 states what the migration must do and what it must not.
>
> **5. RAISING THE ISOLATION LEVEL IS NOT THE SUBSTITUTE AND IS REFUSED.** Measurement 9 shows `REPEATABLE READ` holds the snapshot, so it is a real option and it is the wrong one: it is a change to `transaction()` in `packages/db` that every caller pays, it buys a consistent VIEW of a fact that is still movable rather than making the fact stable, and it costs serialization failures on the one path where a retry is `INV-M5-06`'s own subject. **A snapshot is not a guarantee about the catalogue; it is a guarantee about one reader.**
>
> **6. THE CLASSES ARE NOT THE DEFECT AND ARE NOT MOVED.** `scope.ts`'s own `why` for `planVersionSizes` ([`scope.ts:706`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts)) already refuses the milder-looking version in writing: *"A `derived` rule through `plan_versions` is not a milder mistake, it THROWS: `scopePredicate` recurses into the via table, the via table is `firm`, and a derivation chain terminates at `owned` or at `root` or it does not terminate."* This entry adds nothing to that and takes nothing from it. **Reclassifying either table to make a door fit is weakening a gate to pass it, and the argument against it was already written down by the file it would be written in.**

### 3. Clause 1, and the experiment that decides it

**The reservation, the `BLOCKED` map and this session's own first draft all framed the question as a count of transactions.** `PayoutTx` *"runs every method on ONE transaction"*, no `ScopedTx` read reaches the catalogue, therefore either the transaction serves both doors or the port is blocked. **Every step of that is true and the conclusion does not follow, because it assumes one transaction is one view of the database.**

**IT IS NOT, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS TWO SESSIONS AND SIX LINES.** All 60 migrations applied forward-only from empty; one `plans` row, one PUBLISHED `plan_versions` row, one `plan_version_sizes` row at `buffer_cents = 100000`.

```
A: BEGIN;
A: SHOW transaction_isolation;                       -> read committed
A: SELECT buffer_cents FROM plan_version_sizes ...   -> 100000
B: UPDATE plan_version_sizes SET buffer_cents = 999999 ...   (commits)
A: SELECT buffer_cents FROM plan_version_sizes ...   -> 999999      <-- SAME TRANSACTION
A: COMMIT;
```

**The same script at `BEGIN ISOLATION LEVEL REPEATABLE READ` returned `100000` twice**, which is the acceptance case fired rather than only the refusal: the experiment can distinguish the two isolation levels, so the first result is a property of `read committed` and not of the harness.

**WHAT THIS COSTS THE COMPOSED HANDLE IS ITS WHOLE JUSTIFICATION.** A composed `{ scoped, firm }` over one `BEGIN` is mechanically small -- measurement 6 shows both builders already take `(source, conn)` -- and what it would deliver is that the firm read and the scoped read share a connection and a commit. **The write does not need that** (the firm side is a read, and a read has nothing to roll back), and **the read does not get it** (statement-level snapshots, measured above). So the door would be new authority in `apps/api` in exchange for nothing, and [`db.ts`](../../apps/api/src/db.ts)'s own header is the reason that matters: `test/db.test.ts` pins which file may take which value off the accessor, and every door added there is a capability the whole deployable acquires at once.

**MEASUREMENT 18 CLOSES THE LAST VERSION OF THE IDEA.** If the composed handle locked the catalogue rows -- `SELECT ... FOR SHARE` on the `plan_version_sizes` row for the life of the payout transaction -- the straddle would be shut for that reader. `FirmTx` declares no lock method, so that handle does not exist and building it is a second widening on top of the first. **And it would still be the wrong instrument**, for clause 5's reason: it makes one reader consistent with itself and leaves the published grid mutable for everybody else.

### 4. Clause 2, and why the crossing is a second port rather than a member

**The direction rule is [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) clause 1 and this entry adds nothing to it except an exactness the catalogue route did not have.** There, `plansOfVersions(planVersionIds)` takes the distinct version ids off the scoped rows. Here, measurement 16 says the scoped `accounts` row carries **both** columns of `plan_version_sizes_version_size_uq`, so the firm read is not merely keyed by the scoped result: **it is addressed by it**, and `rowAt('planVersionSizes', { planVersionId, sizeCents })` names exactly one row or none. A firm read that returned a grid the caller does not own has no argument position to arrive through.

**THE PORT IS SEPARATE FOR [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) CLAUSE 2's REASON AND ON `checkout.ts`'s SHAPE.** `CheckoutEnrichment` ([`checkout.ts:936-943`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)) is four fields beside the transaction, one of them a firm reader, and the transaction interface has no member reaching it. The same arrangement here means `PayoutTx` keeps the property `payouts.ts:438` claims for it and currently does not have -- see finding 3 -- rather than acquiring a firm method that makes the claim false in a second way.

**WHAT CLAUSE 2 DOES NOT SAY IS THAT THE CROSSING IS SAFE.** It says what the crossing looks like when it is built. Clause 3 is whether it may be built yet, and the answer is no.

### 5. Clause 3, the refusal, and the standard that decides it

**[ADR-141](ADR-141.md) clause 3 is the standard and it was written to be met rather than cited:**

> *"THE RESPONSE IS NOT A SNAPSHOT AND THE FIELDS IT CROSSES WITH MUST BE IMMUTABLE ONES ... `GET /purchases` survives that on exactly `plans.code` and `plan_versions.version`, and it survives by finding 8 -- a trigger in another package -- rather than by anything the handler does. **A later slice crossing on a MOVABLE catalogue field does not inherit this argument and owes its own.**"*

**THE PAYOUT PATH CROSSES ON `plan_version_sizes` IN ITS ENTIRETY, AND NOTHING PINS IT.** `ResolvedPlan` is four members ([`types.ts:390-395`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) and the two that carry money -- `sizeCents` and every cents field inside `funded` and `eval` -- come from the size row by that file's own transcription of M01 section 2.4. `FundedPhaseRules` alone reads `bufferCents`, `minPayoutCents`, `payoutCapSchedule` and `splitBp` (`types.ts:362-381`). **Measurement 14 moved `buffer_cents` on a PUBLISHED version and then deleted that version's whole grid, and the database raised nothing either time.**

**THE FAILURE IS NOT A STALE RESPONSE. IT IS AN APPROVAL THE CATALOGUE NO LONGER SUPPORTS, RECORDED AS THOUGH IT DID.** `PayoutRequestInsert.eligibilitySnapshot` is `INV-22`'s serialized evaluation, **append-only once written** ([`routes/payouts.ts:385-386`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts)). So a grid that moved between the scoped read and the firm read does not produce a transient wrong number: it produces a permanent, auditable-looking record whose stated basis is a plan version that never carried those figures. **That is worse than a wrong answer, because it is a wrong answer that documents itself.**

**AND IT IS EXACTLY WHAT [ADR-190](ADR-190.md) SEPARATES.** That entry's ground is that a route answering honestly about what it cannot do beats one answering plausibly about what it has not got. `usePayoutBackend`'s two constructible members make the temptation concrete and the `BLOCKED` map already names it: `listPayouts` and `idempotency` are both buildable today (`payoutRequests` is `owned` at `scope.ts:1056`; `databaseIdempotencyStore` exists at [`idempotency-store.ts:144`](../../apps/api/src/idempotency-store.ts)), **and installing them beside a `transact` whose `subject()` cannot be trusted would put a live-looking route in front of the arm that approves payouts.** The 503 is the correct answer and it is the answer today.

**THIS IS A SUCCESSFUL SESSION AND NOT A FAILED ONE**, on the dispatch protocol's own terms: establishing precisely why something cannot be built is the deliverable, and what changed is that the reason is now a measurement rather than a type fact that pointed at the wrong table.

### 6. Clause 4, REPORTED and not minted: what the migration must do

**No number is reserved and none is taken. This section is a specification for the session that gets one.**

1. **A trigger on `plan_version_sizes`, `0028`'s shape one table out.** An `UPDATE` or `DELETE` of a row whose `plan_version_id` names a `published` or `retired` `plan_versions` row is refused. **The pinned set is DERIVED from `to_jsonb(NEW)` versus `to_jsonb(OLD)`, never listed**, for `0028`'s own stated reason: *"a hand-written list of eleven columns is the same object as a hand-maintained count, and this corpus has now found eight of those wrong."*
2. **The movable set is decided by the publish flow and this entry does not know it.** `0028` had to permit three columns on `plan_versions` and named why for each. **The equivalent question here is whether a size row may be ADDED to a published version** -- a fourth size going on sale under an existing version -- and it is a question about M01's publish path rather than about this crossing. **The migration session must answer it in writing before it writes the guard**, because a guard that forbids the insert breaks a publish nobody has built yet, and one that permits it must say why an added row cannot change an existing account's basis.
3. **`plans` is the same gap and is smaller.** Measurement 15: no trigger, and `plans.code` moved. It carries no money, so it is finding 5's subject rather than this clause's, and the same migration is the natural place for both.
4. **What the migration must NOT do.** It must not edit `0004` or `0028`; a merged migration is never edited, only superseded (constitution `E2`). It must not touch the scope classes. **It must not be taken as license to wire the port**: the guard makes clause 2's crossing safe, and the FIRST blocker (measurement 19) is a separate thing that `0065` addresses.

**WHAT THIS ENTRY CANNOT RULE IS WHETHER THE GUARD IS THE RIGHT ANSWER OR ONLY THE AVAILABLE ONE.** The alternative is denormalising the resolved grid onto `accounts` at purchase, so the payout path crosses nothing. Section 7 costs it.

### 7. Alternatives considered

**A composed handle serving both doors over one `BEGIN`** -- the reservation's first alternative. **Refused by measurement 8.** It buys write atomicity a read does not need and no read consistency at all, and it costs a fifth door in `db.ts` plus the `db.test.ts` pin. It is the option whose appeal is entirely in its name.

**Two transactions, the cost named precisely** -- the reservation's second. **This is the ruled shape (clause 2) and its cost is stated rather than waved at.** The `plan_versions.rules` half cannot move: measurement 10 is the guard refusing, and `0028` makes a retired row byte-identical too, so the STRUCTURE half of `ResolvedPlan` is immutable at the database in both terminal states. **The `plan_version_sizes` half is the whole exposure and it is total** (measurements 12, 13, 14). Two transactions are not worse than one here, because measurement 8 says one is the same exposure with an extra door.

**Resolving the plan OUTSIDE the transaction and passing it in** -- the reservation's third, and the row is right that it moves the question. **Refused, and it is worse than the second rather than equal to it.** It needs `plan_version_id` and `size_cents` first, which is itself a scoped read, so at best it is clause 2 with the reads reordered and one more round trip of window. **What a republish between the two reads does is now measurable rather than hypothetical**: with no guard on the size grid, the republish is an `UPDATE` that lands (measurement 14), the evaluation runs on figures the version no longer carries, and the append-only `eligibility_snapshot` records them as the basis. **And the version where the caller SUPPLIES the plan is refused outright**: an input to a payout evaluation that the transaction recording it never read is not a port design, it is the evaluation trusting its caller about money.

**Raising the payout transaction to `REPEATABLE READ`** -- not in the reservation, found by measurement 9, refused by clause 5. It works, for one reader, and it changes `transaction()` for every caller in `packages/db`.

**Locking the catalogue rows for the life of the payout transaction.** Refused twice: `FirmTx` has no lock method (measurement 18), so it needs the composed handle AND a new member; and it is clause 5's objection again, because it makes one reader self-consistent while leaving the published grid mutable.

**Denormalising the resolved grid onto `accounts` at purchase, so the payout path crosses nothing.** **This is the closest call in the entry and it is deferred rather than refused.** It removes the crossing entirely, and it buys back the classic defect [ADR-141](ADR-141.md) named when it refused the same move for `code` and `version`: two copies of one fact, one of them stale. **The difference here is that the copy would be the CORRECT one** -- an account's basis is meant to be pinned at purchase, and `0044`'s ladder unlock already *"changes WHICH `plan_version_sizes` ROW"* an account reads ([`0044:144`](../../packages/db/migrations/0044_fee_back_and_ladder_unlock.sql)). **It is also a migration, a schema question, and a change to what `accounts` means, so it is not this entry's and not the guard session's either.** It is named here so the guard is not mistaken for the only road.

**Ruling nothing, on the ground that the port is blocked either way.** Refused for [ADR-141](ADR-141.md)'s own reason for not doing so: measurement 8 is exactly the kind of fact a later session assumes rather than measures, and the next hand to reach this blocker will otherwise build the composed door, because the reservation asks for it by name.

### 8. What is FORECLOSED

1. **`apps/api/src/db.ts` will not grow a door returning two authorities at once**, and the reason is on the record as a measurement rather than a preference (clause 1).
2. **A `PayoutTx` will not gain a firm method.** The catalogue read arrives beside the transaction or it does not arrive (clause 2).
3. **`plan_versions` and `plan_version_sizes` will not be reclassified to make a door fit** (clause 6), and `scope.ts:706` was already saying so before this entry existed.
4. **`usePayoutBackend` will not be wired on the strength of clause 2 alone.** Clause 3 makes the size-grid guard a precondition, and measurement 19 makes `0065` a second one.
5. **No partial `PayoutBackend` is installed while `transact` cannot be trusted**, on [ADR-190](ADR-190.md)'s standard, however constructible `listPayouts` and `idempotency` become.
