## ADR-112: the accessor learns to name ONE ROW, and the six writes that could not are removed rather than documented  (2026-08-26, status: proposed)

**[ADR-102](ADR-102.md) gave `packages/db` a write and did not give it a place to point one. Every method it landed narrows by tenancy or by nothing at all, so a caller that must name ONE ROW is refused identically at all three authorities, and [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2 ruled that the missing construction is a PREDICATE rather than an AUTHORITY and deliberately did not design it.** This entry designs it. It rules that a predicate is a TYPED EQUALITY over declared columns rather than a `SQL` fragment, that a WRITE must contain a unique key the schema declares while a READ need not, that the tenancy narrowing and the address COMPOSE with the wrong shape refused by `tsc` rather than by a test, and that the six unaddressed writes are DELETED because nothing calls them.

- **Origin:** [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2, session **227**. The number was RESERVED before dispatch. **The design authority is DELEGATED by the founder**, and this entry says so in its own approval line rather than implying it: what is delegated is the SHAPE, and what is not is the [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read, which is owed on every line of what this lands.

- **Context:** three sessions hit one wall from three directions before anybody ruled it. [Session 222](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-222.md) found it from the provisioning saga and reported that `SystemTx.update` renders an `UPDATE` with **no `WHERE` clause at all**; [session 218](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-218.md) found it from the auth surface and declared `AuthBackend` blocked, which is why every auth route on `main` answers `503` today; [session 219](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-219.md) found it from the idempotency layer and wrote [ADR-109](ADR-109.md), whose clause 3 leaves `IdempotencyStore` a port with no implementation. `P3-n` checkout, `P3-o` KYC and `P3-p` enrichment are stacked behind the same construction.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. A PREDICATE IS A TYPED EQUALITY CONJUNCTION OVER DECLARED COLUMNS, AND NEVER A `SQL` FRAGMENT.** `RowFilter<K>` is derived from `TABLES[K]`'s own column map, so a caller may name only the columns that table has and a typo is `TS2353` at the call site. There is no `OR`, no `IN`, no range and no `IS NULL`, and each absence is one decision: a shape a caller can compose freely is a shape a caller can compose wrongly.
>
> **2. A WRITE TAKES AN ADDRESS AND A READ MAY TAKE A FILTER, AND THE ASYMMETRY IS THE RULING RATHER THAN A CONVENIENCE.** An ADDRESS is a filter whose named columns CONTAIN a unique key `schema.ts` declares, checked at run time against Drizzle's own table config, so **an addressed write names at most one row BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by the caller's care**. A filtered READ carries no such requirement, because a filtered read is a NARROWING of a read that already exists at the same authority and therefore grants no reach; a filtered WRITE at non-unique columns is different in kind, because the unaddressed write is removed and there is no wider method for it to narrow.
>
> **3. THE COLUMNS THE HANDLE PINS ARE THE COLUMNS THE CALLER MAY NOT NAME AND THE COLUMNS THAT COUNT TOWARD THE KEY.** One set, two consequences. The predicate that reaches the database is the COMPOSITION, so the composition is what must name a row: `notification_preferences`' primary key is `(identity_id, kind, channel)` and a scoped caller supplies two thirds of it. **A `derived` rule pins NOTHING**, because its narrowing is an `EXISTS` over another table and pins no column of this row.
>
> **4. THE TENANCY NARROWING AND THE ADDRESS COMPOSE, AND THE WRONG SHAPE DOES NOT COMPILE.** `updateStatement` and `deleteStatement` no longer accept `SQL | undefined`. A write predicate is one of two DISJOINT branded types with one producer each, so `undefined` in the `where` position, a bare `scopePredicate` on its own, and **an unscoped predicate handed to the scoped builder** are all `TS2345`. The third is the BOLA failure arriving through the fix, and it is refused by the compiler rather than by a test somebody has to keep.
>
> **5. THE SIX UNADDRESSED WRITES ARE REMOVED, AND `sqlExecutor`'s VOCABULARY DOES NOT MOVE.** `ScopedTx.update`, `ScopedTx.delete`, `SystemTx.update`, `SystemTx.delete`, `FirmTx.update` and `FirmTx.delete` cease to exist. Nothing calls them (section 1 item 5), and [ADR-042](ADR-042.md)'s finding that a comment is not a control is why they are not left behind one. **The three needs [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2 names are served without raw SQL**, so the one-member vocabulary stays at one member and the widening is not spent.

### 3. Clause 1, and what the two rejected shapes would have cost

**The dispatch asked for the rejected shape to be priced rather than named, and both were.**

**READING 1, `SQL`: refused, and it is the shape everybody reaches for first.** It hands every call site the ability to write ANY predicate, which is the surface [ADR-008](ADR-008.md) scoped this wrapper to bound and the same admission [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 5 refused to make about `executeSql`. **It is worse here than there**, and the reason is legibility rather than power: `sqlExecutor('job-enqueue')` is a word somebody wrote and a diff a reviewer reads, while a `SQL` handed to `updateAt` reads like ordinary accessor use. A caller could then write `sql\`true\`` and reach the whole table through the method built to stop exactly that.

**READING 2, `byId(id)`: refused ON EVIDENCE and not on taste.** It does not serve the table [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) exists for. `idempotency_keys`' primary key is `key` ([`0017_events_and_audit.sql:103`](../../packages/db/migrations/0017_events_and_audit.sql)), `treasury_balances`' is `(account_code, as_of)` ([`0009_ledger.sql:150`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql)), and **13 registered tables declare a composite primary key**. An addressing idiom that fails on the first table that needs it is not an addressing idiom.

### 4. Clause 2, and the fold that makes it true rather than hopeful

**An addressed write is safe because the columns it names carry a key THE DATABASE enforces. What the accessor can read at run time is `schema.ts`, which is a TRANSCRIPTION of the DDL that somebody keeps true by hand.** So the two are folded against each other, in the suite, over all 47 migrations, and the direction that can hurt is the one asserted:

| Direction | Measured | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **A key `schema.ts` declares that the MIGRATIONS do not** | **0** | The dangerous direction is EMPTY. Nothing the accessor accepts as an address is unique only in the transcription's imagination. **This is the assertion, and it is `expect(invented).toEqual([])`** |
| **A key the MIGRATIONS declare that `schema.ts` does not** | **34**, over 34 tables | The fail-closed direction. The accessor REFUSES an address the database would have honoured, which is a refused write and not a wrong one. **Asserted as a PROPERTY -- every member is a refusal -- and not as a count**, because eight approval clauses in this corpus have drifted on an enumeration and none on a property |

**A PARTIAL UNIQUE INDEX IS NOT READ AS A KEY, and that is a decision rather than an omission.** `provisioning_queue_intent_uq` is `UNIQUE (account_id, operation, payload_hash) WHERE status <> 'failed'` ([`0007_accounts.sql:317`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql)). It bounds the LIVE rows and says nothing about the failed ones, so a failed attempt and a live retry of one intent are two rows satisfying one equality. **Session 222's whole enqueue idempotency rests on that index, which is exactly why an address must not be built on it**, and the suite watches the address being refused.

**`treasury_balances` IS THE EXTREME CASE OF THE SECOND ROW AND IT IS NAMED RATHER THAN REPAIRED.** [`0009_ledger.sql:150`](../../packages/db/migrations/0009_ledger.sql) gives it `PRIMARY KEY (account_code, as_of)` and [`schema.ts:581`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts) declares no key at all, so it is **the one registered table of 104 with no addressed write of any kind**. `schema.ts` is outside this session's fence; the repair is one `primaryKey({ columns: [accountCode, asOf] })` in that file, and the suite pins the set to exactly that one table so the next drift is a failure rather than a silence.

### 5. Clause 3, which is where the FIRST TWO DRAFTS OF THIS ENTRY WERE WRONG AND THE TREE SAID SO

**Both errors were found by one assertion -- `every scoped table has an address a caller can actually write` -- and neither would have been found by reading.** They are recorded because the reasoning that produced them is the reasoning a later session will repeat.

**DRAFT 1 required the CALLER's half of the predicate to contain a unique key.** It reads correctly and it makes `notification_preferences` unaddressable through `scopedTx`: its primary key is `(identity_id, kind, channel)`, the caller is refused `identity_id` because the handle supplies it, and `(kind, channel)` is not a key. **Four registered tables have that shape** -- `notification_preferences`, `wallet_spend_limits`, `loyalty_states` and `discord_links`. The repair is the ruling: the predicate that reaches the database is the composition, so the handle's `identity_id = $1` counts.

**DRAFT 2 refused a scoped caller naming a `derived` rule's local column**, on the reading that [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) clause 4 makes the tenancy column never the caller's to name. **That clause is about a `SET` and it stays exactly where it was.** An address NARROWS; the handle's `EXISTS` still proves the parent is this identity's; and refusing it left `analytics_snapshots` with **no addressed write at all**, because its primary key is `(account_id, as_of_trading_day)` and an `EXISTS` over `accounts` pins neither -- one identity owns many accounts, so the caller's half really does have to name which.

**So the refused set and the counting set became ONE FUNCTION**, `handlePinnedColumns`, and the guard and the check both read it. Under the ruling as it stands, **all 60 scoped tables have a reachable address**, measured rather than assumed.

### 6. Clause 5, and what removing a method buys over deprecating one

**A method that cannot be used safely and cannot be removed should be made uncompilable rather than documented, and here it CAN be removed.** The dispatch's measurement was re-taken and holds: every mention of the six in the tree is a comment routing around them.

**The old name is gone rather than reused with a new signature, and that is a choice with a reason.** Keeping `update` with a required third parameter would also refuse every old call site (`TS2554`), and it would leave one word meaning two different things to a reader of a 2026 diff and a reader of a 2027 one. `TS2551: Property 'update' does not exist on type 'ScopedTx'. Did you mean 'updateAt'?` is unambiguous and it is greppable: **the word `update` is now not a method on any transaction handle in this workspace.**

### 7. What this FORECLOSES, which is the part every later slice inherits

1. **A whole-table write is unavailable at every authority, permanently.** A nightly batch that genuinely wants to touch every row of a table cannot, and the first caller that does is a diff on [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) with an argument attached. That is [`job-queue.ts`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts)'s own rule, which [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) borrows twice: a primitive admitted before a caller exists is a primitive nobody can remove.
2. **A MULTI-ROW addressed write is unavailable, and that is the same foreclosure at a smaller radius.** "Mark every deferred event past due" is a real act and it is refused here. It is a filtered write, it is expressible the day somebody argues for it, and until then a caller reads the rows and writes them one at a time.
3. **`ON CONFLICT`, `ORDER BY`, `LIMIT` and `FOR UPDATE` have no shape in this accessor.** [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 5's insert-then-rollback pattern exists partly because the first is missing; a claim that needs `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` has no way to ask.
4. **The addressability of a table is decided by `schema.ts` and not by the migrations.** 34 keys the database has are invisible to it. The failure is fail-closed and the repair is per table, and **nothing yet makes the transcription complete** -- the suite catches a key the transcription INVENTS, and does not require it to be exhaustive.
5. **An address is equality only.** A caller holding a range or a set writes N statements or reads first.
6. **The two branded write predicates are a type-only intersection.** A future session that reaches for `unique symbol` to make them stronger will find [session 222](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-222.md)'s landmine: it type-checks, satisfies every use and ERASES under the runtime [ADR-083](ADR-083.md) rules every deployable runs on.
7. **`rowAt` throws on two rows rather than returning the first**, so a drift between the migrations and `schema.ts` surfaces as an exception on the read path rather than as a silently arbitrary row. A caller that genuinely wants "any one of these" has to use `rowsWhere` and choose.

### 11. Alternatives considered

- **A `SQL` predicate parameter.** Refused on section 3, and it is the reading that hands back [ADR-008](ADR-008.md)'s guarantee at every call site.
- **`byId(id)`.** Refused on section 3, on the evidence of 13 composite primary keys and the one table [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) exists for.
- **Deprecating the six rather than removing them.** Refused on [ADR-042](ADR-042.md): a comment is not a control, and section 1 item 5 measures that removal breaks nothing.
- **Keeping the name `update` with a required address parameter.** Refused on section 6. It refuses the same call sites and leaves one word with two meanings.
- **Requiring uniqueness on the READ as well.** Refused on clause 2's own argument, and it would have left [session 222](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-222.md)'s per-account read blocked by the entry written to unblock it.
- **Deriving the unique keys from the MIGRATIONS at run time rather than from `schema.ts`.** Refused: the migrations are not a runtime artifact and parsing DDL inside a query path is a text transform over the primary source, which is the class [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 5 refused about `$n` rewriting. The fold lives in the SUITE, where [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) put the same instrument.
- **Treating a partial unique index as a key.** Refused on section 4: it bounds only the rows it covers, and `provisioning_queue_intent_uq` is the live counter-example.
- **Encoding uniqueness in the type.** Refused on cost and on possibility: which column sets are unique is a fact about the DDL that `tsc` cannot read, which is [ADR-101](ADR-101.md) section 6's own reason for the same shape.
- **Widening onto [`falsify-ci.mjs`](../../scripts/ci/falsify-ci.mjs) to make the seven compile refusals permanent.** **Refused: the fence is absolute.** All seven were watched failing in this session with the output quoted in section 8, and making them permanent is a follow-on with a one-file fence.

### 12. Consequences

- **Four money-path slices can start.** `AuthBackend`, `IdempotencyStore`, `P3-n` checkout and session 222's `ProvisioningAdvancePort` were all blocked on this one construction and none of them is any more. **Every auth route on `main` answers `503` today**, and the reason it does is the thing this entry removes.
