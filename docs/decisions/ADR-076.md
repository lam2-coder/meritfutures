## ADR-076: none of Tier 2's fourteen needs a second fixture format, because a registry row is a COVERAGE obligation and not a fixture obligation  (2026-08-21, status: proposed)

**[WAVE-04 section 2.3](../plans/WAVE-04-fixture-backlog-and-gate-inventory.md) partitions fourteen blocked rows into four groups and says each waits on a ruling. It is right that they are rulings and not builds. It is wrong that any of the four is a question about the fixture FORMAT**, and the evidence was already on disk when the partition was written: **`GS-083` is asserted, both halves, at [`plan-validate.test.ts:874`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/plan-validate.test.ts)**, in a file whose own header says it lives there *because* it cannot live in `fixtures/`. [Section 39](../testing/golden-scenarios/39-fixture-status-and-blockers.md) rows that same scenario `blocked | format-cannot-express`.

**Both statements are true and they are answers to different questions.** The row has no golden fixture. The scenario has an executable assertion. **[ADR-072](ADR-072.md)'s status column can only say the first**, so a reader takes the second from it by inference, and the inference is wrong on at least five of the fourteen.

- **Context:** [ADR-072](ADR-072.md) closed the vocabulary that says WHY a row has no fixture and left **22 M1 rows on `format-cannot-express`**, calling that *"the largest single queue in the corpus ... one question asked twenty-two times: what does a fixture look like when the assertion is not one account's end state?"* This entry answers that question for the fourteen [WAVE-04 section 2.3](../plans/WAVE-04-fixture-backlog-and-gate-inventory.md) names, and the answer is **that it is not a fixture**.

  **Every claim below was checked against the tree rather than against the record**, which is the discipline [ADR-072](ADR-072.md) itself established when it checked the plan's partition and found it wrong by fourteen. Two of this entry's own inputs did not survive that check and they are sections 4 and 5.

- **Decision:** one governing rule, four applications, and **zero new fixture formats**.

## 1. The governing rule

> **A registry row is discharged when its assertion is EXECUTED somewhere a gate can read, not when a file appears in [`packages/rules-engine/fixtures`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md).**

**That directory is one module's day-fold harness and it is exactly what its own README says it is**: `plan`, `calendar`, `account`, `days`, `settlements` in ([`loader.ts:154`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/loader.ts)), `{end_state, events, pins, note}` out ([`loader.ts:190`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/loader.ts)), folded through the two symbols [`run.ts:34`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/run.ts) imports. **A scenario whose assertion is not one account's end state is not a fixture this directory declined to hold. It is a scenario that belongs to a different suite**, and the corpus has been putting them in different suites for five sessions without recording that it was doing so.

**So the fourteen do not need a shape. They need a HOME, stated per row, and eleven of them already have one.**

### The one thing this entry asks section 39 to grow, and it is a status rather than a blocker

**[ADR-072](ADR-072.md)'s blocker vocabulary stays closed at six terms and this entry does not widen it.** That closure is load bearing and re-widening it is the failure it was written against. What is proposed instead is a **fourth value of the `status` column**, which today reads `written`, `writable` or `blocked`:

| | |
|---|---|
| **`covered-elsewhere`** | The scenario's assertion is executed by a named suite outside [`packages/rules-engine/fixtures`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md). The citation is a **`file:line`** that resolves and whose file names the `GS-nnn`. A row in this status carries **no blocker**, because nothing is blocked |

**It is a status and not a blocker for a checkable reason.** A blocker names *which of `W1`, `W2`, `W3` fails*. On a `covered-elsewhere` row **none of the three has failed**: `W2` was never in play, because the format the condition is stated against is not the format the assertion runs in. Filing these rows under a blocker term is what produced the reading that fourteen scenarios are waiting on a format, and `format-cannot-express` is doing that on five of them today.

**And the status is derived rather than declared, exactly as the other three are.** Section 39's own rule is that *"`status` is derived from the directory and not from this file"*; `covered-elsewhere` is derived from a grep for the id in the cited file. **`CI-06/fixture-inventory` can read it**: for every `covered-elsewhere` row, the cited path exists and contains the row's own id. A citation that stops resolving is a finding on the commit that breaks it, which is the property [ADR-072](ADR-072.md) wanted from a citation and could not get from prose.

### THE ASSERTION SITES DO NOT NAME THEIR ROWS TODAY, AND THAT IS SIX OF EIGHT

**This entry proposed the grep before running it, and running it is the half that matters.** Of the eight `file:line` citations sections 2 to 4 produce, **two contain the scenario id and six do not**:

| Cited site | Names its row |
|---|---|
| [`plan-validate.test.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/plan-validate.test.ts) for `GS-083` | **yes**, three times |
| [`rules-f-context.test.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-f-context.test.ts) for `GS-052` | **yes**, once, at [`:140`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-f-context.test.ts) |
| the same file for `GS-076`, `GS-077`, `GS-078`, `GS-141` | **no.** The ids live in [`validate.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/validate.ts) beside each rule, and in the oracle, and not in the suite that executes them |
| [`determinism.test.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/test/determinism.test.ts) for `GS-071` and `GS-073` | **no.** It cites `RE-D-01` and `RE-D-02` |
| [`replay-determinism.property.test.ts`](../../scripts/demo/test/replay-determinism.property.test.ts) for `GS-072` | **no.** It cites `PT-06` |
| [`replay.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/replay.test.ts) for `GS-071` | **no.** It cites `INV-04` and `OI-14` |

> **So `covered-elsewhere` is NOT assertable on arrival, and the gate must not be written before the six comments are.** [`falsify.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs) makes a gate that cannot pass the tree an ERROR, which is the same judgement [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 3 reached about writing `CI-07` against a tree with no build.

**The repair is six comments and it is the cheapest item in this entry**, but it is a precondition rather than a tidy-up, and it is stated here so the session that writes the gate does not discover it at its own bench. **It also says something about the claim itself**: six suites have been discharging registry rows without knowing which rows they discharge, which is why the coverage was invisible to every count taken over the last five sessions.

**This entry does not edit section 39.** That file is outside this session's fence and the edit is [WAVE-05](../plans/WAVE-05-tier2-fixture-shapes.md)'s first item.

## 3. RULING 2. A replay golden runs in `apps/worker/test`, and the nightly is the wrong answer

**`GS-034`, `GS-071`, `GS-072`, `GS-073`, `GS-074`, `GS-075`.**

**The loader's boundary is not widened and this is the ruling's whole first half.** [`run.ts:34`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/run.ts) imports `advanceDay` and `initialState` and the package's `exports` map publishes `.` and nothing else. That is [P1 section 2.2](../plans/P1-monorepo-scaffold.md)'s structural obligation discharged by the module resolver, and a replay scenario is precisely the shape that would ask for the exception. **Refused.**

### The nightly is refused too, and the reason is [ADR-073](ADR-073.md)'s own row

**This is a proposal conditional on what session 114 lands, and it is stated against [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 5 rather than against a workflow file that does not exist in this tree.** That table rules the replay self-audit **`(b) Waiting, 2026-08-20`, on a demo-world seed script**, on the ground that *"having a subject and having an input are different things"*: `runReplayAudit` reads `accountsWithStoredState`, `storedRuleStates` and `accountDaysFrom` from a database, and there is no seeded world. **A replay golden hung on the nightly would therefore be hung on a leg that is itself waiting**, and [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) says what it would report if it ran: *"`accountsAudited: 0`, `diverged: 0` and exit green, every night, over nothing."*

> **RULED: a replay golden runs in [`apps/worker/test`](../../apps/worker/test/replay.test.ts), against the in-memory `BatchPorts` that suite already builds. Not in the golden loader, and not in `CI-09`.** The nightly is the production job [`replay.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts)'s own header says it is; a golden is a test, and the two have been conflated because one file serves both.

### And three of the six are already covered, which the referring plan did not check

| Row | Disposition | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **`GS-073`** | **`covered-elsewhere`** | [STRATEGY section 3.1](../testing/STRATEGY.md) rows `PT-06` as *"the executable form of GS-071 to GS-073"*, **in an approved document**. `RE-D-02` at [`determinism.test.ts:232`](../../packages/golden-loader/test/determinism.test.ts) carries the locale half in a spawned process, and the demo property suite carries the timezone half over a fold of 45 rules |

GS-071 asks for 250 days. **That is a SCALE and not a shape**, and the file's own header explains why four was chosen for the chain test rather than for this one |

### The three that are genuinely open are open on things that are not formats

- **`GS-074` and `GS-034` need a superseding mark.** `R-11`'s `Computed by` cell reads **"The caller's query"** ([M01:454](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)), and `DailyMark` carries no supersession field *"so there is no branch a superseded mark could take"*. **The supersession therefore lives in the PORT**, which is why this is not a format question: `accountDaysFrom` decides which marks a replay sees. [`replay.test.ts:499`](../../apps/worker/test/replay.test.ts)'s chain test already proves the propagation shape a correction produces.

A fifth member on an existing interface is not a second harness.
- **`GS-075` is not a test question.** *"The upgrade protocol produces a diff report and requires approval"* is Appendix B.4 step 4 plus a human, and `ReplayAuditConfig` carries `detect` and `dry-run` and no upgrade mode. **A golden cannot assert that somebody approved something**, which is `VG-12`'s shape and [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 6's finding one level down.

## 4. RULING 3. The multi-account fold is refused, and it is the one group where the format change would FALSIFY a design claim

**`GS-052`, `GS-062`.**

**A shape exists and it is cheap, which is what makes this the dangerous group.** `FIXTURE_KEYS` could take `accounts:` instead of `account:` and the fold could run once per account. Nothing in [`loader.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/loader.ts) makes that hard.

**It is refused because the per-account boundary is an ASSERTION of this corpus and not a limitation of this format.** [M01's AS-09](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) counter reads: *"The engine stays per-account, because cross-account state inside a pure fold would destroy replay."* A fixture directory that admitted a second account would be a harness whose shape contradicts the property the harness exists to protect.

> **RULED: `GS-052` and `GS-062` are NOT worth unblocking, and neither is blocked by the format.**

- **`GS-052`'s external half is already asserted.** [`rules-f-context.test.ts:134`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-f-context.test.ts) is `R-38`'s test and its comment at [`:140`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-f-context.test.ts) names the row: three requests, *"each is individually correct and the reset has not happened yet. GS-052 asserts the refusal."* It pins `noPayoutInFlight.pass`, `contextEligible`, `eligible` and `maxPayoutCents` on both sides of one boolean. **Its internal half — three requests in one transaction window producing one wallet credit — is [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s atomicity claim**, and a request is not a mark.
- **`GS-062` cannot be produced by any fold, per account or otherwise**, and the previously recorded reason names the wrong owner. [`fixtures/README`](../../packages/rules-engine/fixtures/README.md) says the assertion *"is the identity-level forecast, which M6 owns"*. **The forecast is `GET /admin/eligible-forecast` and [M01:785](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) declares it**, with [M06](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md)'s `DEP-M6-01` rowing M1 as the supplier. **M1 owns the computation and M6 owns the surface**, which [M01:778](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) states in its own words: *"M1 owns no endpoint. It is the computation behind four of them."* The assertion is `evaluatePayout` **projected forward over the calendar and aggregated at identity level**, and neither the projection nor the aggregation exists.

**So `GS-052` is one half covered and one half M20's, and `GS-062` is uncovered and correctly so.** Neither moves on a format change and both would move on a wrong one.

## 5. RULING 4. The calendar record does NOT gain a session open and close, and `GS-030`'s own premise is falsified

**`GS-030`.** This is the group whose input did not survive being checked, and the finding is larger than the ruling.

### Ground one: the two keys would reach no engine input

`GS-030`'s coverage column names **`R-01` and `R-05`** ([M01:465](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), [`:469`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)). [ADR-059](ADR-059.md)'s `Computed by` column gives `R-01` **Ingest** and `R-05` **Calendar data** ([M01:452](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), [`:453`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)), and says of `R-05` that *"`CalendarDay` carries neither column, so the timezone conversion R-05 forbids is unwritable here rather than merely unwritten."*

**A fixture stating a session open and a session close would state two fields `advanceDay` never reads.** That is `L-09`'s shape — a stated condition reaching no engine input — one level up, in the record rather than in the account block. `AWAITING_M01_INPUT` is `[]` today ([`loader.ts:132`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/loader.ts)) and `L-14` exists to keep it empty; the two keys would be its first entries and would be entries **no rule could ever discharge**, because the rules they serve are not the engine's.

### Ground two, which is a corpus finding: there is no 25 hour session

**`GS-030`'s pin and [EC-012](../edge-cases/EC-012-to-033-appendix-b4-battery.md)'s both read *"the 23 hour and 25 hour sessions each produce exactly one trading day"*. That describes a 24-hour session, and CME's is not one.**

The transcribed `session_rule` is `open_ct 17:00`, `open_day_offset -1`, `close_ct 16:00` ([`cme-2026-2028.source.json`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/cme-2026-2028.source.json)), which is [P1 S-E section 3.1](../plans/P1-SE-trading-calendar.md) verbatim. **That session is 23 hours long every day of the year, and both US DST transitions fall at 02:00 CT on a SUNDAY — inside the gap between Friday's 16:00 close and Sunday's 17:00 open. No session contains a transition, so no session's length changes.**

**Measured through the committed generator rather than reasoned about.** [`generate.mjs`](../../packages/db/src/seed/calendars/generate.mjs) was run over the two 2026 transitions [`trading-calendar-generator.test.ts:289`](../../packages/db/test/trading-calendar-generator.test.ts) already pins:

| Trade date | The transition it sits beside | `session_open_at` to `session_close_at` | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-03-09` | the first session after spring forward `2026-03-08` | `2026-03-08T22:00:00Z` to `2026-03-09T21:00:00Z` | **23** |
| `2026-03-06` | the last ordinary session before it | `2026-03-05T23:00:00Z` to `2026-03-06T22:00:00Z` | **23** |
| `2026-11-02` | the first session after fall back `2026-11-01` | `2026-11-01T23:00:00Z` to `2026-11-02T22:00:00Z` | **23** |
| `2026-10-30` | the last ordinary session before it | `2026-10-29T22:00:00Z` to `2026-10-30T21:00:00Z` | **23** |

Sixteen sessions were generated across the two transition weeks and **every one is 23 hours.** The 23 is not a DST artifact at all: it is the ordinary length of every CME weekday session, and the same number appearing in the registry row for a different reason is a coincidence that has kept the row from being read.

> **RULED: the record does not gain the keys. `GS-030` is `blocked`, on `format-cannot-express`, and its citation changes from "the record has no open and close" to "the assertion is the calendar layer's and the row's own arithmetic does not hold on the transcribed session rule".**

**Where the surviving half belongs.** [`trading-calendar-generator.test.ts:274`](../../packages/db/test/trading-calendar-generator.test.ts) already discovers the six transitions from IANA, pins their days and their offsets against the published rule, and asserts the CT-to-UTC round trip refuses a disagreeing pair at [`:264`](../../packages/db/test/trading-calendar-generator.test.ts). **The one thing it does not assert is the session count across a transition**, which is `GS-030`'s real content: **one trading day, one row, whatever the clock did.** That is an assertion in an existing file, on data that is already generated.

**What this entry does NOT do about `EC-012` and the registry row.** Both are frozen and both are outside this fence. **The falsified clause is recorded here and repaired nowhere**, and [WAVE-05](../plans/WAVE-05-tier2-fixture-shapes.md) carries it as an item rather than as a footnote, because a wrong sentence in a frozen registry is what put a session at this bench.

> **Zero of fourteen need a second fixture format. Seven rows move to `covered-elsewhere` today, one is writable in a suite that already exists, and six stay open on ports, protocols, module code and one arithmetic that does not hold.**

**The number that should change a reader's mind is the seven.** Those rows have been reported as a fixture backlog since [WAVE-04](../plans/WAVE-04-fixture-backlog-and-gate-inventory.md) was written, and every one of them was already asserted, in files that name the scenario in their own comments. **A backlog that counts covered work is the same defect [ADR-072](ADR-072.md) found one level down**, where a blocker nobody wrote down was indistinguishable from a fixture nobody got to. Here a fixture nobody wrote is indistinguishable from an assertion somebody already executed.

- **Alternatives considered:**

  - **Build the second fixture format for publish-time validation, which is what section 2.3 proposes in its own words.** Rejected in section 2, and the decisive fact is not the cost. It is that `GS-083` is written and its file states, in a header comment, *"IT LIVES HERE BECAUSE IT CANNOT LIVE IN `fixtures/`"*. **A format built for the five would have to move a working assertion into it to justify itself**, and the one it would move is the strongest of the five.
  - **Add `covered-elsewhere` to the BLOCKER vocabulary instead of to the status column.** Rejected. A blocker names which of `W1`, `W2`, `W3` fails and on these rows none does. It would also reopen a vocabulary [ADR-072](ADR-072.md) closed on the argument that *"an open vocabulary is prose with a column's punctuation"*, and reopening it for a term meaning "not blocked" is the worst available first exception.
  - **Widen the loader's import boundary so a replay golden can run in the golden suite.** Rejected in section 3. [P1 section 2.2](../plans/P1-monorepo-scaffold.md)'s obligation is discharged by the module resolver rather than by a convention, and a boundary enforced by `exports` is worth more than six fixtures.
  - **Hang the replay goldens on `CI-09`'s nightly.** Rejected on [ADR-073](ADR-073.md) section 5's row rather than on a workflow file: that leg is `(b) Waiting` on a demo-world seed, so the goldens would run over zero accounts and report green. **Conditional on session 114**: if what lands implements the replay leg with an input, this rejection is re-argued and not merely inherited.
  - **Admit a second account to the fixture format for `GS-052` and `GS-062`.** Rejected in section 4 and it is the rejection to read, because the change is easy and the objection is not about difficulty. `AS-09`'s counter is that the per-account fold is what makes replay possible; a harness that folds two accounts asserts the opposite of the property it grades.
  - **Add `session_open` and `session_close` to the fixture calendar record.** Rejected in section 5 on two independent grounds, and the second was found by running the generator rather than by reading the record. **Either ground alone is sufficient**, which is stated because a future session that repairs `GS-030`'s arithmetic should not read that as clearing the way for the keys.
  - **Rule the four groups as four separate ADRs, taking a block of numbers.** Rejected. Each of the four is the same question with a different subject, and the governing rule in section 1 is what actually decides all four; four entries would state it four times and let it drift three ways. **One number, `076`, and the next session starts from `077`.**
  - **Say nothing about `EC-012` and `GS-030`'s arithmetic, since both documents are frozen and outside this fence.** Rejected. The blocker on that row has been wrong in its diagnosis since it was written, and a session sent to add two calendar keys would have added them and then found the fixture unwritable for a second reason. **Recording a falsified premise is cheaper than rediscovering it.**
