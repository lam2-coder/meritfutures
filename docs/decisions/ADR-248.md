## ADR-248: `ExternalGates` is external to the REPLAYED STATE and not to this estate, three of its five facts resolve off registered tables, and the in-flight leg has no predicate to read because M01 states R-38's grain both ways  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**[ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `248` dispatched this session to establish what `PayoutSubject`'s `gates` field actually needs and then to rule whether it is constructible in this deployable today.** It is not. **Three of the five facts resolve off tables this port already reaches. Two do not, and only one of those two is the missing function [ADR-245](ADR-245.md) sized.**

**THE SECOND ONE IS THE FINDING.** `hasPayoutInFlight` is not a resolver nobody has written. **It is a predicate the corpus states two different ways**, at two grains, in one frozen plan, and the two readings differ by exactly the population of identities holding more than one account. **A resolver written today would be a route or a worker picking a side in that contradiction, on the door where money leaves the firm.**

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED**, separated before the argument starts. ### 1. What an `ExternalGates` IS, established by reading rather than by the name

**THE NAME SAYS EXTERNAL AND THE WORD MEANS EXTERNAL TO THE REPLAYED STATE, NOT EXTERNAL TO THIS ESTATE.** The row required this be settled first, because if the gates were decided outside Merit then this would be a second `setAdminSessionSource` and the honest outcome would be to say so. **It is not one.**

[`packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts:874`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) carries `INV-23` in its own words: "Context gates (frozen, recon, KYC, in flight) NEVER ENTER THE REPLAYED STATE OR ITS HASH", and `SD-06` splits `gate_results` in two for the same reason, because "they were true on the day and may not be true now". [`:898`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) states the consequence for the record itself: "Context, never replayed (INV-23). Every field is resolved by the CALLER."

**SO `ExternalGates` IS THE HALF OF ELIGIBILITY THE ENGINE REFUSES TO REMEMBER, AND THE CALLER IS THIS ESTATE.** [`:905`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) declares five members: `accountStatus`, `kycState`, `payoutsFrozen`, `reconBlocked` and `hasPayoutInFlight`.

### 2. What a gate DECIDES

**IT DECIDES WHETHER A TRADER IS PAID.** [`evaluate.ts:63`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/evaluate.ts) is `evaluatePayout`, [`:68`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/evaluate.ts) binds the supplied record, and the four `R-40` gates are computed straight off it:

| Gate | Reads | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| `accountActive` ([`:81`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/evaluate.ts)) | `accountStatus`, and the engine's own `phase` | status is `active` AND phase is `funded` |
| `kycVerified` | `kycState` | state is `verified` |
| `notFrozen` | `payoutsFrozen` | the flag is false, at either level, already resolved |
| `reconClear` | `reconBlocked` | the flag is false |
| `noPayoutInFlight` ([`:136`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/evaluate.ts)) | `hasPayoutInFlight` | the flag is false |

`R-41` conjoins these with the six engine gates, so **every one of the five is a veto**. A value invented for any of them is a veto that never fires.

**AND THE SAME RECORD IS WHAT THE WORKER STORES.** [`nightly.ts:196`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/nightly.ts) passes `gates: day.external` into `evaluatePayout` and projects the verdicts into `StoredContextGates`, which lands in `rule_states.context_gates`. That column is `jsonb NOT NULL` at [`0015_rule_states.sql:130`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql).

### 3. Where a real value WOULD come from, and the census that says none does

**THE CENSUS, WITH ITS SCOPE STATED BEFORE ITS RESULT, RE-DERIVED ON THIS TREE RATHER THAN CARRIED FROM `ADR-245`.** Over every `.ts` file under every `apps/*/src` and `packages/*/src`, comment lines removed, the object key `hasPayoutInFlight:` appears in exactly two files: [`packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts), which DECLARES the field, and [`packages/harness/src/trial.ts`](../../packages/harness/src/trial.ts), which raises one member on a value its own caller handed it inside a Monte Carlo loop. **Neither resolves one from a row.** The needle is the object key rather than the type name because a record literal must carry every member, so the key finds a construction where the annotation finds a declaration.

**THE FIVE FACTS, LEG BY LEG, AGAINST THEIR OWN PRIMARY SOURCES.**

| Leg | Where a real value comes from | Resolves today |
|---|---|---|
| `payoutsFrozen` | [`identities.payouts_frozen`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql) at `0002_identity.sql:50` OR [`accounts.payouts_frozen`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql) at `0007_accounts.sql:83` | **YES** |
| `reconBlocked` | [`accounts.recon_blocked`](../../packages/db/migrations/0007_accounts.sql) at `0007_accounts.sql:87`, with no identity half | **YES** |

**EVERY TABLE INVOLVED IS REACHABLE ON THE PAYOUT TRANSACTION**, which is `ADR-245`'s clause and it holds: [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) registers `identities` as `root`, and `accounts`, `kycVerifications` and `payoutRequests` as `owned` on `identity_id`. **So this is not a second `ADR-233` and it is not a second `setAdminSessionSource` either.**

### 4. RULING 1: the three resolvable legs are REAL, and one of them is already written

**`payoutsFrozen` IS THE ACCOUNT'S OR THE IDENTITY'S AND THE ESTATE ALREADY WORKS THAT OUT.** [`admin-source/search.ts:163`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/search.ts) states it and cites the same two sources, on the ground that "an account rendered `false` while its owner is frozen would be a search row saying this account can be paid when the engine will refuse it".

**`kycState` IS ALREADY RESOLVED ON ANOTHER MONEY DOOR AND IT ALREADY FAILS CLOSED.** [`currentKycState`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts) at `wallet-withdrawals.ts:471` takes the whole scoped chain, finds the row NOTHING supersedes, and returns `kyc_required` when the head cannot be named uniquely, "because the alternative is reporting somebody verified on the strength of an ordering this table does not declare, on the door where that means paying them". **A payout resolver would call it rather than write a second one.**

**THIS IS RECORDED BECAUSE IT IS THE HALF THAT MAKES THE REFUSAL HONEST.** Three legs resolving is what rules out the easy conclusion that the whole field is somebody else's. **It is also worth nothing on its own**, and that is ruling 4.

### 5. RULING 2: `hasPayoutInFlight` HAS NO PREDICATE TO READ, AND THIS IS WHERE `ADR-245`'s SIZING DOES NOT SURVIVE

`ADR-245` sized this whole field as "five facts, four of them a column read on tables already registered, one refusal to write, and no migration". **Four of the five are column reads only if `R-38`'s in-flight leg has one predicate. It has two, and they disagree about what a payout in flight IS.**

**THE ACCOUNT READING, WITH ITS SOURCES.**

- [`M01:207`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), section 2.1, the block that DECLARES the field: `hasPayoutInFlight: boolean;` carrying the note `approved | transferring | frozen exists for this account`.
- [`M01:861`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), `AS-01`'s own residual: "None at account level. At identity level, ten accounts can each hold one in-flight payout, which is AS-09." **That sentence is only true if `R-38` is account grained.**
- [`EC-040`](../edge-cases/EC-040.md): "no request may be created while another for the same account is `approved`, `frozen`, or `held_pending_review`".
- **The live `SD-09` index**, which `M01:859` calls the reason "the engine is not the only line of defence": [`0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql:105`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql) is `ON payout_requests (account_id)`.

**THE IDENTITY READING, WITH ITS SOURCES.**

- [`M01:531`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) and [`M01:544`](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md), Group F's `R-38` row and its context-gate restatement: "no wallet-to-rail withdrawal for this identity in `approved`, `transferring`, or `frozen`".
- [`ADR-204`](ADR-204.md) row 11, which transcribes `M01:531`.
- **The engine's own contract, which is how the reading reached code.** `types.ts` claimed to reproduce M01 section 2.1 verbatim and then wrote "for this identity" twice, on the interface and on the field. **Section 2.1 says account.**

**THE TWO READINGS ARE NOT A DISTINCTION WITHOUT A DIFFERENCE.** They differ by exactly the identities holding more than one account, which is every copy trader on the plan maximum, and `AS-09` is [**RULED at the gate**](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) at `M01:969`: "no identity-level extraction ceiling in v1. Visibility, not a rule." **So the identity reading would refuse nine of a trader's ten accounts under a ceiling the corpus deliberately declined to impose, and the account reading would answer "no payout in flight" for an identity that has one.** One reading is wrong in the refusing direction and the other is wrong in the paying direction.

**AND THE STATUS VOCABULARY HAS MOVED TWICE UNDER BOTH OF THEM.** `M01:207`'s enumeration is `approved | transferring | frozen`. The live index predicate is `('approved', 'frozen', 'held_pending_review')`: [`ADR-028`](ADR-028.md) retired `transferring` from `payout_requests` and gave it to `wallet_withdrawals`, and [`ADR-040`](ADR-040.md) added `held_pending_review` as a pre-approval state. `evaluate.ts` already records the drift and says it is "recorded rather than fixed because editing a frozen document is an ADR". **`M01:531` compounds it: it names `transferring`, which `wallet_withdrawals` has, and `frozen`, which `wallet_withdrawals` does not.** [`0001_extensions_and_enums.sql:95`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) declares `wallet_withdrawal_status` and there is no `frozen` in it.

**SO NEITHER SENTENCE IS EVEN INTERNALLY SATISFIABLE AS WRITTEN, AND THAT IS THE POINT RATHER THAN A DEBATING WIN.** A resolver has to choose a table, a grain and a status set, and the corpus supplies each of the three twice.

**THIS IS RULED UNRESOLVED RATHER THAN RESOLVED HERE, AND THE REASON IS THE ROW'S OWN.** The account reading has four sources and the identity reading has two, one of which is a transcription of the other. **That is an argument and it is not a ruling**, because `M01` is FROZEN, both sentences are in it, and moving one is an amendment rather than a line of code. **A route that picked the winner would be deciding an open corpus question in a file nobody reads twice.**

### 6. RULING 3: `gates` is NOT CONSTRUCTIBLE, so the port stays blocked and no arm is installed

`accountStatus` is the second unresolved leg and `ADR-245` already registered it: `account_status` declares seven members at [`0001_extensions_and_enums.sql:47`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) and [`AccountStatus`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts) at `types.ts:891` takes six, the difference being `provisioning_pending`, in that direction only. **That registration is inherited and is NOT re-opened here.** `M01:203` carries the same six, so the engine transcribed its source correctly and the gap is the corpus's.

**TWO OF FIVE FACTS HAVE NO VALUE THIS DEPLOYABLE MAY COMPUTE, SO `PayoutSubject.subject()` CANNOT RETURN.** [`routes/payouts.ts:329`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) declares the record and [`:333`](../../apps/api/src/routes/payouts.ts) is the field. `usePayoutBackend`'s own stated rule is that a backend with one live arm and one that rejects is worse than none, and **this is the port that rule is named after**: `listPayouts` and `idempotency` remain constructible and remain uninstalled, because installing them beside a `transact` whose `subject` cannot build two of its three fields would put a live-looking route in front of the arm that approves payouts.

### 7. `ADR-245` SIZED THE RESOLVER AS AN INDEPENDENT FOURTH SLICE AND IT IS NOT ONE: IT IS UPSTREAM OF THE ROW

`ADR-245` section 6 reads that the resolver "is a fourth slice, it is independent of the other three, and it is the cheapest of the four". **The independence half is false in one direction and it matters for sequencing.**

[`apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts:87`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts) declares `readonly external: ExternalGates` on `AccountDay`, and the field is required. [`adapter.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)'s `ACCOUNT_DAY_BLOCKER` names it as one of the six the adapter resolves none of. `nightly.ts:196` feeds it to `evaluatePayout` and the projection lands in `rule_states.context_gates`, which is `NOT NULL`.

**SO NO `rule_states` ROW CAN BE WRITTEN WITHOUT AN `ExternalGates`.** The resolver is independent of `ADR-239` slice A, the codec, and it is a strict PREREQUISITE of slice B and therefore of link 4. **A session that lands the codec and the adapter and stops has not produced a row.** The cheapness half stands for three of the five legs and is what section 5 removes for the fourth.

### 12. Approval

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS `R-38`'s GRAIN, AND THIS ENTRY DELIBERATELY DID NOT.** Section 5 lays out four sources for the account reading and two for the identity reading, one of which is a transcription of the other, and says that counting sources is an argument rather than a ruling. **You may reasonably think that is too cautious**: the index, the edge case, the field's own declaration and `AS-01`'s residual all say account, and a founder can settle it in one line where a session cannot. **The argument for the caution is that `M01:531` is not obviously the stale sentence.** It is the rule's own row, it cites `ADR-019` on its face, and `ADR-019` really did move this leg to the wallet. If the wallet reading is what was intended, then the account grained index is the defect and the fix is a migration rather than a comment, which is a much larger thing than it looks from here.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **The census inherits `ADR-245`'s known blind spot and this entry did not close it.** It searches for one object key, so a producer assembling the five facts at a call site that spread them, typed only by inference, would be invisible to it.
