## ADR-146: two date vocabularies meet on one object, so the contract's suffix rule stops being a convention and becomes a refusal (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 1 states the rule in one line: *"`*_at` are RFC 3339 UTC strings. `*_day` and `*_on` are `YYYY-MM-DD` exchange trading days, never UTC dates."* `GET /economic-calendar` is the first endpoint in this corpus whose response carries one of each ON THE SAME OBJECT, and the two agree on most rows and disagree on evening ones. That is the worst available failure shape: an implementation that derived one from the other would be right all afternoon and wrong at 23:30, and nothing in this repository would say so. The suffix rule is load bearing and nothing has ever checked it. This entry makes it a refusal.**

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md)'s `146` row, **RESERVED BEFORE DISPATCH, CONDITIONAL**, session **258**. The condition was *"a public calendar read is the first surface that must render that distinction to a trader"*, and it fired. The row is amended in place per [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3.

- **NOT MONEY BY CONTENT.** No ledger account, no balance, no eligibility, no payout. [`0039_economic_calendar.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0039_economic_calendar.sql)'s own header states why that does not make the table unimportant: *"A wrong row here does not lose money. It accuses somebody."*

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. A `*_day` IS NEVER DERIVED FROM AN `*_at`, ANYWHERE ON THE API SURFACE.** Not in a route, not in a projection, not in a renderer, and not by a client reading the response. The exchange trading day is data ([`0004`](../../packages/db/migrations/0004_catalog.sql), finding 3) and every surface that publishes one publishes the stored column. **The failure this forbids is not an outage.** It is a panel that agrees with the detector all afternoon and disagrees with it after 17:00 CT, which reads as correct on every day somebody happens to check.
>
> **2. THE SUFFIX RULE BECOMES A REFUSAL AT EVERY SURFACE THAT CARRIES BOTH VOCABULARIES.** A response field named `*_at` is asserted to be an RFC 3339 UTC instant and a field named `*_day` or `*_on` is asserted to be a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` day, at the point of projection, and a value that is not is a REFUSAL rather than a value passed through. The check costs a regular expression and it converts section 1's sentence into something that fails a test.
>
> **3. AN `*_at` IS SPELLED WITH `Z` AND NEVER WITH AN OFFSET, INCLUDING `+00:00`.** A non-zero offset would be a stored timezone arriving through the response, which is the column [`0039`](../../packages/db/migrations/0039_economic_calendar.sql) header item 4 refused to create, reached from the API instead of from the schema. `+00:00` names the same instant as `Z` and is refused with it, because two spellings of one instant is a normalisation step every consumer has to write and a second place the timezone question gets answered.
>
> **4. A ROUTE READS NO CLOCK TO DECIDE FRESHNESS, AND THE FRESHNESS FACT ARRIVES AS DATA.** Finding 4 is why this is a ruling rather than a preference: the comparison `coverage_end_day` sits on one side of is a TRADING day, so a route reaching for `new Date()` would produce a UTC date and compare two different vocabularies. API_CONTRACT's *"`stale` is the server's own answer against its own threshold"* is satisfied by the SERVER answering, which is the deployment where the calendar and the horizon both live, and not by the route file.
>
> **5. `stale: false` BESIDE `covered_through_day: null` IS REFUSED.** `DEP-M4-09`: *"the dangerous failure is not the empty panel, it is the confident one."* A deployment that has never loaded the calendar has no ground to call it fresh, and a panel rendering that pair tells a trader nothing is scheduled on the authority of a calendar nobody loaded.
>
> **6. NO ROUTE ORDERS ROWS BY COMPARING RFC 3339 STRINGS.** Finding 8. Ordering by release instant has an index and a home, `economic_calendar_release_idx`, and belongs to the query. A route that sorted would be doing date arithmetic on a format whose precision the contract does not fix, silently, on exactly the rows whose precision differed.
>
> **7. THE STALENESS HORIZON'S VALUE IS NOT SET HERE AND IS NOT INVENTED.** Finding 5 found no number anywhere. A route that picked one would have made a monitoring threshold a code constant on the standing parameter ruling's wrong side. It is named as OWED in section 5 and the endpoint renders whatever the deployment decided.

### 3. Clause 1, and the date that proves it, because a test on any other date proves nothing

**The whole ruling is unfalsifiable on most rows and that is the reason it needs one.** Take the example [`0039`](../../packages/db/migrations/0039_economic_calendar.sql) gives in its own words, and follow it through with finding 3's mechanism:

| | |
|---|---|
| `2026-03-03T23:30:00Z` | the stored instant. Its UTC calendar date is `2026-03-03` |
| `2026-03-03 17:30 CT` | the same instant in Central Time. US daylight time begins `2026-03-08`, so this date is CST, `UTC-6` |
| `2026-03-04` | **the trading day**, because 17:30 CT is inside the session that opened at 17:00 CT on `2026-03-03`, and that session's trading day is the next date |

**A morning release is the counter-example and it is why one row is not enough.** `2026-03-04T13:30:00Z` is 07:30 CST on `2026-03-04`, inside the session whose trading day is `2026-03-04`, so the instant's UTC date and the trading day are the SAME STRING. A suite holding only that row could not tell *"renders the stored day"* from *"derives the day from the instant"*, and a suite holding only the evening row could not tell *"renders the stored day"* from *"renders the day after the instant"*. **Both are asserted, and the divergence itself is asserted before either**, so the premise the whole test rests on cannot silently stop being true.

### 4. The third implementation of one webhook ordering rule, and why [ADR-114](ADR-114.md) section 4's condition has receded

**Session 258 also landed `POST /webhooks/rise`, and it hit the wall [ADR-114](ADR-114.md) section 4 already described.** That entry ruled: *"WHAT WOULD END THE DUPLICATION IS A THIRD PACKAGE NEITHER PROVIDER SET BRANDS ... It is not created here ... a new package that `apps/api` consumes forces an edit to `apps/api/package.json` and `pnpm-lock.yaml` ... **Lifting it is a slice with two consumers already written, which is the cheapest moment to do it and is not this moment.**"

**The condition that entry named has not arrived. It has receded, in both variables at once.**

| | When ADR-114 deferred it | At this entry |
|---|---|---|
| Consumers of the ordering rule | `@merit/psp`, `@merit/kyc` | those two plus `apps/api/src/rise-webhook.ts` |
| Sessions concurrently editing this tree | three | eight route sessions, each owning its own route file |

**The lift is therefore still not taken, and the reason is stronger rather than weaker**: `apps/api/package.json` and `pnpm-lock.yaml` are a worse write collision under eight concurrent branches than under three, and a package added now would be a merge conflict in the one file every one of those branches would have to resolve. **What changes is that the debt now carries a count**, so the next holder inherits a number rather than rediscovering the wall.

**The third implementation is a FILE IN `apps/api/src` AND NOT A PACKAGE, on `@merit/kyc`'s own stated test for why that one is a package**: *"IT IS A PACKAGE AND NOT TWO ROUTE FILES BECAUSE THE WORKER NEEDS IT."* Nothing outside `apps/api` receives an inbound Rise webhook; `apps/worker` sends transfers, and the outbound half of the rail is [M05](../plans/M05-payout-system.md)'s. [`apps/api/src/idempotency.ts`](../../apps/api/src/idempotency.ts) is the precedent [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 3 set for exactly this: when a construction is needed and the package dependency is the expensive half, the port is declared inside the deployable and the absence stays a fact somebody can grep.

**The binding suite is the control**, on [`packages/kyc/test/webhook-bind.test.ts`](../../packages/kyc/test/webhook-bind.test.ts)'s shape: [`apps/api/test/rise-webhook-bind.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/rise-webhook-bind.test.ts) READS [`packages/psp/src/webhook.ts`](../../packages/psp/src/webhook.ts) and fails if the refusal vocabulary drifts or the ordering steps stop appearing in the same order. It binds to that primary source and NOT to the KYC copy, because binding to a copy would let a drift in the copy propagate silently, which is the failure the instrument exists to prevent.

### 6. What this entry does NOT rule

- **It does not touch [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md).** Every response shape in section 6.1 is transcribed rather than chosen, and the two debts against that document (section 12's missing row, and no stated TTL) are recorded above rather than repaired.
- **It does not widen `SystemReason` or `SqlExecutorReason`.** Nothing in this session reaches `packages/db` at all, which [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 1 refused to solve by widening a vocabulary and this entry has no reason to revisit.
- **It does not rule what a PAGE does with either field.** Session **264** renders the calendar panel and this route is its source; the timezone a trader sees is a rendering, `GS-285` is the assertion that one row renders correctly in two of them, and nothing here constrains it beyond clause 1.
- **It does not filter `tier`.** `0039` header item 3 makes the tier a column rather than an import filter, and a route that rendered only tier 1 would make the field a constant, which is the narrow import that header rejected wearing a route's clothes.

### 7. Consequences

- Every future endpoint carrying both vocabularies inherits clauses 1 to 3 and the projection cost is a regular expression per field. **The alternative was carrying section 1's sentence in a reviewer's head**, which is what it has been carried by until now.
- The freshness fact becomes part of the source contract rather than something a route computes, so a deployment that gets the horizon wrong gets it wrong in ONE place, beside the alarm that reads the same column.
- The Rise receiver ships with no store and no adapter, so a live deployment cannot process a Rise event. That is the honest state: there is no table for the event and no vendor scheme, and a receiver that verified and discarded would answer 200 for an event nobody can re-drive.
- **`apps/api` now holds a webhook port of its own.** The day the neutral package lands, `rise-webhook.ts` is the file that moves, and its binding suite is what will tell whoever moves it whether the move preserved the ordering.

---

### 8. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED. A source row whose `release_trading_day` differs from its `scheduled_release_at`'s UTC date renders the trading day, and the test names a date where the two actually differ.** Executable, one clause, scoped to [`apps/api/test/economic-calendar.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/economic-calendar.test.ts). Section 3 is the date and its derivation.
- **The second approval line, for section 4: a correctly signed Rise payload verifies, and the SAME payload re-serialised is refused.** Both, on one payload, in one test, in [`apps/api/test/webhooks-rise.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/webhooks-rise.test.ts). Either alone proves nothing: a verifier that refuses everything satisfies the second and a verifier that checks nothing satisfies the first.
- **NOT MONEY BY CONTENT**, and the E2 read is not claimed by this entry. `payout_transfers` is not written by anything this session landed, and the applier that would write it is declared as a seam and left unimplemented.
- **Clause 3 is the one a later session will want to relax**, because refusing `+00:00` refuses a legal RFC 3339 spelling of the right instant. Section 2 clause 3 is why it is refused anyway.

**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.** Three of the decisions here are judgements rather than derivations. **Clause 3, refusing `+00:00`,** is named above as the one a later session will want to relax, and it refuses a legal RFC 3339 spelling of the right instant on the ground that two spellings of one moment is a normalisation step every consumer writes and a second place the timezone question gets answered. Nothing breaks under the looser rule today. **Clause 5, refusing `stale: false` beside a null coverage day,** decides that a deployment which never loaded the calendar has no ground to call it fresh, and it turns a contract shape into a refusal on the strength of one sentence in a plan about the confident failure being the dangerous one. **Clause 7 declines to invent the horizon** and the entry found no number anywhere in the corpus for it, so the endpoint publishes a freshness verdict whose threshold nobody has written down. Section 4's fourth judgement is that the neutral webhook package is STILL not lifted at a third consumer, and the entry argues the case for deferring has grown stronger rather than weaker, which is the sentence to read hardest.
