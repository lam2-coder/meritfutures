## ADR-062: The payout gate reads `identities.status = 'active'`, and three more of section 10's ten duplicated guards were contradictions rather than copies  (2026-08-20, status: proposed)

**[STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) section 10 defined ten guards twice, in a table whose own opening sentence is that guards are "defined once in section 10 so the same condition is never written twice". Nobody had read all ten pairs: the [`CI-06u`](../testing/STRATEGY.md) survey counted first cells and never semantics, three pairs were known to differ and seven were unread. This entry reports the reading of all ten. FOUR ARE CONTRADICTIONS AND SIX ARE AGREEMENTS. The fourth contradiction, `G-ENFORCEMENT-RESTRICT`, came out of the seven nobody had read, which is the case for reading them rather than sampling them. The six agreements land as [ADR-061](ADR-061.md) repairs in the same commit and are recorded here for the record rather than ruled.**

- **Context:** [`CI-06u`](../testing/STRATEGY.md)'s `CI06U_REGISTER` pinned 106 duplicate table keys across eight files and ten of them are section 10's guard table, on the money path. [ADR-061](ADR-061.md) ruled the line: a duplicated key is a **repair** landing by commit when the session can state that both rows say the same thing and why, and an **amendment** needing its own ruling in every other case, **including the case where the session cannot tell**. This entry is that ruling for the four keys where the halves do not agree. It repairs the other six under ADR-061 and says per key which half survived.

### 1. `G-ELIGIBLE` reads `identities.status = 'active'`, and `closed` does not reach a payout gate

> **THE RULING. `G-ELIGIBLE` names `identities.status = 'active'`. An identity that is not `active` does not reach the payout gate, and that answer covers `closed` explicitly rather than by side effect.**

[`0001_extensions_and_enums.sql:27`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql) declares `identity_status AS ENUM ('active', 'restricted', 'closed')`. **The witness separating the two halves is `closed`.** A `closed` identity passes `<> 'restricted'` and fails `= 'active'`. There is no `suspended` value and [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) refused to add one, so any reasoning that turns on one is reasoning about a value that does not exist.

**The strongest argument FOR `<> 'restricted'` was checked first and it does not survive.** The brief for this session states that ADR-041's binding table "expresses the wallet and withdrawal gates as `restricted -> blocked`, which is the `<>` shape". **The table was read at [ADR-041:20](ADR-041.md).** It is headed "What `restricted` binds", it has seven rows and every behavior cell reads `blocked`. **It enumerates what a restriction blocks and says nothing whatever about `closed`.** Both predicates block `restricted`, so **both satisfy ADR-041 completely and the table cannot discriminate between them.** The evidence point was inherited from the brief by three documents and it decides nothing.

**What does decide it, in order of weight:**

1. **The witness cannot be shown unreachable, and on a payout path that is dispositive.** [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) section 9 records that **`G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` is named by that machine and defined nowhere**, that section 10 has never carried a row for it, and that the corpus disagrees about what it closes: ADR-041 calls closure for cause "terminal and **per account**" while the drawing routes an **identity** to `closed`. **The only edge into `closed` is undefined**, so nobody can prove a `closed` identity holds no `active`/`funded` account. Under `<> 'restricted'` that unprovable case is paid.
2. **`<>` fails open and `=` fails closed on every value the enum gains later.** A fourth `identity_status` value passes `<> 'restricted'` silently on the day it is added and fails `= 'active'` on the same day, loudly. ADR-041 refusing `suspended` in August is not a promise about the enum, and **the form that breaks safely when a stranger widens a type is the form a payout gate takes.**
3. **The ruling is free today and expensive later.** `grep identities.status packages/rules-engine/src` returns **zero hits**, verified in this session. No code implements this gate. **A predicate nobody has written is a predicate that costs one line to get right**, and `G-ELIGIBLE` is read from eighteen files.

**The surviving row cites this entry**, so a reader of the frozen document finds the ruling rather than a predicate that changed with no trace.

### 2. `G-FREEZE-CLEARED / G-FREEZE-ENFORCED` keeps the expiry disjunct

> **THE RULING. :416. A freeze clears when the investigation is dismissed OR when `freeze_expires_at` is reached, and the window is 48 wall-clock hours.**

:423 carries only "Investigation dismissed / enforcement decided with an evidence pack". **Under :423 a freeze that should self-clear does not**, which is an unbounded hold, and an unbounded hold is a denial nobody had to authorize.

Every clause of :416 resolves to a primary source. `payout_requests.freeze_expires_at` exists at [`0010_payouts.sql:108`](../../packages/db/migrations/0010_payouts.sql) with its sweep index at `:217`. [ADR-040:36](ADR-040.md) closes `OQ-M5-02` **at 48 hours, matching the hold**, and states the reason the longer clock was backwards: it held recognised money longer than unrecognised money. [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) rules the unit, because **Merit computes nothing in business days**. :423 states none of it.

### 3. `G-HOLD-REQUIRED` keeps the severity band and the stated scope

> **THE RULING. :417. An unresolved `risk_flags` row of severity 4 or above, in status `open` or `investigating`, against the account or the identity at request time.**

**:424 says "an unresolved high-severity flag" with no band, and an unbanded adjective is not a predicate.** [`0008_risk.sql:126`](../../packages/db/migrations/0008_risk.sql) scores severity `BETWEEN 1 AND 5`, so "high" has to name a cut and :424 does not.

**:417's band is the corpus's existing one and is not invented for the hold.** [M07:69](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md) gives `sla_due_at` to severity 4 and 5 under `SD-M7-02`; [M07:156](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md) says so in terms; and [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md)'s own `G-EXPIRY-OR-RETRIGGER` already reads "severity 4+ open flag" one table over. **A second high-severity threshold, defined elsewhere and drifting, is this repository's most repeated defect.**

**On scope, the two halves are closer than they look and that is a reason to state it rather than leave it.** `risk_flags.identity_id` is `NOT NULL` and `account_id` is `NULL`able ([`0008_risk.sql:116`](../../packages/db/migrations/0008_risk.sql)), so **every flag names an identity** and "against the identity" read literally already reaches account-scoped flags on the identity's other accounts. :417 says which two joins are meant. :424 leaves a reader to derive it from a nullable column, and the derivation is not obvious enough to be left implicit on the money path.

**:424's one clause that is not in :417 is kept out deliberately.** "It is not a gate failure: a request failing `G-ELIGIBLE` is never created at all" is true and load-bearing, and it is **not part of the guard's condition**. :417 already says the guard is evaluated after `G-ELIGIBLE` and `G-CLAMP` and never instead of them, which is the same fact stated as a rule about evaluation order. **Composing a third row out of two halves is an amendment nobody ruled**, so the row is kept as written.

### 4. `G-ENFORCEMENT-RESTRICT` requires the enforcement decision, not a flag sitting at `investigating`

> **THE RULING. :420. An identity restriction rides the flag lifecycle's `investigating` to `enforced` PATH. A flag standing at `investigating` does not authorize one.**

**This is the finding from the seven pairs nobody had read**, and it is the reason the brief's instruction to read all ten rather than the three known ones was right.

:420 requires the transition. :432 requires only that the flag be **at** `investigating` **or** `enforced`. **The witness is a flag sitting at `investigating` with no enforcement decided**: :432 authorizes a restriction, :420 does not.

**The lifecycle settles it, and it is in this same document.** [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) section 7 binds: **"no automatic transition into `enforced`"**, **"Entering `investigating` is what sets `payouts_frozen`"**, and **"Entering `enforced` requires an exported evidence pack id on the transition."** So the corpus already has a protective measure for the `investigating` stage and it is the **freeze**: per payment, expiring, reversible on dismissal. **The restriction is the enforcement**, per human, halting every surface ADR-041 enumerates, and under :432 it would be available on suspicion with a written reason and **no evidence pack**, which is the one document `enforced` cannot be entered without.

**The DDL corroborates :420 twice more.** `identity_restriction_episodes.opened_by` is `NOT NULL` ([`0031:235`](../../packages/db/migrations/0031_payout_hold_and_identity_restriction.sql)) and **:420 names an actor while :432 does not**. :420 also carries the fail-closed revocation order, `disable_entitlement` then `disable_account`, which is [ADR-041:41](ADR-041.md) verbatim and which :432 drops.

**One imprecision in the surviving half, recorded rather than smoothed.** :420 says "a cited **open** `risk_flags` id" while the transition it rides ends with that flag at `enforced`, and `identity_restriction_episodes.flag_id` constrains no status. **"Open" is read here as unresolved**, matching `G-HOLD-REQUIRED`'s `open` or `investigating`. **The cell is not reworded**: this entry rules which half survives, and rewriting the survivor is a different act needing a different argument.

### 6. `OQ-062-01`: a `closed` identity cannot request a payout and can still spend its wallet

> **`OQ-062-01`. OPEN, and it is a founder question rather than a transcription task. Section 1 makes the payout gate `= 'active'`. [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) `INV-M20-06` at [M20:62](../plans/M20-wallet.md) enumerates the wallet and external-withdrawal gate set as `payouts_frozen`, `recon_blocked`, KYC not `verified` and `identities.status = 'restricted'`, which is the `<>` shape. AFTER THIS ENTRY LANDS, A `closed` IDENTITY CANNOT REQUEST A PAYOUT AND CAN STILL SPEND ITS WALLET BALANCE. The site is `INV-M20-06`. The exposure is the wallet-spend door, and the external-withdrawal door behind it.**

**Stated as an open question and not as a recorded fold, because it belongs to nobody otherwise.** This session's fence stops at [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) and M20 is held by session S3, whose own ruling, `ADR-063`, is reserved at [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) for `M05`'s colliding invariant numbers and which deletes M20's `INV-M20-06` prose duplicate without ruling on its predicate. **A divergence that falls between two sessions is a divergence neither one closes**, and the honest shape for it is a numbered question with an owner rather than a paragraph in a consequences list.

**Three things about it that are already established and should not be re-derived:**

- **The divergence exists in M20 today and this entry exposes it rather than creating it.** `INV-M20-06` has blocked on `restricted` alone since [ADR-041](ADR-041.md) widened it. Whatever `G-ELIGIBLE` says, `closed` reaches the wallet.
- **It runs against `INV-M20-06`'s own sentence.** The invariant's first clause is "**every context gate that blocks the external leg blocks wallet spend too**", and [M20:286](../plans/M20-wallet.md) calls the shared-gate rule "the module's most important single line". A payout gate strictly narrower than the wallet gate is that rule failing in the direction the invariant was written to prevent.
- **The direction is the safe one and that is not a reason to leave it.** `= 'active'` is narrower than `<> 'restricted'`, so nothing this entry rules pays anybody who would not have been paid before. The exposure is what the **other** door already allowed.

**The number is `OQ-062-01` and not `OQ-M20-06`, deliberately.** M20's open questions run `OQ-M20-01` to `OQ-M20-05`, and taking `06` would allocate into a namespace a **concurrently running session holds**, which is the collision shape [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) records twice for ADR numbers and once for migration numbers. **The question is owned by this entry, so it is numbered here**, and it names its site rather than reserving a number inside it.

### 7. The argument AGAINST the recommendation

**Section 1 is the ruling to attack and the attack is consistency.** The `<>` shape is what the corpus writes everywhere else the restriction binds: `INV-M20-06` at [M20:62](../plans/M20-wallet.md) and `G-WITHDRAWAL-CLEARED` at [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) section 10, which reads "the identity **not `restricted`**". **This entry makes the payout gate the only outbound door written the other way**, and one shape written two ways is the defect this corpus names more often than any other. The counter is `OQ-062-01`: the divergence is real, it is named, it has a number, and **the remedy is to move the other doors to `= 'active'` rather than to move the payout gate back to a predicate that pays an identity nobody can prove is closed by accident.** A founder who prefers one shape today over a stricter gate today would be making a defensible choice and would be choosing the door that fails open.

**The second objection is that `closed` may be unreachable and this entry is ruling on a ghost.** If closing an identity necessarily closes its accounts, `G-ELIGIBLE`'s first clause, `active` and `funded`, already refuses the case and the two predicates never differ. **That is probably true and it cannot be shown**, because `G-ENFORCEMENT-CLOSE` is undefined and section 9 says so in terms. **A gate defended by an undefined guard is defended by nothing**, and the cost of being wrong is a payout to a terminated relationship.

**The third objection is to section 4's confidence.** The reading that a restriction requires the enforcement decision rests on section 7's binding note plus the ADR-041 revocation order plus `opened_by NOT NULL`, and **none of the three says "a restriction may not be applied at `investigating`" in words.** It is an inference from three sources that agree. The counter is that it is the **safer** inference and the reversible one: if the founder rules the other way, a restriction becomes available earlier, and nothing already written has to be undone.

**The fourth objection is that six repairs landed under a `proposed` rule.** [ADR-061](ADR-061.md) is `status: proposed` with an unsigned approval line, and this entry applies it to delete six rows. **If ADR-061 is refused or inverted, those six deletions were unauthorized.** They are recorded per key in section 5 with the half kept and the reason, which is exactly what makes them reversible; and the alternative, waiting, leaves the four contradictions in place too, since the same signature governs both.
