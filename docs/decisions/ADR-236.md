## ADR-236: The admin read port is behind the SSO purchase after all, its shape reason is retired as measured false for six of seven, and the one read that is genuinely unbuilt waits on a rule-state writer  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**The row this entry answers asked how many of six admin read shapes are constructible today, on the premise that `setAdminReadSource` is the one admin port NOT behind [ADR-171](ADR-171.md)'s SSO purchase, and that what blocks it is a shape.** The measurement is [`docs/reviews/2026-08-29-admin-read-constructibility.md`](../reviews/2026-08-29-admin-read-constructibility.md) and **it refutes the premise in both halves, in the direction that makes the purchase larger rather than smaller.**

**THE PORT DECLARES SEVEN METHODS AND SIX OF THEM ALREADY HAVE PRODUCERS.** `listEvents` was added by [ADR-184](ADR-184.md) ruling 1, so "six" was one short before the row was written. Five methods are named by `IMPLEMENTED_ADMIN_READS` and `adminReadSourceParts` supplies `exportEvidence` as a sixth. **Not one producer calls `sqlExecutor` and not one imports `@merit/db`.** The sentence the port has carried for many sessions, *"WHAT IS MISSING IS NOT AN AUTHORITY, IT IS A SHAPE"*, was true when written and **is now measured false for six of the seven**.

**SO THE MISSING THING IS THE AUTHORITY, WHICH IS THE INVERSE OF WHAT THE PORT SAID AND OF WHAT [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) CLAUSE 2 RECORDED.** That entry refused the operator door on the measurement that it *"unblocks ZERO of the five"*, and its row for this port read *"the door supplies an authority; the port is missing a vocabulary"*. **The vocabulary is supplied.** What a deployment now lacks is an `AdminSourceBackend`, whose one method takes a `SystemTx`, and `systemDb` is the only name in the accessor that yields one.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `236`, session **426**, dispatched as `claude/the-admin-console-reads-nothing`. **No migration is reserved, taken or spent.**

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH BY FILE, AND IT TOUCHES NO MONEY PATH.** No migration, no ledger posting, no payout branch, no auth branch. The executable changes are one docstring, one refusal message, one blocked reason and one new test file. **It ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line.**

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That the port declares seven methods, that six have producers, that `readLiability` is the one that does not, that no producer calls `sqlExecutor` or imports the accessor, that `src/db.ts` declares no `operator(fn)` and imports no `systemDb`, and that `start.ts` calls no admin setter, are all **MEASURED**, by a derivation that runs on every test run and was falsified by hand before this branch was pushed. That `setAdminReadSource` reduces to `setAdminSessionSource` through [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) section 9 is **DERIVED**, by following the three controls in section 3 below. What is **JUDGED** is that the correction belongs in the port's own header rather than only in a review (section 5), and that `readLiability`'s assembly is registered rather than taken (section 6).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Both line citations the row supplied were re-derived and both held exactly at `:961` and `:967` before this branch moved them. **Its count of six did not hold, and its central claim about which side of the purchase this port sits on did not hold.**

### 1. THE RULING

> **1. THE SHAPE REASON IS RETIRED, AND IT IS RETIRED AT THE PORT RATHER THAN ONLY IN A REVIEW.** `AdminReadSource`'s header said the missing thing is a shape and that *"There is no join and no aggregate to reach for"*. Six producer modules are the counter-example: each reads through `rows(key)`, `rowsWhere(key, filter)` and `rowAt(key, address)` and folds in ordinary TypeScript. **The claim was never that the accessor could not serve these bodies. It was that a JOIN had to happen in SQL**, and the six demonstrate that a fold over keyed reads IS the join.
>
> **2. `setAdminReadSource` IS BEHIND THE SSO PURCHASE, ONE STEP FURTHER ALONG THAN THE FOUR BACKENDS THAT REDUCE TO IT THROUGH `principal(request)`.** Wiring it needs an `AdminSourceBackend`; that needs `systemDb('operator-console')`; `src/db.ts` declares no `operator(fn)`; [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) clause 1 refuses to add one and section 9 makes it takeable only by *"the slice that lands an `AdminSessionSource` a deployment can install"*, which is the identity-provider integration [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) section 11 says nothing in this repository describes.
>
> **3. [ADR-171](ADR-171.md)'s REFUSAL IS UNTOUCHED AND ONLY ITS REASON FOR ONE ROW MOVES.** Section 9's condition is unmet, so the door does not open and this entry does not ask for it. **Nothing here reopens that ruling.** What changes is which side of the purchase line one port sits on, and therefore what a founder is buying when the SSO decision lands.
>
> **4. THE PURCHASE BLOCKS SIX PORTS AND NOT FOUR.** `setAdminSessionSource` itself; `useAdminWriteBackend`, `useAdminPayoutBackend`, `useAdminWalletBackend` and `useCertificateRevokeBackend` through `principal(request)`; and `setAdminReadSource` through ruling 2. **[ADR-171](ADR-171.md) counted four and `wiring.test.ts` carried its figure as *"THREE OTHER PORTS"* against a list that holds four**, because `useCertificateRevokeBackend` landed after that entry was written.
>
> **5. `readLiability` IS THE ONE READ SHAPE THAT IS GENUINELY UNBUILT, AND IT IS NOT BEHIND THE PURCHASE.** Its book reader, its seven-day trading horizon and its payout-velocity evaluator all exist and are exercised. **Nothing folds them into one `LiabilityResponse`.** The figure holding that fold is `eligible_next_7d`, whose one remaining clearing term is a `writeRuleState` implementation under `apps/worker/**` or `packages/**`.
>
> **6. NO AGGREGATE IS INVENTED FOR IT AND THE SESSION STOPS THERE.** `M06` section 1.2 at [`M06-admin-ops-console.md:35`](../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md): *"M6 aggregates numbers other modules computed. It has no arithmetic on a rule"*. A console computing its own eligibility forecast would put a number in front of an operator that no other part of this system agrees with.
>
> **7. THE MEASUREMENT BECOMES A CONTROL, BECAUSE THE DEFECT THIS ENTRY REPAIRS IS A TRUE SENTENCE GOING FALSE WITH NOTHING WATCHING.** [`test/admin-read-constructibility.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/admin-read-constructibility.test.ts) derives every number in this entry from source on every run. **A comment cannot fail, and that is exactly how a wrong reason propagated three citations deep with every gate green.**

### 2. Why the shape reason was believed for so long, which is the part worth carrying

**It was never wrong when it was written, and no single session made it wrong.** Producers landed one method at a time across many sessions, each one narrowing the claim by one, and each one **reported the narrowing rather than editing the sentence**, correctly, because the sentence was in another slice's fence. The composition file says so in terms: *"The entry is `wiring.test.ts`'s, and this directory reports the narrowing rather than editing it."*

**So the tree accumulated four true local decisions into one false global statement.** The narrowings were recorded where the narrowing happened. The claim stayed where the claim was. **Nothing compared them**, because a comment is not read by anything, and the count of implemented reads lives in a different file from every reader that quotes it.

**THE PROPAGATION IS THE COST AND IT IS MEASURABLE.** The port's header said it; [`wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts)'s `BLOCKED` entry quoted the header; [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) section 4 read the entry; the ALLOCATION row read ADR-171; and this session was dispatched on the row. **Four levels, and the session was told to build shapes that had been built.**

### 3. Ruling 2, derived at the three controls that enforce it

**The chain is mechanical rather than conventional, which is what makes this a finding and not an opinion.**

| Step | The control | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A backend is needed | `AdminSourceBackend.operator` takes a `SystemTx` | the composition's own declaration |
| Only one name yields one | `systemDb` is the sole unscoped constructor | [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts) |

**THE LAST ROW IS THE WHOLE RULING.** An installable `AdminSessionSource` is `C-08` hardware-key SSO and the `D3` allowlist on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`, which is a founder decision. **`ADMIN_ORIGIN` IS A PLACEHOLDER AND NO REAL ADMIN HOSTNAME IS WRITTEN ANYWHERE IN THIS BRANCH** ([ADR-012](ADR-012.md)); section 8 pastes the grep.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT ARGUE.** It does not argue the door should open. [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) section 5 is the strongest thing in that entry, and it is untouched here: a door on `ApiDb` is a capability `databaseAuthBackend` acquires, in the file whose whole design is that it cannot have one. **That argument survives this entry intact and is the reason ruling 3 exists.**

### 4. Ruling 5, and the one term that holds it

The module states its clearing condition in three terms, bound by `RI-19` so the module and its case cannot drift. **Two cleared while this was being read and one stands:**

| Term | State |
|---|---|
| 1. A `writeRuleState` implementation | **STANDS.** The only things satisfying the port are test doubles and a demo that refuses, so `rule_states` holds **zero** rows |
| 2. A primary source declaring the stored `engine_gates` encoding | **CLEARED** by [ADR-206](ADR-206.md) |
| 3. `eligible_next_7d` gains its `\| null` | **CLEARED** by [ADR-208](ADR-208.md) |

**`eligible_next_7d` is a forward-looking PER-ACCOUNT forecast and that is why term 1 is decisive.** Six gate groups decide eligibility and exactly one carries a forward-looking date; the other five clear only when a trader trades, so no stored row says when. That one date lives in `rule_states.engine_gates`, and nothing writes it. **EC-074 makes the group whole or nothing**, so a partial answer is not available either.

**THIS IS THE CLEAN OTHER SIDE OF THE ROW'S QUESTION.** It is not behind the SSO purchase, it is not behind any purchase, and it is not a shape the accessor cannot serve. **It is a thing we have not built**, and the thing is the rules engine's state writer, which is the money path.

### 7. Alternatives considered

- **Build `readLiability`'s assembly anyway.** REFUSED. It is a fence away, and the fence rule and the "do not widen a fence to finish" ruling are the same rule. **It is the highest-value next slice on this port and section 9 says so.**
- **Answer `eligible_next_7d` as `null` with an `awaiting_dependency` gap and compose the method.** REFUSED, and this is the closest call in the entry. The shape exists for it, and it would move the port from six of seven to seven of seven. **It is refused because it is a RULING about what an operator is shown on the liability home, made in the wrong fence and by the wrong slice**: the module's own clearing condition says the group needs all three terms, and reading term 3 as permission to decline the OTHER two terms' absence is exactly the kind of local re-reading that produced the defect this entry repairs. **Named here so the slice that takes it starts from the argument rather than rediscovering it.**
- **Wire the read source with a stand-in backend.** REFUSED, on the row's own instruction and on [ADR-171](ADR-171.md)'s. An admin identity or an admin door faked in code is the worst version of this work.
- **Leave the correction in the review and not touch the port.** REFUSED. The review is not read by the next session; **the header is, and it is where four readers took the wrong reason from.**
- **Return the number unspent.** Available, correct for a session that finds nothing, and **not correct here**: the row's central premise about which side of the purchase line this port sits on is wrong, and a wrong reason retires a question in the wrong direction.

### 9. Consequences

**FOR THE FOUNDER'S SSO DECISION.** It buys **six ports rather than four**, and one of the six is the entire operator read surface: six reads that are built, tested and waiting, plus every screen in `apps/admin` that quotes their blocked state. **The console is closer to working than the blocked list has been saying.**

**FOR THE NEXT SLICE ON THIS PORT.** `readLiability`'s assembly is the one piece of read-shape code missing and it is a fold of three existing producers. It needs the composition directory's fence and a ruling on section 7's second alternative. **It does not need the purchase.**

**FOR THE PROJECT'S OWN METHOD.** This is the second time in two days a claim propagated three or four citations deep while every gate stayed green, and both times the repair was a derivation rather than a bigger model. `CLAUDE.md` already says it: *"Prefer a new CI gate over a bigger model whenever the error is checkable."* **A count quoted across files is checkable, and now this one is checked.**

### 10. Approval

**Status `proposed`. The approval line below is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.**

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS WHETHER RULING 2 CHANGES THE SSO DECISION'S PRIORITY.** You have been told for many sessions that the admin read surface waits on a read shape somebody has to invent. **It does not and it has not for a while.** Six of seven reads are built and the whole surface waits on the same identity-provider integration the four write backends wait on. **If the SSO purchase was priced as unblocking the write console, it also unblocks the read console, and that is a different trade.**

  **THE SECOND IS SECTION 7's SECOND ALTERNATIVE**, which is the one thing here a session should not decide alone. `readLiability` could be composed today by answering `eligible_next_7d` as `null` with a named gap, which would take the port to seven of seven. **This entry refuses it on the ground that it is a ruling about what an operator sees during an incident, made in the wrong fence.** If you would rather the liability home render every figure it has with one honest absence rather than refusing the whole method, that is a ruling you can give and the slice is small.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **The six producers have never run against a live database through this port**, because the port has never been wired and cannot be. Each is exercised by its own suite against a recorder. **"Constructible" here means the shape is produced from keyed reads with no SQL and no invented arithmetic; it does not mean the query plan is acceptable or that the whole-table folds are affordable at production row counts**, and two of the modules take whole-table reads where the accessor offers no `ORDER BY`. That is a real cost and no measurement in this branch bounds it.
