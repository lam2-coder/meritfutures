## ADR-270: what posts a ledger entry is decided by who the transaction's opener serves, a posting may move to a clock exactly where its pinned check resolves no caller, and both refusals stand  (2026-08-30, status: proposed)

**[ADR-267](ADR-267.md) ruled that [ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s remedy does not transfer to `LT-06`, and [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 7 had already ruled it does not transfer to `LT-08`. Two ports were then blocked on the same unanswered question: what posts a ledger entry, if not the request path and not a door `apps/api` may open?** This entry answers it.

**BOTH REFUSALS STAND AND NEITHER IS WEAKENED BY A WORD.** `SystemReason` gains no member, `ApiDb` gains no door, and every ground [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) and [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) rested on is re-derived at source below and holds. **The dispatching row said that outcome would be complete and valuable, and half of this entry is exactly it.**

**THE OTHER HALF IS THAT THE QUESTION HAS AN ANSWER ALREADY IN THE CORPUS AND NOTHING HAD PUT IT IN ONE PLACE.** [`packages/ledger/src/tx.ts`](../../packages/ledger/src/tx.ts) states the rule in one sentence: *"the reason is chosen by whoever opens the transaction, which is the caller"*. There are three kinds of opener in this estate, the corpus already holds a word or a refusal for each, and **the third one is a process a clock started, which is `'nightly-batch'`, which `apps/worker` has held since [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 2.** No fourth authority is minted because a third one already fits.

**AND WHICH OF THE THREE A GIVEN POSTING BELONGS TO IS NOT A TASTE. IT IS DECIDED BY THE CHECK THE CORPUS PINS THE POSTING TO.** That is the rule this entry adds, and it makes [ADR-176](ADR-176.md), [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 7 and [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) one thing rather than three: **a posting may move to a clock exactly where the check it is bound to resolves no caller.** `INV-M20-01` binds `LT-06` to a live POSITION, which is read off rows. `INV-M20-02` binds `LT-08` to the PAYING IDENTITY, which is the caller.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `270`, session **461**, dispatched as `claude/what-posts-a-ledger-entry`. **NO MIGRATION NUMBER IS TAKEN OR RESERVED.** The highest on disk is `0074`, this ruling adds no column, no constraint and no insert shape, and section 6 is why it needs none.

- **MONEY PATH: this is the record of what Merit owes whom. `E2` READ IS OWED AND NOTHING HERE DISCHARGES IT.** Ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.** It inherits the `E2` debt on [ADR-165](ADR-165.md), [ADR-172](ADR-172.md), [ADR-176](ADR-176.md), [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) and [ADR-267](ADR-267.md), each `proposed` and each UNSIGNED, because it rules the authority all five argued about.

- **NO PORT IS WIRED. NO POSTING IS BUILT.** The row forbade both by name and this entry cuts no code in `apps/worker`, `packages/ledger` or `packages/db/src`.

### 2. THE RULING

> **Clause 1. BOTH REFUSALS STAND, AND NEITHER WAS EVER A REFUSAL OF A DEPLOYABLE.**
>
> Findings 1, 2 and 3. [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 3 refused a new `SystemReason` member because a reason names a kind of access and not a service. [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) clause 2 refused an `apps/api` door yielding a handle generic over the estate, because that hands **the trader surface** authority over every row Merit holds. **Both grounds are re-derived at source above and both hold unchanged.** Nothing here widens the vocabulary, opens a door, or asks either entry to be read more loosely than it was written.

> **Clause 2. WHAT POSTS A LEDGER ENTRY IS DECIDED BY WHO THE TRANSACTION'S OPENER SERVES, AND THE CORPUS ALREADY HOLDS A WORD OR A REFUSAL FOR EVERY OPENER THERE IS.**
>
> Finding 4 is the rule, in `packages/ledger`'s own sentence. This entry does no more than enumerate its cases and find that they are closed:
>
> | The transaction's opener serves | The authority | Where it is already ruled |
> |---|---|---|
> | **a trader, or nobody authenticated** | **NONE. The request path RECORDS and does not post** | [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) clause 2, [ADR-176](ADR-176.md) clause 1 |
> | **an operator** | `'operator-console'`, a word that already exists, behind [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) section 9's condition | Finding 6, finding 7 |
> | **nobody, because a clock started it** | `'nightly-batch'`, a word that already exists, on the handle `apps/worker` already holds | [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 2, finding 5 |
>
> **SO THE ANSWER TO THE ROW'S QUESTION IS THE THIRD LINE: a job in `apps/worker` running at `systemDb('nightly-batch')`.** It is not a new authority and it must not be built as one. **No fourth opener exists**, because a transaction is opened by a request, by an operator action or by a clock, and the first is refused and the other two already have words.

> **Clause 3. WHICH LINE A GIVEN POSTING BELONGS TO IS DECIDED BY THE CHECK THE CORPUS PINS THE POSTING TO, AND NEVER BY WHERE IT WOULD BE CONVENIENT.**
>
> **A posting may move to a clock exactly where the check it is bound to resolves no caller.** That single sentence is what [ADR-176](ADR-176.md), [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 7 and [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) each found one leg at a time:
>
> | Leg | The check the corpus pins the posting to | Does that check resolve a caller | Ruled |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | `LT-01` | none. M05's hold branch holds the estate's one blessing of a deferred posting (finding 10) | n/a | **moved**, [ADR-176](ADR-176.md) |
> | `LT-06` | `INV-M20-01`, *"checked against the live position inside the same transaction"* (finding 8) | **no.** A position is read off rows | **moves whole**, [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) clause 5 |
> | `LT-08` | `INV-M20-02`, *"compared to the paying identity, in the same transaction as the debit"* (finding 9) | **yes. The paying identity IS the caller** | **cannot move** |
>
> **THIS IS WHY `LT-06` AND `LT-08` PART COMPANY DESPITE HAVING PINS OF THE SAME SHAPE**, which is the thing three entries could not say from one leg each. Both are wallet debits bound to the transaction that checks them. `LT-06`'s check needs no caller, so the decision and the posting move together to a clock. `LT-08`'s check compares a row against a resolved caller, **so its transaction is a request transaction by construction and no change of authority can reach it.**

> **Clause 4. `LT-08` IS THEREFORE OUTSIDE THIS ENTRY'S ANSWER, BY THE PIN AND NOT BY THE FENCE, AND A SUCCESSOR MAY NOT REACH FOR THE WORKER THERE.**
>
> The row's question was about a posting **outside the request path**. Clause 3 establishes that `LT-08` is not one and cannot be made into one: moving it is `DEP-M20-02`'s own consequence column, *"`INV-M20-02` fails, and the wallet becomes a transfer instrument"*. **So this entry rules `LT-08` out of scope on the merits rather than declining it**, and what that arm waits on is the ruling [ADR-238](ADR-238.md) ruling 7 named, *"how a two-party posting is authorised from a request path, in `packages/ledger` and `packages/db`, by a session whose subject that is."* **Section 5 says what that session inherits and what it must not reach for. It is not decided here.**

> **Clause 5. WHAT THE REQUEST PATH RECORDS SO A LATER DOOR CAN POST IS ALREADY RECORDED ON BOTH LEGS, AND THIS ENTRY ADDS NOTHING.**
>
> Finding 11. The row asked for *"[ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s shape without [ADR-176](ADR-176.md)'s remedy"*: what the API records so the worker can post later. **The answer is what it already records.** `PayoutRequestInsert.idempotencyKey` is [ADR-176](ADR-176.md) clause 2's own price, paid; `wallet_withdrawals.idempotency_key` has been `text NOT NULL` since `0011` and `decideWithdrawal` writes it, which [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) clause 4 called *"the cleanest evidence available that the obstruction is neither a key nor a handle"*. **No insert shape moves, no column is added and no migration is taken.**

> **Clause 6. WHAT ACTUALLY STANDS BETWEEN THE RULED AUTHORITY AND A POSTING IS THREE MEASURED ABSENCES, AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A FENCE.**
>
> Findings 12, 13 and 14. **The worker declares no `@merit/ledger`. `ExpiryLedgerPort.postLt01` has no adapter. The job store does not exist, so five of six built jobs are unscheduled.** [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) finding 16 said *"the worker already does it"* and it never did: what that finding read was a port and a sentence describing the adapter that would satisfy it. **The correction matters because a successor reading it would believe the hard part was done.** None of the three needs a ruling to move and each is a slice's ordinary work.

> **Clause 7. NO INVARIANT IS MINTED OVER `@merit/ledger`, AND THE REASON IS [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) CLAUSE 6.**
>
> `RI-08` guards `@merit/db` because that manifest line is where the ability to NAME the accessor is acquired. **`@merit/ledger` is not that kind of line.** It declares no workspace dependency, cannot import `pg` or `drizzle-orm`, and `postTransaction` takes the caller's open transaction as its first argument with no overload that omits it, so **the package cannot reach a database at all**: the authority is the HANDLE, and `apps/worker` already holds one. A manifest line adding `@merit/ledger` there grants that deployable nothing it does not have. **A second list over a property `RI-08` already owns is the two-lists defect `RI-04`'s header names**, and `RI-25` is the next free number and this entry leaves it free.

> **Clause 8. NEITHER PORT IS WIRED, BOTH ENTRIES ARE NARROWED, AND THE 503s STAY.**
>
> `POST /wallet/withdrawals` answered `503` before this ruling and answers `503` after it; so does the checkout wallet arm. **What changes is what each entry is waiting for.** `useWithdrawalBackend` was waiting on an authority nobody had ruled; it is now waiting on a driver whose authority is settled and whose three absences are named, which is a slice somebody can be dispatched to build. `useCheckoutBackend`'s ledger clause was waiting on the same open question; it is now ruled **outside** the answer by `INV-M20-02`, so no successor may reach for the worker there.

### 3. Why the deployable running is not what answers this, said plainly

**THE ROW WAS RIGHT TO WARN AND THE WARNING HOLDS.** Its argument for existing was that when [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) and [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) were written the worker *"loaded a barrel and exited zero"*, that [ADR-241](ADR-241.md) made it run and [ADR-260](ADR-260.md) made the nightly fold complete, and that **a deployable that actually runs is a candidate authority that did not exist when the refusals were made.**

**IT IS NOT A CANDIDATE THAT DID NOT EXIST. IT IS THE SAME CANDIDATE, AND IT WAS ALREADY THE ANSWER ON THESE GROUNDS.** [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 2 granted `systemDb('nightly-batch')` to that deployable on 2026-08-27, two entries before [ADR-241](ADR-241.md) made anything call `main`. Clause 2 of this entry would have been true against the barrel that exited zero, because **finding 3 is that neither refusal's ground mentions a process at all.**

**WHAT [ADR-241](ADR-241.md) AND [ADR-260](ADR-260.md) CHANGED IS DELIVERY AND NOT AUTHORITY, AND THE DISTINCTION IS WORTH KEEPING RATHER THAN COLLAPSING.** Before them, a ruling naming the worker would have named a process that could never perform what it was named for. **That is not an authority defect and it is exactly as fatal to a trader waiting for money**, which is why the row was right that something had changed and wrong about which thing. The honest statement is: **the authority was always there, and until [ADR-241](ADR-241.md) it was an authority held by something that did nothing.**

### 5. What the `LT-08` session inherits, and the two shapes that are not free

**THIS ENTRY DOES NOT RULE `LT-08` AND NAMING THE CANDIDATES IS NOT RULING THEM.** Clause 4 sends it to a session in `packages/db` and `packages/ledger`. What that session should not have to rediscover:

- **A `LedgerTx` view on a scoped transaction is refused at source, and not by this entry.** [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)'s `insertUnder` doc already says why a generic version is refused: *"a generic version of this method would hand a request handler ONE LEG of a double-entry posting"*. A bare `LedgerTx` on `ScopedTx` is exactly that, because `LedgerTx.insert` takes either write key on its own.
- **So any door that works takes the WHOLE posting and returns a projection**, which is [ADR-265](ADR-265.md)'s `effectiveAccountCap` and [ADR-230](ADR-230.md)'s `insertAsParty` construction: `packages/db` performs inside the transaction what the caller must not have the authority to compose, and hands back an answer rather than a handle.
- **And that shape has a cost this entry will not pay on that session's behalf.** Composing a balanced posting is `packages/ledger`'s, and that package *"declares no workspace dependency"* deliberately. A door in `packages/db` that called `postTransaction` would point the arrow the other way; a door that wrote both legs itself would be a second transcription of double entry, which is [ADR-092](ADR-092.md) section 5's two-statements-of-one-fact hazard arriving on the money path. **Neither is free, this entry picks neither, and a session that picks one owes the argument.**

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **MONEY PATH, `E2` READ OWED.** **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  1. **IS CLAUSE 3's RULE THE RIGHT RULE, OR IS IT A PATTERN FITTED TO THREE POINTS?** This is the single judgement the entry rests on. Three legs were ruled one at a time by three entries, and this one says they were one rule all along: **the posting may move where its pinned check resolves no caller.** It fits all three and it predicts the fourth. **A founder who reads `INV-M20-02` as a rule about ROWS rather than about the CALLER removes the `LT-08` line from clause 3**, and the ruling then rests on clause 2 alone, which is an enumeration rather than a rule. It would still hold. It would hold as three facts rather than as a rule, and that is worth knowing before it is signed.

  2. **IS "A CLOCK" THE RIGHT THIRD OPENER, OR IS IT "NO CALLER"?** Clause 2's table says a clock. `'nightly-batch'` is [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) section 5's *"a job that partitions across every account"*, and a `LT-06` driver partitions across approved withdrawals rather than across accounts. **This entry reads that as the same kind of access and [ADR-267](ADR-267.md) clause 5 read it the same way**, on [ADR-126](ADR-126.md)'s rule that the vocabulary that moves is the table and never the reason. **A founder who disagrees does not get a new member out of it**, because clause 1 forecloses that; what they get is the finding that the third opener needs a name the corpus has not written, and that is a bigger question than this row.

  3. **CLAUSE 7 REFUSES A GUARD AND THAT REFUSAL IS THE MOST ARGUABLE LINE HERE.** `@merit/ledger` in a manifest grants no authority, which is why no invariant is minted. **The counter-argument is that `RI-08` exists because a manifest line reads like housekeeping**, and the day a session adds `@merit/ledger` to `apps/worker` is the day money starts moving out of a deployable, which is a diff a reviewer should be made to see. **This entry says the handle is the thing to guard and `RI-08` guards it.** If that is wrong, the remedy is a `covers` line rather than a list.
