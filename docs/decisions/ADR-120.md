## ADR-120: `apps/api` joins the admission list, and wiring the auth surface finds that ADR-112 unblocked everything a session can DO and nothing that makes one  (2026-08-27, status: proposed)

**[ADR-117](ADR-117.md) ruling 3 made `DB_ADMITTED` an EMPTY list and ruling 4 refused to pre-admit `apps/api`, on the argument that a list which admits a member against a need nobody has stated yet is a list joining itself. [Session 218](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-218.md) declared `AuthBackend` blocked rather than reaching for the manifest and [session 220](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-220.md) declined `IdempotencyStore` for the same reason, writing it into [ADR-113](ADR-113.md) section 6: the admission is *"an authority change on the deployable that serves the whole public surface rather than a side effect of a checkout slice"*. BOTH WERE RIGHT AND NEITHER IS OVERTURNED.** This entry admits `apps/api`, and what makes that correct here is the one thing that was not true there: **it is this entry's SUBJECT rather than its by-product.** It then wires both ports against the real accessor, and reports the measurement that wiring produced, which the dispatch did not predict and which is the reason this entry is longer than an admission needs to be.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `120`, session **232**, dispatched as `claude/auth-wiring`. **MONEY PATH, auth, [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) strict.** The number was RESERVED before dispatch and the fence names it REQUIRED. The [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) line-by-line read is owed on every line this lands and is not discharged by this entry's own signature line.

- **Context:** [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 3 left `IdempotencyStore` a port with no implementation and clause 2 named the construction it was blocked on. [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) built that construction and its section 12 opened with *"four money-path slices can start"*. This is the first of the four to run.

### 2. THE RULING

> **1. `apps/api` IS ADMITTED TO `DB_ADMITTED`, AND WHAT MAKES THAT CORRECT HERE IS THAT IT IS THIS ENTRY'S SUBJECT.** [ADR-117](ADR-117.md) ruling 4's argument is not weakened and is not met by a better need; it is met by a different KIND of session. A slice whose subject is a checkout, an idempotency layer or an auth surface must decline, because the admission is then a means. A slice whose subject IS the authority change may take it, because there is then somebody whose whole job was to think about it. **`@merit/api` is the only name that joins**, `RI-08`'s two throws are untouched, and the `guarded.length === 0` refusal is the assertion that matters most now that the list is no longer empty.
>
> **2. THE SESSION TOKEN CARRIES ITS OWN IDENTITY, AND THE CLAIM IS A HINT THE DATABASE IS NEVER ASKED TO TRUST.** The token is `<identityId>.<secret>` and the stored `refresh_token_hash` is a digest of the WHOLE string, so a rewritten prefix is refused TWICE and independently: the digest changes, so the address matches no row; and if it somehow did, `scopedTx` ANDs the tenancy predicate onto the address, so the row still does not come back. **The identity is not READ off the row, it is PROVED by the row coming back**, because `sessions` carries `user_id` and no `identity_id` and the scope predicate is an `EXISTS` over `users`. Section 4 prices what this costs.
>
> **3. THE ACCESSOR IS REACHED THROUGH ONE FILE AND TWO DOORS, AND THERE IS NO THIRD.** [`apps/api/src/db.ts`](../../apps/api/src/db.ts) is the only file in the deployable that names `@merit/db`; it opens `scoped(identityId, fn)` and `firm(fn)` and **declares no `system(reason, fn)`**. A door declared here is a door somebody uses, and the two things this surface genuinely cannot reach are not made reachable by a third word. The one `IdentityId` assertion in the workspace lives there with a UUID guard in front of it.
>
> **4. A METHOD THIS SURFACE CANNOT EXPRESS RAISES WITH ITS OWN REASON, AND NEVER RETURNS A PLAUSIBLE VALUE.** Twelve of `AuthBackend`'s sixteen raise `AuthBackendUnwired` and each carries a DIFFERENT sentence naming its own blocker, so a 503 from `POST /auth/verify` is a different fact in the log from a 503 from `POST /auth/passkey/login/options`. **The reason never reaches the response**: [API_CONTRACT section 2](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) keeps internals out of a problem document and every reason names a table, a scope class or a construction.
>
> **5. THE TWO MISSING CONSTRUCTIONS ARE REPORTED AND NOT BUILT.** An INSERT onto a `derived` table and a pre-identity READ are both `packages/db` rulings and both are the founder's, on [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) clause 2's own precedent: *"the shape decides whether 'reach the database and choose a scope are the SAME ACT' survives"*. `git status --porcelain packages/db` reports zero on the branch that carries this entry.
>
> **6. THE `C-27` UNION IS NARROWED AT THE READ BOUNDARY AND AN UNRECOGNISED FACTOR RAISES.** `sessions.elevated_by_factor` reads back as a `string` and becomes an `ElevationFactor` only by comparison against the two members [`0029:581`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql)'s CHECK declares. A cast would type-check, satisfy every existing case, and turn any value the database happened to hold into a factor `authorize` honours. Section 8 watches it refuse `sms_otp` with the CHECK dropped underneath it.

### 3. Clause 1, and the sentence sessions 218 and 220 were right to write

**THE ADMISSION IS NOT A BETTER-ARGUED VERSION OF THE ONE TWO SESSIONS DECLINED. IT IS A DIFFERENT ACT.**

[ADR-113](ADR-113.md) section 6 states the refusal exactly: an implementation *"must import `@merit/db` into `apps/api`, whose manifest declares no such dependency and was not in this session's fence, and that admission is an authority change on the deployable serving the whole public surface rather than a side effect of a checkout slice."* [Session 218](../sessions/2026-08-26-session-218.md) reached the same line from the auth surface and stopped in the same place.

**Neither of them was blocked by an argument they could have won.** Both were blocked by the shape of their own dispatch: the admission was a MEANS in a slice whose END was something else, and a control whose whole design is *"adding a name here is the admission decision"* ([ADR-117](ADR-117.md) ruling 3) is defeated the first time the decision is taken by somebody who was thinking about a checkout.

**SO THE TEST IS NOT WHETHER THE NEED IS REAL, IT IS WHETHER THE NEED IS THE SUBJECT.** ADR-109 already said the need was real and named `apps/api` as the package that would want the accessor first; ADR-117 ruling 4 refused the admission anyway and gave the reason in one sentence, *"a list that admits a member against a need nobody has stated yet is a list joining itself."* The need is now stated, by a session whose dispatch names the admission as its object and reserves an ADR number for it before it runs.

**AND THE PRICE ADR-117 SECTION 4 NAMED WAS PAID IN FULL AND WATCHED.** With `"@merit/db": "workspace:*"` in [`apps/api/package.json`](../../apps/api/package.json) and `DB_ADMITTED` still empty:

```

       apps/api/package.json: dependencies.@merit/db is `workspace:*`, which names it directly.
       @merit/api is not in DB_ADMITTED ... Admit it in DB_ADMITTED with a reason, or drop the line
```

That is ADR-096 section 4's quiet line becoming a loud one, and making it green again cost exactly the second diff ADR-117 predicted: one in the file whose whole subject is which packages may reach the trader database. **The `RI-08` title moved on its own**, from *"No package in this workspace declares the database accessor"* to *"Only 1 admitted package(s) declare the database accessor"*, because it is computed from the list rather than written beside it.

**WHAT THE ADMISSION DOES NOT GRANT, stated because a reader of that list will ask.** It grants the ability to NAME `@merit/db` and nothing about what may be done with it. `scopedDb` still needs an identity, `systemDb` still needs one of two reasons, `firmDb` still refuses every `owned` key, and an addressed write still refuses an address naming no unique key. [ADR-084](ADR-084.md)'s brands and [ADR-008](ADR-008.md)'s scoping are the controls on the USE and are unmoved by this line.

### 4. Clause 2, and what putting an identity in a cookie costs

**THE PROBLEM IS THAT AN AUTHENTICATION HANDLER IS THE CODE THAT RUNS BEFORE THE IDENTITY IS KNOWN.** `sessionByToken` must find one row out of a table by a value the caller presented, and finding 4 says the accessor has no authority that does that. So either the token tells the handler where to look, or the surface does not resolve a cookie at all.

**THE CLAIM IN THE TOKEN IS SAFE BECAUSE THE DATABASE IS NEVER ASKED TO BELIEVE IT.** Two independent refusals, watched in section 8:

| Attack | Refused by |
|---|---|
| take a valid token and rewrite its identity prefix to another identity's | **the digest.** `refresh_token_hash` is over the whole string, so the rewritten token addresses no row |
| present another identity's secret under your own prefix | **the digest**, the same way |
| suppose a digest collision, or a database whose hashes were computed some other way | **`scopedTx`'s composition.** The predicate is `EXISTS(users of the claimed identity) AND refresh_token_hash = $2`, so the row is not in the result set |

**ONE OF THOSE IS A CONTROL AND TWO IS A CONTROL THAT SURVIVES A MISTAKE IN THE OTHER**, which is the whole reason the digest is taken over the token rather than over the secret. The seeded mutation in section 8 is exactly that mistake.

**IT DISCLOSES NOTHING NEW.** [API_CONTRACT section 3](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) puts `identity_id` in `VerifyResponse` and in `Me`, so the client already holds the value the cookie now also carries. It does become visible to anything that reads the cookie header, which is the same set of things that could read the session secret beside it.

**WHAT IT COSTS, STATED RATHER THAN LEFT TO BE DISCOVERED.** The cookie is no longer opaque, so a later change to how an identity is spelled is a change to a live client's stored credential. And the format has a PARSER in this tree and no MINTER, which is finding 3: nothing can insert the row a minter would mint for. `mintSessionToken` is therefore not written, on [`job-queue.ts`](../../packages/queue/src/job-queue.ts)'s rule that a primitive admitted before a caller exists is a primitive nobody can remove; `sessionTokenHash` and `SESSION_TOKEN_SEPARATOR` are exported so a minter written later cannot drift from the parser silently.

### 5. The two constructions, which are the founder's and are the most useful thing this session produced

**B1: A PRE-IDENTITY READ HAS NO DOOR, AND ADR-109 CLAUSE 1's GROUND HAS MOVED.**

That clause refused a third `SystemReason` member and gave a reason that was correct on the day it was written: *"admitting `'request-handler'` would buy a door that still writes the whole table. Widening the reason vocabulary to solve a predicate problem is the change that looks like a fix and is not one."* **[ADR-112](ADR-112.md) supplied the predicate.** The door a third reason would buy today is a door that names ONE ROW, which is a different offer from the one ADR-109 declined.

**THIS ENTRY DOES NOT TAKE IT AND SAYS WHY IT DOES NOT.** ADR-109 clause 2 is explicit that the shape of a new authority *"decides whether 'reach the database and choose a scope are the SAME ACT' survives"*, and that is a `packages/db` ruling. What this entry adds is the evidence ADR-109 could not have: the vocabulary was closed at two on an argument about a predicate, the predicate exists, and **nothing has revisited the vocabulary since.** [ADR-096](ADR-096.md) clause 3 kept it at two and [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) widened what those two words AUTHORIZE without adding a third.

**B2: `ScopedTx.insert` IS `OwnedTableKey` AND `sessions` IS `derived`.**

This one has never been argued at all, and its reason is written where the type is declared: a `derived` row carries no tenancy column, so there is nothing to stamp, and establishing that the parent is this identity's would be a read the caller could have skipped or a race. [ADR-102](ADR-102.md) section 4 names the construction that would serve it and deliberately does not build it.

**THE TWO TOGETHER ARE WHY A DEPLOYMENT STILL CANNOT MINT A SESSION**, and therefore why the whole authenticated surface is reachable in a suite and in the round trip of section 8 and not by a person with a browser. That is stated here in one place rather than distributed across twelve error messages.
