### 1. The sorting rule, stated before it is applied, because the row did not supply one

**THE ROW ASKS FOR EXACTLY ONE BUCKET PER PORT AND SEVEN OF THE THIRTEEN HAVE MORE THAN ONE OBSTRUCTION.** `useCertificateRevokeBackend` has three, in three different buckets. A rule is therefore unavoidable, and picking it silently is how a sort becomes a guess.

**THE RULE: a port is placed by the obstruction a session dispatched AT IT TODAY would meet first and could not pass.** That is the only question the bucket answers for the dispatcher, and it makes `(C)` decisive wherever it applies: a port that reduces to another port cannot be advanced at all until that port lands, whatever else is also true of it.

**EVERY OTHER OBSTRUCTION A PORT CARRIES IS RECORDED BESIDE IT RATHER THAN DROPPED**, with its own bucket, under the heading *what the bucket does not discharge*. **A port whose bucket clears and which is then still blocked is the failure this rule is chosen against**, and **SEVEN of the thirteen are in exactly that position**, each carrying a *what the bucket does not discharge* line below. The two that do not are `useAffiliateDeps` and `useAdminPayoutBackend`, whose buckets discharge everything they wait on; the vendor and purchase ports of section 4 carry no such line because nothing stands behind those three but the founder act itself.

**THE ROW PUTS "AN UNMADE RULING" IN BUCKET (B) AND THIS ENTRY NARROWS THAT, ONCE, IN WRITING.** This corpus dispatches ruling rows: `304` met [ADR-253](ADR-253.md)'s open question and answered it, `305` ruled a sizing, `309` is a ruling row waiting. So an unmade ruling a session can reach from sources already in the corpus is **(A)**, and only a ruling needing an input the corpus does not contain -- counsel's words, a vendor's answer, a decision about Merit's money or Merit's risk -- is **(B)**. **Read the other way, every port on this list would be founder-owned and the sort would say nothing.**
