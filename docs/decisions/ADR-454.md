### 2. The measurement, on a PostgreSQL this row started itself

A cluster was initialised with `initdb` and started on a unix socket, and a table was built modelling [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts)'s `reconciliations`: an `ALWAYS` identity `id` and a `STORED` generated `delta_cents` computed from two integer-cent balances. A second table carries the `BY DEFAULT` identity column the estate does not have.

```
CREATE TABLE recon (
  id bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
  our_balance_cents bigint NOT NULL,
  platform_balance_cents bigint NOT NULL,
  delta_cents bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS (our_balance_cents - platform_balance_cents) STORED,
  status text NOT NULL
);
CREATE TABLE bydefault_t (
  id bigint GENERATED BY DEFAULT AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
  note text
);
```

### 4. The [ADR-448](ADR-448.md) contrast correction

[ADR-448](ADR-448.md):153, section 8 item 2, closes with a bare four-word clause:

> `428C9`, not `42601`.

**AS A STATEMENT ABOUT THE IDENTITY HALF ALONE IT IS RIGHT.** A write naming a `GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY` column is `428C9`, measured at `Q2` and `Q4`.

**AS A CONTRAST WITH THE STORED HALF IT IS WRONG, AND IT SITS WHERE A CONTRAST IS THE ONLY AVAILABLE READING.** The clause has no subject of its own. It terminates a paragraph whose whole business is the difference between `column.generated` and `column.generatedIdentity`, so "not `42601`" reads as "`42601` is the other one's". **The other one's is `428C9` too.** The correction is that the SQLSTATE never distinguished them and the sentence should never have been asked to.

### 9. The corpus rule question, named and not answered

**MAY A DATED RECORD'S FACTUAL ERROR BE REPAIRED AT ALL?**

[ADR-386](ADR-386.md):169 forbids repairing a dated record's POINTERS, and the reasoning generalises cleanly: a dated record says what was true, and known, on its date. Editing it destroys the evidence of what a session actually believed, which is the only thing the record is for.

**A FACTUAL ERROR IS NOT OBVIOUSLY THE SAME THING AS A STALE POINTER, AND THIS ROW WILL NOT DECIDE WHICH IT IS.** The two readings both have force:

- **NAME IT, NEVER EDIT IT.** [ADR-448](ADR-448.md) genuinely believed `42601` on its date. Rewriting it to `428C9` makes it a record of a session that never existed, and the corpus loses the ability to answer how the error propagated. **Under this reading the 35 mentions stand forever and this entry is the correction, discoverable by anyone who greps the number.**
- **REPAIR IT IN PLACE, MARKED.** A reader who greps `42601` today finds 35 assertions and one correction, and nothing at the 35 sites points at the one. **Under this reading a dated record may carry a marked correction beside the error**, which preserves what was believed and stops the belief being re-inherited.

**THIS ROW NAMES THE QUESTION AND ANSWERS NEITHER.** It needs a founder ruling because it governs every future row that finds a false statement in a landed entry, and this is the third consecutive row to arrive at it: [ADR-451](ADR-451.md) section 11 item 2 is already waiting on the same ruling for a different number, and [ADR-452](ADR-452.md) section 11 item 1 raised it for this one.

**WHAT THIS ROW OFFERS THE RULING IS A CONCRETE PRICE RATHER THAN A PRINCIPLE.** Under the first reading the cost is 35 standing mentions of a false SQLSTATE across 9 files, all of them findable and none of them signposted. Under the second it is 35 marked edits to landed records, and a precedent that a dated record is editable, which is the thing [ADR-386](ADR-386.md) was written to prevent.

**A SECOND AND SMALLER RULE QUESTION IS NAMED HERE TOO, AND IT IS ALSO NOT ANSWERED.** [ADR-452](ADR-452.md)'s section 8 says **26 mentions across 11 files** and its own approval section says **19 places**. Re-derived on that entry's own base `6fb9147`, section 8 is right and the approval section is not. **That is a dated record disagreeing with itself**, and it falls under exactly the question above.
