## ADR-209: a `TableKey` may name a VIEW, its scope class is the class of the relation it PROJECTS, and it is never addressable -- so `economic_calendar_current` is registered and `affiliate_statements` needed no ruling at all  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**[`apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts)'s `BLOCKED` map has said for four waves that `setEconomicCalendarSource` is blocked FIRST on a registry entry rather than on an adapter, and nobody acted on it because acting on it needed this ruling.** The port reads `economic_calendar_current` and never `economic_calendar`, in the route's own words, and that relation is in neither [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts) nor [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts). **It is not in them because it is a VIEW, and it is the only `CREATE VIEW` in sixty migrations.** Every drift assertion in `packages/db` reads a `CREATE TABLE` body; three of them THROW rather than fail on a relation that has none. So the obstruction was never the tenancy question the reservation was written for. **It was the KIND**, and this entry rules on that.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `209`, session **399**, dispatched as `claude/unregistered-relations`. The number was reserved **CONDITIONALLY**, against the possibility that neither relation's class was obvious. **One of the two was obvious and is registered without a ruling; the other was not obvious for a reason the reservation did not anticipate.** The row is amended in place under [ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3.

- **Context:** it runs after [ADR-092](ADR-092.md), whose rule that a relation is registered ONCE by the first session that NEEDS it is why both of these waited; after [ADR-191](ADR-191.md) and [ADR-199](ADR-199.md), the two precedents for what a registration costs and the source of the sentence *registering it makes it readable and nothing else*; after [ADR-101](ADR-101.md), whose two clauses decide which classes are even available on a row; after [ADR-112](ADR-112.md), which made an address a key the TRANSCRIPTION declares; and after [ADR-094](ADR-094.md) and [ADR-103](ADR-103.md), which built the fold every drift assertion here reads and which assume throughout that a registered relation has a `CREATE TABLE`.

- **NOT THE MONEY PATH, AND THE DISTINCTION IS BY SUBJECT.** No migration is written and none is edited. No transaction is opened, no ledger account is named, no `SystemReason` and no `SqlExecutorReason` member is added, no `pg` is imported, no cast past a key type is written, and every merged migration is byte for byte unchanged. **One registered relation carries money** -- `affiliate_statements.total_cents bigint NOT NULL`, signed -- **and this entry decides who may READ it rather than how it is computed**, which is section 3 and is the one place a founder read would change something.

- **THE ENTRY SHIPS `proposed` WITH AN UNSIGNED APPROVAL LINE.** What is **derived** is that the relation is a view and the only one (section 1), that its class follows from the projection (section 4), that it has no key (section 5), and that `affiliate_statements` is the third table on a shape already registered twice (section 3). What is **judged** is ruling 1 itself -- that a view belongs in this registry at all -- and section 8's refusal to rename `TableKey`.

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every premise was re-derived at its file. **One did not survive and it is section 7.**

### 1. THE RULING

> **1. A `TableKey` MAY NAME A RELATION THE MIGRATIONS CREATE WITH `CREATE VIEW`.** The registry's question is *what may a door name*, and the answer has to cover what the readers actually read. `apps/api/src/routes/economic-calendar.ts` declares `EconomicCalendarSource` against `economic_calendar_current` and says in its own header that it reads the view and NEVER the base table. Refusing the view does not keep the `DISTINCT ON` out of the tree; it moves the maximum-revision derivation into an adapter, in TypeScript, where nothing compares it to the view -- **which is the second-source-of-truth failure `FM-M7-08` guards and precisely what `0039` created the view to make impossible.** Section 2.
>
> **2. ITS SCOPE CLASS IS THE CLASS OF THE RELATION IT PROJECTS.** A view declares no column of its own, no foreign key, no constraint and no key, so every reader `scope.ts` uses to argue a class finds nothing on it. The relation whose DDL DOES decide those facts is the one the view selects from. `economic_calendar_current` projects `economic_calendar`, which is `firm`, so the view is `firm`. Section 4. **This is a rule and not a courtesy: a view over an unregistered relation is REFUSED**, because there is then no class to inherit and the session that wants it must register the base first.
>
> **3. A VIEW IS NEVER ADDRESSABLE, AND THE ABSENCE IS THE DDL RATHER THAN AN OMISSION.** `economic_calendar.id` is `bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY` and the view projects it, so a transcription carrying `.primaryKey()` across would compile and would look faithful. It would offer an addressed write into a relation Postgres refuses as not auto-updatable. The transcription declares no key, `uniqueKeys` returns empty, and [ADR-112](ADR-112.md)'s refusal fires before the database is reached. Section 5.
>
> **4. A VIEW IS CHECKED AGAINST ITS OWN `CREATE VIEW` AND AGAINST THE FOLD OF WHAT IT PROJECTS.** The table assertions are not relaxed for it and are not skipped in silence: they are replaced, leg for leg, by two that are strictly stronger in the direction that matters. The transcribed column set must equal the view's SELECT list, and every projected column must be a column of the projected relation carrying that relation's folded TYPE and NULLABILITY. **A base column whose type moves in a later migration is now red on the view as well as on the table.** Section 6.
>
> **5. LEG 2 HOLDS ONLY WHILE THE PROJECTION IS BARE, AND THE READER REFUSES RATHER THAN GUESSES.** A cast, a function call or an `AS` alias makes *the base column's type* an inference. The reader THROWS on such a projected item by name. The day a view carries one, this goes red and that session rules on what the type of a computed view column is, rather than inheriting an answer this entry has no basis to give.
>
> **6. `affiliate_statements` IS `derived` VIA `affiliates` ON `affiliate_id`, AND THIS ENTRY DOES NOT RULE IT.** [`0012`](../../packages/db/migrations/0012_disputes_and_affiliate_settlement.sql) declares `affiliate_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES affiliates(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`, which is the identical edge `affiliate_creatives` and `affiliate_clicks` were registered on by earlier sessions. It is recorded here because it shares a slice and a fence, **not because a number was needed for it**. Section 3.
>
> **7. NEITHER PORT IS WIRED AND BOTH STILL REFUSE.** Each carries a second, independent blocker this entry does not reach. Section 9 states them and states what a wiring session still owes.

### 2. WHY THE VIEW IS THE THING TO REGISTER

**`0039` says it in the migration rather than leaving it to be inferred.** The `CREATE VIEW` is preceded by a header block titled *WHY A VIEW AND NOT A DOCUMENTED CONVENTION*, and its argument is that *"both consumers pick the max revision" is a sentence that has to be re-implemented correctly in the panel and in the detector, and the failure when one of them gets it wrong is the panel showing 08:30 while D-04 clusters against 09:00.* The `COMMENT ON VIEW` in the same file says the M04 panel and M07's `D-04` *"both read THIS and never economic_calendar directly, so a revised release time moves both or neither"*.

**The route already agrees, in a docblock that predates this entry.** `EconomicCalendarSource` carries the sentence *IT READS `economic_calendar_current` AND NEVER `economic_calendar`*, and `EconomicCalendarRow` is transcribed column for column from the view in the database's own snake case.

**So the choice was never between a view and a table.** It was between a keyed read of the relation the corpus designed for this read, and an adapter that reaches for the base table and re-derives `DISTINCT ON (event_key, occurrence_key) ORDER BY revision DESC` in application code. **The second is the design `0039` exists to refuse**, and refusing to register the view is how it would have arrived anyway.

### 3. `affiliate_statements` NEEDED NO RULING, AND SAYING SO IS THE POINT

The reservation provided for a class that might not be obvious. On this relation it is obvious twice over.

| | `affiliate_creatives` | `affiliate_clicks` | `affiliate_statements` |
|---|---|---|---|
| edge to an identity | `affiliate_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES affiliates(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT` | same | same |
| own identity column | none | none | none |
| registered class | `derived`, `hop`, via `affiliates` | `derived`, `hop`, via `affiliates` | `derived`, `hop`, via `affiliates` |
| registered by | an earlier session | an earlier session | **this one, with no ruling** |

[ADR-101](ADR-101.md) clause 1 has nothing to refuse, because the row declares no column against `identities(id)`. Clause 2 is satisfied, because the edge is `NOT NULL`, so there is no row that reaches no identity and none returned in silence. The traversal is a `hop` rather than a `semi-join` because `affiliates.id` is that table's primary key and the reference is single-valued in the direction traversed, so the join cannot multiply this row. The chain terminates one hop out, at an `owned` table.

**WHAT A SCOPED READ OF IT RETURNS IS THE THING WORTH STATING, and it is why this table parts from the one beside it in the same migration.** A statement row carries `total_cents` and no line items. The line items are `affiliate_commissions`, which stays UNREGISTERED (section 7). So a scoped read hands an affiliate their own periods and their own totals and **hands them nobody else's uuid** -- which is exactly the property that makes `derived` admissible here and refuses it one table over.

`total_cents bigint NOT NULL` is signed and a clawback-heavy month is negative, so a wrong rule here shows one affiliate what another is owed. `affiliates.balance_cents` one hop out is signed for the same reason and is already `owned`. `paid_transfer_ref text NULL` is the available mistake and is not a column any rule could name: it carries no foreign key at all, and it names a row in a payment provider's database rather than one in this one, which is `payout_destinations`' `destination_ref` argument arriving on the affiliate rail.

### 4. THE CLASS OF A VIEW, ARGUED ON WHAT A VIEW HAS

`scope.ts` partitions one question: **HOW DOES A ROW REACH AN IDENTITY?** Read against what `economic_calendar_current` actually declares:

| class | what it needs to name | what the view offers |
|---|---|---|
| `root` | `identities` itself | it is not |
| `owned` | a column of this row declared `REFERENCES identities(id)` | a view declares no `REFERENCES` at all |
| `derived` | a foreign key the DDL declares, to a via table | same absence; and `load_id`'s target `economic_calendar_loads` is itself `firm`, so the chain would terminate nowhere |
| `pair` | two identity columns, and a `CHECK` constraining them | a view carries no constraint |
| `either` | a nullable identity column beside a nullable account edge | neither column exists |
| `firm` | no path to an identity | **this** |

**Every one of those refusals is an absence rather than an argument, and that is the finding rather than a convenience.** A view is *structurally* unable to supply what five of the six classes name. If the class had to be argued from the view's own DDL, the answer would be `firm` for every view ever written, including one selecting `identity_id` out of an `owned` table -- which would be a tenancy hole with a mechanical justification. **So the class cannot come from the view. It has to come from the projection, and ruling 2 is that sentence.**

Here the two agree and the agreement is checked rather than assumed: `economic_calendar` is `firm`, the view selects ten of its columns unchanged, and `no firm table carries a column referencing identities` already reads the base table's DDL in the same loop.

### 5. NO KEY, AND WHY THAT IS THE SAFE DIRECTION

[ADR-112](ADR-112.md) reads an address out of the TRANSCRIPTION, because that is what a runtime can read. `uniqueKeys('economicCalendarCurrent')` returns empty, so `updateAt` and `deleteAt` reject with *declares no PRIMARY KEY or UNIQUE in schema.ts* before a statement is built.

**That refusal agrees with the database rather than substituting for it.** A `SELECT DISTINCT ON` view is not auto-updatable in PostgreSQL, so an INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE against it errors. The two refusals are independent and the tree's is the earlier one.

**The trap is real and it is one character wide.** The base table's `id` is a `PRIMARY KEY` and the view projects it. A transcription that carried `.primaryKey()` across would compile, would satisfy every column-name comparison, and would offer an addressed write into a relation that cannot take one. `treasury_balances` is the standing example of the OPPOSITE error -- a key the DDL declares and the transcription omits -- and the suite now names both relations and states that they are there for opposite reasons, so that a later reader "repairing" the view into addressability meets the sentence before the green tick.

### 6. WHAT REPLACES THE TABLE ASSERTIONS, AND WHY IT IS NOT A WEAKENING

Three assertions in `packages/db/test/scoped-db.test.ts` THROW on a relation with no `CREATE TABLE`, rather than failing: the per-relation column-set fold, the per-relation type and nullability fold, and the `either`-shape reader. **A registered view would have taken them down, which is the loud direction and is how this was found.**

The split is **DERIVED from the migrations** and never listed: `VIEWS_IN_MIGRATIONS()` matches `CREATE VIEW` over the same text the fold reads. A hand-written exclusion list would be this registry's own defect one level down -- a second statement of which relations are views, with nothing comparing it to the SQL.

Four assertions replace the three, per view:

1. **the transcribed column set is the view's SELECT list**, read out of `CREATE VIEW`;
2. **every projected column is the base relation's, type and nullability**, read out of the base's fold;
3. **it projects a REGISTERED relation and carries that relation's class** -- ruling 2, made mechanical;
4. **it declares no key**, and the addressed write is watched being refused.

And two guards keep the split honest, because the quiet direction here is a split that empties:

- **every registered relation is created exactly once, as exactly one KIND.** This widens the old `every registered table is created exactly once` from `CREATE TABLE` to `CREATE TABLE` plus `CREATE VIEW` over the WHOLE registry, so a relation created as neither -- which would fall out of both iterations and out of every per-relation drift assertion with them -- is red.
- **the table half and the view half are both non-empty and together are the registry.** A `VIEWS_IN_MIGRATIONS` that matched nothing would empty the view half, and the four assertions above would go from four to ZERO with nothing counting them.

**THAT SECOND GUARD CLAIMS LESS THAN IT WAS WRITTEN TO CLAIM, AND THE SEEDS ARE WHY.** It was written expecting the failure `SQL_NAME`'s own header records -- a suite going GREEN on a smaller test count -- and **both directions were seeded and NEITHER is silent.** With the view half emptied the guard goes red by name and eleven other assertions go red with it, because the view falls back into the table loop whose fold then throws. With the table half emptied the guard goes red and 449 assertions go red with it. **So on this shape the split cannot fail quietly, and the guard is redundant with a loud failure rather than the only thing standing between the suite and a false green.** What it still holds alone is the count: four view assertions going to zero is the one consequence nothing else observes. It is kept for that and its reach is stated here rather than assumed, because a guard believed to cover more than it does is worse than the one that is missing.

### 7. THE PREMISE THAT DID NOT SURVIVE

**The dispatch, the ALLOCATION row and the `BLOCKED` entry all draw a distinction between the two affiliate relations that does not exist.** They say `affiliate_commissions` is *UNREGISTERED in `scope.ts`* and `affiliate_statements` is *not in `schema.ts` at all*, which reads as one relation being a step ahead of the other. `apps/api/src/routes/affiliate.ts` states it outright, in a string a caller meets at runtime: `affiliate_statements` *"is one registration short of the position `affiliate_commissions` is in."*

**Derived at its source: `affiliate_commissions` is not in `schema.ts` either.** There is no `pgTable('affiliate_commissions', ...)`; the name appears in that file only inside two comments, both of them citing it as a refusal. **The two relations were in the SAME position and neither was one registration ahead of the other.**

**It changes nothing about what is registered and it changes what is REPORTED.** Registering `affiliate_commissions` would have needed a `schema.ts` entry, which the fence for this slice admits for `economicCalendarCurrent` and `affiliateStatements` and for nothing else -- so the sentence *"registering it needs a ruling"* was true, and incomplete: **it needs a ruling AND a transcription, and this session had a fence for neither.** The four statements in `apps/` that carry the distinction are listed in section 10 and are repaired by nobody here.

### 8. WHAT IS NOT DONE, AND WHY

**`TableKey` IS NOT RENAMED.** It is `keyof typeof TABLES` by construction and appears at several hundred call sites. A rename is a very large diff carrying no ruling, and a very large diff carrying no ruling is how a real one gets lost in review. The name is left and the docblock says what it now means. **A later session that wants `RelationKey` should take it as its own slice with nothing else in it.**

**`CI-06i` IS NOT WIDENED, AND THE VIEW GETS NO DESIGN RECORD OF ITS OWN.** That gate reconciles `CREATE TABLE` against `### <name>` records in BOTH directions, so a file `docs/architecture/data-model/economic_calendar_current.md` would fail it with *has a design record, no CREATE TABLE creates it*. **The tree had already answered this**: `economic_calendar.md` carries a `#### economic_calendar_current` subsection whose first sentence is *"A view, not a table, so it has no design record of its own beyond this note"* -- four hashes, so invisible to a gate that matches three. The registration is recorded there, in the file a reader checking one of these rows already opens. **Whether `CI-06i` should reconcile views as well is a real question and it is not this fence's**: the answer costs a convention for how a view's record declares itself, and there is exactly one view to design it against.

**NO WRITE-SIDE TYPE EXCLUDES A VIEW.** `unscopedUpdateStatement` and its siblings are generic over `TableKey`, so they type-admit the view today and are refused at runtime by ruling 3 and again by the database. **A `ViewTableKey` exclusion that refused it at COMPILE time would be better and it is a change to `scoped-db.ts`, outside this fence.** It is recorded as owed rather than taken quietly.

### 9. WHAT THIS UNBLOCKS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

**`setEconomicCalendarSource`: the FIRST blocker is cleared and the port still refuses.** `firmDb().rows('economicCalendarCurrent')` is now a keyed read where it was a `TS2322`, so an adapter can be written. The second blocker is untouched and independent: `freshness.stale` is decided against a **configured horizon that lives with the alarm** and not in this deployable, and a route reaching for a clock would compare a UTC date against an exchange CT trading day, which `0039` created `release_trading_day` specifically to stop being derived.

**`useAffiliateDeps`: one of four obstructions is cleared and the port still refuses.** `statements` has a door now. `stats` does not: its figures are sums over `affiliate_commissions`, which stays unregistered. `issueLink` does not: no table in this schema records an ISSUED link. And the port **already holds `productionAffiliateDeps` at module scope**, so calling its setter would install what is already installed -- which raises a count and serves nothing, and `wiring.test.ts` says in its own words that it is not a wiring.

**REGISTERING `affiliate_statements` DOES NOT MOVE `affiliate_commissions` ONE STEP CLOSER, and the direction of the dependency is worth stating because it looks like it should.** `affiliate_commissions.paid_in_statement_id` references `affiliate_statements`, so the statement table is the commission table's PARENT on that edge. It is not a path to an identity for it: the commission row's only such path is `attribution_id NOT NULL REFERENCES attributions(id)`, `attributions` is `pair`, and a derivation chain terminates at `owned` or at `root` or it does not terminate. **A `derived` rule via `attributions` would now COMPILE and would THROW the first time anybody read the table.** What it needs is a ruling about what a row derived from a TWO-PARTY row belongs to, and this entry does not make one. It also needs a `schema.ts` entry, per section 7.

**NEITHER PORT IS WIRED. A port that still refuses is the correct end state of this slice.**

### 11. SEEDS, WATCHED FIRING

> **1. SEED THE VIEW'S CLASS TO `owned`.** `projects a REGISTERED relation, and carries that relation's class` must go **RED**, which is ruling 2 being enforced rather than described.
>
> **2. SEED A COLUMN RENAME INTO THE TRANSCRIPTION.** `the transcribed column set is the view's SELECT list` must go **RED**: this is leg 1, and it is the assertion the `CREATE TABLE` fold could not make for this relation at all.
>
> **3. SEED `revision_reason` TO `.notNull()` ON THE VIEW.** `every projected column is the base relation's, type and nullability` must go **RED** on NULLABILITY, against `economic_calendar`'s fold and not against the view. This is leg 2 reaching through the projection.
>
> **4. SEED `.primaryKey()` ONTO THE VIEW'S `id`.** `declares no key` must go **RED**, and so must the write-accessor's two-relation set. This is ruling 3, and the seed is the exact edit a later reader "repairing" the view would make.
>
> **5. SEED AN `AS` ALIAS INTO `0039`'s SELECT LIST.** The reader must **THROW BY NAME** on the aliased item rather than reading past it. This is ruling 5, and a reader that guessed instead would silently compare the wrong column's type forever.
>
> **6. SEED `VIEWS_IN_MIGRATIONS` TO RETURN EVERY REGISTERED NAME.** `the table half and the view half are both non-empty` must go **RED**. Without it the suite goes GREEN on a smaller test count, which is the failure `SQL_NAME`'s header records happening once already.
>
> **7. SEED THE VIEW OUT OF `TABLES` WITH ITS RULE LEFT IN.** `typecheck` must report an error on the rule, which is the half vitest cannot see.

### 12. Approval

- **Approval:** `___________________________` (UNSIGNED. **Merging is not signing.**)

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO READ IS RULING 1, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY PLACE THIS ENTRY WIDENS A VOCABULARY RATHER THAN APPLYING ONE.** Everything else here is checkable: a reader can count the `CREATE VIEW` statements, can read the route's docblock beside `0039`'s, and can watch the seeds fire. **What is not forced is that a derived relation belongs in a registry whose whole suite was built around `CREATE TABLE`.** The honest alternative was to refuse the view and let the adapter read the base table, and this entry rejects it on one ground: that adapter must re-derive `DISTINCT ON ... ORDER BY revision DESC`, nothing would compare its answer to the view's, and `GS-286` asserts the panel and `D-04` move together *because there is no "both" to move separately*. **A founder who thinks a registry should hold only tables should say so now**, because the cost of reversing later is that same derivation living in an adapter with a test nobody wrote.

  **THE SECOND IS THAT RULING 2 IS A GENERAL RULE INFERRED FROM ONE INSTANCE.** There is exactly one view in this tree and its base is `firm`, so *the class of a view is the class of what it projects* is untested against the interesting case: a view over an `owned` table, where the rule would produce a scoped view whose predicate names a column the view may or may not project. **Section 4 argues the rule is nonetheless forced** -- the alternative makes every view `firm`, including one selecting `identity_id` out of an owned table, which is a tenancy hole with a mechanical justification. **A reader who finds that argument thin should require the next view to come with its own entry** rather than inherit this one.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left to be found.** **Section 8's decision not to exclude a view from the write-side types at COMPILE time.** Today a `TableKey`-generic write statement admits the view and is refused twice at runtime, which is safe and is not the standard this package holds elsewhere -- `ScopedTableKey` and `FirmTableKey` both refuse at compile time and the file's own comments call that the point. **The check that settles whether it matters takes one minute: open `scoped-db.ts` and read whether `unscopedUpdateStatement`'s callers are reachable from a route.** If any is, the exclusion should be commissioned before an adapter lands rather than after.
