### 2. THE MEASUREMENT THAT DECIDED THE DOOR'S SHAPE, WHICH REFUSED THE OBVIOUS ONE

#### 2.1 How it was taken

`0001`..`0082` applied forward-only from empty under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1` against **PostgreSQL 16.13**, the estate [ADR-327](ADR-327.md), [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) and [ADR-332](ADR-332.md) each measured on. **118 base tables in `public` and 12 in `pgboss`**, which is those entries' count reproduced rather than copied. Every statement ran as `merit_app`: `0026` creates that role `NOLOGIN`, so the probe connected as a login role whose `role` is set to it, and the first line of every transcript below reads `current_user` off the connection rather than asserting it.

#### 2.2 THE OBVIOUS DOOR WAS `start()` AND EVERYTHING ELSE, AND THE VENDOR'S OWN CACHE SAID IT DID NOT HAVE TO BE

Both prior rows drove the queue as `start()`, then `declareQueue`, then `enqueue`, and the sequence reads as required. **It is not.** Verbatim, on a queue constructed over `poolSqlExecutor('job-supervisor')` with **NO `start()` at all**:

```
--- A. role: {"current_user":"merit_app"}
--- A. declareQueue BEFORE start() returned: "ok"
--- A. enqueue BEFORE start() returned: "a job id"
--- A. start() returned: "ok"
--- A. declareQueue AFTER start() returned: "ok"
--- A. enqueue AFTER start() returned: "a job id"
```

**THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS ROW EXPECTED THE OPPOSITE AND HAD READ THE SOURCE FOR IT.** `manager.js`'s `getQueueCache` opens `assert(this.queues, 'Queue cache is not initialized')`, and `this.queues` is populated by `onCacheQueues()`, which `manager.start()` awaits; every `send` reaches it. That reads as "enqueue requires start". **The line that decides it is the CONSTRUCTOR's**, `this.queues = {}`, so the assert passes on an empty object and `getQueueCache` falls through to a per-name read of `pgboss.queue`. **A reading of a control flow was wrong and one run said so**, which is why the run was taken.

#### 2.3 AND THE COST OF `start()` IN A ONE-SHOT DEPLOYABLE WAS MEASURED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS

[ADR-241](ADR-241.md) rules this deployable's schedule EXTERNAL, one process per run, and [`db.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/db.ts)'s `closeWorkerDb` docblock states the consequence in its own words: *"this one is a ONE-SHOT JOB ... A batch that completed and then hung is a job a supervisor reads as still running and a dead-man switch reads as never finished."* So the question is what `start()` does to a process that means to exit. Three runs, same estate, `timeout 25`:

```
### the door: declareQueue, then closeWorkerDb, then end of module
--- H. the DOOR declared a queue and closed the pool; end of module
timeout-exit=0

### the raw adapter: declareQueue, start(), then closeWorkerDb
timeout-exit=1
Error: Cannot use a pool after calling end on the pool
    at Object.executeSql (.../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts:4329:43)
    at Timekeeper.onCron (.../pg-boss/dist/timekeeper.js:154:48)
Emitted 'error' event on PgBoss instance at:
    at Timekeeper.<anonymous> (.../pg-boss/dist/index.js:83:43)

### the raw adapter: declareQueue, start(), then nothing
--- I. start() returned; the module has nothing left to do
timeout-exit=124   (still running after 25 seconds)
```

**BOTH HALVES OF THE HARM, MEASURED, AND NEITHER IS HYPOTHETICAL.** A process that starts the supervisor and then releases the pool, which is the only way this deployable can exit, **DIES WITH EXIT 1 on an uncaught `error` event whose stack names a vendor's `dist` and a connection pool and nothing that names the queue.** A process that starts it and does not release the pool **NEVER EXITS**. The door's shape is decided by those two lines, and the third is what it does instead.

#### 2.4 WHERE A SUPERVISE FAILURE ACTUALLY SURFACES, INJECTED AT THE ONE SEAM A CALLER HAS

[ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 10 item 3 and [ADR-332](ADR-332.md) section 10 item 2 both record that pg-boss emits supervise failures on an emitter `pgBossQueue` does not expose. **That is re-derived here deterministically rather than cited**, by wrapping the shipped executor so that the first statement matching the supervise vocabulary rejects, and waiting one interval:

```
--- G. start() returned and the next supervise statement will fail
--- G. waiting 70s for one supervise pass
--- G. failures observed: 1
--- G. >>: "unhandledRejection: Error: ADR-333 probe: the supervisor s own statement failed"
--- G. methods on the value a caller holds: ["consume","declareQueue","enqueue","start","stop"]
```

and, separately, on the value itself: `--- F. any of them an emitter?: false`. **FIVE METHODS, NONE OF THEM AN EMITTER, AND THE FAILURE ARRIVES AS AN UNHANDLED REJECTION.** A caller of `JobQueue` has no channel. That is the gap two rows have now named, and this row is the first that had to decide what to do about it rather than record it.

### 3. THE RULING

> **1. THE ONE DOOR IS [`apps/worker/src/queue.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/queue.ts), AND ONE IMPORTER IS STRUCTURAL BECAUSE THE PATTERN IS ONE FILE PER *PACKAGE*.**
>
> `@merit/db` names `src/db.ts`, `@merit/ledger` names `src/sweeps/ledger.ts`, and `@merit/queue` names `src/queue.ts`. **So the door imports `@merit/db` NOWHERE**: it takes its constructor executor from `src/db.ts`'s new `queueExecutor()`, and the two halves meet structurally, because `SqlExecutor` and `JobTransaction` are the same one-method shape with no import in either direction ([ADR-102](ADR-102.md)). Section 4 is why that placement is the whole of the claim.
>
> **2. THE DOOR PUBLISHES TWO OF `JobQueue`'s FIVE METHODS.** `declareQueue` and `enqueue` are published. **`start`, `stop` and `consume` are WITHHELD**, on section 2.3 and 2.4's measurements: a supervise failure has no channel to any caller, and in this deployable `start()` is a process that either crashes on exit or does not exit. The absence is by CONSTRUCTION, which is `src/db.ts`'s own idiom for the reason parameter it declines to accept: there is no method and no argument position.
>
> **3. SO THE ANSWER TO "WHAT DOES THE DOOR DO WITH THE SUPERVISOR'S FAILURES" IS THAT IT MAKES THIS DEPLOYABLE UNABLE TO PRODUCE ONE, AND SURFACING THEM IS OWED ELSEWHERE.** A `JobQueue` value has five methods and none is an emitter, so **no door written in `apps/worker` can surface them**: the channel does not exist to be forwarded. Widening the interface is [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s narrowness criterion and `packages/queue`'s subject, and [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) clause 5 and [ADR-331](ADR-331.md) section 4 clause 4 both rule that a change of that kind is taken by a row whose OBJECT it is. **Taking it here would be taking it as a MEANS. IT IS OWED AND IT IS NOT TAKEN**, and section 10 item 1 states what the row that takes it must meet.
>
> **4. THE TWO EXECUTORS ARE TWO CONNECTION LIFETIMES AND THE TYPE REFUSES THE SWAP IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** Construction takes the POOL executor spending `'job-supervisor'`; enqueue takes the CALLER's open transaction spending `'job-enqueue'`, in a required first argument position. Section 5 proves the refusal rather than asserting it.
>
> **5. NOTHING IS WIRED AND NOTHING IS SCHEDULED.** [ADR-326](ADR-326.md) section 4 stands entire: the blockers on the eleven unscheduled entry points are ADAPTERS rather than clocks, and **the withdrawal driver gets no clock at all until the payment rail exists**, because a live clock in front of it posts `LT-06` into `approved`, a state with no exit and no cancel. That is founder-owed and nothing here changes it.

### 4. WHAT MAKES ONE IMPORTER STRUCTURAL, WHICH IS THE ONLY QUESTION A DOOR ROW REALLY HAS

**THE HONEST ANSWER STARTS WITH WHAT IS *NOT* STRUCTURAL, BECAUSE THE ACCESSOR'S CASE AND THE QUEUE'S ARE NOT THE SAME.** `RI-08` guards `@merit/db` at the MANIFEST over every workspace package and runs in `CI-01`; `apps/worker/src/db.ts`'s own header says the file-level half is what `RI-08` cannot see and that `test/db.test.ts` is the assertion. **`@merit/queue` IS ON NO ADMISSION LIST AT ALL.** There is no `RI-08` half here. So for this capability the file-level assertion is not the second control, it is the ONLY one, and saying so is the first thing this section owes.

**WHAT IS STRUCTURAL IS THE SHAPE THAT MAKES THE ASSERTION IMPOSSIBLE TO SATISFY TWO WAYS**, and it is three legs.

**LEG 1. ONE FILE PER PACKAGE, WHICH IS THE MANIFEST'S OWN WORDS AND NOT A NEW RULE.** That comment reads *"`@merit/ledger` above names `src/sweeps/ledger.ts` and `@merit/db` names `src/db.ts`, on the ONE-DOOR pattern ADR-165 set"*. **THE CONSEQUENCE IS THAT THE POOL EXECUTOR CANNOT LIVE IN THE QUEUE DOOR.** `poolSqlExecutor` is exported from `packages/db`, so a queue door that reached for it would be a SECOND importer of the accessor, and `test/db.test.ts`'s `expect(importing).toEqual(['src/db.ts'])` — an assertion standing since session 292 — would have had to become a two-element list. **A row that loosens a standing assertion in order to land its own subject has not built a control, it has spent one**, and that is the shape [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) section 3 refuses in a different costume. So `src/db.ts` gains `queueExecutor()` and the door takes it as a parameter with a live default.

**LEG 2. THE ASSERTION IS AN EXACT LIST IN A SHAPE THE ESTATE ALREADY RUNS, AND THE DISPATCH WAS RIGHT THAT ONE EXISTED.** [`ledger-posting-authority.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/ledger-posting-authority.test.ts) states the reason in its own words: *"`toEqual([])` was what made the FIRST adapter fail this case; an exact list is what makes the SECOND one fail it, and a `length` check would have made this case retire itself the moment it was first turned."* That is exactly this row's position: the list was `[]` for three rows and is `['./queue.ts']` now, and a `toContain` or a count would have retired the case on the day it first bit. **Both cases are exact lists** — `queue.test.ts`'s and `schedule.test.ts` 5.1's — and 5.1 asserts the manifest line and the named file in ONE case so neither half can drift alone.

**LEG 3. THE INSTRUMENT IS THE IMPORT STATEMENT AND NOT THE STRING, AND THAT WAS MEASURED HERE RATHER THAN INHERITED.** [ADR-165](ADR-165.md) section 9 records paying for this once: a substring test *"failed on `src/provisioning/ports.ts`, whose header names `@merit/db` in order to state that it does not import it"*. **THIS ROW WALKED INTO THE SAME HOLE AND THE TREE SAID SO.** The first draft of `queue.test.ts` matched `from '@merit/db'` anywhere in a file and reported **SEVEN** importers of the accessor, six of them `ports.ts` headers quoting the import form in order to say they do not use it. The instrument is now `test/db.test.ts`'s: an `import` or an `export ... from` at the head of a line. **The identical mistake was made a second time in the `RI-35` register**, section 8.

**AND WHAT IS NOT CLOSED IS STATED PLAINLY.** A module could still construct `pgBossQueue` by importing it through a relative path from a file that re-exported it, and nothing here would see that; the reason it cannot today is that the door is not a barrel leg and re-exports nothing of the vendor's. **A second deployable taking `@merit/queue` into its manifest is invisible to every gate in this repository**, because there is no `DB_ADMITTED` for the queue. That is a real hole, it is not this row's to close, and section 10 item 3 names it.

### 6. THE SHIPPED DOOR, DRIVEN END TO END AGAINST THE SAME ESTATE

Verbatim, on `apps/worker/src/queue.ts` and `apps/worker/src/db.ts` as they land, with `unhandledRejection` and `uncaughtException` counted:

```
--- B. role on a pooled connection: {"current_user":"merit_app"}
--- B. the door publishes: ["declareQueue","enqueue"]
--- B. declareQueue() returned, with NO start(): "ok"
--- C. enqueue inside a transaction that COMMITS returned: "a job id"
--- C. the caller threw, so transaction() ROLLED BACK: "ok"
--- C. enqueue inside a transaction that ROLLS BACK returned: "a job id"
--- C2. jobs on the queue after one COMMIT and one ROLLBACK: 1
--- C2. the committed id is present: true
--- C2. the rolled-back id is present: false
--- D. inside a transaction, before declareQueue: {"t":true}
--- D. inside the SAME transaction, AFTER declareQueue on the POOL door: {"t":true}
--- E. backends this run opened: {"n":2}
--- END. failures observed: 0
--- END. closeWorkerDb() returned; the process may now exit
```

**`D` IS THE CONTRAST THAT MAKES THE WHOLE SEQUENCE WORTH THREE ROWS, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IT HAS BEEN MEASURED ON SHIPPED CODE.** `now() <> statement_timestamp()` is `true` inside a transaction that has run a statement and `false` in autocommit. [ADR-332](ADR-332.md) section 2.2 measured that pair going **`true` then `false`** across ONE `declareQueue` on the transaction-bound executor. Here it reads **`true` then `true`**: the caller's transaction is still open after a `locked()` plan, because the plan ran on the pool. **The failure ADR-331 and ADR-332 each measured is the one this door structurally cannot have.**

**AND `enqueue` IS RE-MEASURED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE PATH THAT WORKED BEFORE AND THE ONE THING THIS ROW COULD BREAK.** Enqueued through `tx.sqlExecutor('job-enqueue')` inside a transaction that COMMITS, the job is on the queue; inside one that ROLLS BACK, it is not. That is [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)'s property, executed against PostgreSQL through the shipped door rather than through a rig.

**TWO BACKENDS FOR THE WHOLE RUN**, which is [ADR-332](ADR-332.md) leg 4's cost claim holding on the one caller that exists: every statement pg-boss sends here is balanced, so no connection is burned. **AND THE PROCESS EXITED 0**, which is the property `closeWorkerDb`'s docblock is about: the queue needs no `close` of its own, because it runs on the pool that function already releases.
