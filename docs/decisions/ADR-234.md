## ADR-234: The terminal edge is `cancelled`, because it is the only exit money never travels down, and a rail that cannot report cannot be settled by an operator either  (2026-08-29, status: proposed)

**[ADR-232](ADR-232.md) built the approval edge and proved its own dispatch wrong about what that would achieve, and this entry is the half it found actually missing.** `approved` is one of the four members of `OPEN_WITHDRAWAL_STATUSES` ([`wallet-withdrawals.ts:288-293`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts), which is `wallet_withdrawals_open_idx`'s own predicate at [`0011:205-207`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)), so approving a withdrawal moves it from one open status to another and `gateNoInFlight` refuses the identity's next withdrawal exactly as before. **What releases an identity is `settled`, `failed` or `cancelled`, the three arrows [STATE_MACHINES section 3.2](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) draws into `[*]`, and nothing in this tree drove any of them.**

**ONE OF THE THREE IS NOW DRIVEN AND THE OTHER TWO ARE RULED UNREACHABLE, PER STATUS, EACH WITH THE COMMAND THAT SETTLES IT.** The row that dispatched this session asked for a test in which a withdrawal is requested, approved, carried to terminal, and a second withdrawal by the same identity is then accepted. **That sequence is closed on the cancel arm and is measured as still refused on the approved arm**, and the second half of that sentence is the finding rather than a shortfall against the first.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `234`, session **424**, dispatched as `claude/the-terminal-edge-releases-the-identity`. **Migration `0072` is TAKEN AND SPENT.** `0071` is session 423's and is transcribed into the migration table rather than claimed, which is `0069`'s move one wave earlier and for the identical reason.

- **MONEY PATH: money leaving the firm. `E2` READ IS OWED AND NOTHING HERE DISCHARGES IT.** [`0072`](../../packages/db/migrations/0072_terminal_withdrawal_transitions.sql) carries an `E2 READ: MONEY PATH` header. This entry ships `proposed` with an **UNSIGNED** approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **WHAT IS MEASURED, WHAT IS DERIVED, AND WHAT IS JUDGED.** That the seventeen cases in section 6 refuse and permit what they are said to is **MEASURED**, against PostgreSQL 16.13 with all 66 migrations applied forward-only under `ON_ERROR_STOP=1`. That `packages/rail` has no importer, no live adapter and no writer of `transferring` is **DERIVED** by search, at the scope each claim names, and three of those searches are now assertions in `wallet-withdrawals.test.ts` rather than sentences. What is **JUDGED** is that `settled` is not an operator action either (section 2), that a cancellation carries no guard except the halt (section 3), that the halt holds a cancellation at all (section 3), and that the transition is written without a door rather than driven through one this corpus declines to invent (section 5).

- **A DISPATCH PROMPT IS NOT A SOURCE.** Every claim below was read at its own file. The dispatch's own escape hatch did not survive that reading, and section 2 is why.

### 2. Whether an operator may settle a withdrawal the rail never reported. **NO, AND THIS IS THE ONE PLACE THE DISPATCH'S OWN PERMISSION IS DECLINED**

**The row granted, in terms, that "if the rail cannot report, then the terminal edge is an OPERATOR action and not an automatic one, and that is a legitimate ruling".** The rail cannot report. The ruling is still refused on `settled`, and the ground is the sentence immediately after that grant in the same row: *"a withdrawal marked settled that never left is the worst row in this system"*.

**AN OPERATOR ACTION IS A LEGITIMATE SUBSTITUTE FOR AN AUTOMATIC ONE ONLY WHERE THE OPERATOR CAN OBSERVE WHAT THE AUTOMATION WOULD HAVE.** On a rail that exists and merely lacks a webhook, an operator reading the provider's console is a real witness and an operator-driven settlement records a fact. **In THIS tree there is no provider and no console.** `packages/rail`'s own index says it opens no socket and names no vendor SDK; the only implementation of the port is `SandboxRail`, which lives under `src/fakes/`; and nothing imports the package at all, so no code path in this estate has ever asked a rail for anything. **Money cannot have left, so there is nothing for an operator to have observed, and an operator door would record an assertion with no possible referent.**

**AND THE SAME MEASUREMENT KILLS THE AUTOMATIC FORM TWICE OVER, WHICH IS WORTH STATING BECAUSE IT IS THE SHORTER PROOF.** `settled` and `failed` are drawn only out of `transferring`, and `transferring` is reached only by `G-TRANSFER-QUEUED`, which enqueues. Derived over `apps/**` and `packages/**` outside test files: the string `'transferring'` appears at three sites in two files, and all three are vocabulary declarations rather than writes. **The status is not merely undriven, it is unreachable**, and a terminal edge above it would be a door onto a landing that does not exist.

> **RULED: `settled` IS NOT BUILT, IN EITHER FORM, AND THE ABSENCE IS THE DECISION.** It is not held on a ruling anybody can take; it is held on a rail, and the fix is a vendor, an adapter and a webhook rather than a branch of a function. **`failed` is refused on the same ground plus a second one**, section 4.

**THE REFUSAL IS MADE CHECKABLE RATHER THAN LEFT AS PROSE.** `TERMINAL_EDGE_FINDINGS` ([`wallet-withdrawals.ts:1803`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)) carries one finding per status in `LT_07_FINDINGS`' shape, and `wallet-withdrawals.test.ts` **RUNS** three of them: the importer scan, the `'transferring'` scan and the adapter-implementation scan. **The day a consumer of `packages/rail` lands, this suite goes red and finding `A` is due a re-read**, which is the trap the entry exists to set rather than a sentence a reader has to trust.

### 3. The edge that does land, and the two guards it does not carry

**`cancelled` IS BUILDABLE TODAY BECAUSE IT IS THE EXIT MONEY NEVER TRAVELS DOWN, AND `0057` ALREADY SAID SO IN ITS OWN `COMMENT ON FUNCTION`**: `cancelled` discharges nothing *"because LT-06 never posted -- cancelled is reachable only from requested and cooling, both before approval"* ([`0057:257`](../../packages/db/migrations/0057_terminal_withdrawal_obligation.sql)). A cancellation moves no money, observes no rail and posts no ledger transaction. **That is why it is the one arrow a tree with neither a rail nor a ledger authority can draw.**

**`decideCancellation` IS TOTAL AND PURE AND `driveCancellation` IS THE TRANSITION OVER ONE TRANSACTION**, which is `decideApproval` and `driveApprovals`' shape and their reason.

**`CANCELLATION_HOLDS` HAS TWO MEMBERS WHERE `APPROVAL_HOLDS` HAS EIGHT, AND THE SHORTNESS IS THE RULING.** Every one of approval's terms guards a movement: the identity's standing, the freeze, the KYC chain, the provenance, the destination window, the second hand. **A cancellation moves nothing.** The money is where it already was, the trader is withdrawing their own request, and a guard that refused it would trap the row open and the identity behind it, **which is the lockout this edge exists to end**. So there is no identity term, no KYC term, no destination term, and **no dual control at any amount**, and each absence is a sentence in the module rather than an omission.

**THE HALT IS THE ONE HOLD AND IT IS A RULING THE DATABASE DOES NOT AGREE WITH.** EXECUTED, case `Q` of section 6: every constraint on this table **PERMITS** an `UPDATE` carrying a halted `requested` row to `cancelled`, because the halt is orthogonal to the rail status (section 3.2) and nothing in the schema reads it on this arrow.

> **RULED, fail closed, on [ADR-232](ADR-232.md) section 5's direction one edge over.** A halt is an investigation whose SUBJECT is this row; cancelling destroys the subject; and a trader who may cancel a halted withdrawal may open a fresh one the same second, which is the investigation routed around rather than resolved. **A founder or a later ADR may overturn this and it is one branch of one function.**

**A DRIVER THAT CANNOT FIND THE ROW HOLDS, AND DOES NOT THROW.** The accessor scopes every read to the caller's identity before this module sees a row, so an `id` resolving to nothing is either another trader's row or no row, and **those two must be indistinguishable from outside**: a driver that threw on one and held on the other would answer whether an arbitrary withdrawal id belongs to somebody else.

### 4. `0072`, and the sentence `0057` rested on that nothing enforced

**`WD-C1` HOLDS THREE DIFFERENT WAYS AND ITS OWN COMMENT SAYS WHICH, AND ONE OF THE THREE IS A CLAIM ABOUT AN ARROW SET RATHER THAN ABOUT A ROW.** `settled` discharges through `LT-07`, `failed` through `LT-09`, and `cancelled` because `LT-06` never posted. **The third reason is true only if `cancelled` really is unreachable from `approved`, and EXECUTED against this estate before `0072` was written, `UPDATE wallet_withdrawals SET status = 'cancelled'` FROM `approved` LANDS.**

**IT LANDS HARMLESSLY TODAY AND STOPS BEING HARMLESS ON THE DAY THE POSTING ARRIVES.** No door drives the approval edge, so `LT-06` never posts and `WD-C1`'s sum really is over zero rows. The moment a door does, a cancel from `approved` leaves the obligation standing with the trader's wallet claim already extinguished, **and the money is in neither place the trader could be shown it, which is the exact defect `0057` was written to refuse arriving through the one arrow `0057` assumed did not exist.**

**A CONTROL WHOSE CORRECTNESS RESTS ON A TRANSITION NOBODY REFUSES IS HALF A MECHANISM, AND THIS TABLE HAS PAID FOR THAT TWICE.** `0011` made the halt *"representable and unenforced for four migrations"* (section 3.2's own indented note), and `0038` shipped a dual-control threshold with no ceiling that a `$1,000,000` credit walked past eleven days later ([ADR-228](ADR-228.md), `0068`). **The arrow set is the third of the same shape and it is closed before the posting that makes it expensive rather than after.**

**WHAT `0072` ADDS.**

- **`cancelled_at`**, which was **the only terminal status with no clock**. `0011` gave `settled` its `settled_at`; `0070` gave `approved` its `approved_at` and, by exempting only `requested`, `cooling` and `cancelled`, requires one on `failed` too. Two constraints in `0070`'s own pair shape: the status requires the clock and the clock requires the status.
- **`WD-C2`, two assertions under one id**, which is `WD-C1`'s shape and `assert_adjustment_reversal_is_sound`'s with six. **A terminal status is never LEFT**, and **is ENTERED only along an arrow section 3.2 draws, and never at `INSERT`**.

**IT IS `NOT DEFERRABLE` WHERE `WD-C1` IS DEFERRED, AND THAT IS NOT A PREFERENCE.** `WD-C1` reads `ledger_transactions` rows its own transaction is still writing, so it cannot run at statement time; `WD-C2` reads `OLD` and `NEW` of one row and has nothing to wait for. **The ordering this creates with `0031` is load bearing and is EXECUTED rather than argued**: case `P` of section 6 drives `approved --> settled` on a frozen row and the refusal still names `wallet_withdrawals_live_freeze_blocks_settlement`, because a `CHECK` is evaluated as the row is written and an `AFTER ... FOR EACH ROW` trigger fires after the statement's rows are. **`probe_payout_hold.sql` passes unchanged and still tests the halt**, which is why no probe needed repairing and this session's fence was not extended.

**IT IS TWO TRIGGERS OVER ONE FUNCTION AND POSTGRESQL DECIDED THAT, NOT A PREFERENCE.** A `WHEN` clause on a trigger covering `INSERT` may not reference `OLD`, and assertion 1 is a question about `OLD`. EXECUTED: `CREATE TRIGGER` refuses it at `CREATE` time with *"INSERT trigger's WHEN condition cannot reference OLD values"*, and `TG_OP` is not in scope in a `WHEN` clause either. The alternative was one trigger with no `WHEN` clause and the filtering hidden in PL/pgSQL, which fires on every write to this table including the halt sweep's.

**AND THE TYPE ERROR IS RECORDED WHERE IT HAPPENED RATHER THAN QUIETLY FIXED.** The membership test was first written `= ANY (ARRAY['settled','failed','cancelled']::text[])`, which raises *"operator does not exist: wallet_withdrawal_status = text"* **at trigger time rather than at `CREATE` time**, which is a 500 on the money path on a row the trigger was refusing. **`scripts/db/probe_ledger_constraints.sql` went red on it**, on the one drawn arrow any fixture in this estate actually walks, and the `IN` form's literals are `unknown` and coerce to the enum.

### 5. Why no door drives this edge either, and why that is not the same absence as ADR-232's

**The transition is written, guarded, tested and called by nothing**, which is ADR-232's position one arrow over. **THE OBSTRUCTION IS DIFFERENT AND SMALLER AND IT IS WORTH SAYING WHICH.** ADR-232's approval edge has no driver because a driver would have to post `LT-06` in the same transaction and no door in `apps/api` may open the ledger authority ([ADR-165](ADR-165.md), [ADR-172](ADR-172.md) clause 2). **A cancellation posts nothing at all.** Its driver is blocked by one thing only: **there is no route.**

**AND THE ROUTE IS DECLINED RATHER THAN MISSING.** [API_CONTRACT:732](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) states in terms that *"There is no endpoint that cancels a withdrawal"*, that no approved document states a route, a body or an error set for it, and that it is *"named here as owed rather than invented"*. That document is `approved` and is outside this session's fence, so minting the row would be inventing exactly what it declines to invent. **ADR-232 refused the same move for the approval edge and this entry does not weaken that restraint for the neighbouring arrow.**

**SO THE PORT STAYS BLOCKED AND ITS REASON IS CORRECTED FOR THE FIFTH TIME.** `useWithdrawalBackend`'s `BLOCKED` entry in [`wiring.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/wiring.test.ts) has now named five obstructions and **each correction was a claim that did not survive being checked**: an absent `IdempotencyStore` (ADR-172 clause 5 refuted it), the missing approval edge (ADR-232 refuted it), the missing terminal edge, and now **the terminal edge that exists and has no door**. The entry carries two live decision procedures rather than one: `grep -rn driveApprovals apps/api/src` returns **3** lines and `grep -rn driveCancellation apps/api/src` returns **3**, in each case the transition and two docblock references and no caller, and `RI-20` runs both.

**THE HONEST 503 IS KEPT FOR THE REASON IT WAS ALWAYS KEPT FOR.** A 503 and a lockout both refuse and only one of them is reversible.

### 7. What was refused

- **An operator door onto `settled`**, which is section 2 and is the one place this entry declines a permission its own dispatch granted.
- **Inventing an HTTP route for the cancellation**, which `API_CONTRACT` names as owed and which is outside this fence. ADR-232 refused the same move on the neighbouring arrow.
- **An `approved --> cancelled` arrow**, which is not in the drawing, which `0057` assumed did not exist, and which `0072` now refuses at the database.
- **Any guard on the cancellation except the halt.** A guard on an act that moves no money is a lockout with a reason attached.
- **Extending `WD-C2` to the three NON-terminal arrows.** `requested --> cooling`, `--> approved` and `approved --> transferring` stay application-enforced: `0070` owns the approval neighbourhood and binding its arrow from outside it, one migration later, would put two files in charge of one transition.

- **Editing `0011`, `0031`, `0057` and `0070`**, which are merged, and **`MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md`**, which is frozen.
- **`packages/db/src/scope.ts` and `scoped-db.ts`**, which are session 423's this wave and were **read and not written**. `walletWithdrawals` is already registered `owned` on `identity_id`, so nothing there needed to move.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO DECIDE IS SECTION 2, BECAUSE IT IS WHERE THIS SESSION DECLINED A PERMISSION IT WAS GIVEN.** The dispatch said that if the rail cannot report, an operator-driven terminal edge is a legitimate ruling. **This entry refuses that on `settled`**, on the ground that there is no rail for an operator to have observed, so the door would record an assertion about a payment no code in this estate could have made. **If you would rather have an operator-marked `settled` now** -- for a wire sent by hand, outside the system, while the rail is being built -- **say so, and know what it costs**: `wallet_withdrawals` becomes a place where a status can mean "somebody typed this", the ledger has no posting to match it, and `WD-C1` passes vacuously on `settled` by `0057`'s own header item 7. **That is a real product need and it is a different design, with a column recording the out-of-band reference, not a branch of this one.**

  **THE SECOND IS THE HALT, SECTION 3, AND IT IS THE SAME QUESTION ADR-232 ASKED ONE ARROW OVER.** No approved document says whether a halted withdrawal may be cancelled and this entry fails closed. **The cost is a trader whose withdrawal is halted for a review that is later dismissed, who could not withdraw their request in the meantime and could not open another.** If you would rather a trader always be able to take back their own request, that is one branch of one function, and the argument for it is that a cancellation returns money to where it already was.

  **THE THIRD IS THAT THE PORT IS STILL BLOCKED AND THE REMAINING GAP IS NOW SMALL AND NAMED.** Section 5. The cancellation edge needs **one route** and nothing else: no ledger authority, no rail, no posting. That route needs a row in `API_CONTRACT`, which is `approved` and moves by ADR. **This is the cheapest thing on the money path that would turn a permanent lockout into a reversible one**, and it is worth deciding whether the next session is that ADR.

  **THE ONE OBJECTION WORTH CHECKING IN A MINUTE.** `WD-C2` refuses `INSERT` at any terminal status, which means **a future data migration or backfill cannot create a historical settled withdrawal row**. That is deliberate and it is the assertion that makes "born terminal" impossible, but it is a real constraint on operations and the escape is a migration that drops and re-adds the trigger around the load, which is deploy access rather than route access.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** **The whole `settled` and `failed` half of `WD-C2` is unexercised by any writer and will stay that way until a rail exists.** Cases `E`, `F`, `G`, `J` and `L` were executed by hand against the database rather than through a door, and the arrows they bind are arrows nothing in this estate can walk. **The constraints are live from the moment a writer exists and not before**, which is what [ADR-228](ADR-228.md) said about `account_adjustments`, what ADR-232 said about the operator arm of the approval edge, and the third consecutive entry in this neighbourhood to say it.
