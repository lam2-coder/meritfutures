## ADR-200: a verified code creates the identity, the consumption is ruled and guarded, and the two pre-identity doors are narrowed at the door rather than refused  (2026-08-28, status: proposed)

**[ADR-196](ADR-196.md) ruled where an identity comes from and priced the build at four rulings, honestly reporting itself as 1 of 4. [ADR-197](ADR-197.md) was 2 and 3. THIS IS THE FOURTH, and `POST /auth/verify` answers.** What it adds beyond transcription is four rulings those two leave open, and the reason each was open is that it is a property of a HANDLER rather than of a moment, a digest or a door.

- **Origin:** [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) row `200`, session **376**, dispatched as `claude/verify-handler-identity`. **Migration `0063` was reserved CONDITIONALLY and it is TAKEN**, for a hole the reservation did not name and this slice measured. Section 5, and section 5.1 says plainly which limb of the condition failed.

- **MONEY PATH (auth). [`E2`](../../MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md) READ IS OWED** on this ruling and on `0063`. The entry ships `proposed` with an UNSIGNED approval line. **Merging is not signing.**

- **NO MERGED MIGRATION IS EDITED.** [`0002`](../../packages/db/migrations/0002_identity.sql), [`0026`](../../packages/db/migrations/0026_roles_and_grants.sql), [`0029`](../../packages/db/migrations/0029_phone_identity_and_auth.sql) and [`0054`](../../packages/db/migrations/0054_identity_ledger_accounts.sql) are byte for byte unchanged, and `otp_challenges_unconsumed_uq` is NOT dropped.

- **WHAT IS DERIVED AND WHAT IS JUDGED, separated before the argument starts.** That two concurrent verifications of one code mint two sessions without a database object is **EXECUTED**, in both directions, through the shipped handler (section 5). That `otp_challenges_unconsumed_uq` enforces nothing is **DERIVED**, from the column list of the index it is declared on. That `sms` cannot be answered at all is **DERIVED**, from a one-member vocabulary in `packages/db`. What is **JUDGED** is that the two new doors are not the door [ADR-171](ADR-171.md) refused (section 3), that consumption precedes establishment (section 4.3), and the session lifetime (section 6).

- **THE HONEST STATE OF THE PRODUCT, FIRST RATHER THAN LAST: A TRADER STILL CANNOT SIGN UP.** `requestOtp` is blocked on DELIVERY and delivery is a vendor integration in nobody's fence, so **nothing in this deployable writes the `otp_challenges` row this handler reads**. What changed is that a code which DOES verify now creates the identity. The four rulings of `ADR-196` section 7 are complete and the product is one vendor slice from a working sign-up, which is a different sentence from "a trader can sign up".

### 3. RULING 1: `ApiDb` GAINS TWO DOORS, AND `ADR-171` CLAUSE 1 IS NOT OVERTURNED

**THE CONFLICT IS REAL AND IT IS NOT WAVED AWAY.** `ADR-171` clause 1 reads *"`apps/api/src/db.ts` DECLARES NO THIRD DOOR and [ADR-120](ADR-120.md) clause 3 stands unamended"*, and [`db.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/db.test.ts) pins the count so that a fourth is a red suite rather than a line somebody has to notice. **THE CLAUSE'S SUBJECT IS `operator(fn)` AND NOT "a door".** Read at its own section 5, the argument is specific and mechanical:

> *"the day `ApiDb` declares `operator(fn)`, `db.operator(tx => tx.rowAt('users', { email }))` is in scope inside that file, and `B1` ... falls to one line, at the reason word `'operator-console'`, inside a handler serving an anonymous request."*

**THAT ARGUMENT IS ABOUT A DOOR NARROWED AT THE PORT AND SPENT SOMEWHERE ELSE, AND THESE TWO ARE NARROWED AT THE DOOR AND SPENT HERE.** Three differences, each checkable rather than rhetorical:

1. **The vocabulary is one table and one address, in `packages/db`.** `ResolvableTableKey` is the single member `'users'` and `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` is `{ users: ['email'] }`, enforced in both directions by `refuseUnresolvableAddress`: a key the vocabulary does not carry is refused, and the declared key missing is refused. `EstablishmentTx` carries `establish` and **no** `insert`, `updateAt`, `deleteAt`, `rowAt` or `sqlExecutor`. These doors were BUILT for `auth-backend.ts`: `resolutionDb`'s own docblock says it exists *"because `POST /auth/verify` must turn the address a person typed into the identity that owns it"*, and `ADR-197` landed `establishmentDb` naming `ADR-196` as its caller specification.
3. **`ADR-171` section 9's condition is met in its own terms.** That section says the refused door becomes takeable *"when ... that is the first moment the door has a caller that reaches a row rather than a caller that rejects before it opens"*, and requires that the taking slice *"takes an ADR number of its own and the admission is its SUBJECT"*. This is that number and this section is that subject.

**WHAT IS STILL REFUSED, UNCHANGED.** There is no `system(reason, fn)` and no `operator(fn)`. `SystemReason` gains no member ([ADR-165](ADR-165.md)), `SqlExecutorReason` gains no member ([ADR-157](ADR-157.md) clause 7), and the condition `ADR-171` section 9 sets for the admin door -- an `AdminSessionSource` a deployment can install -- is not met by anything in this tree.

### 4. RULING 2: THE HANDLER, AND THE THREE THINGS `ADR-197` LEFT UNRULED

`verifyOtp` in [`auth-backend.ts`](../../apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts). Read the challenge and match; consume; resolve; establish if nobody answered; mint. **Every refusal answers `null`, which the route turns into one 401**, because `API_CONTRACT` section 3 calls a bad and an expired code *"deliberately indistinguishable"* and because `POST /auth/otp` *"deliberately does not reveal whether the destination exists"*: a verify that answered differently for a known address would disclose through this route what the other route withholds.

#### 4.1 Which row a verification answers: THE NEWEST LIVE ONE, AND ONLY THAT ONE

**A handler that walked every live challenge for an address would multiply the lockout budget by a number the ATTACKER chooses.** `attempts` is on the challenge and not on the account, and `0002` says why in its own words: *"so a locked-out attacker learns nothing about whether the address exists"*. `POST /auth/otp` is rate limited rather than forbidden, so the number of outstanding codes for one address is something a caller can drive up; five attempts per outstanding code is a budget with no ceiling. **Answering only the newest keeps it at five whatever they do.**

The merged index built for exactly this read is the corroboration rather than the argument: `otp_challenges_email_created_idx (email_normalized, created_at DESC)`.

**THE COST IS NAMED: A PERSON WHO REQUESTS A SECOND CODE CANNOT USE THE FIRST.** That is the correct direction. Each new request shortens the useful life of every earlier code, and a person holding two codes and typing the older one has a working remedy in their inbox.

#### 4.2 What a wrong code costs: ONE ATTEMPT, AND THE `CHECK` IS NEVER THE THING THAT ANSWERS

`API_CONTRACT` section 11 rows `POST /auth/verify` as *"10/hour/IP, 5 attempts/challenge"* and `0002` declares `attempts smallint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (attempts BETWEEN 0 AND 5)`. **The read EXCLUDES an exhausted challenge rather than letting the increment hit the ceiling**, and that ordering is the ruling: a handler that selected a row at 5 and incremented would hand the caller a `23514` out of a merged migration, and `endpointHandler` has no arm for one. A person typing a sixth wrong code would get a 500 where the contract says the lockout is a refusal.

**Executed, seven wrong codes against one challenge: `attempts` reads 5 and the correct code is then refused.** The CHECK never fired.

**THE INCREMENT IS A READ-THEN-WRITE AND IT UNDERCOUNTS UNDER CONCURRENCY.** Two simultaneous wrong codes both write `attempts = n + 1`. The accessor writes a values object and has no vocabulary for an expression in a `SET`, so `attempts = attempts + 1` is not expressible from a handler in this tree. **The cost is bounded and it is named**: an attacker who parallelises gains at most a few extra guesses out of five, against a six-digit space, and never a row. The repair is an expression in a `SET`, which is `packages/db`'s and is reported rather than taken.

#### 4.3 When the code is spent: BEFORE THE IDENTITY EXISTS

**Consuming FIRST can leave a spent code and no session; consuming LAST can leave a code that has already minted a session still answerable.** The first is an inconvenience and the second is a session, so the ordering is not a preference.

**AND IT IS NOT THIS ENTRY'S JUDGEMENT EITHER, IT IS A PRICE [ADR-126](ADR-126.md) ALREADY PAID.** `resolutionDb`'s docblock states the consequence of the two-unit-of-work split in advance: *"a handler that resolves and then mints does so in TWO units of work, so a crash between them leaves a consumed challenge and no session and the person asks for another code. That is the price of a door that cannot write, and it is paid in an inconvenience rather than in a row."* This handler is the caller that pays it.

**CONSUMPTION AND MINTING CANNOT BE ONE UNIT OF WORK AND THAT IS STRUCTURAL RATHER THAN CHOSEN.** `otp_challenges` is `firm`, `sessions` is `derived` and reached at a SCOPED handle, `users` is read at a door with no `transaction` overload at all, and `identities` is written at a fourth. Four authorities, four units of work, and `packages/db` offers no handle that spans two of them -- which is the property that makes each door narrow and is therefore the property paying for this cost.

#### 4.4 `sms` IS REFUSED BY NAME, AND THE BLOCKER IS A VOCABULARY

`ADR-196` clause 5 rules that `is_new` is always false on `sms`, because `users.email` is `citext NOT NULL UNIQUE` and an SMS verification for an unknown number has no value to write. **This session found the other half: `sms` cannot LOG ANYBODY IN EITHER, at any door this tree admits.** Resolving a phone to an identity means reading `identity_phones` by a hash, and `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` is `{ users: ['email'] }`. There is no address in that vocabulary a phone can be presented at.

**So the arm raises `AuthBackendUnwired` and the 503 says the CHANNEL is not served.** A `null` there would say "your code was wrong" about a channel this deployment cannot answer at all. **It discloses nothing**: the caller chose the channel, so the distinction is about their own request rather than about whether some address exists.

**Widening `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS` is `packages/db`'s and it is REPORTED.** It is also not a one-line change: the phone is stored as a HASH, so the address would be a digest a caller computes, which is a different kind of vocabulary from `users.email` and deserves its own entry.

### 5. RULING 3: `0063` IS TAKEN, AND THE HOLE IT CLOSES IS ONE THE SCHEMA CLAIMS TO HAVE CLOSED ALREADY

**TWO MERGED SENTENCES STATE A CONTROL AND NO OBJECT CARRIES IT.**

`0002` writes *"Single use, enforced by the database rather than by the handler"* and creates `otp_challenges_unconsumed_uq ON otp_challenges (id) WHERE consumed_at IS NULL`. **`id` is the PRIMARY KEY.** A UNIQUE index over the primary key is unique whatever its predicate says, so that object bounds nothing at all: it is a useful partial index and it is not a constraint. [SECURITY section 2](../architecture/SECURITY.md) states the same control from the other end, twice, as *"short TTL, single use, 5-attempt lock"*.

**THIS IS SECURITY ITEM 10's OWN LESSON, ONE TABLE OVER FROM WHERE THAT ITEM FOUND IT**: *"a control that exists as columns and not as a constraint reads as done in every review and stops nothing at runtime."*

#### 5.1 The reservation's own limb FAILED, and the number is spent on a different one. Said plainly.

The row reserved `0063` *"only if making the identity write idempotent needs a database object the schema does not carry"*. **That limb is NOT met and the row was right**: `users_email_key` is the whole answer for the identity write, `ADR-197` ruling 4 measured it, and section 5.3 reproduces it here -- the loser of an establishment race pays **zero** identities and **zero** ledger accounts.

**The number is taken for the CONSUMPTION race, which this slice measured and which no entry had named as needing an object.** That is stated as a departure rather than folded in, because a number taken for a hole other than the one it was reserved against is the shape a reservation gets justified into. **The counterfactual is section 5.3's run [7], which is the same handler with this file dropped.**

#### 5.2 A handler cannot close it, and that is three merged rulings rather than a shortage of care

The consume is "read the challenge, check `consumed_at IS NULL`, write `consumed_at`". Closing the window needs `UPDATE ... WHERE id = $1 AND consumed_at IS NULL`, and:

- **An address is equality only.** [ADR-112](ADR-112.md) clause 1, enforced in `addressPredicate`, and `ADR-157` left it unmoved.
- **`isNull()` is a READ term.** `ADR-157` admits it in a filter and refuses it in a write address on evidence, and this slice **reports rather than proposes to widen it**, on the protocol's own instruction.
- **`FirmTx` carries no `FOR UPDATE` read**, where `ScopedTx` carries `rowAtForUpdate`. That is the one-verb repair and it is `packages/db`'s: **reported, not taken.** Measured to work: with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, the second transaction blocks on the first's row lock and re-reads a row whose `consumed_at` is set.

**THE TRIGGER IS PREFERRED TO THE VERB EVEN THOUGH THE VERB IS SMALLER**, and the reason is the sentence `0002` already wrote. A `FOR UPDATE` read puts single use back in the handler, which is precisely where that comment assumed it was not. A trigger holds it for every writer that ever touches the column, including the admin tool nobody has written.

### 6. RULING 4: THE SESSION LIFETIME, WHICH IS A LAUNCH PARAMETER WITH NO SOURCE

**`sessions.expires_at` is `NOT NULL` with no default and the corpus states no value for it.** The design record calls it *"short-lived access, rotating refresh"* and `refresh_token_hash` carries *"rotation on every refresh"* -- and **`API_CONTRACT` section 3 declares NO REFRESH ENDPOINT.** There is no `POST /auth/refresh` in the contract and none in `AUTH_ENDPOINTS`, so nothing can rotate a session: `expires_at` is the WHOLE login lifetime rather than the access half of a pair the contract never built.

**A SHORT VALUE WOULD THEREFORE BE THE WRONG READING OF THE RIGHT COMMENT.** It would log every trader out on a schedule, on a product whose only other door is another emailed code, and whose delivery is not built.

**`SESSION_LIFETIME_DAYS = 30`, RULED AT THE SITE THAT WRITES IT**, on [`expiry.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/sweeps/expiry.ts)'s `FREEZE_EXPIRING_LEAD_HOURS` precedent -- a value the corpus states nowhere, set where it is used, with the reasoning beside it. **What bounds a long session is C-27 rather than the clock**: `sessions.elevated_by_factor`'s CHECK list admits `passkey` and `dual_channel` and neither is a single factor, so an `email_otp` session can see everything and change nothing -- `0029` writes that sentence about the SIM-swapped case and the CHECK list it describes excludes `email_otp` by the same absence -- and `listSessions` and `revokeSession` -- both wired -- are how the person who owns it ends it.

**THIS IS THE CLAUSE MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG AND THE FOUNDER READ IS OWED ON THE NUMBER SPECIFICALLY.** It is one constant with one caller and changing it is a one-line diff.

### 9. Findings not in the dispatch
