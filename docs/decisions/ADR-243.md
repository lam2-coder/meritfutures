#### 4.1 ADR-140, and this is the one whose assertion did not exist

**THE CENTRAL CLAIM IS A PROPERTY.** Clause 4: *"The predicate is `status === 'active'` and NOT an enumeration of what is refused, so it covers `closed` as well as `restricted` and continues to cover whatever the enum gains next."*

**THE SECOND HALF OF THAT SENTENCE HAD NO ASSERTION ANYWHERE IN THIS TREE.** `identity_status` has three members ([`0001:27`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql)), and every case that drove the door drove `restricted` and `closed`, which is the whole of what is refused today. **`payouts.test.ts`'s own case is named *"admits `active` and only `active`"* and asserts all three members**, and it passes under an enumerate-the-refused predicate exactly as it passes under this one. **The form was stated in prose at four sites**, most fully in [`checkout.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts)'s `gateWalletSpend` docblock: *"An enumeration of the refused values is one migration away from admitting a fourth `identity_status` member to a door that moves value."* [ADR-042](ADR-042.md) already ruled that prose is not a control.

**THE ASSERTION:** [`apps/api/test/adr-140-identity-status-form.test.ts`](../../apps/api/test/adr-140-identity-status-form.test.ts), **11 cases**, four legs. A behavioural leg drives a value outside the enum at the two exported gates and requires a refusal rendering `identity_restricted` at 422. A structural leg reads the predicate of the gates that are module-private. A coverage sweep requires every identity-status comparison under `apps/api/src/routes/` to sit at a named site. A non-vacuity leg reads the enum off `0001`.

**THE MODULE-PRIVATE GATES ARE READ WHERE THEY ARE WRITTEN RATHER THAN EXPORTED.** `checkout.ts`'s two gates are not exported and this session did not export them: widening a module's surface to make an assertion convenient is the same move as widening a fence to finish.

### 6. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: the classifier and the rule are different predicates

**ADR-227 SIZED THE RESIDUE BY ASKING WHETHER AN ENTRY'S `What a founder read adds` BLOCK DECLARES AN OUTSTANDING JUDGEMENT. THAT IS NOT THE QUESTION SECTION 3 RULES ON.** Section 3 classifies a diff by **the shape of its CENTRAL claim**. The classifier asks whether the entry names **any** residual choice anywhere in a block that [`RI-13`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs) REQUIRES every unsigned entry to write.

**RE-RUN OVER THE 52, THE CLASSIFIER SCORES 46 AS RESIDUE, AND IT SCORES ALL SIX ENTRIES THIS SESSION READ IN FULL** ([review](../reviews/2026-08-29-class-b-residue.md) section 5). **Four of those six are now class B with an assertion watched failing.** [ADR-176](ADR-176.md) is the clean case: a central claim that is a property, asserted at four sites, and a founder block naming three genuine choices none of which is that claim.

**SO 46 IS NOT THE CLASS C RESIDUE, AND THE HONEST FORM OF THAT SENTENCE IS THE SHORT ONE: THE TRUE RESIDUE IS SMALLER THAN 46 BY AN AMOUNT NOBODY HAS MEASURED.** **No replacement number is proposed here and that refusal is deliberate.** ADR-227 corrected its own classifier upward from 30 to 36 and wrote that a measurement corrected only where the correction flatters the measurer is not a measurement. **This correction runs the other way**, toward a smaller queue, which is exactly the direction a session clearing a backlog would want, **so it is offered as four demonstrations and not as an estimate.** Four of fifty-two is not a sample.

**THIS IS THE THIRD TIME A PATTERN OVER PROSE HAS BEEN CAUGHT MISSING IN THIS THREAD, AND THE FIRST TIME IT ERRED IN THE FLATTERING DIRECTION.** ADR-227 section 10.3's own repair is the right one and it is now three for three: **read each entry's own declaration rather than pattern-matching around it.**

**WHAT REPLACES THE CLASSIFIER IS NOT ANOTHER PATTERN.** It is the question ADR-227 section 3 already wrote, asked one entry at a time: **what does this entry's heading claim, and is that claim a property or a choice?** Six entries were asked it this session. Forty-six were not.

### 9. Approval

- **Approval line: PENDING, UNSIGNED.** **NOT the money path by file.** No `src/` file is changed and no migration is taken.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE ONE THING THIS ENTRY MOST NEEDS AND CANNOT SUPPLY IS A RULING ON WHETHER SECTION 6's CORRECTION IS ACCEPTED.** It shrinks a backlog, it was produced by the session that benefits from shrinking it, and **its whole defence is that it is four demonstrations rather than an argument.** A founder who thinks four cases cannot refute a classifier over fifty-two entries is holding a defensible position, and the consequence of that position is that ADR-227's 46 stands and the four entries below stay owed reads regardless of their assertions.

  **AND THE CONSTITUTIONAL QUESTION UNDERNEATH IS STILL [ADR-227](ADR-227.md) SECTION 10's AND HAS NOT MOVED.** Every class B approval recorded here rests on an amendment that has been PROPOSED and not enacted. **If the answer is no, this entry has produced four transcripts and zero approvals**, and the transcripts are still worth having, because an assertion watched failing is worth something whatever the approval regime turns out to be. That is written here rather than argued, so a refusal does not look free and does not look expensive either.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left to be found.** **Section 4's class assignments each rest on reading one entry's HEADING as its central claim**, and a heading is a summary somebody wrote. [ADR-192](ADR-192.md) is the uncomfortable one: its heading claims an ordering property, and its own bullets say what is JUDGED is *"clause 2, which spends an operator's ability to tell `I am not logged in` from `this deployment cannot log anyone in`"*. **This entry calls that a residual choice beside a property. A reader could call it the point of the diff**, in which case ADR-192 is class C and one of the four falls. **The other three do not turn on that reading**, and saying which one is fragile is the only useful form of this admission.

  **AND THE SECOND-MOST LIKELY, WHICH IS ABOUT WHAT AN ASSERTION IS WORTH RATHER THAN ABOUT WHICH CLASS AN ENTRY IS IN.** **Three of the four assertions are STRUCTURAL: they read source text with a regular expression.** A structural assertion is defeated by any rewrite that preserves the text and changes the behaviour, and by any rewrite that changes the text harmlessly. **The behavioural leg of section 4.1 and the surface sweep of section 4.4 are the two that execute something**, and they are the two worth most. **ADR-191's are rendered SQL, which is in between**, and section 4.3 says so. A regime that counts a structural assertion as an earned approval is buying less than it looks like it is buying, and **nothing here measures how much less.**
