### 3. The ruling on divergence

**THREE THINGS ARE RULED AND THE THIRD IS AN OPEN QUESTION RATHER THAN A DECISION.**

**RULING 1. A DIVERGENCE NEVER REWRITES STORED STATE, AND THE AUDIT HAS NO PATH TO ONE.** `B.4` puts every rewrite behind four steps: a dry run across the whole book writing nothing, a report naming *"specifically whether any changed day underlies a settled payout"*, a founder approval recorded as an `admin_actions` row carrying the report's digest, and only then an audited rewrite job. **`B.4` step 5 is the boundary that cannot move**: no settled payout's `eligibility_snapshot` is ever rewritten (`INV-22`), because *"the snapshot is what was true when the money moved, and an upgrade cannot retroactively make a payment wrong"*. This row builds no rewrite job and gives the audit no writer that could become one. **A self-audit that silently rewrote history is the catastrophe the dispatch named, and the corpus already forbids it in terms.**

**RULING 2. THE FINDING IS ONE EVENT PER DIVERGING FIELD, AND IT MAY NOT BE QUIET.** `B.2`: *"Divergence emits one `replay.divergence_detected` per diverging field, so the page says which number moved, not merely that something did."* [`ports.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts) already carries the grouping and the expansion obligation, and this row does not move it.

**RULING 3, AND IT IS AN OPEN QUESTION. `B.1`'s HALT HAS NO COLUMN AND THE CORPUS DOES NOT NAME ONE.** The commitment is *"Any difference halts payout eligibility for that account and pages."* The schema carries exactly two flags that halt payout eligibility beside the account machine, and both are another channel's:

| Column | Whose it is | Why the audit may not take it |
|---|---|---|
| `accounts.recon_blocked` | RECONCILIATION's. [`0064_reconciliation_runs.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0064_reconciliation_runs.sql) opens by stating that a `mismatch` sets it *"and blocks eligibility until a human clears it"*, and `M01`'s own `FM-01` and `FM-04` rows both name it as the automatic response to a DATA problem | [`ports.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts) keeps the two channels apart BY NAME and says why: reconciliation is *"the fold REFUSED TO RUN"* and a divergence is *"the fold RAN AND DISAGREED WITH STORAGE"*, and collapsing them *"would make a replay divergence indistinguishable from a vendor arithmetic failure on the page"*. `B.3` gives them different causes and different responses |
| `accounts.payouts_frozen` | An INVESTIGATION's. [STATE_MACHINES](../architecture/STATE_MACHINES.md) section 1 calls it the investigation blocker and section 6 makes entering `investigating` the thing that sets it, *"and it requires a written reason and a ToS clause"* | A job has neither a reason it can write nor a ToS clause it can cite. A flag set by a nightly with an empty reason is an investigation nobody opened, and `identity.payouts_frozen` and its `unfrozen` twin are ADMIN events in [EVENTS](../architecture/EVENTS.md) with an operator on both edges |

**SO THE HALT IS A DECISION AND NOT A TRANSCRIPTION, AND THE CONSTITUTION'S RULE FOR THAT CASE IS THE ONE FOLLOWED HERE.** `CLAUDE.md`: when the constitution is silent, propose an ADR and proceed on approval. This is the proposal. **`OQ-346-01` is the open question and it is written out in section 9 with the three shapes an answer could take**, so the founder is choosing between named options rather than being asked an open-ended question at merge time.

### 4. The second blocker, which is checkable and was not in the dispatch

**`raiseDivergence` CANNOT TELL `B.4` STEP 1 FROM `B.4` STEP 2, AND AN ADAPTER THAT HALTED WOULD HALT THE WHOLE BOOK ON THE FIRST ENGINE UPGRADE.**

`ReplayAuditConfig` carries `mode: 'detect' | 'dryRun'` and `ReplayDivergenceFinding` carries an account, a trading day, an engine version and the fields that moved. **It carries no mode.** `B.4` step 2's dry run is defined by writing NOTHING, and step 4 says its findings are emitted *"as an audit trail rather than as alerts"*. An adapter handed a finding therefore cannot tell an alert from an audit trail, and the only safe behaviour for a value that could be either is to do the smaller thing, which is nothing.

**THE COST OF GETTING THIS WRONG IS MEASURED IN THE CORPUS RATHER THAN GUESSED.** `B.3`'s fourth row: at 5,000 accounts a calendar correction that fell into "anything else" is *"5,000 pages on one morning"*, and `B.4` opens with *"the first legitimate bugfix produces thousands of divergences, buries the one real one, and teaches everyone to ignore the alarm"*. **A halt on every finding turns that from 5,000 pages into 5,000 halted accounts.** `B.3`'s own sentence is the one to keep: *"The alarm does not fail by being wrong; it fails by being right five thousand times."*

**THE REPAIR IS NAMED AND NOT TAKEN.** `ReplayMode` lives in `replay.ts` and `ports.ts` cannot import it, because the dependency runs the other way. The shape is to move `ReplayMode` into `ports.ts`, re-export it from `replay.ts`, and add it to `ReplayDivergenceFinding`. That is a contract change to a money-path port and it belongs with the halt ruling rather than ahead of it: a mode on the finding buys nothing until there is something the two modes do differently. **Both must land together and neither is taken here.**

### 5. What the two read ports do, and the one thing `accountDaysFrom` costs

**`storedRuleStates` IS `toRuleState` PLUS FOUR, AND [ADR-250](ADR-250.md) SECTION 7's OWN ARITHMETIC IS WHAT MADE IT SMALL.** That entry priced this port as *"twenty-odd columns read back and rebuilt"* and named three of the four: `contextGates` has its own stored shape, `calendarRevisionId` crosses a `bigint` column into a `number | null` field, and *"ONE `jsonb` LEAF DECODED IS NOT A ROW REBUILT"*. **Every clause is true and the list is COMPLETE.** [`adapter.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/adapter.ts)'s own header had already enumerated the three fields a `RuleState` does not carry, while arguing that `prior` was a strictly smaller read than this port. The fourth is the account id, which the caller passes in. So the row is the prior reader plus `accountId`, `contextGates`, `stateHash` and `calendarRevisionId`, and it is **COMPOSED on `toRuleState` rather than transcribed beside it**.

**COMPOSING IT IS THE RULING AND NOT A STYLE.** Two readers over one column list is `FM-16`, and the direction it would fail in here is the worst available: `priorState` feeds the NIGHTLY FOLD and this feeds THE AUDIT OF THAT FOLD, so a field read one way here and another way there would make the audit agree with a batch that was wrong, or diverge from one that was right, **and in both cases the disagreement would be between two copies of one file rather than between the engine and storage**. The same argument is applied to the supersession rule, the settled-status filter, the mark rendering and the prior resolution, all of which are now single expressions shared with `loadAccountDay`.

**`accountDaysFrom` IS THE WALK, AND THE RETIRED BLOCKER WAS RIGHT ABOUT WHY IT HAD TO BE.** `resolveAccountDay` answers ONE day in six reads, five of which answer questions about the ACCOUNT: the account row, the plan version, the size grid, the identity and the KYC chain. Repeating that per day over `B.5`'s figure of 250 trading days is 1,500 reads to fold one life. **Here each is read once and only `daily_marks` and `rule_states` are added**, which the suite asserts on a READ LOG rather than on wall-clock, so it cannot pass by being fast over five days.

**AND `accountDaysFrom` COSTS ONE THING, STATED HERE RATHER THAN DISCOVERED IN AN INCIDENT.** `AccountDay.external` is required by the type and the audit never reads it: `INV-23` keeps the context gates out of the replayed state and [ADR-026](ADR-026.md) `C-07` keeps them out of the hash, so `replay.ts` builds no context side and compares none. The walk resolves it once per account off real rows, inventing nothing, **and `resolveExternalGates` REFUSES a leg it cannot derive**. `runReplayAudit` catches nothing and its account loop is sequential, so one account whose KYC chain has no single head stops the audit for the whole book **over a field the audit does not consume**.

**THE ALTERNATIVES WERE WEIGHED AND BOTH ARE WORSE ON THIS ROW.** Inventing a value for `external` is the one thing this adapter never does, and `R-41` conjoins all five as VETOES, so a defaulted leg is a veto that never fires. Narrowing the port to a type carrying neither `external` nor `prior` is the honest shape and it is **the named repair**: it would also make `replay.ts`'s claim that `prior` *"is not reachable"* structurally true rather than documented. It is a contract change to a money-path port on a row whose deliverable is the adapter, so it is registered as `OQ-346-02` and not taken. **The cost is real, bounded and loud**, which is the same trade every other refusal in that file makes.

### 6. What a green audit covers, which is less than it looks and NOT for the reason the file said

**THE DISPATCH ASKED WHETHER THE FOUR UNIMPLEMENTED RULES AFFECT WHAT A REPLAY CAN HONESTLY CLAIM TO HAVE AUDITED. THEY DO, AND THE EXISTING SENTENCE ABOUT IT IS WRONG IN A SPECIFIC WAY.**

[`replay.ts`](../../apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts)'s header item 4 says a green audit *"covers less than it appears to, because the engine does not yet implement every rule group"*. **That reads as a gap pending work and it is not one.** [`rules.ts:180-206`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/rules.ts) records all four as discharged OUTSIDE the engine, by design:

| Rule | Where `rules.ts` discharges it | Why a replay cannot audit it |
|---|---|---|
| `R-01` | `trading_calendar` and the ingest path. *"a containment lookup over a fill's execution timestamp"* and `DailyMark` *"carries `fillCount` and no instant"* | The instant it needs is not in the fold's input at all |
| `R-05` | The same. *"R-05's session bounds are two columns"* `CalendarDay` does not carry | The same |
| `R-11` | *"the caller's live-mark predicate"* | Section 6.1 |
| `R-20` | *"the platform setpoint"* | It never enters the fold |

**SO IMPLEMENTING MORE ENGINE RULES WOULD NOT SHRINK THIS LIST**, and a reader told the coverage gap is about unimplemented rules will expect it to close on its own. **The sentence is corrected in place and kept beside its correction, per `RI-14`.**

#### 6.1 `R-11` is the one a replay is STRUCTURALLY blind to, and this row is what made it so

**THE WALK APPLIES `R-11` ITSELF.** `liveMarksByDay` drops the superseded rows before the engine sees anything, so **the audit folds marks selected by the very rule it would have to check**. If the ingest path superseded the wrong mark, the nightly batch folded the same wrong mark, storage holds the result, and the replay reproduces it byte for byte. **The audit is green over a book whose inputs are wrong, and it is right to be**: `INV-04` is a claim about determinism and never a claim about selection.

**THIS IS ASSERTED RATHER THAN SAID.** `replay-adapter.test.ts` case 5.2 builds two estates that differ ONLY in which of two marks for one trading day carries `superseded_by`, folds and stores each the way the nightly batch would, and gets **two green reports over two books whose closing balances differ by fifteen thousand cents**. A pair of green audits over two different histories is the finding; a single green audit would have been a tautology.

**AND THE REPORT CARRIES NO RULE-COVERAGE NUMBER AT ALL.** `ReplayAuditReport` counts accounts, stored rows, in-scope rows, matched and diverged. **Nothing on it says which rules the fold exercised**, so a reader has counts of DAYS and no way to discover that four rule ids were never in the fold. **That is registered as a finding and not repaired**: a coverage number on the report would have to be derived from the engine, which is `packages/rules-engine`'s and outside this row's fence.

### 10. The reservations: six written, none surviving, and the rules that resolved them

`CI-06f` reads gaplessness over the ADR files PRESENT plus the numbers [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) NAMES. **`346` landing beside six unnamed numbers would be six holes the gate reports at numbers nobody lost.** So six rows were written against `9906c43`, each naming the sibling branch that held the number, each measured absent at the moment its own cell was typed, and each saying its owner amends it in place at merge ([ADR-065](ADR-065.md) T3).

**ALL SIX ARE GONE AND NOT ONE WAS REMOVED BY THIS ROW DECIDING ANYTHING, WHICH IS THE MECHANISM WORKING.** Across two merges, `340`, `341`, `344`, `347` and `351` landed as REAL rows with files behind them, and `342`, `343` and `345` are now `main`'s own placeholders in `main`'s own words. **This row's diff adds exactly one row to the table: `346`.**

**THE RESOLUTION RULE IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING, BECAUSE THE OBVIOUS ONE IS WRONG.** Resolving a conflicted allocation table by SIDE loses a real row whenever the other side happened to carry a placeholder for the same number, and the second merge is the case that proves it: **`main` arrived carrying a placeholder for `346`, this row's own number**, and a side-wise resolution in either direction would have dropped either that row or this document's. **The rule applied is PER NUMBER, and the test for a placeholder is whether `docs/decisions/ADR-N.md` EXISTS**, run against the file tree rather than against the cell's wording, because this wave invented at least seven placeholder phrasings and any phrase test would mis-sort a sibling that chose different words. Each number keeps exactly one row, which is what `CI-06w` reads.

**THE SESSION NUMBER MOVED ONCE, AND WHAT IT TAUGHT IS ABOUT THE RULE RATHER THAN ABOUT THIS ROW.** This entry was written as `531`, which `claude/the-parser-only-tests-install` held, producing an ADD/ADD conflict rather than a silent overwrite. It was renumbered to `543` by taking the first free number above the maximum across every pushed ref, and `543` is the number this row keeps: it is what landed on `origin/main`, in the log's filename, this document's `- **Origin:**` line, the [ALLOCATION](ALLOCATION.md) cell, the `docs/sessions/README.md` row and the [STATE](../STATE.md) heading.

**A DETERMINISTIC DERIVATION DOES NOT PREVENT COLLISIONS BETWEEN CONCURRENT BRANCHES. IT GUARANTEES THEM.** Row `349` derived `543` independently, from the same refs, by the same rule, and got the same answer, because that is what a deterministic function of shared state does when two callers evaluate it at the same moment. **The rule reads like an allocation and behaves like a guess two branches can make identically**, and it fails in exactly the way that is hardest to see: each branch's derivation is individually correct and verifiable, so neither has any evidence of the other. This row's `531` collision and its `543` collision are two instances of one defect and not two accidents.

**SO THE REPAIR IS NOT A BETTER DERIVATION, IT IS NOT DERIVING.** A number handed out centrally and VERIFIED by the row that receives it cannot collide, because the assignment happens once rather than N times; a rule refined to "first free above the maximum across every ref" is the same defect with a longer search. That is the general statement and it is what makes this paragraph worth keeping after the particular collision it came from was resolved: **this row was briefly renumbered to `547` on the old reasoning, `547` turned out to be assigned to row `352`, and the move would have created a fresh collision while trying to avoid one.** The rename was reverted; the argument is what survives.

**AN INTEGRATION MERGE RESOLVED THE TIE ON ARRIVAL ORDER AND THAT IS ALSO NOT AN ALLOCATION.** `543` went to whichever branch merged first, and row `349` moved to `544`. That works and it is not a rule: it settles the tie after both branches have already written the number into five places each, which is the cost this section exists to record.
