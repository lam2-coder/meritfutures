### 3. THE RULING

#### Clause 1. `EVENTS.md` is right, and its reason is structural rather than stylistic

[`EVENTS.md:291`](../architecture/EVENTS.md) argues that *"provenance is what value is MADE of, and it is a property of a credit; a debit consumes a composition rather than having one"*. **That is not a preference about naming, it is an observation about arity.** [M20 section 3.4](../plans/M20-wallet.md) composes a withdrawal **FIFO** out of many credits, and `P-1` and `P-3` both evaluate against that composition rather than against a single class. So what a debit consumes is a **mixture**, and a scalar `text` column cannot hold one.

**The corpus already has the plural column, and it is on the other table.** `wallet_withdrawals.source_provenance_summary jsonb` ([`0011:169`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql), `SD-M20-03`) is the composition a debit destroys, reported by `wallet.withdrawal_approved` and not by `wallet.debited`, exactly as [`EVENTS.md` section 6.1](../architecture/EVENTS.md) says. **A debit vocabulary on `wallet_entries.provenance` would therefore be a one-of-many answer to a many-valued question, stored beside a column that already answers it correctly.** That is the argument for the direction this ruling takes, and it is the argument the alternative has to beat.

**The contract had already committed to this reading.** [ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 2 types `WalletEntry` as a union discriminated on `direction`, in which `WalletDebit` declares **no** `provenance` field, and it names the cost of the alternative: *"a client reading a debit labelled `payout` would render a credit class on a withdrawal"*. [`wallet.ts`'s `projectEntry`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet.ts) already builds that union by branching, and [`checkout.ts`'s `WalletDebitInsert`](../../apps/api/src/routes/checkout.ts) already declines to carry the field, citing this defect by name. **Ruling the other way would require amending an approved architecture document AND an approved contract AND rewriting two route files, to store a value that cannot be correct.**

**So `EVENTS.md` is neither amended nor superseded, and this entry edits no line of it.**

#### Clause 2. The schema moves in `0080`, and `0011` is not edited

Constitution `E2`: a merged migration is sacred, superseded by addition from outside it and never edited. [`0080_wallet_debit_provenance.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0080_wallet_debit_provenance.sql) does two things and nothing else.

```sql
ALTER TABLE wallet_entries ALTER COLUMN provenance DROP NOT NULL;

ALTER TABLE wallet_entries
  ADD CONSTRAINT wallet_entries_provenance_follows_direction CHECK (
    CASE direction
      WHEN 'credit' THEN provenance IS NOT NULL
      WHEN 'debit'  THEN provenance IS NULL OR provenance = 'correction'
      ELSE false
    END
  );
```

**IT COMPOSES WITH `0011`'s CHECK RATHER THAN REPLACING IT, AND THAT IS THE SHAPE DECISION.** `wallet_entries_provenance_check` is not dropped. It answers *is this a legal member*, and it keeps answering it: `provenance = 'deposit'` is still refused in both directions, so `INV-WALLET-NO-DEPOSITS` and `0011` header item 3's exclusion of `promotional_credit` are untouched by this migration. `0080` answers a different question, *may this direction carry it*, and **names exactly one literal**. The three-member vocabulary is therefore still written down in exactly one place. Restating it in the new CHECK would have produced a second copy that agreed with `0011` on the day it was written and was free to drift from it every day after, which is the copy [ADR-216](ADR-216.md) refused for the same reason one column over.

**A `CASE` AND NOT A DISJUNCTION, BECAUSE THE DISJUNCTION IS SILENTLY VACUOUS.** The spelling that reads most naturally, `(direction = 'credit' AND provenance IN (...)) OR (direction = 'debit' AND ...)`, evaluates to `NULL OR FALSE` = `NULL` for a credit carrying no provenance, and **a CHECK that evaluates to NULL admits the row**. It would have installed cleanly, refused both mislabelled debits, and let through the one row the constraint exists to refuse. Every branch of the `CASE` is total and returns a real boolean. `ELSE false` is unreachable while `direction`'s own CHECK closes it at two members, and it is there so that a later widening of `direction` is refused until somebody rules what provenance means for the new member, rather than admitted by falling off the end as `NULL`.

#### Clause 3. `correction` stays admissible on a debit, and the biconditional is REFUSED

The tidy shape is `(provenance IS NOT NULL) = (direction = 'credit')`. **It is wrong three times over and finding 6 is why.**

1. It makes `ADJ-C3` unsatisfiable for every reversal. A `trader_wallet` debit adjustment must have exactly one matching wallet row with `provenance = 'correction'`, and a biconditional forbids that row from existing.
2. It makes the debit half of a **built** admin endpoint unwritable. [`admin-wallet.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-wallet.ts) writes `provenance: CORRECTION_PROVENANCE` beside a `direction` of `WalletDirection`.
3. It contradicts `API_CONTRACT`'s `WalletCorrectionResponse`, which carries `provenance` beside a `direction` of `"credit" | "debit"`.

**Three merged or approved artifacts would have to move to buy a tidier CHECK, and the semantics do not want it anyway.** `payout` and `refund_wallet_funded` name **where value came from** and are credit-only. `correction` names a **mechanism**, and [`0011:64`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) defines it direction-agnostically: *"a compensating entry, never an update"*. `0011`'s header calling all three "THE CLOSED CREDIT LIST" is the imprecision, not the DDL.

**The DDL is permissive here and `0038` is what makes it exact.** `0080` admits the pair `(debit, 'correction')`; `ADJ-C3` is what requires it on the only debit that may take it, and no CHECK on `wallet_entries` can tell a correction debit from a withdrawal debit, because the discriminator is `reference_id` and it is polymorphic with no foreign key ([`0011:78`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)). **A constraint cannot be tightened past what the row can see about itself**, and this is that boundary, stated rather than papered over.

#### Clause 4. `INV-M20-04` is now a property of the schema

> *"Every debit records its **cause and reference**, and every credit records its **provenance class**"* ([M20:60](../plans/M20-wallet.md))

| Half | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| every debit records cause and reference | `cause text NOT NULL`, `reference_id uuid NOT NULL` for both directions ([`0011:77`](../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql)) | **unchanged.** `0080` does not touch either column |
| every credit records its provenance class | `NOT NULL` on **every** row, so the invariant was over-enforced on one direction and the sentence was never testable as written | `wallet_entries_provenance_follows_direction`: a credit with no class is **refused**, a debit needs none |

**The invariant reads the same and is now true of the DDL rather than aspirational.** Note what was NOT done: the sentence is not weakened, and no plan text is edited. [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md)'s `SD-M20-01` row states the delta as `provenance check in ('payout','refund_wallet_funded','correction')` and says nothing about nullability, so it stays true unchanged.

### 5. What this means for slice 7, in one paragraph

**[ADR-305](ADR-305.md) section 7 slice 7's `LT-06` withdrawal-approval driver is UNBLOCKED on this obstruction and is not unblocked in general.** It is the first writer of a `wallet_entries` row in this application ([ADR-316](ADR-316.md) finding 3, re-derived as finding 4 above), and the row it has to write is a debit with no provenance; before `0080` that row raised `not_null_violation` and the only writable alternative was a debit labelled with a credit class, which [ADR-158](ADR-158.md) clause 2 forbids the API to emit. Both halves are now settled in the database, and `ACCEPTANCE 1` is the standing assertion that the row stays writable. **Two things are still owed and neither belongs to this row.** The read paths narrow `provenance` with a helper that refuses a null string ([`wallet.ts:479`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet.ts) and [`wallet-withdrawals.ts:574`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts) both call `member(...)`), so the **first** null-provenance debit ever written will make `GET /wallet/entries` throw a `WalletRowError`; nothing is broken today because nothing writes, but slice 7 must land that narrowing as a union in the same commit as its first insert, and it is a route change outside this fence. And `balance_after_cents >= 0` is still unexercised, so slice 7 is also the first thing that ever tests `INV-M20-01`'s DDL half. **Slice 7's remaining size is those two, not this one.**

### Approval

**Status: `proposed`. The approval line is UNSIGNED and merging is not signing.** No `status` field anywhere moves and nothing is marked `accepted`. **`E2` READ OWED**, and it is inherited as well as owed: [ADR-158](ADR-158.md), [ADR-316](ADR-316.md) and [ADR-305](ADR-305.md) are all `proposed` and UNSIGNED, and this entry rests on all three.

  **What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**

  **THE FIRST THING TO CHECK IS THE ONE NO GATE CAN: THAT A WALLET DEBIT SHOULD SAY NOTHING ABOUT WHERE THE MONEY CAME FROM.** Every gate in this diff can tell you the constraint refuses what it must and admits what it must. **None of them can tell you that a trader reading their own statement should see a withdrawal line with no class on it.** That is the customer-visible consequence of clause 1, and it is exactly the kind of thing a founder overrules. The counter-case is real and worth a minute: an operator investigating an extraction ring would rather every debit carried the class it consumed, and this ruling puts that information one join away, on `wallet_withdrawals.source_provenance_summary`, where it is a jsonb summary rather than a column anybody filters on casually. **If you want the debit line to carry a class, say so now**: the repair is a debit vocabulary, it means amending [`EVENTS.md`](../architecture/EVENTS.md) and [ADR-158](ADR-158.md)'s union, and it gets an order of magnitude more expensive after slice 7 has written rows.

  **THE SECOND IS CLAUSE 3, AND IT IS THE ONE WHERE A READ MIGHT CHANGE THE ANSWER.** `correction` is admitted on a debit because `0038`'s `ADJ-C3` requires it, and the honest way to describe that is: **a merged trigger, written by a session that was not thinking about this question, is deciding a vocabulary question for us.** The alternative ruling is that `0038` was wrong too, that a correction debit is a debit like any other, and that `0080` should be a clean biconditional and supersede `ADJ-C3`'s provenance term at the same time. **That is a defensible reading and this entry did not take it**, for two reasons that a founder may weigh differently than a session did: superseding a money-path constraint trigger to buy a tidier CHECK is a large blast radius for an aesthetic gain, and `correction` genuinely is a different kind of word from the other two. **Ruling the other way is one more migration, not a rewrite of this one.**

  **THE THIRD IS A COST THIS ENTRY ACCEPTS AND STATES RATHER THAN HIDES.** `provenance` on a debit is now **two-shaped**: `NULL` for the two consuming debits, `'correction'` for the reversing one. A single column with two meanings by row is a thing this corpus normally refuses, and the defence is that they are two answers to one question, *is this a compensating entry*, which is a real distinction the operator console needs and the trader screen discards. **A reader who thinks that is one distinction too many should say so**, because the fix is the biconditional above and it is the same decision as the second point.

  **WHERE THIS ENTRY IS MOST LIKELY WRONG, named rather than left for a reader to find.** Section 5's claim that slice 7 is unblocked is a claim about **one** obstruction, and the paragraph names two more that this row could not touch. **If the read paths' null refusal is not landed in the same commit as slice 7's first insert, the first real wallet debit in production makes `GET /wallet/entries` throw for that trader and every trader after them on the same page.** Nothing in this diff prevents that; only the next session's discipline does, which is why it is written down in three places rather than one.
