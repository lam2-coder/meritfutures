- **Blockers:** None. M02 cannot leave `review` while the Rithmic call is outstanding (ADR-005), which is by design.
- **Landmines:**
  - **OQ-1's own cadence estimate was wrong and I corrected it while folding in the ruling.** With win days confirmed at 5 and resetting to the basis day, Merit Rapid's cycle is bound by the **win-day gate at 5 trading days**, not by its 1 day cadence gap. The honest published figure is roughly weekly. Two consequences: the 1 day gap is a dominated gate (EC-049) and must not be marketed as what makes the plan fast, and **an instant settlement rail does not unlock true daily cadence**, because the settlement leg already hides behind the win-day gate. Only `win_days.required_count` moves it, and lowering it to 1 raises extraction to roughly 1.6x the design ceiling on the current rail or 2.4x on an instant one. Raised as OQ-12.
  - **The founder's `max(trail minus DD, lock)` formulation is literally correct only because the high-water balance stops updating at the lock.** I adopted that reading explicitly and wrote it into section 3.4, because the alternative reading (trailing continues after locking) is economically impossible: it breaches every account on its second payout. If the founder meant something else, section 3.4 is the one paragraph to correct and everything else follows from it.
  - **Removing the post-payout reset created a new config hazard that did not exist before.** With no reset the floor stays put while a payout drops the balance, so on a plan with the lock **disabled** a cap at or above the drawdown pays an account into a breach. CV-17 catches it at publish. No v1 plan can reach it (all three enable the lock), which is exactly why it had to be validated rather than remembered.
  - **A validation-walk arithmetic trap.** The dominated-cadence-gap warning cannot be `gap <= required_win_days`, because the two gates measure from **different anchors**: the gap from the effective day, win days from the basis day. The correct form adds a `min_settlement_lag_trading_days` term (v1 value 2, a published config constant). Without it the warning fires wrongly on Core EOD, where the gap genuinely binds at 7 to 8 trading days.
  - **`effective_trading_day` must be observed, never predicted** (M05 section 3.2). It is set when M2 sees the matching `adjustment_cents` on a mark. That is what makes replay deterministic years later and makes a missing balance reflection visible instead of silent. It also creates a real sequencing dependency: M5 must publish `payout.settled` before that day's ingest file is processed (DEP-M2-02).
  - **The single scariest finding in batch 1 is M05 AS-M5-01.** A settled payout whose withdrawal never reaches the platform balance lets a trader withdraw the same cents again, every gate passes, and **reconciliation cannot catch it** because our balance and the vendor's agree: both think the money is still there. Only an explicit reflection observation catches it.
  - **M06 AS-M6-01 is a cross-module control that neither half can enforce alone.** Evidence packs go to traders in disputes and contain the numbers behind each flag, so one deliberately burned evaluation buys the firm's detection thresholds. The fix needs M6's audience-scoped redaction **and** M7's detector-parameter registry with an `is_sensitive` flag. Building either alone accomplishes nothing.
  - **M07 AS-M7-01 changed the detector set, and the finding generalizes.** The flagship inverse-pair detector needs a 20 trading day window, and the minimum extraction path is 5 trading days, so it does not defend the first cycle at all. Any statistical detector must be checked against the timeline of the thing it is meant to stop, not against a comfortable window length.
  - Three of M06's six adversarial scenarios name **Merit itself under pressure** as the adversary (a muted alarm, an overridden breaker, a dangerous action taken fast). That is deliberate: on an ops console the failure that actually happens is a control that gets ignored, not one that gets attacked.
  - **Two heredocs plus apostrophes in one Bash call intermittently breaks the shell parser here.** Writing content and commit messages to scratchpad files and using `cat` plus `git commit -F` is reliable; it cost two failed calls before I switched.
