### 2. What was ruled

**Verify.** Removing the field was the cheaper diff and loses on three grounds:
it discharges no part of `D2`, it is a contract break where verifying is a
tightening, and it deletes the half of the control that already exists.

**An absent secret refuses.** Never a pass, per call rather than at start, and
the deployment-time behaviour is the ruling rather than a `TODO`.

**Fail closed on the outage**, with the cost stated: new sign-ins stop, nobody
is logged out, and the alternative spends `otp_send_budget.spend_cents` on the
attacker's behalf on the endpoint whose own threat row is SMS pumping.

**In the handler, ahead of the budget, behind validation.** `requestOtp` is
blocked, so this is the only placement that is live today.

### 3. What was refused

- **Removing the field**, which the row admitted as the second of two answers.
- **Passing when no secret is set**, and the ruling names it as the shape most
  integrations ship with.
- **Failing open on a vendor outage**, and **a retry**.
- **`remoteip`**, on the `trustProxy` reading in section 5 of the entry.
- **Branching on Cloudflare's `error-codes`**, which would transcribe a vendor
  vocabulary into a control and could be wrong in the direction of admitting.
- **A new canonical code** and **a `required_factor`** on the `403`.
- **An environment variable for the `siteverify` URL**, which would be a way to
  point the control at something that always says yes.
- **`checkout.ts`** (session 415's), **`server.ts`** (session 413's),
  **`start.ts`**, and **`MERIT_BUILD_MASTER_PROMPT.md`**, which is frozen.
- **Any migration number.** The control holds no state, so **none was minted
  and none is owed.**
