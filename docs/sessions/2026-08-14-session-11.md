- **Blockers:** None.
- **Landmines:**
  - **Liability is monotone-decreasing in `max_payouts`, so the direction of every calibration error is known and safe.** The `w=3` recalibration and the workbook's risk engine were both computed at the longer ladder, so **the recorded margins are now conservative rather than exact**. That is the right way round, and it still needs correcting: exact recalibration is flagged for when `mc_lifecycle.py` lands. Do not quote the current margins as precise.
  - **The shorter ladder made [M14](../plans/M14-loyalty-retention.md)'s progressive-cap-release finding worse, not better, and it looks like the opposite.** The market mechanic is "after five payouts your cap goes up", and **on a 5-rung ladder there is no after: five payouts is the whole ladder.** The mechanic cannot be copied; it has to be re-keyed to an earlier ordinal, and re-keying it earlier is exactly what makes it more expensive, because the raised cap applies to more of the remaining rungs. Release at ordinal 5 is +20 percent on the lifetime bound; at ordinal 4 it is +40 percent. **A shorter ladder does not make cap release safer; it eliminates the cheap version and leaves only the expensive ones.**
  - **EC-137's disclosure problem got sharper.** The trader who discovers finiteness only when it fires now discovers it on the fifth payout rather than the eighth, so it arrives sooner and after less trust has been built. That is why the plan-page sentence carries the continuation clause in the same breath as the limit ("**then completes. Open another anytime.**") rather than a page away, and why the tracker counts **down**.
  - **The decoupling matters even though no live program exists, and the reason is easy to lose.** An engine that emits an invitation on ladder completion **has already made the promise**; the promise commits Merit, not the program. Retrofitting discretion onto a population that already believes the ladder leads somewhere is far more expensive than designing it in now, while the population is zero.
  - **Topstep's 0.71 percent is the number that settles the argument** and is worth keeping to hand. It is what makes "complete the ladder, get live capital" a description of something no firm actually operates, and it is the empirical backing for a framing Merit would otherwise be adopting on principle alone.
