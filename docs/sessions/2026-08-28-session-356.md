### 2. THE FEED'S IDENTITY SCOPE IS THE DISJUNCTION, AND THAT IS THE ONE JUDGMENT IN THIS SLICE

**`AdminEventQuery.scope` is a closed union and this module has three cases. Two are
one keyed read each. The third is the ruling.**

`ADR-191` clause 2 is that the predicate is the disjunction "and neither half may be
lost", and its section 4 counted both halves out of EVENTS.md's catalogue rather than
asserting them: of the 29 rows carrying the `TL` consumer, **ten name an account and no
identity** and nine name an identity and no account. An identity feed built on
`identity_id = $1` alone compiles, returns rows, raises nothing, and omits the first ten.

**The accessor offers an equality, so the disjunction is TWO READS.** That is
[`graph.ts`](../../apps/api/src/admin-source/graph.ts)'s move for
`identity_links_canonical_order` adopted rather than invented, and it is why `accounts`
is the second table in a module serving a feed over one.

**WHAT IT COSTS IS ON THE PAGE AND IS STATED.** Landmine 3. **What it still cannot reach
is `ADR-191` section 11 item 5 unchanged**: a merged identity's identity-level events
carry the dead uuid and no account, and no leg reaches them from the survivor.

**`scope=account` IS ONE LEG AND THAT IS NOT AN INCONSISTENCY.** An account-scoped
question is about the account; answering it with the owner's identity-level rows would
answer the identity scope's question on a page licensed for one account.

**`scope=operational` READS THE WHOLE TABLE.** `events` is append-only with retention
forever, so this grows in the operating age of the firm and `EventFeedCost.scanned` is
where that is admitted. **What would bound it is `ADR-157`'s range term and it is not
reached for**: minting one needs an import of `@merit/db` inside this directory, which
`test/db.test.ts` pins against, so the bound is owed to whoever moves that rule and the
cost is measured in the meantime.

### 3. THE DRILL-DOWN, AND THE TWO COLUMNS ITS OWN CONTROL CANNOT ADMIT

**This is the finding the dispatch did not have.**

[`admin-reads.ts:343`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-reads.ts)'s `assertContractScalars`
runs over every body this module returns, and `:326`'s `TRADING_DAY_KEY` is
`/_(?:day|on)$/`: anything under a name of that shape must be a `YYYY-MM-DD` string.
**That is correct for every typed response in API_CONTRACT section 8 and it collides
with the schema on the one route the corpus does not type.**

| Column | What it is | What the sweep does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`daily_marks.traded_day`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts) `:670` | `boolean NOT NULL` | **refuses it.** Measured: the route answers 500 with the column carried |
| `daily_marks.win_day` `:671` | `boolean NOT NULL` | the same |
| eleven nullable `date` columns across four sections | `date NULL` | **refuses `null` too**, because `typeof null === 'object'` fails the walk guard and falls through to the string check |

**Three answers were available and two are worse.** Carrying the booleans is a 500 on
every drill-down that has a mark. Renaming them puts a field name in an operator's hands
that no column has, on the screen whose whole discipline is that it shows the stored
row. **So they are omitted, the omission is asserted in both directions** -- the two keys
absent, and every other column of `daily_marks` present as a full key list -- **and the
sweep is reported rather than edited**, because `admin-reads.ts` is not this fence and a
gate is never weakened to pass it. The nullable days are absent keys for the same
reason, which is landmine 2.

**`gate_results` IS THE SECTION THE CORPUS WARNS ABOUT HARDEST AND IT NEEDED NOTHING.**
`M06` section 3.2 makes it load bearing and there is no such table; `SD-06` split it into
two `jsonb NOT NULL` columns on a registered table. Both are carried verbatim, the
stored `engine_eligible` is carried beside them unreconciled, and **a case reads this
module's own source with comments stripped and requires exactly two appearances of
