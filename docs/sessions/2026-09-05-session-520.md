### 5. The sweep, and the reader defect it found

**1,374 columns compared across 118 design records against 118 tables folded from 75 migrations. Three findings, all of them the two records the row named. There is no third stale page.**

**THE FIRST READER PRODUCED SIX FINDINGS AND THREE WERE AGAINST RECORDS THAT ARE TRUE.** It tested the whole `Constraints` cell for `not null`:

- `account_adjustments.promotional_credit_grant_id` reads `` null, unique where not null, fk ... ``, where the phrase belongs to an INDEX PREDICATE.
- `trading_calendar.session_open_at` and `.session_close_at` read `` **null exactly when `is_holiday`** (`0032`, was `not null`) ``, where the phrase is the record QUOTING WHAT IT USED TO SAY, beside the migration that changed it.

**Those two cells are the best-maintained nullability lines in the directory**, and the `trading_calendar` one did by hand exactly what this row makes mechanical. Working agreements section 9: **if a true line trips the gate, the gate is wrong**. Both cells stand byte for byte, the reader now splits the cell on top-level commas and reads the first term that speaks, and both cells are cases.

**FIVE OF 1,374 ROWS STATE NO NULLABILITY AND ALL FIVE ARE `GENERATED ALWAYS AS (...)` COLUMNS.** So silence is a FINDING unless the DDL makes the column generated, keyed on the DDL rather than on a written list: it costs zero repairs today and it is what stops the check being satisfied by deleting two words from a row.
