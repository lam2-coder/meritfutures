### The objective named four rules and not one of them is writable

**R-01, R-02, R-05 and R-06 were named as "the group A fixtures that are now writable". The two rows
that actually came unblocked are `R-03`'s.** A group named by RULE and a group named by SCENARIO do
not have the same members, and [M01 section 3.5](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)'s coverage column is
the mapping between them. Every cell below was checked against its own primary source, not against
[session 58](2026-08-18-session-58.md)'s report:

| Rule | Coverage column | Writable | The primary source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R-01** fill containment | GS-001, GS-030 | **NO** | `DailyMark` is `{tradingDay, opening, closing, high, low, realizedPnl, adjustment, fillCount, sourceHash}` ([`types.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/types.ts)) and carries **no instant**. [`rules.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/rules.ts) files R-01 among the four undeclared rules: "transcribing the CME year adds rows, not columns" |
| **R-02** counter advance | GS-002 | **ALREADY WRITTEN**, batch 3. It was the only group A fixture in the directory |
| **R-03** half day | GS-003, GS-032 | **YES. THIS SESSION.** `kind: "half"` maps to `CalendarDay.isHalfDay` in [`calendar.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/calendar.ts), `R-03` is in `IMPLEMENTED_RULES`, and `2026-11-27` is transcribed |
| **R-04** halted | GS-004, GS-031 | **NO** | `calendarRowsFromRecord` writes `halted: false` as a constant. No calendar moves it |
| **R-05** session bounds | GS-030 | **NO** | `CalendarDay` is `{tradingDay, isHalfDay, halted, sequence}`. Session bounds are two columns it does not carry, and R-05 is undeclared |
| **R-06** last closed day | GS-035 | **NO**, and it is the one row whose blocker is not the engine. **R-06 IS declared.** GS-035 is "payout at 23:59:59 versus batch at 00:05": it needs a **clock**, `INV-01` forbids the engine from reading one, and it has been a group B row all along |

**So the objective's four rules and the two writable rows do not intersect at all.** The rules named
are the group A rules a calendar cannot reach; the rows that moved are the ones a calendar was the
only thing holding. **The re-derivation agrees with session 58's table row for row**, which is worth
saying plainly: the instruction to re-derive rather than inherit found no error in what was inherited,
and the check was still the right one to run because the objective built on top of it was wrong.

**Groups F and H gained nothing, re-checked and unchanged.** Every group H row is behind `L-11`'s
settlement block, which needs `payout_request_id`, `ordinal`, `approved_cents`, `basis_trading_day`
and `effective_trading_day` and has no calendar clause at all. Group F's one calendar row, `GS-059`,
lost its calendar clause when the file spanned three holiday clusters and keeps `cadenceAnchorDay`,
which only a settlement sets. `GS-060` and `GS-080` are `compare.ts` diffing flat fields against a
nested `engine_gates`.

### The absorbed session is the part of the objective that cannot be written, and it is not close

The objective called it "the one worth pinning": trade date `2026-09-08` opens Sunday `2026-09-06` at
17:00 CT where a naive `session_rule` computes Monday 17:00, so a fill in that gap lands in no
session. **That is real, it is [ADR-055](../decisions/ADR-055.md)'s `absorbs_into`, and it is
`R-01`'s** — the first row of the table above. The format has no instant to place, the record states
no open to place it against, and `advanceDay` reads neither. The calendar file says so in a key of
its own (`what_this_format_cannot_carry`); this session re-checked it against `DailyMark` rather than
believing it, and `DailyMark` has nine fields and none is a timestamp.

**What a fixture CAN see of a holiday is the absence**, which is why both files sit on the
Thanksgiving week: `2026-11-26` is not a session, and `2026-11-25` and `2026-11-27` are adjacent in
`sequence` two calendar days apart. That is R-02's distinction, and **neither file pins it**, because
`sequence` subtraction reaches no field this format carries — the same sentence GS-002's sibling
already had to write.
