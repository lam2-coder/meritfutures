### 4. The sort, and the rule it needed

The sort is [ADR-312](../decisions/ADR-312.md) section 2. **What is worth recording here is that the row's stop condition needed a rule the row did not supply**, and the session chose one rather than picking silently:

- **Seven of the thirteen ports carry more than one obstruction**, and `useCertificateRevokeBackend` carries three that fall in three different buckets. "Exactly one bucket" is therefore a choice about which obstruction counts.
- **The rule chosen: the obstruction a session dispatched at that port TODAY would meet first and could not pass.** It is the only question the letter answers for a dispatcher, and it makes `(C)` decisive wherever a port reduces to another port.
- **Every other obstruction is recorded beside its port with its own bucket**, under *what the bucket does not discharge*, because three ports would still be blocked after their bucket clears and a letter alone hides that.

**The row's bucket (B) was also narrowed, once, in writing.** It lists *"an unmade ruling"* among the founder-owed things; read literally that makes most of what this corpus dispatches founder-owed. This session ruled that a ruling reachable from sources already in the corpus is Merit's, which is what rows `304` and `305` did in practice, and only a ruling needing an outside input is the founder's. **Both judgements are the first two things [ADR-312](../decisions/ADR-312.md)'s approval block asks the founder to check.**
