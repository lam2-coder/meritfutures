### 1. The objective, and the answer

**Row `271` granted the fence [ADR-268](../decisions/ADR-268.md) lacked, so that the defect it measured could be repaired: `pg` and Drizzle were rendering every `date` column through the process's local timezone, and the database day `2026-08-28` arrived in Merit code as `'2026-08-27'` on any deployment east of UTC.**

The ruling is [ADR-271](../decisions/ADR-271.md), six clauses, `proposed` and UNSIGNED.

**The repair is one `setTypeParser(1082, wire => wire)` call.** The session is not one line because the parser is global, and the enumeration of what that changes is the work the row was actually asking for.

---

### 2. The defect, re-derived rather than taken on trust

ADR-268 measured this and this session did not cite it. Both libraries were read at source and then run.

| Step | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `pg` registers a `date` parser | `pg-types@2.2.0/lib/textParsers.js:174` | `register(1082, parseDate)` |
| `postgres-date@1.0.7` on a bare day | its `getDate()` | `new Date(year, month, day)`, **the process's LOCAL midnight** |
| Drizzle renders it | `drizzle-orm@0.45.2/pg-core/columns/date.js` | `value.toISOString().slice(0, -14)`, **UTC** |

And the dispatcher was confirmed: **`client.ts` set no type parser at all**, and the only `setTypeParser` text anywhere in the tree was in the three assertions ADR-268 left behind.

**MEASURED BEFORE A BYTE CHANGED**, on the database day `2026-08-28`:

```
TZ=UTC                 merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Europe/Berlin       merit "2026-08-27"   *** DIFFERENT ***
TZ=America/Chicago     merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
TZ=Pacific/Kiritimati  merit "2026-08-27"   *** DIFFERENT ***
TZ=Pacific/Midway      merit "2026-08-28"   SAME
```

**THE ASYMMETRY IS THIS SESSION'S ADDITION TO THE FINDING.** Only offsets east of UTC failed. A local midnight west of UTC is the same UTC day a few hours in, so `toISOString()` returned the right answer there, and **`America/Chicago` is the exchange's own zone and is one of the ones that never failed.** The row's requirement that the proof cover a positive and a negative offset was right for a stronger reason than it stated: the negative side is not a second control, it is the side that hid the defect.

---

### 3. The enumeration, which is why this was a session

The row's trap was that `setTypeParser` is global. Four facts bound the blast radius, and three of them are structural.

1. **The type does not move.** No `{ mode: 'date' }` anywhere, so every one of the 52 columns is a `PgDateString`, whose mapper returns a `string` on **both** branches. A value change, never a shape change.
2. **The change is read-direction only.** Parsers convert wire text to JS values. Every `WHERE` and `ORDER BY` over a `date` in this tree is evaluated inside PostgreSQL and is untouched.
3. **There is no raw SQL read path.** `.execute(` appears nowhere in the source directories, so nothing bypasses drizzle's mapper.
4. **All 14 `.select(` in the tree are in [`scoped-db.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts)**, which is [ADR-102](../decisions/ADR-102.md)'s architecture doing what it was built for. The reader enumeration is the door enumeration, and it is [ADR-271](../decisions/ADR-271.md) section 3's table of ten.

**THE ROW'S KYC WARNING TURNED OUT TO POINT AT THE RULE THAT PREVENTS IT.** `kyc_verifications.expires_at` is `timestamptz`. [ADR-146](../decisions/ADR-146.md) clause 2's suffix rule keeps instants and days in disjoint vocabularies, so a parser aimed at one cannot reach the other. **That rule was written as an API-surface refusal and it is load-bearing three layers below the API.**

**Then it was confirmed by running rather than left as an argument.** With the parser installed, the whole estate ran **292 files with exactly one failure**, and that failure was the assertion ADR-268 pinned to the defect.

`RI-25` was watched red under four seeds on the real tree: the call commented out, the call deleted, `1184` given a parser beside the correct one, and a second parser added to `scoped-db.ts`. **The first of those passed on the first version of the check**, which is section 6.

### 5. The three pinned assertions, and the one that could feel it

| # | Assertion | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"a `Date` on a calendar row is rendered as a UTC day"* | **KEPT, value unchanged, claim rewritten** |
| 2 | *"THE RENDERING IS `toISOString` ... the DAY BEFORE"* | **KEPT, value unchanged, was the bug report and is now the motive** |
| 3 | *"nothing in this package installs a `date` type parser"* | **INVERTED. The only one that turned red** |

**WHY 1 AND 2 COULD NOT TURN RED IS THE FINDING.** That suite composes its own rows and answers them through `drizzle-orm/pg-proxy`, so it exercises drizzle's mapper and never `pg`'s parser. **A test that supplies its own driver values cannot see a driver defect.** Their expected values are genuinely unchanged, because drizzle's coercion is unchanged; what they stopped being is bug reports.

Seeded on the real tree by **commenting the call out**, which is the exact shape a revert takes, it reported **PASS on the restored defect** — because `client.ts`'s own header explains the repair at length and the matcher found the explanation rather than the call.

### 7. Done / next / blockers / landmines

**Done.** [ADR-271](../decisions/ADR-271.md) written, `proposed`, UNSIGNED. The parser installed once in [`client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts). The three-offset proof landed as a permanent control. The three pinned assertions moved with an account of each. `RI-25` minted with a seeded red-watch. Row `271` marked TAKEN.

**Next.** The findings in [ADR-271](../decisions/ADR-271.md) section 9 are unrowed: the `calibration_observed_at` rename (a migration), the six unsuffixed `date` columns (a convention ruling), and `TZ` plus `DateStyle` in the deployment contract. **The first is cheapest now and gets more expensive the day something reads that column.**

**Blockers.** None. Nothing here waits on a decision.

**Landmines.**
- **The parser is global to the `pg` module and [ADR-006](../decisions/ADR-006.md) puts `pg-boss` on this pool at session 147.** `pg-boss`'s own schema is `timestamptz` and not `date`, so nothing it reads is in 1082's path **today**. The narrower construction, a `types` object on the `Pool`, is cheap now and means proving the queue unaffected afterwards.
- **Facts 3 and 4 of the enumeration are true until somebody adds a file**, and only one of them has a gate: `merit/no-raw-db-client` keeps the pool in one place, and nothing mechanically keeps `.select(` in `scoped-db.ts`.
- **`docs/STATE.md` carries the `gen:adr_count` paragraph at `RI-12`'s ceiling.** This append adds no further copy, as sessions 447 and after have each recorded.

**Files touched.** [`packages/db/src/client.ts`](../../packages/db/src/client.ts), [`packages/db/test/date-column-timezone.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/date-column-timezone.test.ts) (new), [`packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts`](../../packages/db/test/last-closed-trading-day-door.test.ts), [`packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs`](../../packages/tooling/checks/repo-invariants.mjs), [`packages/tooling/test/repo-invariants.test.ts`](../../packages/tooling/test/repo-invariants.test.ts), [`docs/decisions/ADR-271.md`](../decisions/ADR-271.md), [ALLOCATION](../decisions/ALLOCATION.md) row `271`, [decisions/README](../decisions/README.md), [STATE](../STATE.md), this log.
