### Why a one-line repair took four sessions to land

Four sessions is the honest count and none of them did anything wrong. [Session 146](2026-08-24-session-146.md) found the workspace line and recorded it *"OUT OF FENCE, RECORDED AND NOT REPAIRED"*; [session 155](2026-08-24-session-155.md) carried it forward as an open item; [ADR-085:77](../decisions/ADR-085.md) named it a third time under the same heading. On the STATE line, [ADR-073:142](../decisions/ADR-073.md) predicted the exact cell that would carry the losing assignment and left it because that fence did not admit STATE, and [ADR-080:193](../decisions/ADR-080.md) found it still there and left it for the same reason, writing *"this fence is append-only on that file. It is a later session's one-line repair and is named twice so it is not found by a gate."*

**Each of those refusals was correct and the aggregate is a four-day-old known defect in the file the next session reads first.** That is the actual cost of the fence and it should be stated as a cost rather than explained away. A session that reaches outside its fence to fix an obvious one-liner is a session whose diff no longer matches its dispatch, which is the failure the fence exists to prevent, and the repair it makes on the way past is made without reading the rulings that govern it. **The corpus has one live example of exactly that**: [ADR-086:131](../decisions/ADR-086.md) records session 145 contradicting its own finding in the file that carries it.

**What the four days actually bought is the audit trail.** By the time this session opened the file, the defect had been named three times with a citation each, so verifying it took reading two block quotes rather than re-deriving a ruling. **The repair was cheap because the refusals were expensive.** The correct target for improvement is not the fence, it is the **latency between naming and dispatch**: ADR-080 named this on 2026-08-22 and the dispatch arrived on 2026-08-24, and nothing in between promotes a twice-named one-liner into a claim row on its own. A named-and-unrepaired register that a dispatcher reads would have closed this in a day without loosening anything.

**And the counter-example is in this same session.** ONE was dispatched from a backlog entry that had already been discharged, because the discharge and the entry landed on either side of a merge. **A backlog is a claim about the tree and it goes stale in exactly the way the lines it tracks do.** Verify the target before repairing it; the finding may already be gone.
