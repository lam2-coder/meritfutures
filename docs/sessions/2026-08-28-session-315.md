### 1. The measurement that changed the ruling

The dispatch and the allocation row both describe finding (A) as a hole in `LT-07`'s **credit**
slot: [M05:136](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) writes `debit firm_treasury; credit the payout
wallet position`, `0009`'s `ledger_accounts_code_is_declared` declares seven codes, and no pooled
payout position is among them. That is true, and it invites the repair that fills the hole.

**Reading the row beside it says the slot next door is wrong too.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `LT-06`, [M05:135](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) | debit `trader_wallet` (identity) `amount_cents`; **credit `firm_treasury` `amount_cents`** |
| `LT-07`, [M05:136](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) | **debit `firm_treasury`**; credit the payout wallet position |
| the sign, [`posting.ts:235-236`](../../packages/ledger/src/posting.ts) | `+t.amountCents` on the debit, `-t.amountCents` on the credit |

So the external leg contributes `-amount_cents` then `+amount_cents` to `firm_treasury` and
**returns it to exactly where it started.** [M05:141](../plans/M05-payout-system.md) names
`firm_treasury` as the account where a cash movement books, in the same paragraph that rules cash
moves *"at LT-02 and LT-07, not here"*, and `LT-07`'s own row calls itself *"The external leg's
cash movement"*. **A leg whose purpose is to move cash out moves that account by nothing.**

**It needs no `kind`, and that is what makes it usable.** The dispatch says (C) may be recorded as
an absence and that no direction may be inferred from silence. The measurement above is invariant
under every value `kind` could take, so the ruling on (A) does not wait on (C) and does not have
to guess it. **Substituting any code into the credit slot leaves it exactly where it is**, because
it reads the debit slot, so `0052` was returned rather than spent on a repair that would not
repair.

**`LT-07`'s row also names no amount**, where every other row of that table does
(`approved_cents`, `trader_cents`, `firm_cents`, `amount_cents`, and `LT-02`'s explicit
`-trader_cents`). It is derivable from zero-sum and is recorded rather than ruled.

### 2. `trader_wallet`, refused on arithmetic, and what that does to `ADR-124`

The allocation row refuses `trader_wallet` by citing [ADR-124](../decisions/ADR-124.md), which
records `LT-02` and `LT-07` as the FIRM-ONLY postings a global halt must refuse.
**[ADR-124:33](../decisions/ADR-124.md) reads *"`LT-02` and `LT-07` both move `firm_treasury`
against the payout wallet position"*, which is [M05:131,136](../plans/M05-payout-system.md)
quoted.** It is a citation of the defective row, so it cannot be the authority that repairs it.

**The refusal survives on stronger ground.** Under `SD-M5-07`, `LT-06` already debits
`trader_wallet` by `amount_cents` at approval. An `LT-07` crediting `trader_wallet` restores it,
so the trader's wallet would end an external withdrawal holding exactly what it held before it,
**and the money would have left the firm.** No scope argument and no `kind` is needed for that.

So `LT-07` stays firm-only, [ADR-124](../decisions/ADR-124.md) clause 3 keeps its conclusion, and
what is recorded is that a later session may not read that clause as evidence ABOUT the row.
**The entry is not amended**; the finding is filed where the repair will be read.

### 3. Finding (C), and the thing behind it that was not in the dispatch

**PostgreSQL 16.13, `0001` through `0051` applied forward-only from empty under
`ON_ERROR_STOP`, 51 of 51.** Three things were EXECUTED rather than read out of DDL:

| Executed | Result |
|---|---|
| `SELECT count(*) FROM ledger_accounts` | **0** |
| `INSERT INTO ledger_accounts (code,kind,scope) VALUES ('firm_treasury', <kind>, 'firm')` for `'asset'`, then `'liability'`, then `'revenue'` | **all three accepted** |
| the same with `'payouts_payable'` | refused by `ledger_accounts_code_is_declared` |

**Nothing seeds the chart of accounts.** `firm_treasury` has no `kind` because it has no ROW, in
any environment this tree can build, and the schema constrains `kind` on the row rather than
against the code. `treasury_balances.account_code` is `text NOT NULL` with no foreign key and no
CHECK into `ledger_accounts`, and its own design record says the table is *"anchored outside our
own ledger"* deliberately, so it does not bind the class either.

**The only `kind` literals in this tree are three fixtures inside a probe** whose purpose is to
watch `LEDGER-C1`, `LEDGER-C2` and zero-sum fire. A fixture chosen to make a constraint reject
something is not a statement of the chart. **Four of the seven codes have no `kind` written
anywhere at all**: `firm_treasury`, `psp_clearing`, `reserve` and `promotional_credit`.

**The evidence for `asset` was weighed and NOT taken.** Five sites call `firm_treasury` the
account where a cash movement books ([M05:141](../plans/M05-payout-system.md),
[ADR-027:48](../decisions/ADR-027.md), [ADR-033:20](../decisions/ADR-033.md),
[ADR-067:77](../decisions/ADR-067.md), [`0038:48-49`](../../packages/db/migrations/0038_account_adjustments.sql)),
and cash is an asset in every accounting convention. **It is still not written**, for two reasons.
The corpus's own arithmetic disagrees with it: `LT-06` credits `firm_treasury` at approval, and if
the account is cash then the external leg derecognizes cash at approval, which is the timing four
documents each refused on `LT-01`. And nothing in this fence needs the answer, because section 1's
measurement holds for every kind. **An entry that rules a fact it does not need, from evidence that
contradicts a row it is not repairing, is guessing with a citation attached.**

### 4. Why the split, and why (B) is not blocked by (A)

The reservation left the split to be argued. **They are two, and they live in two tables.**

- **(A) is `ledger_accounts` and `ledger_entries`**: which account a leg names, and which side it
  sits on. BLOCKED on section 3's absence.
- **(B) is `ledger_transactions`**: one `text NOT NULL UNIQUE` column on the header row. A
  transaction's key is decided by which event it records and against which reference, and `kind`,
  `reference_kind` and `reference_id` are all columns on that header, **so the key is decidable
  with the entries still undecided.**

Folding (B) into (A) would file a convention governing every posting in the system inside an entry
whose own ruling is a refusal, and a reader arriving at `LT-02` or `LT-08` would have to know that
`LT-07`'s entry is where their key is spelled. [ADR-124](../decisions/ADR-124.md) section 5 names
that hazard in its own words.

**And the two conventions were not in conflict.** `PAYOUT_ENDPOINT` is a module constant three
call sites share, and [`admin-payouts.ts:909`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-payouts.ts) says
why in terms: *"`PAYOUT_ENDPOINT` IS IMPORTED RATHER THAN RETYPED"*. So `LT-01`'s prefix does not
vary with the door: **it names the payout-approval event and is merely SPELLED as a route**, which
is the identical rule [`wallet-withdrawals.ts:69-71`](../../apps/api/src/routes/wallet-withdrawals.ts)
states when it refuses a prefix. What neither spelling generalises to is a SECOND posting about
one referent, which is what `LT-07` is.

**Every leg EXECUTED against the same database:**

| Executed | Result |
|---|---|
| `LT-06` under the withdrawal's bare stored key | accepted |
| `LT-07`, same withdrawal, same bare key, different `kind` | refused, `ledger_transactions_idempotency_key_key` |
| the same key under a DIFFERENT `reference_kind` and `reference_id` | **refused too**, so the UNIQUE is GLOBAL and per-reference scoping is unavailable without superseding a merged constraint |
| `'POST /webhooks/rise wd-key-02'` and `'POST /admin/withdrawals/:id/settle wd-key-02'` | **both accepted, count for that settlement = 2.** The endpoint prefix watched failing exactly as `LT-06` predicted |
| `'wallet_withdrawal_settlement wd-key-01'` beside `LT-06`'s bare key, then replayed | accepted, then refused |

`LT-01`'s three doors are **not** re-spelled: `ledger_entries` is append-only with no `UPDATE` and
no `DELETE` grant, so re-spelling a landed key costs a compensating posting rather than an edit.

two postings for one settlement, in section 4's table, is the corpus's own predicted failure
