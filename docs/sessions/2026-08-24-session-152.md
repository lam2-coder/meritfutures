## 2026-08-24 - Session 152: six Railway services, and a prose phrase that became a service identity in three transcriptions and no ruling

- **Objective:** rule on [INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s Railway service table, which names four services where [ADR-083](../decisions/ADR-083.md) requires more, and decide what the compound name `portal-api` was ever naming.
- **Done:** **[ADR-088](../decisions/ADR-088.md)**, `status: proposed` and unsigned. **Six services**, derived rather than counted. **`portal-api` ruled a STALE FUSED PROCESS NAME and not a shared-origin label**, so it retires. INFRA amended under it in four places: section 2's list, a new section 2.1, section 3's third hard rule, and one sentence in section 13.2. **28 of 28 gates, 7 of 7 invariants, 104 test files at 1,512 passed and 1 skipped, `falsify.mjs` all 28 clean and dirty with 56 scope cases and 10 loader cases, `pnpm run verify` exit 0.**
- **Next:** the six `portal-api` code sites in [`apps/portal`](../../apps/portal) and [`apps/api`](../../apps/api), listed below and in the entry. `146`, `147` and `148` are unaffected by this branch and touch none of its files except the three shared registries.
- **Blockers:** none.

### The second question is the one that decides anything, and the dispatch was right to say so

**Six is the easy half and [ADR-083](../decisions/ADR-083.md) section 7 had already published the number.** It was still derived here rather than accepted, because the count rests on a claim about the platform that nobody in the corpus had stated: **is `apps/api` deployed twice two Railway services, or one service running two instances?**

**It is two, and the argument is a variable rather than a preference.** [`surface.ts`](../../apps/api/src/surface.ts)'s `resolveSurface` reads `MERIT_API_SURFACE`, has **no default**, and throws on unset, in its own words *"There is no safe guess, so there is no guess."* [INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md)'s why-cell buys *"per-service environment variables"* and [section 7](../architecture/INFRA.md) repeats it: secrets are *"scoped per service."* **A scope stated per service cannot separate two instances inside one service**, so one service hands both instances the same value and there is no second surface at all.

> **That failure is silent, which is the reason it is the approval clause's first conjunct.** Both processes start cleanly and serve a coherent route set. If the shared value is `operator`, the public origin serves `/admin/*` and `/internal/*`, and [API_CONTRACT section 12](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s required **404 becomes a 200** with nothing anywhere reporting it. The whole of ADR-083's structural argument, that the 404 is the router's rather than a check somebody maintains, dies on one merged environment.

Two further chains close it independently: the deployments answer on two origins, and `ADMIN_ORIGIN` carries *"its own Cloudflare rules and its own IP allowlist"*, which a single service spanning both origins would have to hold twice.

### `portal-api` was never ruled as a service name, and that is the finding the brief did not have

[ADR-007](../decisions/ADR-007.md)'s `Decision:` line, read in the file rather than recalled, says **"Railway for the four services (site, portal API, admin, worker)"**. **Two words of prose. No hyphen and no code span.**

| Step | File | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [ADR-007](../decisions/ADR-007.md) `Decision:` | *"portal API"*, prose, describing a service that serves the portal and its API |
| 2 | [INFRA section 2](../architecture/INFRA.md) | `` `portal-api` ``, hyphenated into a code-spanned identifier |
| 3 | [`apps/portal/src/index.ts:41`](../../apps/portal/src/index.ts) | ``export const SERVICE = 'portal-api' as const``, a string constant |

**A description became an identity in three transcriptions and no ruling.** That is [ADR-074](../decisions/ADR-074.md)'s subject arriving from the direction that document does not watch: not one item in two places disagreeing, but **one item acquiring a second KIND on the way between them**. Nothing in the corpus can see this shape, and no gate is proposed for it here, because the seam is prose-to-identifier and the corpus has one instance of it.

### The steelman was answered rather than waved at, and one reading killed it

**The origin reading is real: `portal-api` may always have been shorthand for the shared origin, in which case ADR-083 is right that nothing moves.** Five readings refuse it, and reading 4 is the one that closes it mechanically:

> **If a name in that four-name list were an origin, `admin` would be the admin origin. [ADR-083](../decisions/ADR-083.md) put TWO services on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`, `admin` and `api-admin`, so an origin-naming convention had already collapsed inside its own list before this session touched it.** Under the process reading, every one of the four names is exactly one `apps/*` directory, and **`portal-api` is the only compound and the only name that is not a directory.**

The other four: the table's why-cell buys process properties (environment variables, private network addresses, an OS), and **an origin has none of the three**; INFRA has a separate place for origins at section 13.2 and **section 2's table has never written a hostname in any row**; section 3 uses both nouns in one sentence, *"deployed as its own service ON `ADMIN_ORIGIN`"*; and the fusion the name describes is the one [API_CONTRACT section 1](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md)'s *"no privileged back door"* forbids.

### ADR-083 says two things about this and only one of them is right

**Section 3:** *"All three remain true under this ruling, because sharing an origin and sharing an edge zone are not sharing a process."* **Section 7, one page later:** *"a service name that describes one thing and is named for two."*

**The stated reason has two limbs and it is offered over three claims.** The origin limb covers INFRA section 13.2. The edge-zone limb covers [OVERVIEW section 7](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md). **INFRA section 2 is a list of processes and neither limb reaches it.** So section 7 is the accurate half, and section 3's blanket sentence is amended to the two documents its own reason covers. **Nothing in ADR-083's case for a separate deployable moves**: that case stands on API_CONTRACT section 1 and on the three arrows into the `API` container, both untouched.

### The cost ADR-083 declined to pay has not been incurred yet

ADR-083 declined the rename with *"renaming it is not free: it is a Railway service identity."* **True of a service that exists.** Measured on this tree rather than assumed from *"nothing is deployed yet"*: **no `railway.json`, no `railway.toml`, no Railway configuration file of any name anywhere in the repository, and no deploy step in any of the four files in [`.github/workflows/`](../../.github/workflows)**. The project is unprovisioned, so the entire cost sits on the far side of provisioning, and today the rename is **six strings, one of which is a definition.**

### What INFRA gained, and the one thing in it that is not a naming change

New **section 2.1** is a five-column deployment table: Railway service, codebase, `MERIT_API_SURFACE`, origin, what it serves. Two things in it are new information rather than a restatement:

1. **Two services now share each public-facing origin, so Cloudflare routes `/api/v1/*` by path** on `app.meritfutures.com` and on `ADMIN_ORIGIN`. **That obligation was created by [ADR-083](../decisions/ADR-083.md) and hidden by the fused name**, which needed no path routing on the public origin because one process answered everything.
2. **Section 3's third hard rule named one service on `ADMIN_ORIGIN` and two run there.** The repair is an inventory repair and **not** a control repair, and the difference is worth the sentence it takes: the Cloudflare rules and the IP allowlist are scoped to the **origin**, so they covered `api-admin` from the day it was ruled into existence. A reader who saw the stale sentence and concluded C-08 had a hole would have been wrong, and one who saw it and allowlisted only the admin console would have been right by accident.
