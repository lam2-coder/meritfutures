### 1. The count, measured before anything was built

The dispatch's heuristic was a grep over a handful of phrasings and it said thirty-one unsigned with nineteen carrying a question. **Reproduced here it is a different population, and the shape of the difference is the finding.**

| Reading | Measured |
|---|---|
| Entries carrying the literal `PENDING, UNSIGNED` | **31.** The dispatch's number, exactly |
| Entries whose approval statement is withheld and carries no disposition | **54** |
| Of those, carrying the marked block before this session | **20** |
| Of those, carrying nothing of the kind | **34** |
| Entries whose approval status this check cannot read at all | **32**, every one a pre-FREEZE ruling with no approval statement |

**`PENDING, UNSIGNED` is one spelling of several and it is not the newest one.** `ADR-161` through `ADR-164` and `ADR-166` write `**Approval line: UNSIGNED.**` with no `PENDING`; `ADR-113` and `ADR-158` write `**Unsigned. One checkable clause.**` under a section heading; `ADR-144` and `ADR-155` write `NOT GIVEN`; `ADR-145` writes a blank signature rule. **A check keyed on the dispatch's phrase would have gone green while the convention moved out from under it**, which is why the scope is a status token in an approval statement rather than a string.

### 2. Why a marked block and not a phrase list

The tempting implementation is a list of the ways an entry can gesture at a founder judgement, and every one of them is in the tree: *"the clause a later session will want to reverse"*, *"the part that needs the founder"*, *"that judgment is the founder's and no grep reaches it"*. **A phrase list is a second copy of a convention**: it drifts on the first session that writes a twenty-fourth phrasing, and it passes an entry that merely mentions a founder.

**The corpus had already chosen a marker and thirty-two entries were using it.** `**What a founder read adds and this entry cannot.**`, bolded, and once as a section heading in `ADR-164`. The check adopts that rather than inventing a rule it would then have to police, and normalising the twenty that already complied cost nothing because they already carried it.

### 3. Both misreads were watched, and each is now a seeded case

**`ADR-111` IS SIGNED AND THE CHECK REPORTED IT UNSIGNED.** Its approval section opens by recording that the entry landed with no approval line, runs a shell block whose comments start with `#`, and signs at the bottom: `**SIGNED ON DELEGATED AUTHORITY, 2026-08-26**`. Reading the section as ending at the first `#` inside the fence lost the signature. **A fence is not a heading now**, and the seeded case is that shape.

**`ADR-119` AND `ADR-137` ARE UNSIGNED AND THE CHECK DROPPED THEM OUT OF SCOPE.** One has `**REFUSED**` in a table cell about an empty world; the other cites a recommendation another entry `GRANTED`. A disposition read from anywhere in the section drops an entry silently, which is this file's first rule broken where nobody sees it. **A disposition is read only at the front of a line**, and a withholding token is still read anywhere, and `covers` states why the two sides are not symmetric.

**`git status --porcelain` was empty after each.** The eight cases in [`repo-invariants.test.ts`](../../packages/tooling/test/repo-invariants.test.ts) carry the same directions, plus the two misreads above and the case that matters most: **"nothing here is a judgement, and here is why" passes on exactly the same terms as three named judgements**, because the check reads the block's length and never its meaning. An entry forced to fabricate a question is worse than one that had none, and `ADR-153`'s answer is the real instance of it.

### 5. What is deliberately not caught

**An entry whose approval status this check cannot read is OUT OF SCOPE and not a finding.** Every pre-FREEZE ruling that records no approval statement is in that position. Guessing in that direction would fail a shelf of documents for a convention that did not exist when they were written, and it is a second defect with its own argument to make.
