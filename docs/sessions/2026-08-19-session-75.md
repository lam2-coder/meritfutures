### RE-P-15, and what it can and cannot catch

`packages/rules-engine/test/engine-eligible-conjunction.property.test.ts`, four sections:

1. **The property.** `engineEligible === AND(the six)` on every state of four fold shapes, including breach rows, which is what [ADR-057](../decisions/ADR-057.md) turned on.
2. **The record is closed at six.** Without this, section 1 is half a property: a seventh gate conjoined by the engine would leave it green while `engineEligible` depended on a term `INV-15` does not enumerate.
3. **Non-vacuity, measured.** Both verdicts are reached, all six gates are observed passing and failing, and the three terms the folds isolate as a **sole** failure are asserted by name.
4. **Every term is load bearing.** Directed states over `evaluateEngineGates`, breaking one gate at a time.

**The four fold shapes are not decoration.** A plain funded fold reaches `engineEligible: true` **zero** times in 2,583 states, which would have made section 1 compare `false` against `false` forever. Banking `winDaysCount` in the prior hands the decision to the profit gates and is the only way a generated fold reaches `true` at all. `R-33` is configured 0 on all three v1 plans, so a `withFundedMinTradingDays` variant is the only way a generated state fails it outside a breach.

### The landmine: R-35's term cannot be killed, and that is a fact about the rules

`gates.ts:185` makes `buffer.pass` equal to `withdrawableCents > 0`, and `R-39` requires `min(withdrawable, cap) >= min_payout_cents`, which `CV-15` fixes at 10,000c. So **`buffer.pass === false` implies `minimumAmount.pass === false` in every state that exists**, and deleting `gates.buffer.pass` from the conjunction changes no verdict anywhere. The engine's own comment already called this PW-01 domination.

**The compensating control is to assert the domination instead of faking a sole failure.** The suite's last test asserts, over every generated state, that a failing `R-35` is always accompanied by a failing `R-39`. If that ever stops being true the term becomes killable and the suite says so on the day it changes. **Writing a test that appeared to kill the mutant would have required a state the fold cannot produce, which is a green line and not a control.**
