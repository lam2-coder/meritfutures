### 1. The tripwire, and the fix that was refused

The dispatch named the forbidden fix and the reason. **The counterfactual was
executed rather than argued**, which is the part worth keeping:

```
--- COUNTERFACTUAL: what the pre-0065 twenty-three-column writer would have stored ---
  a BREACHED account stored by the old writer reads back: breached=false breach_kind=null lifetime_settled_cents=0
  the engine said breached=true breach_kind=trailing_eod_floor. NOTHING REFUSED IT.
```

Widening case `1.2`'s expected list from three names to six makes the suite
green and produces exactly that row for every account, which
`databaseAccountReads` -- wired in production at
[`start.ts`](../../apps/api/src/start.ts) -- would then serve as truth. **A
wrong answer is worse than the honest rejection it replaces.**

### 2. The fence could not hold the objective, and this is the report

The dispatch's work statement is *"extend `RULE_STATE_WRITE_COLUMNS` and
`ruleStateValues` ... to carry the three from the engine's `RuleState`"*.
**`ruleStateValues` never sees a `RuleState`.** It takes a `RuleStateRow`, which
is declared in `apps/worker/src/batch/ports.ts` and carried none of the three
either. `RuleStateValues` is a mapped type over the column list, so extending
the list alone is a compile error and no arrangement of the three fenced files
lands the objective.

Two files off the fence were taken, minimally, under the dispatch's own clause
that a fence colliding with a stop condition is the thing written carelessly:
`ports.ts` for the three fields and `nightly.ts` for the three assignments.
**Nothing else in either file moved.** Both were watched by `tsc` seeds (section
4, seeds 6 and 7) rather than assumed to be load-bearing.

### 3. No ruling was missing, so the number goes back

`ADR-210` was reserved CONDITIONALLY for *"an equality the `CHECK` does not
already fix, or a `breach_kind` member the engine produces and the vocabulary
lacks"*. Neither exists, established at the engine rather than assumed:

| `0065` constraint | Why transcription satisfies it |
|---|---|
| `rule_states_breach_flag_matches_kind` | `day/breach.ts` returns `(false, null)` or `(true, <kind>)` and never a mixed pair; `day/advance.ts`'s breach branch is GUARDED on `breach.breached && breach.kind !== null`; `day/progression.ts`'s expiry writes `(false, null)` on an account that closed without breaching. Every other state carries the pair through a spread |
| `rule_states_breach_kind_is_a_breach_kind` | The `CHECK` lists `BreachKind`'s three members and `packages/db/test/rule-state-breach-vocabulary.test.ts` is the comparator. Case `2.9` adds the writer-side half: the kind the ENGINE produced is read against the vocabulary read out of `0065` |
| `rule_states_no_settlements_no_lifetime_total` | `payout/settle.ts` increments `payoutsSettledCount` and moves `lifetimeSettledCents` in ONE object literal, so the total cannot move without the count |
| `lifetime_settled_cents >= 0` | The fold opens at `0n` and only that literal adds, of `fact.approvedCents`, which `0010` bounds at `>= 0` |

**THE BREACH PAIR IS TRANSCRIBED AND DELIBERATELY NOT DERIVED.** `breached` is
`breach_kind IS NOT NULL` and the mapping could compute it. Deriving one side
from the other satisfies `rule_states_breach_flag_matches_kind` **by
construction**, which turns the constraint into a tautology and stops it
detecting the thing `0065` stores a derivable column FOR. Case `2.8` pins the
opposite: an inconsistent pair reaches the values object unrepaired.

### 4. Executed, and the constraints watched refusing

Rebuilt for the run: **61** migrations, **115** tables, `rule_states` at **29**
columns and **16** `CHECK` constraints, all read from the catalogue.

```
--- what the mapping would send ---
CLEAN   (no breach, day 2026-08-10)
  lifetimeSettledCents=0 (bigint)  breached=false  breachKind=null  payoutsSettledCount=0
BREACH  (trailing floor, day 2026-08-11)
  lifetimeSettledCents=0 (bigint)  breached=true  breachKind=trailing_eod_floor  payoutsSettledCount=0

--- ACCEPTANCE: the writer against the real table ---
  WROTE  CLEAN   (no breach, day 2026-08-10)
  WROTE  BREACH  (trailing floor, day 2026-08-11)

--- read back, all 26 written columns ---
  day=2026-08-11 phase=closed lifetime_settled_cents=0 breached=true breach_kind=trailing_eod_floor
  day=2026-08-10 phase=eval   lifetime_settled_cents=0 breached=false breach_kind=null
  rows=2
  mismatches over the three new columns=0

--- REJECTIONS: the pair the mapping refuses to repair, at the store ---
  REFUSED  breached true with a null kind      ->  rule_states_breach_flag_matches_kind
  REFUSED  breached false with a kind          ->  rule_states_breach_flag_matches_kind
  REFUSED  a kind outside the vocabulary       ->  rule_states_breach_kind_is_a_breach_kind
  REFUSED  a lifetime total with no settlements->  rule_states_no_settlements_no_lifetime_total
  REFUSED  a negative lifetime total           ->  rule_states_lifetime_settled_cents_check
```

**THE ACCEPTANCE DIRECTION IS WATCHED AND NOT ONLY THE REFUSALS.** A probe that
only attempts forbidden things passes against a constraint that rejects
everything, and the breached fold is the case that would have been missed: it is
the only branch on which the engine sets a non-null kind at all.

**`rule_states_lifetime_settled_cents_check` IS POSTGRESQL'S OWN NAME**, not
`0065`'s. That migration names its three table-level constraints and leaves the
inline column `CHECK` to be named by the server. It is recorded here because a
later supersession by `DROP` and re-`ADD` has to know the name it is dropping,
and nothing in the corpus writes it down. Reported, not changed: `0065` is
merged and merged migrations are not edited.

### 5. The state hash: the question is now ANSWERABLE, and it is NOT ruled here

The dispatch asked for this and the answer is yes.

[`hash.ts:515-519`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/hash.ts) gives the reason
`HashedState` is a `Pick` of eighteen fields rather than `RuleState` itself:

> `RuleState` carries three fields that no `rule_states` column holds --
> `lifetimeSettledCents`, `breached`, `breachKind` -- so a `RuleStateRow` read
> back out of storage can never BE a `RuleState`, and the replay comparison
> would have to fabricate them to hash the stored side.

**THAT SENTENCE IS FALSE ON THIS BRANCH, AND IT WAS PROVED WITH THE COMPILER
RATHER THAN REASONED.** A one-line probe, `(row: RuleStateRow): RuleState =>
row`, typechecks. `Cents` is `bigint` unbranded, so nothing else stood between
the two types.

What that changes is the OBSTRUCTION and not the ruling. `ADR-207` section 5
already argues that on the merits the three belong in the hash; what it could
not do inside its fence was move `HASHED_COLUMNS`. **The obstruction was never
only the fence: before this landing a twentieth hashed column had nothing to
render on the stored side**, because `subjectOf(storedRow)` in
`apps/worker/src/batch/replay.ts` would have been reading fields the row did not
carry. That is the failure hash.ts's own next paragraph names -- *"the stored
side renders a value no row ever held"* -- and it is now gone. **So the decision
is one edit to `HASHED_COLUMNS` and `HashedState` plus an `ADR-026` C-07
amendment, rather than a writer slice first.**

**`HASHED_COLUMNS` WAS NOT TOUCHED AND THE QUESTION IS NOT ANSWERED HERE.** It
is `packages/rules-engine`'s and it is a founder ruling. Two things belong in
the founder's read of it:

1. **The nineteen still stand today and every row this writer produces is hashed
   over eighteen state fields that exclude the three.** If the ruling later goes
   the other way, every row already written diverges on replay.
2. **It is still free and the thing that made it free is thinner now.** `0065`
   argued the cost is zero while the table is empty. It is still empty in
   production, but only because no adapter implements `BatchPorts` over Postgres
   (`scripts/demo/world.ts` item 1). That is now the ONLY thing between this
   ruling and its first row.
