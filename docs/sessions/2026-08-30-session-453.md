### 2. The thing the row said to establish first, and it goes the easy way

The fold's declaration described the COUNTERPARTY of somebody else's purchase and carried
`affiliates.identity_id`. The portal's describes THE CALLER'S OWN ROW, read under `scopePredicate` on
the identity the request proved, and carries `code` and `status` instead. **One field of two overlaps
one field of three, and the two shapes answer different questions about different people.**

So the finding the row anticipated -- two declarations drifting apart -- is not what is here.
**Nothing drifted, because they were never a copy of each other.** Exactly one was this row's
subject. The other is untouched except for a docblock recording the collision, so the next session to
reach for the obvious merge reads why it is refused: a single name over a self-read and a
counterparty projection means a field added for one surface arrives on the other, and on this pair
that is a field describing the affiliate arriving on the shape a BUYER holds.

### 3. What was built

**3.1 The registry answers where a `pair` row's counterparty comes from.** `PairWriter`'s `party` arm
gains a REQUIRED `counterparty`, two arms with a reason each. ADR-230 wrote its one answer as prose
-- *"the identity it names is one the caller already held"* -- and that sentence is true of
`identity_links` and false of `attributions`, which is where ADR-238 ruling 2 found it. The identity
column is NOT restated on the rule: `via` names a table the registry already classifies, so the stamp
reads `SCOPE_RULES[via].column` and there is one statement of it.

**3.2 The insert takes neither identity.** `resolvedCounterparty` refuses the counterparty column in
both spellings, requires the non-identity column it resolves from, reads the `via` row on the
caller's own transaction, throws with nothing written when it matches no row, and stamps both
columns. **ADR-230 made the writer column unsuppliable; this makes the counterparty unsuppliable on
the same table by the same construction.**

**3.3 Two named doors, and the bit.** `attributionAffiliate` and `attributionClick` on `ScopedTx`. A
general "is this owned row mine" verb was refused: it would be an oracle over every `owned` table.
The click door projects three fields and none of the five the registry's own rule calls the trap.

**3.4 `packages/affiliate` holds no affiliate identity at all**, which is asserted by reading the
file rather than argued.
