**Objective.** Give a one-transaction port the `firm`-class read it needs, so
that checkout and payout stop being blocked by the same missing thing.

**Outcome.** **The read is built and neither port is unblocked.** Both were
blocked by the same missing thing and both were blocked by something else as
well, and the something else is different on each and is named on neither.

### 2. What was ruled

**A `firm`-class read on `ScopedTx`, over a closed list of five, at three verbs
and no fourth**, guarded at run time as well as by type.

**The objection is ABSENT rather than outweighed.** A `firm` row belongs to
nobody, so there is no party to disclose and no tenancy to read around. What the
narrowness is bought for instead is that **not every `firm` table is catalogue**
and that **a read is not a write**.

**An explicit list rather than a registry derivation**, which is the opposite of
[ADR-230](../decisions/ADR-230.md)'s choice made on ADR-230's own reasoning: that
entry derived because the ruling was already in the registry, and here the
registry holds nothing to derive from, so a derivation would yield the class.

**The cross-identity read is refused**, and the refusal registers a contradiction
in ADR-230 rather than resolving it.
