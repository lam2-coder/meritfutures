### 4. The three things worth carrying forward

**FIRST, THE SUITE FOUND A HOLE IN THE CHECK AND THE ALTERNATIVE WAS TO REWORD MY OWN HEADING.** Section 39's heading names `0080` in a cross-reference while recording `0081`. The first reader counted that as a landing record and reported the tree clean with `0080` unrecorded. Renaming the section would have made the case green and left the reader admitting any heading that mentions a number. **The reader moved instead**, to claiming only the numbers before its verb, which is how all twenty-eight landing headings in the file are written.

**SECOND, TWENTY GAPS IS A DIFFERENT ROW AND THE REGISTER IS HOW IT STAYS ONE.** Sixteen of the twenty are money path and seven are absent from the manifest entirely. Writing them here would have been twenty records nobody measured, which is the one thing a landing section may not be, and it is exactly the imprecision section 5.3 of [ADR-334](../decisions/ADR-334.md) just caught two ADRs in on a figure neither was wrong to state.

**THIRD, AN ABSOLUTE OBJECT COUNT IS A FACT ABOUT A BASE AND NOT ABOUT A SCHEMA.** Two entries written a day apart both report 412 indexes; this tree reports 414; the difference is `0078`, which was a reserved-unwritten row on both their bases and is on disk here. **The DELTA each recorded is right to the unit.** A reader comparing absolute counts across sibling branches will meet this again, so it is written into both sections rather than only into the ADR.
