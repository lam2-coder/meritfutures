### 2. `GS-120` is the slice, and it is written so the wrong answers fail it

`GS-120`: *"Fifty innocent D-01 clusters do not outrank one identity with three independent detector families implicated."* `AS-M7-03` clause 3 is the mechanism: *"The queue sorts by the number of **independent** detector families implicated on an identity, not by raw flag count ... poisoning one detector does not move an identity up the queue."*

**The fixture gives the manufactured noise BOTH of the wrong keys.** Fifty flags against three, and severity 3 against severity 2. So a queue ordered by count puts the noise first, and so does a queue ordered by severity, and both fail. A fixture where the corroborated identity also carried the higher severity would have passed under three different implementations and proved nothing about which one was running.

**Independence is the INPUT and not the detector, and that is where the control actually lives.** `M07` section 3.2 gives each detector an Input, and `detectors/canary.ts:122` already read that column as the family boundary: *"the four input families M07 section 3.2 gives the detector set: `fills`, `daily_marks`, `payout_transfers` and the identity graph"*. D-01, D-04 and D-05 all read `fills` and write three DIFFERENT `flag_type`s (`copy_cluster`, `news_window`, `martingale`, at `detectors/fills.ts:505`, `:810` and `:1059`). **So one poisoned `fills` stream is three detectors and three flag types, and a queue keyed on either scores it at depth 3.** That is `AS-M7-03` succeeding against the control written to stop it, it reads entirely plausible in a diff, and it is seeded and watched failing.

**No detector contributes more than one family, ever.** A detector reading two inputs takes the first its Input cell names, because crediting it with both lets one poisoned stream buy two families through one detector.

**An unknown detector is its OWN family and not NO family.** Fifty flags from an unclassified detector is still depth 1, so the property holds for a detector nobody has met; and a new detector agreeing with two known ones still reads depth 3, so it is not invisible on the day it lands. Both directions are asserted.

**Depth counts `open` and `investigating` only, and the band is borrowed rather than minted.** It is the set `G-HOLD-REQUIRED` reads under [ADR-040](../decisions/ADR-040.md) and the set `G-EXPIRY-OR-RETRIGGER` reads. **A dismissed flag is a detector an operator already judged wrong**, and counting it would let an adversary manufacture a hundred clusters, have them dismissed one by one, and bank every dismissal as permanent corroboration.

**Corroboration is computed over the identity's whole set and never over the filtered page.** An operator narrowing to `?severity=5` would otherwise get a queue ranked by a different key than the one they narrowed, and the corroboration a filter hides is exactly what decides whether the remaining flag matters.

### 3. THE FINDING, AND IT NEEDS A RULING: two approved documents state two different sorts for one queue

**This is the section for the founder.**

| Source | What it says about `GET /admin/flags` |
|---|---|
| [API_CONTRACT](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) section 8 | *"Sorted by severity then age. Filterable by type, status, severity."* |
| `0008_risk.sql:172` | `risk_flags_queue_idx` on `(status, severity DESC, first_detected_on)`, commented *"The queue's read path: worst first, oldest first within a severity"* |
| [`admin-reads.ts`](../../apps/api/src/routes/admin-reads.ts) `assertFlagOrder` | THROWS when severity is not monotonically non-increasing across the page |
| [M07](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md) `AS-M7-03` clause 3 | *"The queue sorts by the number of independent detector families implicated on an identity"* |

**They are different sorts and only one of them survives the attack.** A corroboration page contains a severity 3 flag above a severity 5 flag whenever the 3 is corroborated and the 5 is not, so `assertFlagOrder` refuses this adapter **by construction** rather than on some pathological input.

**What this slice did about it.** It built `AS-M7-03`'s ordering, because that is the half that is a control rather than a convention and because the plan row names it as the whole point of the slice. It did NOT edit `admin-reads.ts`: that file is `P7-b`'s, P7 section 9 rows `P7-i` as implementing the port it declares and not touching it, and moving a frozen contract needs an ADR that this session has no number for. And it made the collision MECHANICAL: `test/admin-source-flags.test.ts` reads both sentences out of their own documents, finds the refusal still at its source, and asserts that the `GS-120` page inverts severity.

**Nothing 500s today.** `setAdminReadSource` is in `wiring.test.ts`'s `BLOCKED` list and stays there, because this directory implements two of the port's six methods and no complete `AdminReadSource` exists to install. **The collision is a blocker ON the wiring rather than a defect IN a deployment**, and the wiring slice will now meet it as a red test rather than as a 500 in front of an operator.

**A reconciliation exists and this session does not take it.** The two sentences are compatible if the QUEUE is read as a queue of identities ordered by corroboration and the CONTRACT's sort is read as the order WITHIN one identity, which is exactly this adapter's second and third keys. Whether that is what API_CONTRACT means, or whether `AS-M7-03` should be narrowed, or whether `assertFlagOrder` should read a depth it currently has no field for, is a founder ruling and an ADR. **[CORRECTED BY [ADR-178](../decisions/ADR-178.md) SECTION 1 FINDING 7, 2026-08-28, and left standing beside the correction rather than overwritten.]** That reconciliation is NOT what the adapter builds. `compareRanked` compares `corroboration.depth` and never `identityId`, so two identities at the SAME depth interleave by severity: the band is a DEPTH, not an identity. `ADR-178` ruled the collision, `AS-M7-03` clause 3 keeps the first key unamended, `API_CONTRACT` section 8 is amended in place to scope its "severity then age" to the order WITHIN one corroboration band, and `FlagListItem` gained `corroboration_depth` because `assertFlagOrder` cannot check a key it cannot see.

### 4. What the read shape turned out to be, against the sentence that said there was none

`admin-reads.ts:667` stopped at the port in writing: *"WHAT IS MISSING IS NOT AN AUTHORITY, IT IS A SHAPE ... None of the seven bodies above is a projection of one table ... `IdentityGraph` is a walk. There is no join and no aggregate to reach for, so a live adapter written today would have to go through `sqlExecutor`."*

**For these two methods that is measured FALSE, and `P7-g` reached the same measurement one deployable over.** The accessor withholds SQL rather than answers.

| What it looked like | What it is |
|---|---|
| A join from `risk_flags` to `detector_runs` | `rowAt('detectorRuns', {id})` per distinct run, and a `Map` |
| A group-by identity | `rowsWhere('riskFlags', {identityId})` per identity, and a `Map` |
| A graph walk | Two `rowsWhere('identityLinks', ...)` per identity and a breadth-first search over the rows that come back |

**The disjunction is two reads and that is the schema's shape rather than the accessor's limit.** `identity_links_canonical_order` CHECKs `identity_a < identity_b`, so an edge is stored once and which column a person lands in is decided by uuid ordering. "Every edge touching X" is therefore an OR, a filter is a conjunction of equalities, and the honest spelling is two calls unioned in memory rather than a reach for `sqlExecutor`.

**The one thing `ADR-157` genuinely refuses is genuinely refused.** `withdrawable_cents` lives on `rule_states`, one row per account per trading day, and the current figure is the row with the greatest `trading_day`: a per-group maximum, which is a scalar aggregate. **It is done in memory**, at the cost of reading every stored day of every account in the cluster to answer one day each, and `IdentityGraphCost.ruleStatesRead` reports the ratio rather than leaving it to be found later. No clause of that ruling is bent and no `SqlExecutorReason` member is added.

### 5. Three rulings the graph takes, each argued from a primary source

**A suppressed edge is SHOWN and NOT TRAVERSED.** `SD-M7-04` and `INV-M7-09` put the dispute on the graph so *"an admin sees it BEFORE acting rather than after"*, so it cannot be hidden. `schema.ts` says a suppressed edge *"stays visible as history and STOPS CONTRIBUTING TO ENFORCEMENT"*, and cluster membership IS that contribution, because the aggregate this response returns is what an operator reads before restricting a set of people. The two together make the graph **closed**: the walk crosses live edges only, and the edge list is every edge whose BOTH endpoints the walk already reached. An identity reachable only across a suppressed edge is not a member, which is what suppression means.

**The node cap REFUSES rather than truncates.** A truncated graph carries an `aggregate` that is wrong and looks right, and `AS-M7-04` is the scenario where an operator restricts a family on exactly that screen. **No corpus number rules the bound**, which is reported: it is a parameter with a stated default and the default moves the day a number is ruled.

**Two money figures that look like one sum are computed separately.** `nodes[].total_withdrawable_cents` is every account the identity holds; `aggregate.open_liability_cents` is FUNDED accounts only, because `0009_ledger.sql:168` defines open liability as *"the sum of withdrawable across funded accounts"* and `P-M6-01` is that sentence. They coincide whenever an evaluation account carries zero withdrawable, which is most of the time, so summing the nodes to get the aggregate is correct-looking and wrong. The suite asserts they DIFFER on a fixture where an eval account carries a balance. `payouts_lifetime_cents` is `trader_cents` on SETTLED requests: `firm_cents` is revenue that never left the building, and an approved request that failed on the rail moved nothing.

### 7. The composition file, built so a merge cannot empty it

P7 section 5.5 and section 9 name the hazard from the incident: a keep-both merge of `apps/worker/src/index.ts` **silently deleted both sides of a hunk and typechecked**, because a type checker cannot see an export that is simply gone.

**So this composition file is not a re-export list.** `IMPLEMENTED_ADMIN_READS` names the methods as DATA and the composed object's return type is `Pick<AdminReadSource, ...>` over it:

- drop a method from the object and the `Pick` has a member the literal does not, which is a missing-property error naming the method;
- drop a name from the array and the literal has a member the `Pick` does not, which is an excess-property error naming the method.

**Neither half can be lost quietly and neither can be added alone.** Both directions are seeded and both are caught by `tsc`. The suite closes the run-time half by reading `AdminReadSource`'s declaration out of `admin-reads.ts` and asserting that the composed object's own keys are exactly the list.

**`P7-j` adds three lines**, all appends to sorted lists: `& EvidenceTx` on the handle, the method name in the array, the arm in the object. Nothing else moves.
