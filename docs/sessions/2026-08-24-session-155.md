### A renumber's scope is every document that CITES the entry, and this session's dispatcher got that wrong

**[Session 154](2026-08-24-session-154.md) and [session 152](2026-08-24-session-152.md) were both dispatched to *"take the lowest free number"* without either reserving it first.** Both wrote `docs/decisions/ADR-088.md`. The resolution moved session 154's entry to `ADR-089` and rewrote every self-reference **inside those two files** -- and stopped there.

**[INFRA](../architecture/INFRA.md) cites the ruling in five places and none of them moved.** So `main` carried, for one commit, a section headed *"The six services ([ADR-088](../decisions/ADR-088.md))"* pointing at the entry that rules the ADR registry is generated. **The session found it and opened `#197` against its own merged work.**

> **The renumber script's scope was the two files it MOVED. The correct scope is those plus every file that CITES them.** A grep for the old identifier over the whole tree is one command and it was not run, which is the same class as every other unchecked claim this corpus keeps recording.

### The clause that drifted was in the entry about drift

**[ADR-088](../decisions/ADR-088.md)'s approval clause counts**: *"reproduces all 85 rows"* and *"adds exactly two rows"*. By the time the signature was given, `ADR-089` had landed and four sessions had been logged, so the tree read **88 and 190**.

**Signing it as written would have signed something false**, which is the standard [session 149](2026-08-23-session-149.md) set when it refused [ADR-078](../decisions/ADR-078.md)'s *"exactly the fourteen names"*. The clause is anchored to its own commit and the standing assertion is the **property**: one row per ADR file, one row per `##` section, no loss in either direction. Measured on the merged tree: **88 to 88, and 190 to 190.**

> **This is the third approval clause in two days to name a count that then drifted, and this one is inside the entry whose subject is registries going stale.** [ADR-074](../decisions/ADR-074.md) section 3 ruled years ago that a count of identifiers is a fact about a moment rather than a transcription target. **An approval clause is where that ruling keeps being forgotten**, because a number reads like a stronger promise than a property does.
