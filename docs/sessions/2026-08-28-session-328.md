### 1. The dependency was checked rather than trusted, and it had not landed

`P7` section 8's `P7-m` row names one dependency: **"`P5-b` for `events`, because a lifecycle
dispatch reads the event it is dispatching."** `P5-b` has landed. It **refused `events`**.

[`sessions/README`](README.md)'s struck row for session 274 says it in the register's own words:
*"`events` IS REFUSED for the reason already standing in `scope.ts` since session 195, one identity
column beside one ACCOUNT column with neither required and no CHECK tying them, which needs a sixth
scope class and therefore an ADR this session was forbidden to take."*

And [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts) still carries the argument, updated by `P5-b` itself:
*"all five members of the vocabulary were tried against the shape and every one is either refused by
a mechanical assertion or silently lossy ... WHAT THE TABLE NEEDS IS A SIXTH CLASS."* `owned` on
`identity_id` compiles and drops every account-level row; `derived` through `account_id` is refused by
`ADR-101` twice; `pair` needs a second IDENTITY column and this row's second column is an ACCOUNT;
`firm` is refused by the suite's own assertion; `root` is `identities`' alone.

**So the read this slice was told it would have does not exist, and the reach-around is one line that
`P7` section 11 rule 10 forecloses.**

**The slice is buildable anyway and the reason is worth stating, because it is what the row's own
sentence implies.** A contract is a declaration about an **event name** and a **field name**. Both are
known without opening a row. `integration_contracts` is the artifact, the projection is what enforces
it, and neither needs a transaction. So the module takes the event as a value, holds no port, and
**imports nothing at all**, which `10.1` asserts as a specifier scan rather than a substring scan for
the reason [`db.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/db.test.ts) already wrote down.

**What is genuinely owed and is not built here is the dispatcher** `M10:36` describes, the half that
reads `events` and writes `integration_dispatches`. It is owed by the session that gets `events` a
scope class, and that session needs an ADR number this one had none of and took none.

### 3. The field lists are not this session's, and `0018` had already made room for that

`M10:31` says the module carries *"the minimum payload each needs"*. **The corpus never says what
that is, for any of the twelve.** `EVENTS` section 11 gives a message and a guard per row. The
payloads in `EVENTS` sections 4 through 8 are what the **event** carries, which is precisely the
superset a contract exists to cut down.

Deriving twelve allowlists from twelve payloads would be this session deciding what Merit discloses
to a third party, by inference, in a file. That is `P7-k`'s disposition of `OQ-M6-02` and session
322's of `floor_ms`, and both were right.

**So all twelve ship `state: 'undeclared'`, an empty allowlist and `enabled: false`, each carrying the
citation that says an allowlist is owed and the quote that says the corpus does not supply it.**

**`0018` had already anticipated exactly that row and the CHECK is the evidence:**

```sql
CONSTRAINT integration_contracts_enabled_has_fields CHECK (
  enabled = false OR array_length(field_allowlist, 1) >= 1
)
```

A **disabled** row with an **empty** allowlist is legal. An **enabled** one is not. The schema can
hold *"declared as owed, not yet declared"* and cannot hold *"enabled and sending nothing"*, which is
the exact pair this slice needs, and `3.2` applies the constraint's own predicate to every shipped row.

**No row carries `approved_by`.** `0018`: *"A contract is APPROVED, by a person, on a date. An enabled
contract with no approver is a disclosure nobody authorised."* No person approved these, and a
placeholder would put this session's name in the column whose whole job is to say who authorised a
disclosure.

**An undeclared contract is not a gap in the control, it is the control at rest.** `M10:184`:
*"Contract allowlists are additive-by-approval; a new field is absent from every contract by
default."* Nothing reaches the vendor today, which is the correct behaviour for a vendor nobody has
chosen and a field list nobody has approved.

### 4. The second layer, which is the half that survives the first being filled in

An empty allowlist is a control a later session retires by declaring one. `FORBIDDEN_FIELDS` is the
layer that does not retire: **a field the corpus forbids by name cannot be dispatched even if somebody
adds it to an allowlist.**

It has one member and the member is sourced. `EVENTS:407`, on `payout.held`: *"The fact, the clause
and the date, **never the evidence and never the detector**."* `hold_flag_id` in that event's payload
(`EVENTS:250`) is the flag a detector raised and the evidence pointer at once, and `6.1` asserts both
halves out of the document rather than restating them.

**It REFUSES rather than filters, and that is the design decision in this section.** A filter would
leave a contradictory contract sitting in the table looking approved. The contradiction is between an
approver and a frozen document, and only a person resolves that.

**`INV-M10-04` is deliberately not in that set.** `M10:57` forbids *"no document, no biometric, no
PAN, no full device fingerprint, and no raw IP"* and no column in any of the twelve payloads is named
as one of those. A column list inferred from those five words would be this session deciding which
columns are biometrics. **The invariant's own stated enforcement is the allowlist**, `M10:57`: *"The
contract allowlists make it structural rather than a review item"*, which is section 3's emptiness
doing the work it was designed to do.

### 5. The finding: `guard_expression` cannot express the guard this module exists for

[`0018`](../../packages/db/migrations/0018_integrations.sql) declares the column as:

> An optional predicate that must hold before this event is dispatched at all, **evaluated over the
> allowlisted fields only.**

`EVENTS:403` suppresses `breach.detected` *"if the identity is restricted or has an open severity 4+
flag"*. `M10:232` has the dispatcher *"re-read restriction status, open flag severity, and account
state immediately before the send"*.

**None of those three is a field of `breach.detected`.** Its payload is `account_id`, `trading_day`,
`breach_kind`, `low_balance_cents`, `floor_cents`, `shortfall_cents`, `phase_at_breach`,
`auto_liquidated_by_platform`, `platform_trigger_note` and `engine_version`, and it **carries no
`identity_id` at all**. A predicate over the allowlisted fields is structurally unable to see any of
the three terms the guard needs.

So `LoopsGuard` is a discriminated union transcribed from `EVENTS` section 11, every contract ships
`guardExpression: null`, and `7.9` asserts the contradiction from both documents rather than leaving
it in prose. **The column is not wrong and was not widened here.** It is a narrower facility than this
module needs; the finding is reported and a session holding `packages/db/migrations` owns whatever it
costs.

### 6. Send time, as a parameter list

`AS-M10-03`'s ordering is the whole reason `INV-M10-08` exists. The breach fires at 00:20, the
detector flags the ring at 00:40, a human restricts at 09:15. A guard evaluated at enqueue sent the
commiseration **and a reset offer with a discount code** at 00:21, to a ring Merit was in the middle
of detecting, in writing.

`digests/ports.ts` section 1 is the idiom and it is applied unchanged: **the alarm cannot be told "the
run said it worked" because no such field exists on its input.**

- `LoopsLiveState` carries restriction, open-flag severity and the last send, and **carries nothing a
  producer could set to say the guard already passed.** `7.6` asserts the absence by name.
- `dispatchToLoops` takes it as a **required** argument and is the only dispatch entry point, so a
  caller that has not re-read live state has no value to pass. `7.6` asserts the arity and reads the
  declaration as text.
- **The guard runs BEFORE the projection**, so a suppressed message has no body at all. A projected
  body is a body that exists, and a body that exists is one edit from being sent.

`7.2` carries the near miss beside the positive: severity 3 sends and severity 4 suppresses. `7.3`
does the same on the throttle boundary, where six days suppresses and seven days sends, because *"once
per account per week"* is satisfied at a week and not after one.
