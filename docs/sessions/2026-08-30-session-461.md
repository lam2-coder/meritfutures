### 1. The objective, and the answer

**Row `270` asked one question and both halves of it were live: what posts a ledger entry, if not
the request path and not a door `apps/api` may open, and does the worker now running change either
refusal?**

**THE REFUSALS STAND AND THE QUESTION IS ANSWERED ANYWAY.** The full ruling is
[ADR-270](../decisions/ADR-270.md), eight clauses, `proposed` and UNSIGNED.

The row was explicit that a ruling leaving both ports blocked would be complete and valuable, and
that the grounds had to be addressed at source either way. **Both were re-derived rather than
cited, and the answer to the row's central worry is that it named the right change and the wrong
consequence**: something did change when [ADR-241](../decisions/ADR-241.md) made the worker run, and
it was not the authority.

already HAS a `SystemReason`, so nothing is widened to give it one, while a request handler has none
... **WHICH DOOR IS ASKING IS THE WHOLE OF THE DIFFERENCE**"*. This entry does no more than find that
the enumeration is closed and that the third line answers the row.

### 3. Why the deployable running is not what answers it

**[ADR-165](../decisions/ADR-165.md) clause 2 granted `systemDb('nightly-batch')` to `apps/worker` on
2026-08-27, two entries before [ADR-241](../decisions/ADR-241.md) made anything call `main`.** Clause
2 of the ruling would have been true against the barrel that exited zero, because neither refusal's
ground mentions a process at all.

**WHAT [ADR-241](../decisions/ADR-241.md) AND [ADR-260](../decisions/ADR-260.md) CHANGED IS DELIVERY
AND NOT AUTHORITY**, and the distinction is worth keeping rather than collapsing: before them, a
ruling naming the worker would have named a process that could never perform what it was named for.
**That is not an authority defect and it is exactly as fatal to a trader waiting for money.**
