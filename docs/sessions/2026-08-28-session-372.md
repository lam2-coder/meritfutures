### 1. The class, and why it needed an entry at all

`0049` created the table thirty-three migrations after `liability_snapshots` and nothing could read it. That is
session 349's wall on a different table: a `Tx` naming an unregistered name is `TS2322` against `TABLE_KEYS`,
which session 363 seeded and watched from the `readLiability` end.

**What made it a question rather than a formality is that six classes exist and five of them are wrong here in
different ways.** The argument is in ADR-199 section 2 and it runs on the DDL rather than on the name. The one
member with a candidate is `derived`, through the anchor, and it fails three separate ways -- inexpressible
(the edge is composite and `DerivedRule` names one column), lossy (the one-column narrowing multiplies rows
against `treasury_balances`' two-column key), and non-terminating (`treasury_balances` is itself `firm`).

**The registration needed `schema.ts` as well as `scope.ts` and the dispatch said it would not.** `TABLES` is
built from `schema.ts`'s declarations, `events` was already transcribed when `ADR-191` registered it, and this
table was not.

### 2. The census, confirmed, and the inference refused

**58 migrations, 735 distinct column names, none of the three figures among them.** Re-derived with session
363's own parse rather than carried.

**Then the ruling that the dispatch left open:** three separate derivations, none of which needs a column.
The table in ADR-199 sections 4 to 6. The one worth repeating here is `integrations.batch`, because it was
sitting in an approved catalogue the whole time: `batch.completed` carries `duration_ms` in its payload, and
`events` became readable one entry ago.

### 3. What the fence did not contain, and why it moved anyway

**A test written to expire, expired.** Session 363's blocker-1 case said in its own comment that registering
this table would turn it red. It did. Inverting it is the case doing its job, not a gate being weakened, and
the three siblings that stayed green needed their comments repointed because a green case with a false comment
is the worse of the two failures.

**And this session moved lines other files cite.** Eight of them. `RI-15` found four; reading every citation
into the two files found the rest.

### Landmines

- **`scope.ts` and `schema.ts` are heavily cited from `apps/` and the citations are integers.** This session
  moved 35 and 72 lines and eight citations had to follow. Derive them LAST.
- **`admin-source-liability.test.ts` is a suite of absences and its cases are written to expire.** Three of the
  four still stand and their meaning has changed: the absences are now RULINGS holding rather than gaps, so a
  case going red there is a ruling being overturned by DDL.
- **The `integrations.batch` derivation reads inside `payload jsonb`.** A scope rule says which rows reach an
  identity and nothing about what is inside one; whether the accessor can express the read is
  `scoped-db.ts`'s question and `ADR-157`'s, and it is identical under a stored column.
- **`eligible_next_7d`'s fold reads `plan_versions.rules jsonb`, whose shape no CHECK constrains.** ADR-199
  marks this the weakest of the three derivations in its own words.
