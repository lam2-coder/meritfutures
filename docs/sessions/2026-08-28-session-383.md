### 4. The three things found rather than transcribed

**1. `payout_transfers_settled_has_timestamp` is ONE-DIRECTIONAL.** `0010` declares
`CHECK (status <> 'settled' OR settled_at IS NOT NULL)`. It forbids a settled row with no
instant and **permits a `failed` or `retrying` row that carries one**. So a reader
selecting on `settled_at IS NOT NULL` -- which is the natural reading of "settled cents"
and is what `B2`'s own note in `liability.ts` describes -- would count money the rail never
moved and **overstate the numerator of a pager**. Selection is on `status`; the CHECK makes
that safe in the other direction, and the permitted rows are counted and returned rather
than silently skipped.

**2. Ruling 4 forces a thirty-first day to be READ.** Attribution is "the first trading day
on or after the UTC date of settlement", and a settlement attributes to the window's OLDEST
day only when its date is also strictly after the trading day BEFORE it. Without that
second bound every settlement in recorded history attributes to the oldest day the walk
can see. The boundary day is read from the calendar and never assumed; the cost is that the
control needs 31 trading days inside one covered interval rather than 30, which is one
extra day at launch against an attribution rule that would otherwise be unfalsifiable.

**3. Ruling 6's arm is reachable with a NON-ZERO numerator.** Section 7 reasons about "no
payout settled in the trailing 30 trading days" and rules on the VALUE: *"When
`avg_30d_cents` is `0`, `ratio_bp` is `0` and `alarm` is `false`."* Those differ for a
30-day total under five cents, where `floor(total * 7 / 30)` is `0` beside a numerator that
is not. The transcription follows the ruling's words, the outcome (no page over four cents
of payouts) is obviously right, and the case is pinned so a later reader finds it here.
