### 3. What the ruling decided, in the three questions the dispatch put

**1. Which fields.** Four, each traced to the panel or invariant that needs it, under a completeness rule stated as a **projection of one row** rather than as a list. The two columns deliberately not carried are `id` and `computed_at`, and the entry says why each.

**2. Whether the shared name survives.** **It survives.** The case for renaming is real and the entry states it first. It is refused on four grounds, and the fourth is the one that is not obvious: a rename would **delete the console's one stated mapping site**. Kept as the column's name, the adapter's line is a rename a reader stops at; renamed on the wire, it is an identity assignment, and the boundary where the column stops being the panel is then marked nowhere. **The rename's cost was priced on both sides**, including the adapter that does not exist yet, and that adapter now inherits the constraint as landmine 2.

**3. Whether "separately and summed" needs a computed field.** **It does not, and the entry states the rule rather than the preference.** A value is SENT when the server knows something the reader does not, and COMPUTED by the reader when it is a pure function of fields already on the response. Both patterns already sit on this one response (`payout_velocity.alarm` is sent, `breaker_armed` is recomputed), which is why the rule had to be written rather than assumed.
