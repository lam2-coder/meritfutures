### 1. The objective, and the question the row would not let be dodged

**Row `280` dispatched one question: is ONE WIRE NAME CARRYING TWO WIRE TYPES a defect?** It named both traps in advance. Do not rename a live response field to tidy a vocabulary, because a wire name is a contract. And do not rule it fine merely because [ADR-276](../decisions/ADR-276.md) ruled the columns fine, because that entry ruled about STORAGE, said so, and registered this precisely because it could not answer it.

**Both traps held and the answer is not symmetric between them.** The name is fine. The pair is fine. **What was broken is that only one of the two wire sites ever said which type it held, and the site that said nothing is also the one that parses a stranger's string.**

### 3. The clause that is not about one field

**A DECLARATION RULE THAT LIVES AT THE READER'S SEAT MEANS A DIFFERENT SEAT AT THE WIRE THAN AT THE SCHEMA.**

[ADR-276](../decisions/ADR-276.md) clause 4 says a silent name that denotes more than one temporal type must declare which one it holds **at every site, in the design record**. At the schema the design record is where a Merit author reads. **At the wire a consumer never sees a migration and never sees a design record.** The contract is the only seat there is, and one of the two sites was blank in it.

**And the wire's version of the failure is worse than the schema's for a reason that is not the count.** A silent column is a legibility problem for somebody who can open the DDL. **A silent request field is filled in by a caller's guess, and the guess is durable**: it lands in `wallet_spend_limits` and in `admin_actions.after`, both append-only, both revoked from `UPDATE` and `DELETE` by `0026`. A misdeclared column can be documented later. A misparsed input is a record that cannot be corrected.

### 4. The third shape, and why a scan could never have found it

**The row named two admitted shapes. The test found a third.**

```
2026-13-01T00:00:00Z      INVALID
2026-09-01T25:00:00Z      INVALID
2026-09-01T23:99:00Z      INVALID
2026-09-01T00:00:00+99:00 INVALID
2026-09-31T00:00:00Z      2026-10-01T00:00:00.000Z   *** ROLLED ***
2026-02-29T00:00:00Z      2026-03-01T00:00:00.000Z   *** ROLLED ***
2026-02-30T00:00:00Z      2026-03-02T00:00:00.000Z   *** ROLLED ***
```

**The parser is strict about the month, the hour, the minute, the second and the offset, and it rolls the day of the month over.** A reader checking two or three cases concludes it is strict and stops. **`Number.isNaN` was a real check for four fields and no check at all for the fifth**, and the fifth is the one an operator types by hand under incident pressure.

**This is why the guard is two checks rather than one.** The pattern refuses shapes `Date` accepts; the round trip refuses a day `Date` accepts and MOVES. **Neither is redundant and the code says so where a reader is standing.** The round trip is on the date part alone at UTC midnight, because a legitimate offset may put the instant on a different UTC day from the one written, and comparing the parsed instant's UTC date against the string would have refused `2026-09-01T05:00:00+05:00` as a calendar error.

### 5. The judgement that is most likely to be argued with

**A non-zero offset is admitted, and the estate's other two instant patterns do not admit one.** [`economic-calendar.ts:177`](../../apps/api/src/routes/economic-calendar.ts) requires `Z` on [ADR-146](../decisions/ADR-146.md) clause 3's ground, that two spellings of one instant is a normalisation step every consumer writes.

**That ground is about a RESPONSE, and this value is an INPUT.** Nobody consumes it; it is normalised on the way out by a `toISOString()` that renders `Z` and only `Z`, and the suite asserts that a `+05:00` body produces `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z` in both the row and the response. **And the failure that was measured is an ABSENT offset, not a non-zero one.**

**The case for the other answer is not weak and is written into the entry rather than dismissed**: a `Z`-only rule is simpler, makes three patterns agree, and any client that can produce an offset can produce `Z`. **The assertion that would flip is named**, so taking the stricter rule later is a known change rather than a discovery.

**Landmines.**

- **`admin_actions.before` and `after` are opaque JSON on a live response and every projection guard on this surface misses them.** Empty today for this field, and unreported if it fills. **A declaration rule cannot reach a key that appears in no type.**
- **`Date` rolls a day-of-month over.** The class question the entry leaves for a founder read is where else in this estate a `new Date` return value is trusted because it is not `NaN`. That is a different census from [ADR-274](../decisions/ADR-274.md) section 5's.
- **`apps/api` now holds three instant patterns and two calendar round trips**, deliberately not lifted, because the three disagree about what they are asking for and a premature module would hide the disagreement behind a parameter.
