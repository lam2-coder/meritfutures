### 3. `ADR-157` section 5's measurement, taken, and it refutes the expectation

The dispatch's honest possibility was that some of the five cannot be built through the accessor as it stands. **Measured: all five can be READ through it.** `identity_links`, `identity_signals`, `accounts`, `daily_marks` and `fills` are five `rowsWhere` calls and the joins are ordinary code. **What stops four of the five is `OQ-M7-02` and, for `D-14`, a missing table. The join is not the blocker.**

**So this slice does NOT ask for the entry that section 5 left owed**, and the question it wrote down for that entry -- *"a joined read has two tables and the tenancy narrowing has to hold on BOTH of them, or the accessor is a BOLA hole with an extra table in it"* -- stays unanswered. Reaching for it would spend a ruling on a cost that is real, measured, and not currently blocking anything.

**THE COST, EXECUTED** ([`detectors-graph.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/detectors-graph.test.ts) section 9), at 60 marked accounts, a 3-account hedged trio, a 20 day window:

```
rowsByStream.marks     1200        (60 accounts x 20 days)
rowsByStream.accounts    60
rowsByStream.signals     60
rows_scanned           1320
the match needed         60        (3 members x 20 days)
                       ----
                         22        rows read per row that mattered
```

**The ratio is the marked population divided by the group, and it GROWS as the group gets smaller**, which is the opposite of what a reader would guess about a narrower finding.

**Two more costs showed up and no clause of `ADR-157` names either.**

1. **A filter carries ONE narrowing per column, so a `BETWEEN` is half a read.** `RowFilter` is `Partial<Record<AddressableColumn, unknown>>`, so `tradingDay` holds `atLeast(start)` **or** `atMost(end)` and never both. The lower bound crosses the boundary and **the upper bound is applied in memory**. This is the shape of the filter object rather than a widening the entry withheld.
2. **A read must NAME a column, so "the whole table" is spelled as a narrowing that is true.** `NamesAColumn` resolves an empty filter to `never`. Every stream declares a narrowing this module can defend -- `openedOn <= tradingDay`, `suppressed = false`, `firstSeenAt <= now` -- rather than a filter chosen to satisfy the type.

**And one thing the plan expected to be a stop was not one.** `correlation_groups` **IS** registered in [`schema.ts`](../../packages/db/src/schema.ts) and [`scope.ts`](../../packages/db/src/scope.ts), at scope class `firm`, with the refusal argued in its own `why`. `P7-g` needed no registration and made none.

### 5. Four things that need a ruling and are reported rather than repaired

**A. `SD-M7-02`'s clock has no stated duration, so NO detector can write a severity 4 or 5 flag today.** `risk_flags_high_severity_has_sla` reads `severity < 4 OR sla_due_at IS NOT NULL`. `SD-M7-02` asks for *"a stated time-to-first-touch"*. The nearest thing to one is **`OQ-M7-03`, which is an OPEN founder question**, which **proposes** rather than states (*"4 hours to first touch during business hours, 24 hours otherwise"*), which covers **severity 5 only** where the CHECK binds 4 as well, and whose business-hours arm needs a calendar this tree does not have. **This is a fourth blocker on `D-03` that is invisible from its seed row**, and it is half the reason `D-02` caps at 3.

**B. `D-12` has no home in `ports.ts`.** `M07:122`: *"Output is a watched-cluster set, NOT a flag."* `runner.ts` computes `found < expected ? 'degraded' : 'ok'` and `mintBattery` refuses a detector that seeds no canaries, so **a `Detector` must return a finding or a group or be degraded every night forever**, and both of those become rows. `correlation_groups` cannot hold a watched cluster either: `statistic numeric NOT NULL` and `threshold numeric NOT NULL`, and a row there is a group FINDING that `P7-i`'s queue and `P7-j`'s packs read. **Writing one would convert "watched" into "found"**, which is the sentence `M07` wrote that row to forbid. So `D-12` ships as `discoverClusters` and the other three call it. **Its `runs_at: "funding"` is separately unserved**: nothing in this tree emits or consumes an event at funding time, and `CRON_INVENTORY` rows the detector service as nightly.

**C. A canary cannot be sized to the registry parameters it must satisfy.** `Detector.canaries(mint)` is handed a `CanaryMint` and **not** a `DetectorScanRequest`. `D-02`'s battery is therefore a constant at twice the seeded window. **`D-13`'s is coupled in BOTH directions at once**: fewer marks than the window and the window is unfilled, more and the account is not young, so it must carry **exactly** `window_trading_days` and a window stated at anything but 5 makes a healthy detector report `degraded`. Reading the request during `streams()` and stashing it would make the battery depend on the runner calling one method before the other, which `ports.ts` does not promise.

**D. The `flag_type` vocabulary has no GROUP member.** `0008_risk.sql:119` lists ten types in a comment and the column is `text NOT NULL` with no CHECK. `D-02` and `D-13` **are** inverse pairs. `D-03` and `D-14` are group findings and the word does not fit, and minting `group_inverse_exposure` would put a vocabulary into the queue that `P7-i`'s per-type evidence schema does not know. **Both of those decline today, so no row exists to be mislabelled.**

**And a fifth, confirmed rather than raised**: `ports.ts` left `D-14`'s input as `P7-g`'s finding to make. **There is no `positions` key in `scope.ts` and no `CREATE TABLE` in `packages/db/migrations` whose name contains `position`.** A net position is derivable from `fills` as the signed quantity sum, and the derivation is end-of-day only and can never be the *"live"* half `M07` names.

### 6. `D-02` is capped at severity 3, and the cap is the money decision

`M07` section 3.3 makes severity contextual and says moving a detector from 3 to 4 *"changes who gets held"*. `M07:151` gives `D-02` the row *"severity 4: `D-02` below the floor with both accounts funded."* **This module writes 3 and never 4, for two independent reasons either of which would be enough**: *"below the floor"* means `M07:109`'s statistic, which is the correlation **and comparable size**, and half a statistic does not reach that row's condition; and severity 4 needs an `sla_due_at` whose duration nobody has stated. **What `D-02` does instead of thresholding size is report it**: `size_cents_ratio_bp` and `abs_pnl_ratio_bp` on every flag, unthresholded, beside `comparable_size_evaluated: false`.

### 7. The suite found a defect while it was being written

An early `D-02` evidence object reported `accounts_in_window`, and the measurement case read **62 against a population of 60**. The two extra were the canary pair. **`AS-M7-05` note 1 is unqualified** -- a synthetic subject is *"excluded from every aggregate, statistic and published number"* -- and a detector is deliberately BLIND to which of its rows the runner merged in, **so any figure a detector computes over its own input counts canaries and cannot be corrected from inside `scan`.** No `pairs_compared`, no `accounts_in_window`, no `candidate_groups_considered` appears in any evidence object now; the runner's `rows_scanned` and `rowsByStream` are canary free because it takes them before the battery is merged, and they carry the same information.

### 8. No float anywhere, including in a correlation

A Pearson correlation is decided as `(10000*|Sxy|)^2 >= floor^2 * Sxx * Syy`, an inequality between two `bigint`s with **no division and no square root in the decision at all**. The bp figure that reaches the evidence is a separate, truncated quantity and **is never used to decide anything**. A variance ratio needs no root: the `D^2` of a population variance cancels between numerator and denominator. `correlation_groups.statistic` and `.threshold` are written as **exact decimal strings** (`-0.9500`), and a `bigint` reaching a `jsonb` evidence object becomes a `number` only when it is exactly representable and a decimal string otherwise, which is [ADR-157](../decisions/ADR-157.md) section 5 finding 8's lesson applied in the other direction.
