### 1. What was transcription and what was a ruling

**The dispatch's instruction was that a number is taken only for a question the two landed entries genuinely leave
open, and that returning it unspent is a good outcome.** Six of the ten questions this session had were answered by
a clause, and `ADR-200` section 2 tabulates them: the moment, the unit of work, the row's contents, when `is_new`
is true, what makes the identity write idempotent, and what the digest is taken over.

**Four were not, and one of them is a conflict rather than a gap.** `ADR-197`'s own approval line names three in a
sentence -- *"Nothing here says which row a verification selects, how `attempts` is incremented on a wrong code, or
that consumption and session-minting are one unit of work"* -- and the fourth is `ADR-171` clause 1, which says
`apps/api/src/db.ts` declares no third door while this handler needs two.

**Consumption and minting turn out not to be one unit of work, and that is structural.** `otp_challenges` is
`firm`, `sessions` is `derived` at a scoped handle, `users` is read at a door with no `transaction` overload at
all, and `identities` is written at a fourth. Four authorities, four units of work, and no handle in `packages/db`
spans two of them -- which is the property that makes each door narrow and is therefore the property paying the
cost.

### 2. The migration, and the number spent on a limb its row did not name

`0063`'s row asked for an object only if **the identity write** needed one. It does not, and the row said so:
`users_email_key` is the arbiter, reproduced here at the door with one winner, one `IdentityAlreadyEstablished`
and a loser paying **zero** identities and **zero** ledger accounts.

**What this slice measured instead is a control claimed in two merged places and carried by nothing.** The
argument, the counterfactual and the A/B are in `ADR-200` section 5 and in the migration's own header. The part
worth repeating here is the shape rather than the object: **a partial unique index over a table's primary key
reads like a constraint in every review and bounds nothing**, and the sentence above it had been telling
implementers for fifty-six migrations that the handler did not need to hold single use.

### 3. The two doors, and why this is not the diff `ADR-171` refused

That entry pinned the door count in a suite precisely so a fourth door is a decision somebody sees. It is seen:
`db.test.ts` moved in the same diff, and `ADR-200` section 3 is the argument. The short version is that clause 1's
subject is `operator(fn)` over `SystemTx`, its section 5 worry is a door narrowed **at the port** arriving on the
object `databaseAuthBackend` already holds, and both new doors are narrowed **at the door** by types in
`packages/db` that `apps/api` cannot widen.

### 4. What the fence did not contain, and why two things moved anyway

**[`apps/api/src/db.ts`](../../apps/api/src/db.ts).** The fence held `auth-backend.ts`, and a handler composing
four doors cannot reach two of them without the file that declares them. The alternative -- importing
`resolutionDb` and `establishmentDb` into `auth-backend.ts` -- would have broken the one-file control
`db.test.ts` holds rather than moved it.

**Six suites that build an `ApiDb` by hand.** Widening the interface is a compile error in every one, which is the
interface doing its job. They take a shared `NO_PRE_IDENTITY_DOORS` that **rejects**, so no adapter quietly
acquires a door its own suite asserts it does not use.

**And one file in the fence was not opened at all.** `apps/api/src/routes/verify.ts` is `GET /verify/:code`,
`ADR-170`'s public certificate oracle, and its own header says the shared `VerifyResponse` name is a contract
spelling rather than a relationship.

### Next

- **DELIVERY IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT STANDS BETWEEN THIS AND A WORKING SIGN-UP.** `requestOtp` writes the
  `otp_challenges` row, consults `otp_send_budget`, and needs a per-send price for the SMS branch. It is a vendor
  integration and it is the next slice on this path.
- **That slice should read `ADR-200` section 4.1 before it writes copy.** A system that issues codes freely and
  answers only the newest will confuse anybody who requests twice, and the remedy is a resend affordance rather
  than a handler change.
- **`rowAtForUpdate` on `FirmTx`** is one verb in `packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts` and it is measured to work.
  With `0063` landed it is no longer needed for correctness; it would turn a blocked-then-refused loser into an
  immediately refused one.
- **An expression in an `UPDATE`'s `SET`** is what the attempt counter needs to stop undercounting under
  concurrency. Also `packages/db`'s.
- **A phone address in `RESOLUTION_ADDRESS`** is what `sms` login needs, and it is not a one-line change: the
  phone is stored as a hash, so the address would be a digest a caller computes.
- **`users.email_verified_at` is written by nothing** and an email OTP verification is the one event that proves
  the address.

### Blockers

- **None for this objective.** The handler is wired, the key is read from the environment, and a deployment
  without one boots and answers 503 on `POST /auth/verify` alone.

### Landmines

- **`otp_challenges_unconsumed_uq` IS NOT A CONSTRAINT AND ITS COMMENT SAYS IT IS.** `0063` adds the missing
  object and does not drop the index, so the misleading sentence is still in `0002` where it cannot be edited.
  A reader who trusts it will assume single use is free.
- **THE ATTEMPT COUNTER IS A READ-THEN-WRITE.** Two simultaneous wrong codes both write `attempts = n + 1`. The
  cost is a few extra guesses out of five against a six-digit space and never a row, and the fix is not
  expressible from a handler in this tree.
- **`ADR-196`'s CENSUS SPELLINGS MISS THE ONLY WRITER THAT EXISTS.** Any future "nothing writes X" claim over
  this tree has to include the accessor's own insert helper, or it measures a vocabulary rather than a
  capability.
- **`SESSION_LIFETIME_DAYS` IS A NUMBER NOBODY HAS EVER SET.** It is one constant with one caller and the founder
  read is owed on it specifically. The fact underneath it is worth checking independently: the contract has no
  refresh endpoint at all.
- **THE `sms` ARM IS A 503 AND THE EMAIL ARM IS A 401.** That difference is about the channel the caller chose
  and not about whether an address exists, and a later session that "harmonises" them into one answer would be
  making the verify route disclose what `POST /auth/otp` withholds.
