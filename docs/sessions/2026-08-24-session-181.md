### The finding that changed how wide this wave could be

[ADR-092](../decisions/ADR-092.md) section 1 is a sentence [P4](../plans/P4-portal-and-site.md) could not have written, because P4 asked which of three readings was best and the answer was that the question was on the wrong axis:

> **All three readings P4 names differ in how they SLICE the work and not at all in how much of it can RUN AT ONCE. All three permit exactly ONE concurrent transcribing session, because all three treat the pair as a resource to be held.**

The ruling replaces that ceiling with **N, bounded by table-set disjointness rather than by the file**, by defining the two terms P4 left undefined: **the owner is the TABLE and not the module**, and **the queue is the TYPE CHECKER and not a document**. Both halves are load bearing and either alone still serialises.

**So the dispatch is six sessions where P4 section 12 wrote two serial pairs**, and the shape of the change is worth stating plainly: four of the six were not made possible by the ruling at all. They were **already dispatchable and were being waited on**. Section 10 item 6 blocks `P4-e` alone and item 4 blocks `P4-j`; each is a ruling of about a page; neither needed the other five to finish first. **A blocked slice and an unanswered question are different objects, and treating the second like the first is how a plan with ten slices runs two at a time.**
