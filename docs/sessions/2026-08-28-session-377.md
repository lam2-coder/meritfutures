**Objective.** Register `trading_calendar`, which a merged comment still said could not be
registered, on a ruling that had been superseded.

**Outcome.** Registered `firm`, read back live, five stale sentences repaired rather than
the one the dispatch named, and the dispatch's own fence found self-contradictory and ruled
on by its author mid-session.

### 1. The premise, and the one supporting premise that failed

The dispatch's central claim held at source, exactly as written. `schema.ts` said
`trading_calendar` "cannot be registered" because `0032` carries two
`ALTER COLUMN ... DROP NOT NULL` statements and ADR-094's fold refused that shape;
[ADR-103](../decisions/ADR-103.md) clause 2 superseded that clause and only that clause,
and section 4 of the same entry names *"`otp_challenges` and `trading_calendar` become
REGISTRABLE"*.

**What did not hold is the dispatch's precedent.** It reads that ADR-191's registration
"touched `packages/db/src/scope.ts` alone". `5333b45` touched **six** files, `scoped-db.ts`
among them, and the nearer precedent, `0bec092` for `reserve_coverage_snapshots`, touched
**three** and was followed one commit later by `71150bb`, whose whole subject is the
citations that registration moved in `apps/api` and `apps/worker`. **That is the shape this
session turned out to have**, and the correction is what made the fence conflict in
section 5 legible rather than surprising.

### 2. The class, ruled on the predicate

Read from the DDL and not from the neighbour's answer:

| Member | Why it is not available |
|---|---|
| `root` | `identities`' alone |
| `owned`, `pair`, `either` | No column of the row is declared against `identities(id)`, and none against `accounts(id)` either |
| `derived` | **The row declares no foreign key at all.** Read on the whole CREATE body rather than on the column definitions, because a table-level `FOREIGN KEY ... REFERENCES` clause is not a column, and re-read across every migration because `0048` adds one to a NEIGHBOUR out of a file that touches both |
| `firm` | What is left, and what the row is: a day belongs to nobody |

`trading_calendar_loads` is registered `firm` on *"the reading of the DDL and not a
default"*, and this is that standard met independently rather than that answer inherited.
The three tables of the family have three different histories, which the suite now asserts:
the calendar carries two `ALTER COLUMN` relaxations, the revisions table carries two
CONSTRAINT statements, and the loads table carries no `ALTER TABLE` at all.

### 3. The widening, checked rather than accepted

ADR-103 clause 3 closes the `ALTER COLUMN` sub-vocabulary at one shape with a default of
FAIL, so a registration under clause 2 is only as good as the reading of the table's own
history. Every `ALTER TABLE trading_calendar` in the tree:

| Statement | Fold |
|---|---|
| `ALTER COLUMN session_open_at DROP NOT NULL` (`0032`) | Folded, nullability moves |
| `ALTER COLUMN session_close_at DROP NOT NULL` (`0032`) | Folded, nullability moves |
| `DROP CONSTRAINT trading_calendar_session_ordered` (`0032`) | Touches no column, passed over |
| `ADD CONSTRAINT trading_calendar_session_ordered` (`0032`) | Touches no column, passed over |
| `ADD CONSTRAINT trading_calendar_holiday_has_no_session` (`0032`) | Touches no column, passed over |

No `ADD COLUMN` anywhere, so `0004`'s CREATE body IS the column set, and the transcription
declares both session columns nullable where a reader of `0004` alone would write
`.notNull()` on both. **That is the whole of what clause 2 bought, and the suite watches it
on this table specifically.**

### 4. Five stale sentences, and the fifth is a different error

The dispatch named one. There were four of the same kind: the `trading_calendar_loads` and
`trading_calendar_revisions` headers in `schema.ts`, and both satellites' `why` strings in
`scope.ts`, each saying the neighbour cannot be registered.

**The fifth was found while repairing them and is not the same mistake.** Both the
`trading_calendar_revisions` header and its reason said the table *"carries no `ALTER TABLE`
of any shape"*. `0048` carries two: it DROPS `trading_calendar_revisions_trading_day_fkey`
and re-ADDS it `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`, so that a backfill can record the absence
before it adds the day. **The conclusion survives and the reason does not** -- neither
statement touches a column, so the CREATE body is still the column set.

That same fact decides a question the registration re-opens. `trading_day` declares its FK
inline in `0032`, and `trading_calendar` is one of this file's tables now, so the header's
rule would admit a `.references()`. It still carries none, for a NEW reason: the constraint
standing as of the last migration is the ALTER-added one, which this file leaves to the
database, and drizzle-orm cannot state `DEFERRABLE` at all, so the claim would be narrower
than the constraint it named.

### 5. The fence contradicted itself, and its author ruled

Registering a table adds lines to `scope.ts` above every rule, and `RI-15` binds citations
in live reason files to line numbers within a two-line window. So *"`apps/**` is not yours
at all"* and *"end mergeable"* could not both be satisfied: the registration alone left
`RI-15` red with **8** findings and two `apps/api` clearing-condition cases failing.

The session stopped and reported rather than guessing. The dispatch's author ruled: **the
fence was the thing written carelessly**, a stop condition is a property of the tree and a
fence is a guess about which files reaching it needs. Both repairs were authorised, in two
commits, with everything else in `apps/api` still out of scope.

### Landmines

- **A REFUSAL IN A COMMENT OUTLIVES THE RULING THAT SUPERSEDED IT.** Five sentences in two
  files said this table could not be registered, one of them written after ADR-103 landed.
  `schema.ts`'s own header had already recorded the same failure once, about
  `otp_challenges`, and it happened again in the same file.
- **REGISTERING A TABLE MOVES EVERY CITATION BELOW IT.** `RI-15` catches some of them, and
  section 5 lists what it structurally cannot see. A registration is a scope commit plus a
  citation commit, always, on `71150bb`'s precedent.
- **A CLEARING CONDITION FIRES ONCE.** Both of B1's were spent here. A dispatch that plans
  to read a red must check whether the lifting session already consumed it.
- **THE SUITE COUNT MOVES BY MORE THAN THE CASES WRITTEN, AND THAT IS THE CONTROL.** Eight
  cases were written and `packages/db` went 616 to 626, because the per-table drift and
  type-and-nullability comparisons are generated from `TABLE_KEYS`. A registration that
  failed to add its own comparisons would show as a count that did not move.
- **`trading_calendar` HAS NO REVOKE AND THREE TRIGGERS.** `0026` grants `merit_app` all
  four verbs and `0032` revokes only on the two satellites, so what stands between a row
  and a rewrite is CALENDAR-C1, C2 and C3 rather than a grant. Registering the table makes
  it readable and changes none of that.
