### 3. THE RULING, and why it does not narrow ADR-241

**RULED: the drain is OWED. The shape is a ONE-SHOT PULL, invoked per cycle by the external schedule, whose failure signal is the process exit code. `consume` and `start` stay withheld, permanently.**

The argument is [ADR-355](../decisions/ADR-355.md) section 2.1 and it turns on four of ADR-241's own sentences rather than on a preference:

1. *"A LONG-LIVED PROCESS HAS NO EXIT CODE TO FAIL WITH. That is the whole of it."* The objection is to a process that **stays up**. A pull that claims, settles and exits leaves an exit code every cycle.
2. Section 7: *"**The thing that must not happen is the timer.**"* The prohibition is named, and a one-shot pull has no timer.
3. *"A queue is how a job is HANDED OFF, not how it is STARTED on a clock, **and the nightly batch has no producer to hand it off from**."* The dismissal of `@merit/queue` is **qualified on an absent producer**. Provisioning has one, on one transaction. **The qualifier carves provisioning in rather than out.**
4. Section 6: *"Each needs its own entrypoint decision and its own row."* **ADR-241 declined jurisdiction over this job in terms.**

**The test of "not narrowed" is that nothing ADR-241 closed reopens**, and nothing does: no timer, no supervisor, no process that stays up, no status reporting only its last moment, and no job whose completion a dead-man switch cannot see.

**The opposite reading is stated in the entry rather than left to a reader**, because it is coherent: `src/queue.ts` clause 3 refuses `consume` on the ground that *"a consumer is a process that stays up"*, and somebody may read that as refusing consumption altogether. The answer is that `consume` is such a process **because `boss.work()` makes it one**, not because taking a job off a queue requires it, and that is measured rather than asserted.

### 6. What it costs today, and why ADR-039's phrase does not fit

**Zero, and not because of the drain's absence.** The only occurrences of `runProvisioningSaga` outside the two barrels are its definition, a quoted `entryPoint` string in the job register, and a comment. **Not one is a call.** `enqueueProvisioningOp`'s only call site in the workspace is inside the saga itself (`saga.ts:251`). So nothing enqueues and the store cannot grow, which is [ADR-338](../decisions/ADR-338.md)'s measurement re-derived, and **it is a fact about the tree rather than a control: it expires the moment a caller lands.**

**[ADR-039](../decisions/ADR-039.md)'s *"a queue nobody drains is a fail-open with extra steps"* does not describe this.** That queue is fail-open in the strict sense: the breaker degrades, registration continues, and the queue is what lets an unverified identity through. **Provisioning is fail-CLOSED**, which [STATE](../STATE.md)'s surviving-items list states at item 1: *"fail-closed provisioning brings no account online at all."* An undrained provisioning queue admits nobody. **The failure is a trader who paid and cannot trade, not a risk door left open.**

**What transfers is the visibility half**, which is the more general half: *"a degraded mode nobody is watching becomes the normal mode"*, and ADR-039's actual control is that `deferred_registrations` is a **reported figure**. Provisioning has no such figure, and `0007` already ships both indexes that would serve one.

### 9. Landmines

1. **`ST-M2-9` versus the fifteen-minute push is a reading, and it is section 6 of the entry's weakest load-bearing claim.** M02 section 3.1 gives the drain nine words in a table cell. If the founder reads `ST-M2-9` as the job-store drain, the cadence ruling inverts.
2. **The `'nightly-batch'` `SystemReason` word is still strained and is still not repaired**, which is [ADR-107](../decisions/ADR-107.md) section 6's own finding named a second time. Widening `SystemReason` edits `packages/db/src/scoped-db.ts`, which this fence does not hold.
3. **Case 2's regex is deliberately broad** (`.fetch(`, `.complete(`, `.fail(`). It will fire on an unrelated method with one of those three names arriving under `apps/worker/src`. That is the safe direction for an absence claim and it is a false red waiting to happen; the row that meets it should narrow it to the queue handle rather than delete it.

4. **`A..B` IS UNRELIABLE IN A SHALLOW CLONE, AND THIS ROW NEARLY REPORTED THE WRONG MERGE STATE BECAUSE OF IT.** Every row in this wave is told to merge `origin/main` when it moves, which requires being able to tell whether it moved. **This container had no checkout at all**, so the tree arrived as `git clone --depth 1` plus depth-limited fetches, and under those boundaries `git log origin/main..HEAD` reported **1 commit in `main` that HEAD lacked** while `git merge-base origin/main HEAD` printed **nothing at all**: with truncated histories the two refs look disjoint and the range is computed against a grafted boundary rather than a real ancestor. **The reading was wrong in both directions at once** and the first pass through it was misread as an empty listing, which reached the right answer by luck rather than by method. **After `git fetch --depth=600` the relationship resolves**: the merge base is `b2230392`, `origin/main` **is** an ancestor of HEAD, and `git rev-list --count HEAD..origin/main` is **0**, so there was genuinely nothing to merge. **THE RULE FOR THE NEXT ROW IS TO DEEPEN BEFORE CONCLUDING ANYTHING ABOUT ANCESTRY**, and to trust `merge-base --is-ancestor` over a range count: an empty `merge-base` is not a disjoint history, it is a clone that cannot see far enough to answer. **The session-number sweep in section 2 is NOT affected**, because it reads `git ls-tree` at each fetched tip rather than walking ancestry, and a depth-1 tip carries a complete tree.
