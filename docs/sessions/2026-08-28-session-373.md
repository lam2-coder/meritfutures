### The premise, re-derived rather than assumed

Session 368's dispatch was refuted at its source, and this one was not. `pnpm --filter @merit/portal build` before a line changed:

```
/  /_not-found  /accounts  /accounts/[account]  /calendar
/calendar/[accountId]/rules  /calendar/[accountId]/timeline
/certificates  /kyc  /payouts  /purchases  /referrals
```

**Twelve routes, covering `SC-M4-02` through `SC-M4-09`, and neither `SC-M4-10` nor `SC-M4-11`.** Session 368 reached the same count from the other direction and rowed the three missing screens as `SC-M4-01`, `SC-M4-10` and `SC-M4-11`. **After this session the build prints fourteen and the two new ones are dynamic.**

**All four endpoints were confirmed through `CompositionReport.registered` over a real `compose()`** rather than by grep, per protocol section 5. **And then a second time for WIRING, which is a different question and is the one that mattered here:**

### Finding 1, and it is the one worth the session: `SC-M4-10`'s required copy has no row to live on

`M04` section 3.5 requires the wallet screen to state a great deal, and then rules how: **"The wording is a `copy_blocks` entry (INV-M4-08) and a counsel-review item."** The withdraw exit must carry "KYC, the 48 hour destination-cooling window, a $100 minimum, and 2 to 3 business days", and **"No withdrawal fee"** must be stated "rather than merely absent".

**Every one of those is a parameter, and this application may state none of them.** `INV-M4-08` is "no rule text is authored in the portal", and `M20` carries the numbers as policy parameters.

**So the sentences have to be published, and there is nowhere to publish them.** `copy_blocks` is a column on **`plan_versions`**, and `copy-block.ts`'s `PinnedPlanCopy` is keyed on a plan id and a version because that is what the column hangs off. **A WALLET HAS NO PLAN VERSION.** It is per-identity, it outlives every account, and `GET /wallet` returns no copy field of any kind. `grep -r copy_blocks docs/` returns prose and no key vocabulary either.

**The screen therefore renders the absence, by name, rather than authoring the sentence or leaving a blank.** `WalletExitView.published_copy` is a `CopyBlock | null`, and `CopyBlock` is a branded string whose only constructor is `copyBlock()` over a published plan version, **so a literal cannot be assigned to that field and the file would not compile**. The rendered screen carries `data-copy="wallet.balance_framing"`, `data-copy="wallet.spend"` and `data-copy="wallet.withdraw"`, each saying the wording is published with the plan version.

**The populated branch is real and the suite renders it**, through `copyBlock()` over a `PinnedPlanCopy` fixture, which is `payouts/source.ts`'s `readyFrom` argument applied to a sentence. **Reported and not repaired: giving the wallet a copy source is a schema and contract question and this fence holds neither.**

### Finding 2: `POST /sessions/:id/revoke` is registered AND wired, and the portal has no verb to call it with

**The gap on the one action `SC-M4-11` exists for is entirely on the portal side.** `ApiClient` in `http/client.ts` declares `get` and nothing else; `surface.test.ts` fails on a second file in this application growing a `fetch(`; and ADR-083 section 3 with ADR-095 ruling 3 forbid a route handler or a Server Action here. **This application has no write path of any kind.**

**The widening was refused rather than taken.** Giving the portal its first write verb is a transport ruling with a CSRF posture, an unsafe-method cookie policy and an idempotency question attached, **on a path that revokes credentials**. `payouts/view.ts` declined the same widening for `POST /accounts/:id/payout` and rendered its control inert; this is the same call on a narrower endpoint. `ActiveSessionView.revokes_at` is typed as the literal `null`, the control renders disabled, and the screen says so, because the promise an enabled-but-dead control would make here **is the worst one in the product: a trader who believes they have just thrown an attacker out and has not.**

### Finding 3: `AS-M4-05` promises a creation IP that `API_CONTRACT` does not carry

`M04` section 3.1's own note says `AS-M4-05` counter 2 promises the trader "every active session with its **creation IP**, user agent, and last-seen time", and `SD-M4-03` added `created_ip inet` and `last_seen_ip inet` to `sessions` to serve it. **`API_CONTRACT` section 3.1's `SessionRow` carries neither.** Both documents are approved and they disagree; `M04` section 4 binds the portal to the contract ("consumes API_CONTRACT verbatim, and adds no field to any of them"), **so the field is absent from the transcription and the divergence is reported rather than closed by a portal session inventing a column onto a response.**

**And a second of counter 2's three fields carries no information today.** `apps/api/src/auth-backend.ts`'s `listSessions` falls back to `created_at` and says why in its own comment: **"NOTHING STAMPS `last_seen_at` IN THIS TREE"**, because stamping it would be an UPDATE on every authenticated request on the hottest path that deployable has. That is ADR-120's owed write-amplification ruling, and **it lands on this screen**: last-seen is one of the three things a trader recognises a session by, and it is currently the one that is always the creation time. **Reported and not repaired: `auth-backend.ts` is another session's ground this wave.**

### What each screen renders, and the absences that are the design

**`SC-M4-10`.** The balance with **its two components read and never summed** -- `API_CONTRACT` section 6.2 states `balance = withdrawable + held` on the server "because the two components are computed from different inputs and a client that derived one by subtraction would render a stale figure" -- the holds with an `available_at` rendered as an absence because no landed column carries the card networks' dispute window, **both exits** in section 3.5's order, and the full statement with a provenance on credits and none on debits, which is the schema reported honestly rather than a field forgotten.

**`INV-M20-08` is honoured by an absence and the absence is asserted.** The float is the FIRM's aggregate and belongs on `M06`'s liability dashboard with the RCR computed from reserve alone; what renders here is one identity's own balance. **There is no aggregate, total, float or reserve anywhere in the wallet view model**, and the suite asserts each of those words is absent.

**`SC-M4-11`.** Every live session with its establishing factor as a word, elevation as a state, the device family, and a revoke control on the same ROW as the factor -- because `M04:185` says revocation is on the same SCREEN as the factor and a trader recognises and disowns in one movement. **The current session is labelled and never offered for revocation**, because the server would carry out a self-revocation and the trader would sign themselves out of the screen they opened to sign somebody else out.

**There is no password row and no reset link**, because Merit is passwordless in the SCHEMA and not merely in the UI (`0002:280`, ADR-039). **A security screen is exactly where a password row gets added without thought**, so the absence is asserted rather than trusted: the suite fails on `password`, `passphrase`, `forgot`, `reset link` and `type="password"`.

**No factor is ranked, marked weak, or called a fallback**, on `M04:263`'s ground that a UI calling one factor a fallback "is describing a hierarchy the server does not have". **And elevation carries no countdown**, on section 3.7's ground that "a visible countdown is a prompt to hurry and hurrying is the attacker's ally".

### Two defects the suites found while being written, fixed in the source rather than by trimming the assertion

1. **`entry_id` reached the view model and never the markup**, so the contract's "a client must not parse it" was unobservable end to end. The identifiers are now on the element, which also gives a trader disputing a line something to quote.
2. **The inert-control check searched for `action=` and FIRED, on this screen's own `data-ledger-transaction` attribute.** A guard that fires on its own data is one whose next author deletes it, so it was made precise -- no `<form`, no `formaction`, and every rendered button carries `disabled` -- rather than deleted.

**And `eslint` caught a third, in this session's own test.** `no-loss-of-precision` failed on the literal `9007199254740993`, correctly, and in a way that made the test's own point: **a source literal above the safe range is not the value it appears to be.** The assertion now demonstrates the lost digit instead of describing it, and adds the case that matters more -- a SMALL numeric id is refused too, because the guard checks the type rather than the magnitude.

### Fourteen seeded defects, fourteen reds, tree clean after every one

Applied to the committed shape, restored from a copy taken before the seed, `git status --porcelain` empty after each.

`force-dynamic` dropped from `/wallet` and from `/security` (two seeds); `SessionRow` dropped from the barrel; a provenance rendered on a debit; **the balance computed from its two components rather than read, which fires `inv-m4-01.test.ts`, the standing substitute for INV-M4-01's unwritten lint rule**; a password row added to the security screen; **the session guard blinded in BOTH directions**; `is_current` coerced rather than checked; the wallet entries guard blinded; a rule parameter authored into the wallet copy; the revoke control enabled; the current session offered for revocation; and the withdraw exit's elevation requirement dropped.

**The two blinding seeds are the ones worth having**, and both directions were caught: accept-everything fails the refusal cases, refuse-everything fails the acceptance cases. That is the protocol's "a probe that only ever attempts forbidden things passes against a guard that rejects everything", watched rather than assumed. **The `is_current` seed is the one that matters most in the product**: `undefined` is falsy, so a response that dropped the field would coerce to "not the current session" and the screen would offer a trader a revoke control for the very session they were reading it on.
