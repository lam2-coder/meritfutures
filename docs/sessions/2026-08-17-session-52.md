Beyond them, [`golden-loader/src/calendar.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/calendar.ts)'s header still says the fixture calendar "holds five consecutive sessions"; the substance of that paragraph survives, because seven contiguous sessions still make position equal the dense index, but the **number is now stale and the file is outside this session's fence**.

- **Landmines:**

  - **THE DIAGNOSIS WAS RIGHT ABOUT THE CAUSE AND HALF RIGHT ABOUT THE REPAIR, AND THE MISSING HALF WAS MEASURABLE IN ONE RUN.** [Session 51](2026-08-17-session-51.md) and the brief for this one both concluded that five of the six failures were "one calendar two sessions short". **Extending the calendar fixed exactly one of the five.** The session after `2026-11-06` is what `GS-020` needed, because `R-31` asks the slice for the next trading day and the slice could not answer. The session before `2026-11-02` is **necessary and not sufficient** for the other four: `opened_on` is a fixture INPUT, so lengthening the calendar under a file does not move that file's anchor off its own first mark. `advanceDay` refuses on `mark.tradingDay <= input.prior.tradingDay` ([`advance.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/advance.ts) DO-1), and both sides of that comparison read `2026-11-02` no matter how many sessions surround them. **A diagnosis that names the right cause can still name a repair that does not reach it**, and the only thing that told the two apart was running the extension and counting.

  - **CHANGING A FIXTURE INPUT AND CHANGING A FIXTURE EXPECTATION ARE DIFFERENT ACTS, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS CHECKABLE RATHER THAN RHETORICAL.** The brief said the fixtures are correct and forbade editing their expected values. Moving `opened_on` is neither an expectation nor a free choice: **`opened_on` reaches exactly one rule.** It arrives on `DayInput` under [ADR-051](../decisions/ADR-051.md), feeds `R-32`'s elapsed-trading-day count in [`progression.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/progression.ts), and `R-32` runs **only in the eval phase**. A funded fold cannot read it, so a funded fixture cannot compute a different number from a different anchor. The proof offered is the outcome and not that paragraph: **with the anchor moved, `GS-054`, `GS-055` and `GS-061` match every pinned field**, including AS-02's `R-29` cross multiplication turning over at 335,000c and `GS-061`'s pinned NEGATIVE `floor_locked` with 5,000c of margin. An edit that made a fixture agree with the engine by construction could not have reproduced numbers M01 derived independently.

  - **THE FOUR THAT REFUSED WERE VIOLATING A CONTRACT THE LOADER ALREADY STATES IN PROSE, AND EIGHT NEIGHBOURS WERE FOLLOWING IT.** [`run.ts`](../../packages/golden-loader/src/run.ts) says it outright: a funded fixture "is handed an explicit prior anchored at `opened_on` and its first mark must be strictly after it", because `initialState` cannot open an account in `funded` on a plan that has an evaluation phase. **All eight funded fixtures that were already green open the session before their first mark.** The four that failed were the four that did not, and the convention was documented, followed by two thirds of the directory, and enforced by nothing. **A convention with a majority and no gate is a convention that has already been broken and will be again.**

  - **`GS-064` IS A SECOND CAUSE THAT THE FIRST ONE WAS HIDING, AND IT IS A THREE-WAY DISAGREEMENT RATHER THAN A TWO-WAY ONE.** Twelve of its thirteen fields now match, including everything the scenario is named for. The thirteenth is `withdrawable_cents`: the fixture pins **20,000** and the engine produces **0**. The engine states its reading in its own comment, that "R-35 on a closed account is `0n` ... recomputed rather than carried from `prior`". The fixture derives 20,000 from `R-35` on the **pre-breach** balance, `5,120,000 - 5,000,000 - 100,000`, on its own line 44. **Neither number is `R-35` on the closing balance, which would be 40,000** against the pinned `balance_cents: 5,140,000`. So there are three defensible readings of which balance `R-35` measures on the row that closes an account, the corpus picks one, the engine picks another, and **the third is the one a reader would derive from `R-35`'s text and the fixture's own pinned balance**. Not settled here: picking a side by editing either file is exactly the TR-01 failure the stage exists to catch.

  - **A REFUSAL ON MARK ONE MAKES EVERY PINNED FIELD LOOK WRONG AND MAKES THE DIRECTORY LOOK WORSE THAN IT IS.** Thirty-one of the forty diffs this session cleared were not disagreements at all: they were the opening balance and zero, reported field by field, because no state had been written. `GS-055` is the sharpest case. Its whole claim is that five days is the **cheapest** path clearing every gate, and its note says shortening the path by one day should break a **named** gate; a refusal on day one shortened it by five, so the file was proving nothing about the claim it exists to pin while presenting nine confident-looking numeric disagreements. **The count of failing FIELDS is not evidence about the count of failing FACTS**, and one refusal upstream inflates the first without touching the second.

  - **THE CONTROL THE BRIEF DEMANDED WAS RUN FIRST AND IT IS WHY THE ANCHOR WAS FINDABLE.** The eight funded fixtures opening on `2026-11-02` with a first mark on `2026-11-03` all passed before anything was edited. That single fact rules out the plan, the calendar name, the funded start path and `initialState` itself, and leaves the relationship between `opened_on` and mark one as the only variable. **Verifying the control cost one command and was the difference between reading the four fixtures and guessing at them.**
