### What landed

**One case**, `replays 250 funded days: the hash first, and then field by field`, in a new `describe` at the end of [`replay.test.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/replay.test.ts).

**THE ORDER IS ASSERTED AND IT IS NOT A STYLE PREFERENCE**, which the header states at length because the next reader will otherwise think one of the two is redundant and delete it.

| | What it asserts | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The hash** | One comparison of 250 hex digests. A divergence ANYWHERE fails it, **including in a field nobody thought to compare and in a field added to the row after this test was written** | Say WHERE. Thirty-two bytes over nineteen columns localise nothing |
| **2. Field by field** | 43 comparisons a day: the eighteen `RuleState` columns, `account_id`, and column 19 expanded to its twenty-five leaves. A failure prints the trading day, the column, and both values | Catch a field nobody compared. On its own it is a hand-maintained list, and a hand-maintained list goes stale silently |

That is M01 Appendix B.2's own ordering (*"compare `state_hash` first, then diff field by field"*) asserted at test scale rather than only implemented at run time inside `diffStoredAgainstRecomputed`.

**A third thing is asserted that neither of those two can say: that the comparison is not vacuous.** `leafValue` walks a dotted path by hand, so a mistyped or renamed path reads `undefined` on BOTH sides, agrees with itself, and removes a field from the comparison without removing a line from the list. The case asserts that no field reads `undefined` on any of the 250 rows. Seeded (`buffer.pass` -> `buffer.Pass`) it reports `expected [ 'engine_gates.buffer.pass' ] to deeply equal []`, which is `DELTA_MANIFEST` section 13's shape one level up: a check that passes because it checked nothing.

### 250 is the row's number, and the scale is asserted BEFORE the claim

**A determinism assertion does not report what it ran over.** The same four expectations pass at four days, so the case asserts the shape of the life first: 250 stored rows, every one `funded`, the R-31 reset visible on the first, four settlements, no locked floor, `high_water_balance_cents >= balance_cents` on every row, and a peak of exactly 219,000c of profit. A future edit that shortens the stream fails on the count rather than passing quietly under the `GS-071` name.

**The existing fixtures cannot reach this and the reason is stated rather than worked around.** [`fixtures.ts`](../../apps/worker/test/fixtures.ts)'s `CALENDAR` covers **five sessions** (`2026-08-10..2026-08-14`) and this file's `MARKS` is **four days that never leave the eval phase**: day 1 closes 30,000c up against a 300,000c target. Neither is a defect and block 5's chain test needs exactly four days. So the 260-session calendar and the 250-mark stream are built in `replay.test.ts`, which is the fence, rather than in `fixtures.ts`, which would put them in `nightly-batch.test.ts`'s import graph for no caller.

**Every figure in the stream is bounded by a rule rather than chosen**, and the binding ones are worth recording because they are what makes a Core EOD funded life hard to write at all:

| Rule | The bound | What it forced |
|---|---|---|
| **R-15** with `0015`'s `rule_states_high_water_bounds_balance` | A close at 5,260,000c locks the floor permanently and freezes `highWaterBalanceCents` ([`floor.ts:105`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/floor.ts)), after which any higher balance produces a row Postgres rejects | The stream never reaches 260,000c of profit. It peaks at **219,000c**, asserted |
| **R-35** | withdrawable is `balance - size - buffer`, buffer 100,000c, so **the first 100,000c of profit is not payable** | A 110,000c payout needs 210,000c of profit standing, so the first settlement is on day 100 and not on day 10 |
| **R-49** | `payoutsSettledCount >= max_payouts` graduates and `advanceDay` returns at DO-2 | **Four** settlements, not five. A fifth closes the account and every session after it folds into a graduated state |
| **R-09 against R-29** | a win day is `>= 15,000c`; the best day must be `<=` 30 percent of the period's profit | The two pull opposite ways. The cycle carries five win days per ten sessions at 15,000c to 18,000c against a `+20,000c` drift, so **both gates move across the life** instead of sitting at one verdict |

**Each settlement is at or under the withdrawable that was standing the day before, and the case ASSERTS that rather than stating it**, because a transcribed figure goes stale the moment the cycle moves.

### Watched failing, in both directions

**Direction 1: one day's INPUT perturbed, and the hash must reject.** `fillCount` on day 137 set to `0`, which is a coherent perturbation (R-08 stops counting the day as traded) rather than one that breaks INV-18 and gets refused instead of compared:

```
AssertionError: expected [ …(250) ] to deeply equal [ …(250) ]
-   "183a1073209878f048b0c0fb8f6a984f0009d568f8c300b7f00cd34880b569bb",
+   ... 113 digests replaced, from index 137 to the end
```

**The shape of that rejection is the second half of what it proves.** It diverges from day 137 forward and not on day 137 alone, which is the chain propagating through `tradedDaysCount`: a replay reading its prior from storage would have diverged on one row.

**Direction 2: one FIELD perturbed in a way the hash catches, and the field comparison must NAME it.** Day 137's `winDaysCount` moved by one and the row **re-hashed** so it is self-consistent, exactly as a row written by a buggy engine would be:

```
AssertionError: expected [ Array(1) ] to deeply equal []
+   "2026-07-18 win_days_count: stored 19, replay 20",
```

**And the same for a gate leaf**, because `leafValue` is this file's own code and half the comparison runs through it:

```
+   "2026-07-18 engine_gates.cadenceGap.nextEligibleTradingDay: stored null, replay 2026-08-14",
```

Not a generic mismatch, and not the bare parent `engine_gates`. `null` renders as `null` rather than vanishing, which is the case a `JSON.stringify` renderer would have dropped.

### Two corpus findings, RECORDED AND NOT REPAIRED

Both are money-path, both are outside this fence, and both need an ADR rather than a commit. **Neither was known before a funded life was folded**, which is the part worth noticing: `replay.test.ts`'s existing four-day stream never leaves the eval phase and never settles, so no test in the tree had produced either row until this one did.

**1. THE EVAL PASS ROW VIOLATES ITS OWN CHECK CONSTRAINT.** [`progression.ts:339`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/progression.ts) sets `consistencyPeriodStartDay` to `nextTradingDayAfter(calendar, mark.tradingDay)` on the pass day, which is R-47 and AS-12 read correctly: the period starts **strictly after** the anchor and the pass day is excluded ([M01:987](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md)). But the row that day writes carries `trading_day` = the pass day and `consistency_period_start_day` = the day after it, and [`0015_rule_states.sql:193`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql) is

```sql
CONSTRAINT rule_states_consistency_period_started CHECK (
  consistency_period_start_day IS NULL
  OR consistency_period_start_day <= trading_day
),
```

Observed on this session's own row 0: `trading_day=2026-01-01 period_start=2026-01-02`. **Every eval pass in the book would be rejected by Postgres on insert.** The settlement rows are fine and equality is what they get, because R-47's basis day is the previous closed day, so the period starts on the settlement day itself. **The rule is right and the constraint is right about the case it was written for**; what nobody checked is that a period may legitimately start on a day that has not happened yet. `0015` is merged, so only an ADR moves either side.

**2. R-15's PERMANENT LOCK MAKES A RISING FUNDED LIFE UNSTORABLE AFTER THE LOCK DAY.** [`floor.ts:105`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/floor.ts) guards the R-13 trail with `!floorLocked`, so once the lock engages `highWaterBalanceCents` never moves again. [`0015:208`](../../packages/db/migrations/0015_rule_states.sql) requires `high_water_balance_cents >= balance_cents`. So the first day a locked account closes above its lock-day close produces a row the database refuses. On Core EOD the lock trigger is 260,000c of profit, which is **below** the eval target of 300,000c, so this is reachable on any funded account that earns its way past its own lock-day high. [ADR-052](../decisions/ADR-052.md) reasoned about this constraint and used it to refute a `max` on the lock, and it addressed the lock day itself; it did not address the days after. **This is what bounded this session's fixture**: the stream peaks at 219,000c and never locks, which is why the case asserts `floorLocked` is false on all 250 rows and asserts the peak beside it.

### Landmines

- **`storedLife()` is called four times per run and each call folds 250 days.** That is the point rather than waste: `stored` and `replayed` must be independent folds, and the falsification runs are what show the comparison is not comparing an array with itself. Anyone caching it removes the assertion.
- **The 219,000c peak is asserted with `toBe`.** Editing `PNL_CYCLE` or `SETTLEMENT_SCHEDULE` will move it, and the failure is the point: the next number to check is whether the stream now locks.
- **The calendar is twenty sessions a month over thirteen months, enumerated and not computed.** Ten sessions are spare on purpose: R-31, R-47 and R-37 all look FORWARD past the last mark, and ADR-049 makes a lookup outside coverage a typed refusal, so a slice that ended on day 250 would make the fold refuse days it should close.
- **`SETTLEMENT_SCHEDULE` is keyed by index into the life, not by funded day.** It was written the other way first and the withdrawable assertion is what caught it, off by one day.
