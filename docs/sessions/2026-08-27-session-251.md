### 3. What the schema says, read before the contract

`ADR-113` is the precedent: when the contract and the schema disagree, the schema is
the fact. Here they **agree**, and the agreement is worth recording because it is what
makes this transcription rather than a ruling.

| Contract field | Schema |
|---|---|
| `phase: "eval" \| "funded" \| "closed" \| "graduated"` | `CREATE TYPE account_phase AS ENUM ('eval', 'funded', 'closed', 'graduated')`, `0001` line 45. **Member for member** |
| `status: "provisioning_pending" \| "active" \| "breached" \| "expired" \| "closed_admin" \| "closed_chargeback" \| "graduated"` | `CREATE TYPE account_status`, `0001` lines 47 to 50. **Member for member** |
| `platform: "rithmic" \| "tradovate" \| "cqg"` | `accounts.platform text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'rithmic' CHECK (platform IN ('rithmic','tradovate','cqg'))`, `0007` |
| `size_cents`, `opened_on`, `funded_on`, `closed_on`, `close_reason`, `front_end_permissions`, `platform_account_ref` | Columns of `accounts`, `0007`, same names |
| `blocked.payouts_frozen`, `blocked.recon_blocked` | `accounts.payouts_frozen`, `accounts.recon_blocked`, `0007`. **Account level, not identity level**, and `0007` says why: *"an investigation can be about one account or about a person"* |

**The four money fields are NOT on `accounts` and that is the one thing a reader of the
contract alone gets wrong.** `balance_cents`, `floor_cents`, `withdrawable_cents` and
`as_of_trading_day` come from `rule_states` (`0015`), one row per account per trading
day, and the endpoint reads the row for the latest `trading_day`:

- `balance_cents` <- `rule_states.balance_cents` (*"end-of-day balance"*)
- `floor_cents` <- `rule_states.floor_cents` (*"the floor AFTER this day"*, and **never
  `floor_open_cents`**, which is `SD-04`'s separate column for the floor the day was
  JUDGED against)
- `withdrawable_cents` <- `rule_states.withdrawable_cents`, `CHECK (>= 0)`
- `as_of_trading_day` <- that row's `trading_day`, which is exactly the contract's
  *"the last closed day; every number above is as of this date"*
- `floor_distance_cents` <- `balance_cents - floor_cents`, computed, in `bigint`

**All four come off ONE row, deliberately.** Taking the balance from
`daily_marks.closing_balance_cents` and the floor from `rule_states` would be two rows
and two days, and the contract's `as_of_trading_day` sentence promises they are one.

An account with no `rule_states` row yet (`provisioning_pending`, or funded today and
not yet closed) has no as-of day. **It is reported as absent rather than as zero**: a
zero balance beside a zero floor is a readable and false statement about money.

### 5. The scoped door, and the read that does not fit through it

`accounts` is `class: 'owned', column: 'identity_id'`; `ruleStates` is
`class: 'derived', via: 'accounts'`; `dailyMarks` the same. So the whole trader read is
`db.scoped(identityId)` and `ADR-112`'s keyed accessor:

- `tx.rows('accounts')` for the list.
- `tx.rowAt('accounts', { id })` for the detail. **Tenancy is ANDed onto the address**,
  so naming another identity's account writes and reads ZERO rows. That is the BOLA
  property and it is structural rather than checked.
- `tx.rows('ruleStates')` once, grouped by `account_id` in the handler, rather than one
  `rowsWhere` per account. The accessor offers no ORDER BY and no LIMIT, so the latest
  day is a fold over the identity's own rows. **That cost is real and is stated rather
  than hidden**: roughly 250 rows per funded account per year, by `0015`'s own estimate.

`plans` and `planVersions` are `class: 'firm'`, so `plan.code`, `plan.name`,
`plan.version` and `rules_url` come through `db.firm()`. `scope.ts`'s own rule text says
the catalogue is firm because *"EVERY identity is sold the same plan version"*.

**`kyc_required`** is the identity's current `kyc_verifications.state` being anything
other than `'verified'`. `kyc_status` is
`('kyc_required','pending','verified','rejected','expired')` and the word is the enum's
own. No row at all is `kyc_required`, which is the fail-closed direction.

### 8. The BOLA test, in two halves, and what each one proves

The dispatch calls this the one that matters, and `db-recorder.ts` has already written
down the honest limit: a recorder proves *"WHICH DOOR was opened, WHOSE IDENTITY was
handed to it, WHICH TABLE was named, WHAT ADDRESS was written"* and proves **nothing**
about whether the composed predicate reaches one row or many, because that is
`packages/db`'s and *"a case here that claimed it would be agreeing with its own fake"*.

So the case is written in two halves and each says which one it is:

1. **The structural half, which is this package's.** A session holding identity A that
   names identity B's account reaches `db.scoped(A)` and `rowAt('accounts', { id: B })`
   and nothing else. Recorded and asserted: the `firm` door is not opened for it, no
   `sqlExecutor` is reached for, and the address is not widened. Given that, tenancy is
   ANDed by `scopePredicate`, which `packages/db/test/keyed-accessor.test.ts` asserts.
2. **The behavioural half, which agrees with its own fake and says so.** Over a store
   that models the AND, the response is `404 not_found`: **not a `403`, and not B's
   data.** API_CONTRACT section 1 requires the 404 by name, *"so the API does not
   confirm the existence of other people's resources"*, and section 6 repeats it:
   *"`not_found` (including for accounts owned by someone else, deliberately)"*.

A negative control goes with it, on `DELTA_MANIFEST` section 13's lesson: an adapter
that returned `undefined` for every address would pass both halves. Identity A naming
its OWN account returns the account.

#### 10.1 The BOLA property, against a REAL PostgreSQL rather than a fixture

`db-recorder.ts`'s honest limit says a fixture in this package proves the door, the identity, the table and the address, and proves **nothing** about whether the composed predicate reaches one row or many. The container has PostgreSQL 16, so the second half was executed rather than reasoned about: **all 47 migrations applied to an empty database, two identities seeded with one account each, and the process started against it.**
