### The one difference between the copies, ruled rather than averaged

Diffed with comments and blank lines stripped, **before anything moved**. The four copies of `materializedFrom` are byte-identical across all 67 lines of projection; `sliceOf` is byte-identical; the differences are the `export` keyword and the wording of `materializedFrom`'s throw. One difference was substantive: `settlement-fold.ts` wrote `BigInt(x) as Cents` on `toEngineMark`'s six money fields where the other three wrote `BigInt(x)`.

**The uncast form is the stricter one, and it is not close.** `src/types.ts` line 38 declares `export type Cents = bigint`, a plain alias and not a brand, and the arrow's return annotation is `DailyMark`, whose six money fields are already `Cents` (lines 723 to 735). So the uncast form makes the compiler **check** that `bigint` satisfies the field type, while `as Cents` **asserts** it and would keep compiling if the alias ever became a brand. **A cast is weaker than a check**, so the check is what a shared module carries. PT-02 and PT-08, whose harness supplied the cast form, are both watched failing on their own mutants after the change.

### Landmines

- **TEST COUNTS ARE THE WEAK CHECK ON A SHARED-HELPER REFACTOR AND MUST NOT BE PRESENTED AS THE EVIDENCE.** A `fast-check` property reports as **one** test no matter how many cases it ran. Had this collapse degraded an adapter -- a projection returning one plan for every input, a `sliceOf` answering a single day whatever the sequence -- every property downstream would still have folded something, still passed, and still counted one. **`60 files, 908 passed` survives exactly the failure this session was at risk of.** `generator-bridge.test.ts` exists for that reason and each of its three families was watched failing on a degraded bridge: `expected 1 to be 3`, `expected 1 to be greater than 1`, `expected false to be true`.

- **TWO DEGRADATIONS PASSED AND THE PASS IS WORTH MORE THAN THE FAILURES.** Forcing the funded drawdown `type`, then forcing both `type` and `drawdown_cents`, both left `materializedFrom`'s three lineup projections distinct, because `withStaticDrawdown` moves `type`, `drawdownCents` **and** `lock` at once. The distinctness assertion catches an adapter that has stopped reading its argument, which is the failure mode it is written for; it does **not** catch every single-field drift, and a reader who assumed otherwise would be trusting it past its reach. Recorded in the file's own commit message and here.

- **THE COLLAPSE ORPHANED SEVENTEEN IMPORTS AND `tsc` SAW NONE OF THEM.** `pnpm typecheck` was clean immediately after the definitions were deleted; `pnpm lint` then reported seventeen `no-unused-vars` errors across the four definer files. `noUnusedLocals` is not on, so **the typecheck stage cannot see a dead import at all** and only CI-01's lint stage can. Worth knowing before a future session reads a green `tsc` as evidence that a deletion was complete.

- **A NEW PEER RATHER THAN PROMOTING `property-harness.ts`, AND ITS OWN HEADER IS THE ARGUMENT.** That file reads "IT IS BUILT FOR TWO CALLERS AND MUST NOT BE GENERALISED FOR MORE", citing Appendix F2's rule of three. Making the PT-04/PT-07 harness the parent of the PT-02/PT-08 harness is the generalisation that sentence forbids, so the adapters went to a third peer both import. `generator-bridge.ts` states the only admission criterion it has: **four sessions independently wrote the same code**, which is the rule of three met by evidence rather than by forecast.
