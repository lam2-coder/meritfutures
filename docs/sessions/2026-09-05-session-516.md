### 4. The two things that were not transcription

**THE FINDING.** [ADR-316](../decisions/ADR-316.md) section 3.3 declares `postLt06(tx, facts): Promise<void>`. Section 3.1 of the same entry puts `walletEntries` in `APPROVAL_TABLES` **because this job appends the debit**, and excludes `ledgerTransactions` deliberately *"so nothing here can write a ledger row by naming a key"*. `wallet_entries.ledger_transaction_id` is `uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES ledger_transactions(id)` at `0011:83`. **Three sentences of one entry, and the id can be neither invented nor read back.** The port returns the id; nothing else of the ruling moves. It is reported as a finding rather than adjusted quietly, which is what the row asked for.

**THE RULING.** A short position is a **HOLD** named `insufficient_position`, evaluated last, never a cancellation. [ADR-316](../decisions/ADR-316.md) section 3.5 recommended a hold and refused to rule it, naming a real counter-argument: a hold that never clears sits inside `G-NO-IN-FLIGHT` forever. **The counter-argument is answered by `0072:211-218`**, which raises `WD-C2` only when the old status is outside `('requested', 'cooling')`. A held row is one of those two, **so the trader's own cancel is still live** and the lockout is under their hand rather than Merit's. The full argument is [ADR-325](../decisions/ADR-325.md) section 5, and its honest cost is in that entry's approval block: nothing in this tree tells a trader why their withdrawal is held.
