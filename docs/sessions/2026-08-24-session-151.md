### The re-derivation, and what each comment should say

**Two of `0015`'s eight constraints are retired and neither name is ever re-added.** The check is mechanical over the whole directory: `rule_states_high_water_bounds_balance` is defined at `0015:208`, dropped at `0037:124` and never re-added; `rule_states_consistency_period_started` is defined at `0015:193`, dropped at `0046:104` and never re-added. **Both replacements carry new names on purpose**, which both migrations state in the same words: reusing the name would leave every existing reference pointing at a constraint whose meaning had silently changed.

| Site | What it said | What the migrations say | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| `:826`, the `R-15` band | `0015`'s `rule_states_high_water_bounds_balance` **rejects** every later row whose balance exceeds the lock-day close | `0037:143` installs `rule_states_high_water_bounds_balance_unlocked`, `floor_locked OR high_water_balance_cents >= balance_cents`. | `:1197`, the life's three properties | every row satisfies `0015`'s `rule_states_high_water_bounds_balance` | The property still holds, on the **binding branch** of `0037`'s replacement, because `floor_locked` is false on all 250 rows | **STALE** |

**THE INVERTED ONE IS NOT A NAME THAT MOVED, IT IS A JUSTIFICATION THAT CHANGED OWNER.** `:826` is the load-bearing reason the 250-day stream is capped at 219,000c of profit, and the reason it gave was that a locked life is *"a life whose rows no database would accept"*. Under `0037` the database accepts it: `floor_locked` is the first disjunct, so the locked row is exactly the row the live constraint **exempts**, and `0037`'s own stated cost is that **post-lock the schema asserts no relation between the two columns at all**. So the cap is still real and is still asserted, but what holds it is this file's own two assertions, `floorLocked` empty and `hwb >= balance` on all 250 rows, and no longer the schema. The comment now says that, and says which branch of the live disjunction the stream keeps itself on.

**THE SECOND ONE MOVED FROM CLEARING BY EQUALITY TO CLEARING STRICTLY, WHICH IS THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION FROM THE ONE "STALE" SUGGESTS.** Under `0015` the fixture sat exactly on the boundary: period start `== trading_day`, admitted by `<=` and by nothing else. Under `0046` the comparison is against the anchor, and the anchor is the BASIS day: [`settle.ts:148`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/settle.ts) sets `payoutAnchorDay: fact.basisTradingDay` and [`settle.ts:105`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/payout/settle.ts) sets the period start to `nextTradingDayAfter(basisTradingDay)`. The fixture's basis is the previous session and its sessions are consecutive, so the row now clears the live constraint **by one session** rather than by equality. **The site got safer and its comment still described the tight case.**

### No assertion is wrong, and that was checked rather than assumed

The brief's standing instruction was to report rather than repair if re-deriving showed an assertion wrong. **Each of the three comments was traced to the assertions it justifies and all of them still hold:**

- `expect(stored.filter((row) => row.floorLocked)).toHaveLength(0)` and `expect(stored.every((row) => row.highWaterBalanceCents >= row.balanceCents)).toBe(true)` are properties of the engine's output, not of the schema, and `0037` does not touch either.
- `expect(peakProfitCents).toBe(219_000n)` and its `toBeLessThan` against `lock.atProfitCents` are the same: the stream's own arithmetic.
- The settlement fact's `basisTradingDay` and `effectiveTradingDay` are unchanged and clear the live constraint strictly, as above.

**What changed is the reason, not the result.** That is why this was a comment repair and not a finding.
