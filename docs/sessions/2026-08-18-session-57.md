### The result, in the order a reader needs it

**EVERY PREMISE ADR-056 ARGUES FROM IS TRUE, AND ITS MECHANISM IS NOT.**

| The ADR's claim | Executed |
|---|---|
| Every v1 eval pass is also a lock day, so every funded account arrives locked | **TRUE.** The pass day emits `rule.floor_locked`, `phase.passed`, `day.closed`, in that order, on both plans that have an eval phase |
| `floor_lock_floor_at_cents` is `size_cents + 10,000c` and `R-31` writes exactly `size_cents` | **TRUE** on all three v1 plans, resolved from the published rows |
| So a funded account whose floor was re-assigned to the locked value stands 10,000c under water | **TRUE**, and it is the ADR's whole point |
| **`DO-7` "assigns `floor = floor_lock_floor_at_cents` on every day the account is locked"** | **FALSE.** This is the sentence the ruling's table rests on |
| Therefore every funded account breaches on its second funded day, on all three v1 plans | **DOES NOT REPRODUCE** |

**`advanceFloor` ASSIGNS THE LOCKED FLOOR ONCE, ON THE DAY THE LOCK ENGAGES, AND CARRIES IT
AFTERWARDS.** `R-15`'s block is guarded by `!floorLocked` ([`floor.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/day/floor.ts)),
so on an already-locked day the assignment is not reached and `floorCents` is `input.priorFloorCents`
untouched. **The file's own header states the expression form** (`floor(d) = floorLocked ?
floor_lock_floor_at_cents : max(...)`) **and the implementation is stateful**, and the two agree on
every day of every ordinary account, because nothing between the lock day and today ever moves the
floor. **They part company at exactly one place: a state whose floor was moved by something other
than the lock while `floorLocked` stayed true.** That state exists nowhere in the corpus except the
counterfactual this session built.

**AND THE SIGN IS REVERSED, WHICH IS THE MEASUREMENT WORTH READING TWICE.** On the walked stream, with
a funded day two whose low is 4,800,000c:

| | Carried lock (the literal `INV-07`) | Shipped (what the engine does) |
|---|---|---|
| Funded day 1 floor | 4,750,000c, frozen | 4,850,000c, trailed (`R-13`) |
| Funded day 2, low 4,800,000c | **survives**, 4,800,000 >= 4,750,000 | **BREACHES**, 4,800,000 < 4,850,000, shortfall 50,000c |

**The carried lock is STRICTLY MORE PERMISSIVE than the engine it was supposed to be more dangerous
than**, because its floor is frozen at the lowest value the funded phase ever has. The property sweep
puts it beyond one stream: over **3,442 folded funded days across 2,000 generated streams**, with
**1,790 shipped breaches and 1,485 carried breaches**, the carried lock breached on **zero** days the
shipped fold survived. The reason is structural rather than statistical: both accounts leave `R-31`
with the same floor, the carried one is frozen there by the two `!floorLocked` guards, and the
shipped one is non-decreasing from there by `R-13` and `R-14`, so `R-18`'s comparator is at or below
the shipped one on every day.

**WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS UNDER THE CARRIED LOCK IS ADR-056's OTHER ARGUMENT, AND THAT ONE REPRODUCES
EXACTLY.** The funded floor freezes at `size_cents` minus the funded drawdown and never moves again:
it never trails a cent of the profit, and the lock can never re-engage, because `R-15`'s trigger is
guarded by the same flag. **The sweep watched the shipped account re-lock 232 times and the carried
account not once.** The ADR's words for it are still the right ones: "That is not a constrained
funded floor. It is the funded floor deleted." **The ruling's conclusion survives its own table.**

### Landmines

  expression over the day**, and the implementation is a **stateful carry**. Anyone deriving the
  counterfactual from the header gets ADR-056's answer; anyone running it gets this one. **The two
  forms are observationally identical on every account the corpus admits**, so no fixture, no property
  and no gate can tell them apart, and the disagreement is only visible in the one state the corpus
  has not decided is reachable. **This is the shape of defect the constitution's own caution is
  about**: not a capability failure, a failure to check a claim against the primary source, where the
  primary source here is the executable and not the comment above it.
