## 2026-08-18 - Session 57: two accepted rulings that left a row behind (ADR only, docs fence)

**Written as a stub before any work, so the session was on the record before it had a result, and
completed in place.** The fence is `docs/`.

- **Objective:** Rule, in one ADR, the two residues that accepted rulings left behind and that
  neither applying session was in scope to fix. **First**, `R-15`'s row in
  [M01](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) section 3.5 still carries the claim
  [ADR-052](../decisions/ADR-052.md) refuted, while `CV-12`'s own row was corrected. **Second**,
  M01 section 3.6's breach block enumerates **eight** written fields and the engine writes **nine**,
  the ninth being `engineGates` via `gatesAfterBreach`, which `RE-U-025` now pins;
  [ADR-054](../decisions/ADR-054.md)'s applying session recorded this as residue and deliberately
  did not decide it.

- **Done:** **[ADR-057](../decisions/ADR-057.md) is written and applied, `status: accepted`, and it
  amends six rows across three documents.** `057` was claimed in
  [ALLOCATION](../decisions/ALLOCATION.md) before the ADR was written and after all twenty-eight
  remote refs were read, by file and by claim; every one maxed at `056`. **The first repair turned
  out to be four sites rather than one. The second is ruled against the divergence: section 3.6's
  block gains `engineGates` and is nine.** **No engine file, no fixture and no test assertion
  changed**, which is the strongest available evidence that neither ruling moves behaviour. 17 of 17
  gates pass, and the whole diff is 19 inserted lines across `M01`, `GLOSSARY` and `ADR-014`.

- **Next:** **`RE-P-15` is unblocked and is one session in `packages/rules-engine/test`.** It is
  already registered in M01 section 8.2, it is unwritten, and it is now writable against a section
  3.6 that it does not contradict. **Before this ruling, writing it faithfully to the document
  produced a test that fails on `GS-064`'s shape.** Two comment-only staleness repairs follow it and
  are named below.

- **Blockers:** None. **The signature is a read rather than a gate**, and the ADR says which half
  needs it.

- **Landmines:**

  - **THE BRIEF'S FIXTURE PREMISE WAS FALSE AND `GS-024` IS THE FILE.** The objective as received
    states that "no fixture in the directory catches it" and asks that this be verified before
    writing. It does not hold. **`GS-024` pins `floor_cents: 5010000` on a lock day that overshoots
    the trigger by 40,001c**, which is the one value the refuted claim cannot produce, and it went
    red for exactly as long as the defect existed. [ADR-052](../decisions/ADR-052.md)'s own
    consequences and [STATE](../STATE.md) line 916 both already call it "the only fixture in the
    corpus whose pinned floor differs between the two readings". **The instruction to name a fixture
    and not write it therefore had nothing to name for that half**, and the correct statement is
    narrower: `GS-081`, `GS-015`, `GS-016` and `GS-056` all close their lock day at exactly the
    trigger and cannot see it; `GS-017`, `GS-019` and `GS-044` overshoot and end after `R-31` has
    rewritten the floor; `GS-018` overshoots and pins no floor at all.

  - **AND THE HONEST LIMIT OF THAT: NO FIXTURE CAN CATCH PROSE.** `GS-024` catches an engine that
    implements the refuted claim. The engine has been correct since session 55 and the document has
    been wrong since before ADR-052, **which is the exact interval in which a reader repairing the
    code toward the document reintroduces the defect and it looks like diligence.** That is
    [ADR-056](../decisions/ADR-056.md)'s landmine in the opposite direction and it is why the
    documentary repair is worth a session on its own.

  - **`ADR-052`'s AMENDMENT TABLE HAS FOUR ROWS AND ITS APPLYING SESSION APPLIED THREE.** The fourth
    is **`ADR-014`'s decision block**, which still carried the `max` and the sentence ADR-052 names
    in its own table. **That was not in the objective and is the larger half of the finding**: the
    brief named one unapplied site and there were four, one of them explicitly enumerated by the
    ruling that refuted it.

  - **`R-15` WAS LEFT ON PURPOSE, AND THE REASON WAS SOUND AT THE TIME.** Session 55's commit says
    "DELIBERATELY NOT TOUCHED: R-15's row and CV-11 carry the same 'never jumps' phrasing", citing
    ADR-052's own "`R-15` ... unchanged". **That declaration is true of the rule and false of the
    sentence in the same cell.** R-15's Effect column is an effect; what follows it is a rationale,
    and the "unchanged" swept it up. **An amendment table enumerates locations and a claim
    propagates by restatement**, which is the whole lesson and is cheaper to fix as a habit than as
    a gate: a ruling that refutes a sentence should grep for the sentence. That grep found four of
    the five copies.

  - **`CV-11` IS A FALSE LEAD AND IT IS IN THE COMMIT HISTORY, WHICH IS THE WORST PLACE FOR ONE.**
    The same commit message groups `CV-11` with `R-15` as carrying the same phrasing. **It does
    not.** `CV-11` reads "post-lock it **equals** it", which is **TRUE** under the assignment and is
    the premise ADR-052 section 2 turns on: the `max` makes it false, that is what makes `INV-21`
    unenforceable, and that is what decided the ruling. **`CV-11` is the row ADR-052 upheld.** It
    was read at its line rather than trusted from the commit message, and it is named in the ADR's
    not-touched list so the next reader does not repair a correct row.

  - **THE SECOND REPAIR IS DECIDED BY A PROPERTY M01 ALREADY STATES, NOT BY THE TYPE.** The tempting
    argument for the ninth field is that `RuleState` requires it, and the tempting counter is that a
    specification should not carry TypeScript furniture. **Both are beside the point.** `RE-P-15`
    reads "`engineEligible` equals the conjunction of `engineGates`, for every generated state", and
    section 3.6 spreads `{ ...s }`, so an unlisted field **carries**. On any breach after an
    eligible day the carried conjunction is `true` beside `engineEligible: false`. **`GS-064` is
    that day and is already in the directory**, and its own note says every eligibility input "is
    still standing at the value that made the account eligible at the previous close". A field a
    stated property quantifies over is part of the specification by construction.

  - **THE OBVIOUS FORM OF THE AMENDMENT IS WRONG AND IT IS ONE WORD AWAY.** Writing
    `evaluateEngineGates` into the breach block would be `R-24` broken: `R-37` can **refuse** a day
    whose calendar slice does not cover the cadence anchor, and a breach the fold declined to record
    on a caller's window choice is a breach that did not happen. The gates on that row are
    **stated**. `gatesAfterBreach` is a separate function for exactly this reason and the amendment
    names it.

  - **A DECISION WITH NO AUTHORITY WAS SITTING IN `gates.ts` AND THIS RULING IS THE FIRST TO CITE
    IT.** Its comment rules that every `pass` is `false` including the two that can be `skipped`,
    because "a `pass: true, skipped: true` on a breach row would put a satisfied-looking gate on the
    worst row in an account's life". **That is trader-visible and it cited nothing**, which is the
    class [session 56](2026-08-18-session-56.md) named as sitting one level below `CI-06q`'s reach.
    The gate's own stated blind spot has now produced two instances in two days.

  - **FIVE CODE SITES ARE STALE AND ALL FIVE ARE OUTSIDE THE FENCE, SO THEY ARE NAMED AND NOT
    TOUCHED.** [`plan/validate.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/src/plan/validate.ts) and
    [`test/generators/validate-plan.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/generators/validate-plan.ts)
    both quote `CV-12`'s **superseded** "Rejected because" text verbatim. **Those two are
    constitution C10's writer and reviewer, transcribed independently in different sessions, and
    they are stale identically**, which is what a shared stale source looks like from the outside
    and is worth more than either copy alone. Also
    [`plan-validate.test.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/plan-validate.test.ts) line 379,
    [`rules-c-floor.test.ts`](../../packages/rules-engine/test/rules-c-floor.test.ts) lines 163 to
    166, and `GS-015`'s yaml header, which quotes `R-15`'s sentence as a `TR-01` source citation and
    **goes stale the moment this ADR lands**. `RE-U-025`'s comment joins them: it says the ninth
    field is "RESIDUE recorded in STATE.md", and section 2 decides it. **All six are comment-only,
    no assertion moves, and that is the check on the claim that this ruling changes no behaviour.**

  - **`GS-015`'s TITLE READS AS THE GENERAL CLAIM AND ITS BODY DOES NOT.** "Floor lock engages
    continuously with no jump" is the refuted sentence in five words; the registry row's body, "at
    profit 260,000 the trailing floor reaches exactly `size + 10,000`", is true of that stream
    because it lands on the trigger to the cent. **Renaming it is a sweep** across the fixture
    filename, its `.expected.json`, the registry row and STATE, and a fixture id's title is its
    identity. Named rather than done.

  - **`CI-06n` CAUGHT THIS SESSION'S FIRST COMMIT, EXACTLY AS SESSION 56 PREDICTED IT WOULD.** The
    stub was committed before anything else, per the brief, with no row in the sessions README.
    Session 56 recorded the same finding and recorded it as a demonstration that the gate is live;
