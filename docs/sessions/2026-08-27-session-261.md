### 1. THE DISPATCH'S PREMISE IS WRONG, AND THE CITATION IT RESTS ON RESOLVES TO SOMETHING ELSE

The dispatch, and the `149` row in [ALLOCATION](../decisions/ALLOCATION.md) and the `261` row in [README](README.md) that carry the same sentence:

> A certificate is a PUBLIC artifact and `GS-302` is `restricted` (cited to `0001:27`; `ADR-041` refused a fourth value, so the vocabulary is closed at three). **A rendered certificate is the first real test of that classification in this repository.**

**Every citation in it resolves. The conclusion drawn from them does not.**

| Claim | Checked against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `0001:27` declares a closed vocabulary and [ADR-041](../decisions/ADR-041.md) refused a fourth value | [`0001_extensions_and_enums.sql`](../../packages/db/migrations/0001_extensions_and_enums.sql):27 is `CREATE TYPE identity_status AS ENUM ('active', 'restricted', 'closed');` | **TRUE** |
| `GS-302` is `restricted` | [`37-gs-300-to-gs-304`](../testing/golden-scenarios/37-gs-300-to-gs-304-impersonation-and-admin-parity.md):19, *"Impersonation of a **`restricted`** identity"* | **TRUE** |
| A certificate is a public artifact | `certificates` is in `0020_public_surface.sql`, and [M11](../plans/M11-certificates-social-proof.md) section 4 makes `GET /verify/:code` public and unauthenticated | **TRUE** |
| ...therefore a rendered certificate tests **that classification** | **`identity_status` IS AN IDENTITY'S STATUS AND NOT A CLASSIFICATION OF DATA.** `GS-302` rules who may VIEW a restricted trader's screens during impersonation. It says nothing about what a certificate may carry | **FALSE** |

**There is no data-classification vocabulary in this corpus.** `grep -i classification` over [SECURITY](../architecture/SECURITY.md) and the rest of `architecture/` returns nothing of the kind; the only occurrences of `restricted` beside data are `identity_status` and the `on delete restrict` on [`impersonation_sessions`](../architecture/data-model/impersonation_sessions.md). So the sentence in the two registries names a test that cannot be performed, because the thing to be tested does not exist.

**This is the corpus's own recurring error class in a new costume**, and [session 95](2026-08-20-session-95.md) already named it: *"a claim about a table checked against the file that created it rather than against the table as it now stands."* Here the citation was checked and the INFERENCE from it was not. The number resolved, the line said what it was said to say, and the word `restricted` was carried from an enum of identity states into a sentence about data sensitivity by nothing more than the word being the same.

### 2. WHAT A PUBLIC CERTIFICATE MAY CARRY IS SETTLED, IN TWO PLACES THAT AGREE

So the real question was asked at the real source instead. It is answered twice and the two answers are the same answer.

| Source | Text |
|---|---|
| [M11](../plans/M11-certificates-social-proof.md) `INV-M11-01` | *"A certificate's claims are **minimal**: the account's plan, size, trading day, the kind-specific value, and nothing else \| Claim schema per kind, validated at issuance. **No identity, no email, no display name, no cumulative total, no lifetime figure.**"* |
| [M04](../plans/M04-trader-portal.md) `AS-M4-03` rule 3 | *"Claims are minimal by construction: no identity, no email, no cumulative totals. A certificate is a fact about an account on a day, and the smaller the claim, the less there is to forge usefully."* |

[M11](../plans/M11-certificates-social-proof.md)'s governing sentence is the same rule written as a warning aimed at exactly this session: *"a certificate is a small, signed, revocable claim about one account on one day, and **every temptation in this module is a temptation to make it bigger than that**."*

**`ADR-149`'s condition was "if the corpus does not settle what a public certificate may carry". It does. The number is NOT TAKEN**, its row is amended in place under [ADR-065](../decisions/ADR-065.md) T3, and the founder can reverse that on the strength of section 1 alone if the registry sentence is thought worth an entry rather than an amendment.

#### 2.1 The control that ships instead, and why it is an allowlist

`apps/portal/src/app/(purchases)/model.ts`'s `publishedCertificate()` **constructs** its result field by field from `PUBLISHED_CERTIFICATE_FIELDS`. It does not spread, it does not copy and delete, and it does not filter.

**The difference is what happens on the day somebody adds a field to `CertificateView`.** A spread carries the new field onto a public artifact by default and a reviewer has to notice. This refuses to carry it until somebody writes its name in the list. A redaction pass is a denylist wearing an allowlist's clothes, and a denylist is wrong about every field invented after it was written.

#### 2.2 THREE THINGS THAT DO NOT CROSS, EACH FOR A REASON READ OFF A ROW

| What | Why not | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **`certificate_id`** | `SD-M11-01` adds `code` and states the reason in its own row: it *"is distinct from `id` so the public token can be rotated after an incident without rewriting the primary key"*. A screen that prints the id publishes the half the split keeps private, on the one screen whose purpose is copying something out. It is not even a React key here: `verify_url` is unique and already public, so the id never enters the render tree | `model.ts`, asserted in `page-certificates.test.ts` |
| **Any account or identity identifier** | `SD-M4-01` puts `account_id` and `identity_id` on the row; `CertificateResponse` puts neither on the wire. **The screen is therefore not grouped by account**, which is the obvious layout: grouping would put an account id on the page and would make the absence assertion unstateable, because "none inside the card element" depends on which element somebody screenshots | `certificates-screen.ts` |
| **Any total** | Two rules, one absence. `INV-M4-01` bans client-side money arithmetic and `AS-M11-07` bans a certificate becoming an aggregate publisher, because *"a total assembled from cards is a statistic with no method page"* | Both screens |

#### 2.3 The finding the test found, which is the reason the test reads bytes

**The first draft of the refusal path passed `UnverifiableCertificateError.message` straight to the screen.** That message names the `certificate_id`. So the page refused to render the card, said why, and **published the identifier the allowlist exists to withhold, through an error string** rather than through a field.

An assertion written over the model's fields would have passed. The assertion is written over the rendered markup, and it failed. The reason is stated in the test file: a value reaches a page through an `alt`, a `title`, a `data-` prop or an error message without ever appearing in a visible field, so an absence claim has to be made about the bytes.

[M11](../plans/M11-certificates-social-proof.md) section 4's public `GET /certificates/:code/image.png` is the image that re-renders from the live row, and it is served off `code`, which the response does not carry.

Neither file is a route handler and neither carries `'use server'`, so nothing here serves `/api/v1` or an operator path ([ADR-083](../decisions/ADR-083.md) section 3, [ADR-095](../decisions/ADR-095.md) ruling 3).

**`.ts` and not `.tsx`, and it is not a stylistic choice.** [`tsconfig.base.json`](../../tsconfig.base.json) sets no `jsx` option and `apps/portal/tsconfig.json` includes `src/**/*.ts` only, both of which arrive with session 250's `app/layout.tsx`. **A `.tsx` page written here would have been INVISIBLE to `pnpm run typecheck` rather than checked by it**, which is a green gate that read nothing, and this session's own verification list opens with that gate. `ts` is a default `pageExtensions` entry in `next@16.3.2` (`node_modules/next/dist/server/config-shared.js`:107, read out of the installed package), so these are pages Next.js routes and files the existing gate checks. Converting after 250 lands the compiler option is mechanical.

**Mobile first without a stylesheet this session does not own.** The global stylesheet arrives with the root layout, which is 250's. So the layout is carried by the elements: stacked `article` and `dl` blocks reflow at 375px with no CSS at all, and a wide `table` would not. A page that looked right only once somebody else's CSS landed would be a page whose mobile claim nobody could check today.

### 6. `SC-M4-06`: THE OMISSION THAT WOULD READ AS A POSITIVE CLAIM

M04 section 3.1's one thing the purchases screen must get right is *"the rule diff when versions differ (`SD-M4-02`)"*.

**`GET /purchases` states each purchase's `plan.version` and states no pairing.** So the pairing is this screen's: a `reset` row is paired with the nearest **earlier** purchase on the same `plan_id`, where "earlier" is a position in the server's own list order and never a comparison of `created_at`, which would be this file quietly deciding the list is sorted by time.

**And a reset whose partner is not on the page renders as UNPAIRABLE rather than as unchanged.** A cursor list has a second page, so the earlier purchase is legitimately absent, and a panel that rendered nothing there would say *"your terms did not change"* about a comparison it never made. That is [`view/purchases.ts`](../../apps/portal/src/view/purchases.ts)'s own rule about its own walk, pointed one level up: *"An omission that reads as a positive claim is the worst failure available on this screen, because the screen exists to be the record of what the trader agreed to when they repurchased."*

**The purchases screen carries no absence assertion of the certificate kind, and that is the point.** A purchase is the trader's own record on the trader's own authenticated screen (`INV-M4-10`), so its identifiers are theirs and are not a leak. Applying the certificate rule here would be cargo cult; applying the purchase rule there would be the failure the certificate rule exists to prevent.
