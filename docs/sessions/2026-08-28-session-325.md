### 1. The objective, and what made it a ruling rather than a repair

**`P7-i` reported a collision it could not resolve and this session held the number for it.** Two `approved` documents order one queue and they order it differently. That is not reconcilable by reading harder, because the disagreement is about what the queue is FOR, and `ADR-178` is the instrument for moving an approved document.

**Both sentences were read at their own sources on this branch**, at [`API_CONTRACT.md:1017`](../architecture/API_CONTRACT.md) and [`M07:323`](../plans/M07-risk-abuse.md), rather than taken from the dispatch or from the allocation row. That mattered: one premise the record carried came back false, and section 4 is it.

### 4. The premise that came back false

[STATE](../STATE.md) and [session 318](2026-08-28-session-318.md) both record the available reconciliation as *"the queue is a queue of IDENTITIES ordered by corroboration and the contract's sort is the order WITHIN one identity, which is exactly this adapter's second and third keys."*

**`compareRanked` compares `corroboration.depth` and never `identityId`.** Two identities at the SAME depth interleave by severity. **The band is a DEPTH, not an identity**, and the two readings describe different queues: one interleaves two corroborated identities by severity, the other blocks them.

**This is not pedantry, it decides what the amended sentence says.** An amendment written from the recorded reconciliation would have promised a grouping the adapter does not perform, and the next reader would have met it as a bug. Both records are corrected **beside their originals rather than overwritten**, and each names `ADR-178` section 1 finding 7 as the source.

### 5. What the ruling refused, and what each refusal costs

`ADR-178` section 3 carries the table. The one worth naming here is **the second-best answer, which is genuinely good: two orders for two audiences.** It costs nothing on paper, both documents stand and no sentence moves.

**It is refused because it restores the attack in full on the surface that carries it.** `AS-M7-03`'s failure mode is *"the operator learns that D-01 means nothing"*, and an operator with a severity-ordered queue on the same screen works the poisoned one whenever it looks busier. **A control an operator can switch off by clicking a tab is not a control.** It also needs a second cursor vocabulary: `decodeCursor` already refuses a cursor whose component count belongs to a different ordering, so the second queue arrives with its own way of being handed the wrong one.
