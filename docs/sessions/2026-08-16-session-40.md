### The partition, and how twenty-seven scenarios came to be owned by nobody

`FOLD-01` added `GS-258` to `GS-272` and `FOLD-02` added `GS-273` to `GS-284`. **Neither fold touched section 33.1**, which is the table that says who owes each scenario, and the table went on summing to **257** while the registry moved to **284**.

That matters beyond tidiness because **[P2's done-condition quotes this table**: it owes M1's owned set of **73** rather than the registry total. A scenario owned by nobody is a scenario **no phase has promised to make green**, and it would have reached launch unclaimed with every individual row still looking right.

The twenty-seven are assigned by reading each scenario's own text for the module whose suite holds the fixture: **M19** takes nine (the phone-identity ceremony and the restriction episode), **M4** four (the authority boundary, which M04 §8.2 already owed), **M5** five, **M6** three (M06 §8.3's owed set), **M16** two, **M3** two, **M20** one, **M8** one. **Verified by execution rather than by adding up a column**: the partition covers `GS-001` to `GS-284` exactly once, no gaps and no duplicates.

### What `CI-06d` now asserts, and the one thing it deliberately does not

Both directions plus the declared counts: a scenario in the registry owned by nobody, a scenario owned twice, and an owner whose stated count disagrees with its own cell. Each was **watched failing on its own finding** before the fix was called done, and `CI-06d/unowned-scenario` keeps the first one watched.
