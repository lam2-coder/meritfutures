### The wave reproduced the defect it exists to repair, in its own merges

**Seven of the eight pull requests conflicted, and almost every conflict was [`docs/sessions/README.md`](README.md)**: every session appends one row to one table, so every merge after the first collides there. **That is the keep-both mechanism that produced the 106 keys**, met head-on by the wave written to remove them.

**The resolutions are not one rule, and assuming they were is what went wrong.**

| File class | Resolution | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `sessions/README.md`, `decisions/README.md` | **Union** | Two rows for two sessions are two entries. Nothing is chosen |
| `CI06U_REGISTER` in [`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs) | **Deletion, never union** | The register **shrinks only** |
| `gen:` spans in [INDEX](../INDEX.md) and [STATE](../STATE.md) | **Regenerate** | Each side is right about its own registry and wrong about the other's |

**The register was unioned once, on the `S4` merge, and the count went UP from 91 to 93.** It resurrected the entries `S2` and `S3` had already cleared, and `CI-06u` failed on the merge that created it. **A union is correct for an append and wrong for a deletion**, and the two look identical in a conflict hunk. The fix was main's register with that session's deletions applied on top.

### Finding 1: no gate detects a conflict marker, and 22 of 22 passed over two

**`<<<<<<< HEAD` stood in [INDEX](../INDEX.md) at line 13 and in [STATE](../STATE.md) at line 32, and `node scripts/corpus/gates.mjs check` reported 22 of 22.** `CI-06a` resolves links, `CI-06b` reads frontmatter, `CI-06g` regenerates spans, and **not one of them looks for the three markers `git` writes into a file it could not merge.**

**This is the failure class the corpus keeps finding**: a control that exists, stays valid, and enforces nothing. An unresolved conflict marker in a frozen specification is not a formatting defect, it is **two contradictory statements shipped as one document**, which is `CI-06u`'s own subject at a different granularity.

**It needs no letter.** [ADR-065](../decisions/ADR-065.md) section 5 rules the successor identifier is **a slug rather than a letter**, so this is `CI-06/conflict-markers` and costs nothing from a registry that has run out of alphabet.

### Finding 2: the falsification seed decayed for the second time, and the second anchor was the row count

**Merging `S5` left [`docs/sessions/README.md`](README.md) as the only file in the register, and `falsify.mjs` threw `seed anchor not found` on a tree where the gate was working.**

`CI-06u/a-repaired-register-entry-is-itself-a-finding` seeds a **repair** and then reads back which claim was repaired. It deleted **one row** and looked for a key carrying **fewer than two**. **That assumes every registered claim carries exactly two rows, and the session index does not**: the parallel-session convention gives sessions 31, 49, 50 and 56 **four rows each**, so removing one leaves three and the read-back found nothing.

**This seed has now outgrown two anchors.** It was hardcoded to `inv-m20-06` in [M20](../plans/M20-wallet.md) until `ADR-063`'s session repaired that duplicate and the case reported `DID NOT FAIL`; `S3` generalised it to the register's first claim and left the row count assumed. **The fix is to seed the whole repair rather than a fragment of one**: keep the first row carrying the key, drop the rest.

**It was found by running `falsify.mjs` against the MERGE RESULT rather than against the branch.** Every branch was green on its own, because `main` still carried `docs/STATE.md` in the register at the time each one ran. **A harness anchored to corpus state decays as the corpus is repaired, and the wave that repairs fastest breaks it soonest.**

### Finding 3: the session registry could not hold a three-digit session number

**This log is session 105 and `CI-06c` refused it**: `not in INDEX: docs/sessions/2026-08-20-session-105.md`.

[`gates.mjs`](../../scripts/corpus/gates.mjs)'s `REGISTRIES` entry read `session-\d{2}\.md`, **exactly two digits**. A session file numbered 100 or above is therefore not a registry entry at all: `CI-06c` reports it missing from [INDEX](../INDEX.md) because the `sessions/` directory row does not cover it, and `CI-06n` cannot see it in either direction.

**The cap was invisible for ninety-nine sessions and then blocked five at once.** [FOLD-05](../plans/FOLD-05-plan-config-and-designer.md) had already allocated **98 to 104**, so `P3` through `P7` would each have hit it. Widened to `\d{2,}`.

**It belongs to `ADR-064`'s subject** rather than to this merge: the session registry is the one registry with no allocation table, no ruled identity function, and now a demonstrated format bound. `S9` should carry it.
