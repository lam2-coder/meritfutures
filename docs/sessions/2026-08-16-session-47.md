### What each fixture pins, and the one number that makes it separate a real engine from a plausible one

**GS-021 and GS-022, the consistency denominator.** `R-30` skips the gate entirely unless period profit is `> 0`. Both files run a funded account through a +40,000c day and then a losing one, and the losing day's size is the only independent difference: **-40,000c lands the denominator on zero, -90,000c lands it 50,000c below.** In both, the numerator is 40,000c and stays there, because [section 3.6](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) takes the **maximum** rather than the last value. So `R-29`'s comparison is **false** on both states while `R-30` rules the gate to pass, and the two rules give opposite answers: `40,000 * 10000 = 400,000,000` against `3000 * 0 = 0` and against `3000 * -50,000 = -150,000,000`. **A fixture whose best day were also zero would satisfy `0 <= 0` and pass for a reason that has nothing to do with `R-30`.** That is the trap both files are built to avoid, and it is `FM-15`'s: the failure mode is not a crash, it is a `NaN` that compares false and silently blocks eligibility.

**GS-055, AS-03's minimum-variance path.** [AS-03](../plans/M01-rules-engine.md) computes the cheapest sequence that clears every gate and names it: five days at exactly 50,000c on CORE-50K. The fixture is that sentence transcribed. Best-day share **2000bp** against a 3000bp limit, win days **5** against a required 5, and the withdrawable landing **exactly** on the 150,000c cap, which is what makes it a full-cap extraction rather than an eligible one. **Drop any day and two different named fields move**, which is what `AS-03` means by calling five "the floor on days-to-first-payout". The sharpest field is `floor_locked: false`: **the cheapest full-cap cycle finishes 10,000c short of the 260,000c lock trigger**, so a trader executing it arrives at their first payout with the floor still trailing.

**GS-069, AS-13's monotonicity counterexample.** GS-023's stream with day one's P&L raised from 150,000c to 250,000c. The ratio goes from **3000bp exactly**, which passes, to **4166bp**, which defers, and the trader who did it made 100,000c **more**. The asymmetry is the whole point: `R-29` multiplies the numerator by 10000 and the denominator by only `max_day_share_bp`, so an increase shared between them makes the ratio **worse**. Adding the same profit to the last day instead is dilution and is GS-020's subject.

### AS-13's numbers are carried at a scale of five, and the scale is stated rather than silent

`AS-13` illustrates with best 30,000c on period profit 100,000c moving to 50,000c on 120,000c. **Its period profit does not meet the 300,000c eval target on any 50K plan**, so the ratio is not reproducible at `AS-13`'s own magnitudes inside a fixture that has to reach the gate. Multiplying **all four** numbers by five gives 150,000 on 500,000 moving to 250,000 on 600,000, which is exactly GS-023's stream moving to GS-069's, meets the target, and preserves both ratios to the basis point. **The scale is applied to the whole quadruple or to none of it**, and it is written in the fixture header so the next reader does not have to reconstruct why the numbers are not `AS-13`'s.
