### 1. The starting measurement, which is the first finding

The dispatch's instruction was to run session 363's twelve cases first and report which had gone red,
"because a case that goes red is a blocker that lifted". **Twelve of twelve were green**, and that is not a
disagreement with session 363's design: it is that design having already paid out. Session 372 inverted
the blocker-1 case inside its own diff, in the same commit that registered the table, and said so in its
own entry's section 12. **A clearing condition can only fire once, and the session that lifts the blocker
is the session that spends it.**

So the news had to be derived. What follows is that derivation.

### 2. Where derivable and readable come apart

ADR-199's ruling 6 is exact and this entry does not weaken it: *"a figure is owed a column when it cannot
be derived, never when it is merely not stored"*. All three figures are derivable and none is owed a
column. **`0062` stays returned and nothing here asks for a migration number.**

What the entry did not rule, and in two of three places says so itself, is whether the derivation can be
EXECUTED by this tree today. It cannot, for two of the three, and a fourth blocker on a field nobody had
looked at makes four.

| # | Group | Leaves | What stands | Clearing condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | `eligible_next_7d` | 5 | `trading_calendar` is not a `TableKey`, so the fold's fourth input cannot be named. ADR-199 section 6 named this and left it to "the session that NEEDS it" | `TABLE_KEYS` contains `tradingCalendar` |
| B2 | `payout_velocity` | 4 | Three documents fix the 2.5x threshold and none states the 30-day window | A ruling fixes the window |
| B3 | `per_plan[].cusum` | 3 | ADR-167 clause 5 renders it ABSENT until `DEP-M6-05`, and all three copies of the response type it REQUIRED | The calibration lands, or the shape gains an absent form |
| B4 | `integrations.recon.last_run_at` | 1 | Nothing records a reconciliation RUN, and the only fold available is the one ADR-199 refuses for the batch | A `recon.completed` event or a run record |

**Not one of the four is a column and not one is repaired by a migration.** Two are rulings owed and two
are a session's work in another fence.

### 3. B1, and the stale refusal sitting beside it

`schema.ts`'s `trading_calendar_loads` header reads: *"`0032` carries `ALTER TABLE trading_calendar ALTER
COLUMN session_open_at DROP NOT NULL` and the same for `session_close_at`, which ADR-094's one-member
vocabulary REFUSES, so `trading_calendar` cannot be registered."*

That was true when it was written and **[ADR-103](../decisions/ADR-103.md) clause 2 superseded it**: that
entry replaced ADR-094's stated proxy with the type-and-nullability comparison the proxy stood for, added
`ALTER COLUMN ... DROP NOT NULL` as the fold's second member, and names *"`otp_challenges` and
`trading_calendar` become REGISTRABLE"* among its consequences. **So B1 is a registration nobody has
taken, not a refusal that stands**, which is what ADR-199 section 6 said and what a reader of `schema.ts`
alone would conclude the opposite of. Reported and not repaired: `packages/db` is another fence.

The distinction is not academic. It decides whether the next dispatch for this method needs an ADR-092
registration slice (it does) or a widening of a closed vocabulary that is itself an ADR (it does not).

### 4. B2, and why an unstated window is worse than an unstated figure

The threshold is stated three times. The denominator is stated nowhere, and the field name does not settle
it: `avg_30d_cents` reads equally as a 30-day total, a daily mean over 30 days, or that mean re-expressed
over a seven-day window so the ratio is comparable to `last_7d_cents`.

Only the third makes the corpus's own threshold mean what it says. Under the second, a seven-day total
divided by a daily mean sits near 7.0 whenever nothing at all is wrong, so **a threshold of 2.5x fires on
the first day it is switched on and every day after.** `FM-M6-07` already names that failure for the
CUSUM -- *"either constant alarms or none, which is the same as no chart"* -- and `INFRA:160` attaches a
PAGE to this one.

**Choosing here would be calibrating a firm-wide pager inside an adapter**, which is `AS-M6-02`'s
mechanism: a control that cries wolf gets overridden, and the overriding is what the incident later runs
into. The numerator is producible on its own (`payout_transfers.amount_cents` over a `settled_at` range
term ADR-157 admits on the read path), which is what makes this a denominator problem and not a schema
one.

### 5. B3, and two rulings whose conjunction is empty

This is session 366's shape arriving on a different pair, and it is worth stating in that form because the
form is what makes it findable.

- ADR-167 clause 5: the field is rendered **ABSENT** until `DEP-M6-05` supplies `mu_0` and `sigma`, and
  *"the wire shape is `P7-b`'s to carry, not this entry's to invent"*.
- ADR-188 and its code half: `cusum` is a **REQUIRED** object of `statistic`, `threshold` and `alarm`, in
  API_CONTRACT, in `admin-reads.ts` and in `api/types.ts`, and `RI-18` binds all three.

Each is right alone. Together they leave an adapter two moves, both forbidden: manufacture a statistic
clause 5 refuses, or change a shape three declarations carry and another slice renders. **The shape is
reported and not changed**, which is what the dispatch required of exactly this case.

`apps/worker/src/digests/produce.ts` already renders the absence in terms -- `absent: blocked on ...
(ADR-167 clause 5, FM-M6-07)` -- so this tree has an absent form on the one surface whose shape nobody
fixed in advance, and none on the one that matters here.

### 6. B4, ADR-199's own refusal one field to the left

ADR-199 section 5 refuses `max(rule_states.computed_at)` as the batch's clock, because
[OVERVIEW section 5.2](../architecture/OVERVIEW.md) makes the run resumable at the account boundary, so
*"a fold over per-account clocks reports a SUCCESS for a run that crashed"*.

`integrations.recon.last_run_at` is the same field one column to the left, and the same fold is the only
thing available: `reconciliations` is one row per account per trading day, and EVENTS section 5 carries
`recon.mismatch_detected` and `recon.resolved` and no `recon.completed`. **Taking the fold here would
overturn that reasoning by writing code rather than by writing an entry**, which is the move ADR-199
itself refuses about `cusum` and DDL.

The contrast is what makes the finding precise: the BATCH has exactly the event this field would need,
which is why ADR-199 clause 4 could rule its two figures readable, and this one is not.

### 7. What the module does, and the one design choice that was measured

Eight `TableKey`s, two whole-table folds where the accessor offers no `ORDER BY`, two typed equalities,
one range-free filtered read, no join, no aggregate, no `sqlExecutor` and no `pg` import.

**THE TREASURY ANCHOR IS READ BY `rowsWhere` AND NEVER BY `rowAt`, AND THAT WAS MEASURED RATHER THAN
REASONED.** `scoped-db.ts`'s `refuseUnaddressed` names this table in its own docblock as *"the only
registered table with no addressable key at all"*, because `0009` gives it `PRIMARY KEY (account_code,
as_of)` and `schema.ts` declares none. Run live, `rowAt` is refused with that message on a predicate the
database honours, and the equality pair returns exactly one row. `LiabilityTx` therefore declares no
`rowAt` at all: a method the module cannot use correctly is a method the next reader uses incorrectly.
**The transcription gap is registered and not repaired**, on session 371's `coupons.code` precedent.

**`LiabilityBook` IS A SUBTRACTION AND NEVER A COPY.** Every member is an `Omit` or an index into
`LiabilityResponse`, so a field added to the response is on the book by the rule, a blocker that lifts is
one deleted `Omit`, and the tree gains no fourth declaration of a served shape for `RI-18` to have to
bind.

### 8. The arithmetic, and the fold that got it wrong first

The subtraction is checked rather than asserted: the suite reads `LiabilityResponse`'s paths out of
API_CONTRACT through `RI-18`'s own `copiesOf`, folds them to leaves, runs the reader over a fixture, and
compares.

**A CONTAINER IS NOT A FIELD AND THE FIRST FOLD COUNTED THREE OF THEM AS FIELDS.** `copiesOf` emits both
`per_plan` and `per_plan[].code`, so a leaf rule reading only the dotted extension leaves `per_plan`,
`integrations.mid_health` and `eligible_next_7d.by_day` in the set and reports **43** leaves where the
response has **40**. The rule reads both extensions now and the case that reports the three numbers is
what caught it.

### Landmines

- **A clearing condition fires ONCE, and the session that lifts the blocker spends it.** Session 372
  inverted blocker 1's case in its own diff, so the next session's "run the suite first" reported nothing.
  A dispatch that plans to read a red should check whether the lifting session already consumed it.
- **`derivable` and `readable` are different claims** and ADR-199 is precise about the first. A future
  entry citing it for the second is citing a ruling it did not make.
- **`LiabilityBook` is a subtraction and must stay one.** The day somebody hand-writes its members it
  becomes a fourth copy of a served shape that `RI-18` does not bind.
- **`copiesOf` emits array containers as paths.** Any count of "fields" derived from it needs both
  extensions or it is three too high on this response.
- **The reads are whole-table where the accessor offers no `ORDER BY`.** `liability_snapshots`,
  `reserve_coverage_snapshots`, `plan_breaker_state`, `mid_health` and `batch.completed` all grow without
  bound, and a retention window no document states is not this module's to invent. `LiabilityReadCost` is
  what makes the growth visible.
