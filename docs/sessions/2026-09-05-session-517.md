### 4. The two things that were not transcription

**THE PRIVILEGE RULING.** [`0079`](../../packages/db/migrations/0079_pgboss_job_store.sql) and [`probe_pgboss_job_store.sql`](../../scripts/db/probe_pgboss_job_store.sql) both say that making the queue usable by the application role means granting `CREATE` on the `pgboss` schema. **Read at `pgboss.create_queue`'s own body, that is true of a PARTITIONED queue and false of the one this workspace declares**: the function `RETURN`s above its `EXECUTE format('CREATE TABLE pgboss.%I ...')` unless `partition = 'true'`, and `pgBossQueue.declareQueue` passes no options. So the grant is `USAGE` plus table privileges, and `CREATE` on a schema inside the ledger's restore boundary is refused. The residue is stated rather than hidden: the exact privilege set needs a live `start()` under the role and this row had no estate to run one against.

**THE REFUSAL OF A LIVE CLOCK.** Slice 8's stop condition asks for a clock and this row gives the driver a row, an expected-by time and a dead-man switch and no schedule. A schedule that starts `runWithdrawalApprovals` against a live adapter posts `LT-06` and extinguishes a trader's wallet claim into `approved`, out of which the only arrow is `transferring`, which `packages/rail` cannot reach, while `WD-C2` refuses the cancel at the database. **That is slice 9's harm and [ADR-305](../decisions/ADR-305.md) forbids slice 9 until a rail exists.** A clock is a slower door, not a different one.
