## 2026-08-21 - Session 119: the WAVE-04 merge, and the collision was a missing claim rather than a numbering mistake
<!--index: the WAVE-04 merge: six pull requests, `#154` to `#159`, and three logs renumbered because six concurrent sessions each read the highest row and each took the next number correctly. **The collision was a missing claim, not a numbering mistake**, so a claim table now sits above these entries. `W8` ships three gates of four and `CI-06/gate-inventory` moves to `W9` whole rather than being softened to pass before `CI-09` exists. `adr_count` was wrong on BOTH sides of one conflict and the merged truth was neither -->

- **Objective:** merge `W1` to `W6`, and dispatch everything that can run in parallel from the result.
- **Done:** **six pull requests merged, `#154` to `#159`, `main` at 24 of 24 gates and 1404 tests.** Three session logs renumbered at merge under the founder's ruling, one plan brief amended on a dependency the merge exposed, and a claim table added to [sessions/README](README.md) so the collision that forced the renumber cannot recur silently.
- **Next:** four sessions dispatched concurrently, `114`, `115`, `119` and `120`, and `121` behind the first two.
- **Blockers:** none. `CI-04` stays open on a provisioned Neon branch, which [ADR-073](../decisions/ADR-073.md) section 4 gives as its activation condition and which no session can grant.

### The renumber was mechanical and the reason it was needed is not

**Three WAVE-04 sessions wrote themselves as `110`, `111` and `112` while three app sessions were taking the same three numbers**, and every one of the six read the highest row in [sessions/README](README.md) and took the next number correctly. **Nothing was done wrong and the numbers still collided**, which is the whole content of the finding: a number was free when no row claimed it, and a row is written at the END of a session, so six concurrent sessions saw six identical trees.

The founder ruled the direction at [WAVE-04 section 5](../plans/WAVE-04-fixture-backlog-and-gate-inventory.md): the app sessions keep `110`, `111` and `112`, and `W3`, `W4` and `W5` renumber to `116`, `117` and `118`. **Applied here to the file name AND the `##` heading in each of the three**, because [ADR-064](../decisions/ADR-064.md) makes those two together the identity and moving one alone is a rename rather than a renumber.

> **The remedy is the claim table, not the renumber.** [sessions/README](README.md) now carries `CLAIMED and not yet written` above the entries, and a session dispatched against a number claims it BEFORE it runs rather than after. That inverts the order that produced the collision.

### `W8` ships three gates of four, and gate 3 moves rather than softens

**`CI-06/gate-inventory` is the only one of `W8`'s four gates that depends on `W7`**: it must see `CI-09` implemented in a workflow, and `W7` is what implements it. `W8`'s other three touch `scripts/corpus/` and [STRATEGY](../testing/STRATEGY.md); `W7` touches `.github/workflows/` and `scripts/ci/`. **The fences are disjoint and serialising the two to satisfy one gate of four buys nothing.**

**The alternative was to let gate 3 tolerate an unimplemented `CI-09` and it was refused.** `W8`'s own brief says *"IT MUST SEE `CI-09` AS IMPLEMENTED. If it does not, `W7` has not merged and you are running out of order. Stop."* A gate-inventory check that passed over a missing `CI-09` would be the third gate in this wave justified by a claim the tree refutes, which is the failure class [ADR-072](../decisions/ADR-072.md) and [ADR-074](../decisions/ADR-074.md) were both written against. **So it moved to `W9`, session `121`, whole.**

### Two things the merge itself proved, both about resolution rather than about content

**`adr_count` was wrong on BOTH sides of the `W6` conflict and the merged truth was neither.** `HEAD` read 73, `origin/main` read 74, and the tree after the merge holds 75. **Picking a side would have shipped a wrong number with a clean conflict resolution in front of it.** `node scripts/corpus/gates.mjs generate` is the only correct resolution of a generated span and it was used on every one of the six.

**And a keep-both resolution is wrong for a one-line span.** Applied blindly to [INDEX](../INDEX.md) and [STATE](../STATE.md), whose conflict was one sentence differing only in that count, it produced the sentence twice. Caught by reading the resolution rather than by a gate: **`CI-06g` checks that a span matches its query and has nothing to say about a second paragraph that also matches.**

### Verification

`node scripts/corpus/gates.mjs check` **24 of 24** on `main` after the sixth merge. `node scripts/corpus/falsify.mjs` clean, 37 scope cases and 10 loader cases. `pnpm vitest run` **1404 passed, 45 skipped, 101 files**. `pnpm run format:check`, `pnpm run lint` and `pnpm -r run typecheck` all clean, run before every one of the five pushes rather than after the last, which is the gap the `#155` prettier failure left in the previous session.

- **Landmines:** **the claim table is a convention with no gate.** Nothing checks that a dispatched session claimed its number, and nothing strikes a row when the session lands. `CI-06/identifier-series` is `W9`-adjacent work whose scope is [ADR-074](../decisions/ADR-074.md)'s, and whether this table is a register in that ADR's sense is a question for whoever writes that gate, **not a licence to widen it here**.
