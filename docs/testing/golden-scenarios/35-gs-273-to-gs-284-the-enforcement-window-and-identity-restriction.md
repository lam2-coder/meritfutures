## 35. GS-273 to GS-284: the enforcement window and identity restriction (FOLD-02)

[ADR-040](../../decisions/ADR-040.md) and [ADR-041](../../decisions/ADR-041.md), planned by [FOLD-02](../../plans/FOLD-02-enforcement-window-and-suspension.md). **Ruling A's four and Ruling B's four**, which is section 7's stated minimum of eight, plus four that the fold's own sessions asked for by name.

**The range continues from the registry maximum, derived rather than quoted**, per the discipline that has now found eighteen hand-maintained counts in this corpus wrong.

**Five of these discharge citations owed in words by earlier sessions of this fold**, named in [M06 section 8.3](../../plans/M06-admin-ops-console.md), [M03 section 8](../../plans/M03-billing-checkout.md) and [M08 section 8](../../plans/M08-affiliate-system.md). Those sessions declined to write a `GS-nnn` before the row existed, because `CI-06d` fails on any identifier cited under `docs/` that does not resolve, and claiming in a registry with no claim mechanism is the drift [ADR-034](../../decisions/ADR-034.md) exists to stop. **The debt is settled here**, and [GS-114](12-gs-112-to-gs-117-admin-and-ops-console-m6.md)'s row is amended in the same commit to name four unsuppressible alarms rather than three.

**Two of the twelve are the negative half of a pair and neither is decoration.** GS-275 pins that a hold which reaches expiry **pays even if the account breached during the hold**, which is the one place a careful implementer would write the opposite; GS-276 pins that the alarm fires when the sweep is **reporting success**, which is the failure a dead-man switch cannot see.

| ID | Scenario | Pins |
|---|---|---|
| GS-273 | **Ruling A** A payout is requested while an unresolved severity-5 flag stands, and the flag resolves inside the window | The request enters `held_pending_review` **before approval**, carrying `held_at`, `hold_flag_id`, `hold_expires_at`, `hold_tos_clause` and `hold_reason` together or not at all (`payout_requests_hold_is_complete`). **The full evaluated decision is stored at request time**: the eligibility snapshot, `approved_cents`, the split, the ordinal and the pinned plan version. Release is then **mechanical and re-evaluates nothing**, which is `INV-M5-02` executed rather than asserted: the number shown is the number sent. Pins that the hold defers **only the ledger posting**, so no existing `NOT NULL` and no existing CHECK on the money table had to be relaxed |
| GS-274 | **Ruling A** The same hold ends in enforcement rather than release | The request goes to `failed` with a **cited flag, a ToS clause, a written reason and an exported evidence pack**, and nothing is reversed because nothing was posted: no LT-01, no wallet credit, no LT-03. **The ordinal is released** by the move to `failed`, which `payout_requests_account_ordinal_uq` permits and EC-037 already ruled. The discriminator against `frozen` is the whole assertion here: a released freeze lets settlement proceed and an enforced freeze needs `payout_reversal`, and neither verb applies to this state |
| GS-275 | **Ruling A** A held request reaches its 48 hour expiry and **the account breached during the hold** | It **pays.** `INV-M5-09`'s first clause governs, the snapshot was true when it was taken; its second clause, the money was already the trader's, does not apply and is not needed. **The alternative is that Merit's own hold cost the trader money**, which is the exact shape zero denial exists to make impossible, and it is pinned by a fixture rather than left to reasoning because the opposite reads as prudence. The auto-release is the load-bearing control of the whole window and this is the case that proves it is not conditional on the investigation's outcome. EC-150 |
| GS-276 | **Ruling A, the negative half** The hourly sweep runs, completes and **emits its completion signal**, and a held request sits past `hold_expires_at` anyway | The **nightly assertion fires**, because it reads the query rather than the job: no row past its own expiry, evaluated independently of whether the sweep reported success. The dead-man switch is **satisfied** in this scenario and that is the point, since it watches for an absent signal and the signal is present. It pages as the **fourth unsuppressible alarm**, and it covers all three clocks the sweep carries. **[M02](../../plans/M02-rithmic-bridge.md) `FM-M2-11`'s idiom applied to the releaser**, and `CI-06l` is the same fact checked from the tree with no database. EC-150, EC-151 |
| GS-277 | **Ruling A** A flag is raised **after** wallet credit and the withdrawal is halted before settlement | `wallet_withdrawals_live_freeze_blocks_settlement` refuses `settled` while `frozen_at` stands, which is the enforcement the columns never had: [`0011`](../../../packages/db/migrations/0011_wallet.sql) gave this table the whole freeze vocabulary and `wallet_withdrawal_status` has no frozen value, so the halt was **representable and unenforced**. The **halted row stays visible** in the re-created open index, because a halt that removes the row from the only index anyone scans is a halt nobody can find. Release **resumes the rail and does not re-pay**, since the money is already the trader's. Pins the deliberate asymmetry: the payout hold is a status and this halt is not, because it is orthogonal to the rail state (`SD-M5-06`). EC-156 |
| GS-278 | **Ruling A** A second payout request is opened on an account whose first request is held | Refused by `payout_requests_no_in_flight_uq`, **a genuine database constraint and not a handler check**, whose predicate now reads `('approved','frozen','held_pending_review')`. **A held request is outstanding.** Pins the `C-02` repair verbatim ([ADR-028](../../decisions/ADR-028.md)): a predicate that stops matching is a gate that still exists, is still valid, enforces nothing and fails no test, and both `SD-09` objects were moved together under their own names so a future change to one is visibly a change to one of two. EC-040, which stated this gate in the pre-ADR-028 terms until this fold |
| GS-279 | **Ruling B** An identity is restricted, and every surface [ADR-041](../../decisions/ADR-041.md) enumerates is exercised | **All six refuse**: purchases and resets at checkout, payout requests through `G-ELIGIBLE` now naming `identities.status`, wallet spend, external withdrawal, affiliate settlement, and platform trading revoked through the bridge. And the seventh row is the assertion that is easiest to lose: **account state is preserved intact.** No account status moves, no ladder rung is consumed, no entitlement history is rewritten, because the restriction is a layer over the account machine exactly as `payouts_frozen` and `recon_blocked` already are. M06 section 8.3's first owed scenario |
| GS-280 | **Ruling B** A restricted identity attempts to buy, and to reset, **on each of the three payment methods** | Refused **server side at the resolved-identity step** on card, on wallet and on `mixed`, with a stated reason and a defined error code, as a **hard limit rather than a compensation**: nothing about the payment is begun and then unwound. The refusal **survives the identity presenting a second email that resolves to the same identity**, which is GS-094's assertion with a different hard limit behind it. Pins the general rule this found: a hard limit asserted against one method passes while another refuses for an unrelated reason. M03 section 8's owed scenario, EC-155 |
| GS-281 | **Ruling B** An affiliate whose identity is restricted reaches a settlement cycle, and is later restored | The statement is **held and not voided** (`INV-M8-12`), is **not settled while the restriction stands**, and is **settled in full on the next cycle after a documented restore**. **The positive control is the half that matters**, because a gate asserted only on the refusal passes just as well when it refuses everybody. Voiding would make a reversible enforcement permanently cost the affiliate money, which is ADR-040's "Merit's own hold cost the trader money" one door over. `FM-M8-11` is the quiet half pinned by the second act: a restore with no release leaves the money held forever and raises nothing. M08 section 8's owed scenario, EC-154 |
| GS-282 | **Ruling B** A restore is attempted while the account's `set_risk` is unconfirmed | **Refused**, and the episode **stays open** with `restored_at` null. `0007` already makes it unwritable for a `set_risk` row to reach `confirmed_inferred`, which is exactly the guarantee restoration needs, because re-enabling an entitlement against an unconfirmed setpoint is an unenforced funded account and `INV-M2-13` forbids it. Pins the published asymmetry rather than a caveat: **suspension is always available and restoration is contingent on `V-M2-15`**, so a restricted trader is revocable and, until that artifact exists, not restorable. M06 section 8.3's second owed scenario, EC-153 |
| GS-283 | **Ruling B** A restore succeeds, and is then contested | **The episode row is the proof.** `restored_at`, `restored_by` and `restore_evidence` are written together or not at all (`identity_restriction_restore_is_complete`), so there is no state in which the database says a restriction was reversed and cannot say by whom or on what basis. `identity_restriction_restore_follows_open` refuses a restore dated before the restriction it reverses. Pins why the episode is a row rather than a column on `identities`: **a repeat restriction would overwrite its predecessor and the restore would be unprovable at exactly the moment it is contested**, and `identity_restriction_open_uq` keeps at most one open episode per human so there is never a question of which one was reversed |
| GS-284 | **Both rulings** A restriction is opened over an account that already has a held payout | **The payout pays at its own 48 hours, with the restriction still standing.** `hold_expires_at` does not move, and no admin route can move it (`INV-M6-13`); the episode's `sla_due_at` carries ADR-040's clock so the **restriction inherits the payout's deadline** rather than the payout inheriting the restriction's open-endedness. **This is the property that stops Ruling B from becoming a route around Ruling A**, and it is the one case where each half is individually defensible and the failure lives only in the join. M06 section 8.3's third owed scenario, EC-152 |
