## EC-104: Progressive cap release moves the lifetime bound for the cohort best able to reach it  (2026-08-14, module: M14, status: specced)
- **RULED 2026-08-14 ([ADR-025](../decisions/ADR-025.md)): the mechanic is REJECTED for v1, not deferred.** The correct behavior below is superseded by a prohibition rather than a process, and the scenario is retained because the arithmetic is what the rejection rests on. The replacement is **cross-account loyalty** ([EC-139](EC-139.md)). **Every option, tabulated:** the market's own shape (release after ordinal 5) is **structurally impossible on a 5-rung ladder** because five payouts is the whole ladder; release at ordinal 5 costs **+20 percent** of the lifetime bound and delivers one raised payout at the very end of an account's life; release from ordinal 4 costs **+40 percent**. A shorter ladder did not make the mechanic safer. It made the cheap version impossible and left only the expensive ones. **INV-M14-11 now states the outcome directly**: no loyalty mechanic moves a per-account bound, asserted by GS-243 as an equality to the cent.
- Scenario: "After five payouts your cap goes up" sounds like pure goodwill and actually moves the lifetime bound INV-17 establishes, selectively, for accounts that have already demonstrated repeated extraction. At Core EOD 50K the ladder is **5 payouts** at a 150,000c cap ([ADR-024](../decisions/ADR-024.md)), so lifetime extraction is bounded at **750,000c gross and 675,000c to the trader**; doubling the cap at ordinal 5 gives four at 150,000c and one at 300,000c, a **20 percent increase**, and doubling from ordinal 4 gives a **40 percent increase**. **The market mechanic this models, "after five payouts your cap goes up", cannot be copied at all on a 5-rung ladder, because five payouts is the whole ladder**; re-keying it earlier is what makes it expensive. ADR-018's defense of Merit Rapid's headline rate names the 5-payout ladder as the second of three protections, and this mechanic attacks it directly. The cohort reaching ordinal 5 is also not a random sample of good traders: it disproportionately includes hedged pairs and rings for whom repeated extraction is the designed outcome, so a cap release pays the residual undetected fraction more.
- Correct behavior: The mechanic is genuinely good for genuine traders, so the answer is process rather than prohibition. It is **always a plan-version publish**, carrying ADR-010's dual control, the delay window, and the full publish diff including CV-10, CV-11, CV-17, and the INV-21 post-payout-versus-floor check. The proposal event carries `projected_lifetime_delta_cents` and the founder never sees the proposal without it, with the reserve model rerun at the new schedule before publish because a cap change is a CVaR99 input. It is **published in the plan's rules page from the beginning rather than granted per trader**, so a buyer sees the second step before purchase and no per-trader state exists. The ladder's length stays structural and does not move.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-179.
