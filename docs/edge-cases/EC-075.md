## EC-075: The flagship correlation detector does not defend the first extraction cycle  (2026-08-13, module: M7, status: specced)
- Scenario: The B4 ring rehearsal requires a six-account hedged syndicate to be flagged by day 3, and that number was never checked against how fast money can actually leave. The minimum-variance path to a first full-cap payout is 5 trading days, plus 2 to 3 business days of settlement, so the earliest cash leaves is roughly trading day 7 to 8. Detection at day 3 wins, but the margin is thinner than it appears: the inverse-P&L pair detector needs a 20 trading day rolling window, so on a 5 day account life it has almost no data. A ring that funds, extracts once, and abandons every account never generates enough history for the detector the constitution names as the answer to the number one industry threat.
- Correct behavior: Split the detector set by what it needs. Fill clustering and group exposure are **pattern** detectors that operate from day 1 on short windows, and fill clustering runs **on ingest** rather than only nightly, because same-second clustering is computable the moment fills land. The inverse-pair detector keeps its long window and is explicitly labelled a **second-cycle** detector that catches persistence rather than entry. The strongest early control is not a detector at all: it is biometric dedupe at funding, which fires before the first trade, plus the identity-level eligibility forecast, which makes a correlated cluster visible before it is eligible. Honest bound to carry: a single-cycle abandon-after-extraction ring on Direct plans is bounded by the cap and the buffer rather than by detection, and the counter is entry cost plus verification.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-118, GS-050.
