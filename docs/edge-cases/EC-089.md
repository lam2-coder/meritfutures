## EC-089: A vendor becomes load bearing without anybody deciding it should  (2026-08-14, module: M10, status: specced)
- Scenario: The adversary is convenience and there is no incident. The welcome email is wired into the provisioning saga because that is where the data is. The reset offer is generated by the messaging vendor because its template engine is better. A support macro starts depending on a sidebar field. Over six months three vendors move from "notified by" to "part of", each step reasonable and none of them a decision. It is discovered during a vendor outage, when a purchase fails because a marketing platform returned a 500.
- Correct behavior: A policy against this decays exactly as fast as the drift, so the controls are structural. **All dispatch is post-commit and asynchronous**, with the enqueue participating in the transaction that caused it (ADR-006), so a saga step has no vendor call to await. **A CI chaos test runs purchase, provisioning, payout request, and payout settlement with every vendor returning 500 and then timing out**, asserting all four complete; that assertion is the control, because it fails at the moment the drift happens rather than being a paragraph everyone agrees with. **Message content is Merit's**, rendered from M16 templates and stored in `notifications` with the vendor as transport only, since a message whose text exists solely inside a vendor cannot be reproduced in an evidence pack or resent after a migration. The quarterly integration review asks one question per vendor: what breaks if they are gone for a day, and the answer must stay "messages are late".
- Golden scenario ref: GS-154.
