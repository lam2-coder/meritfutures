## EC-127: The geo-consistency triangle fires on cross-border lives  (2026-08-14, module: M19, status: specced)
- Scenario: Constitution (d) requires consistency checks across the IP, document, and payment country triangle, and a mismatch is a real signal: dossier item 6 names VPNs and synthetic identities and item 3 names login geography versus KYC mismatch as a paid-passing-service signal. It is also, far more often, the ordinary condition of legitimate customers: expatriates with a home-country passport and a local card, migrant workers, dual nationals, students abroad, anyone travelling, and anyone whose bank issues cards from another country. Under a zero-denial brand, refusing or freezing on a mismatch reproduces M05 AS-M5-02's name-matching failure **on the same population**, so the trader whose name transliterates inconsistently is disproportionately the same trader whose passport and card disagree, and Merit would have built two independent controls that fail on the same people twice.
- Correct behavior: **Signal, never automatic refusal.** The mismatch is recorded, flagged, and joins the graph; it contributes to a picture and makes no decision. Score it rather than treating it as boolean, since the pairs differ in weight: document-versus-payment is weaker than IP-versus-both, and a stable long-term IP country differing from the document is close to meaningless alone. Monitor disparate impact as a named metric: mismatch rate by document country, and downstream flag and enforcement rates for mismatched identities against everyone else, because a control nobody measures for skew will have it. Keep restricted-jurisdiction enforcement a **separate mechanism**, so a fraud heuristic never quietly becomes a jurisdiction policy. And the trader-facing consequence of a mismatch alone is **nothing**: no message, no extra step, with any eventual action coming from M7 on its own evidence.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-214.
