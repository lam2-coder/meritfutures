## EC-079: Commission is cash paid before the sale is final  (2026-08-13, module: M8 + M3, status: specced)
- Scenario: Commission becomes payable after the refund window, measured in days, while chargebacks arrive on the card networks clock, commonly up to 120 days. An adversary buys evaluations with stolen cards through their own affiliate code, collects commission in the next monthly statement, and is gone before the cardholders notice. Merit pays real money on purchases that were never real, then pays chargeback fees on the same purchases, then absorbs a chargeback ratio spike that threatens the processor relationship. At a $99 evaluation and a 30 percent commission each fraudulent purchase yields about $30 of clean cash and costs Merit more than the purchase price. It is more attractive than plain card fraud because it converts a stolen card directly into cash with no trading at all.
- Correct behavior: The two clocks are separated (SD-M8-01), and the commercial choice to pay before the chargeback window closes is made explicitly rather than by omission, because waiting 120 days is not commercially available in this market. Three controls carry the accepted risk. A **carried negative balance** so a clawback always lands and nets against future commission. A **per-affiliate reserve holdback** for the first 90 days of a relationship, so new affiliates carry the risk they introduce (OQ-M8-01). And **chargeback rate as a payment gate**, holding the statement pending review rather than only appearing on a dashboard, which is what catches the attack in its first month rather than its fourth.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-123.
