## EC-097: A structurally constant 100 percent is the least believable number on the page  (2026-08-14, module: M12, status: specced)
- Scenario: The share of eligible payout requests approved is 100 percent, always, because M05 INV-M5-01 has no denial code path. It is simultaneously the strongest available claim and the least believable, since every reader has seen a firm claim it and read the reviews saying otherwise; TOP10_FIRMS records exactly that pattern at Apex, with payouts approved but unpaid 15+ business days and accounts "under review" after profitability. The adversarial sharpening is that a skeptic attacks the definition rather than the number, and there is a real gap to attack: frozen requests, deferred ones, `recon_blocked` accounts, and requests never made because the trader was not eligible. A reader will assume all of those hide inside the word "eligible", and that is a reasonable inference.
- Correct behavior: Turn the weakest number into the strongest by publishing the denominator's **full decomposition** rather than the ratio alone: requests made, approved, **frozen** with count and median duration, released on freeze expiry, and accounts blocked from requesting. The frozen count is precisely what a skeptic is looking for and publishing it unprompted is worth more than the 100 percent is. Publish the freeze bound as a fact about Merit, since M05 SD-M5-01 makes freezes expire and expiry **releases** the payout: "freezes: 3, median 4 days, bounded at 10 business days by policy, 100 percent released or resolved" is a claim no competitor can make and is directly checkable against trader reports. State the structural reason in the method page, that no denial status exists in the schema, because a claim backed by an absence in a data model is unusually strong and unusually easy to explain. And publish external settlement time beside it, because instant approval means nothing if settlement is slow.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-166.
