## EC-155: A hard limit that binds one payment method and reads as binding all three  (2026-08-16, module: M03, status: specced)
- Scenario: `INV-M20-06` has blocked **wallet spend** on a restricted identity since [ADR-041](../decisions/ADR-041.md) folded, and nothing in checkout read `identities.status`. The result is precise and backwards: **a restricted trader could buy with a card and could not buy with money Merit already owes them.** The `mixed` method refused on the wallet leg *after* the card leg was already underway, reaching the right answer through a compensation path rather than through a check, which is the worst of the three outcomes because it is the one that looks like it works. The general shape is what makes this an edge case rather than a bug: **a hard limit asserted against one payment method passes while another method refuses for an unrelated reason**, and the test that would catch it is the one nobody writes, because the first method tested is the one the author was thinking about.
- Correct behavior: **The restriction check goes at the resolved-identity step**, server side, where every other hard limit already sits, joining the existing `geo_restricted` and `account_cap_reached` refusal set ([M03](../plans/M03-billing-checkout.md) section 3.5, `INV-M3-15`). It refuses purchases **and resets**, with a stated reason and a defined error code, and it is a **hard limit rather than a compensation**: nothing about the payment is begun and then unwound. **The test rule that comes out of this is general and binds beyond this limit: every hard limit is asserted against all three payment methods**, card, wallet and `mixed`. The scenario also requires the refusal to **survive the identity presenting a second email that resolves to the same identity**, which is `GS-094`'s assertion with a different hard limit behind it, because a per-human control checked against a per-login artifact is not a per-human control.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-280
