## EC-081: Last-touch attribution is stealable by volume  (2026-08-13, module: M8, status: specced)
- Scenario: A 30 day last-touch cookie can be captured wholesale. An affiliate embeds invisible click-throughs in unrelated traffic, so anyone who later buys through search or word of mouth carries their token, and Merit pays commission on conversions it already had. The fingerprint is arithmetic: a legitimate affiliate converts a few percent of clicks, a stuffer converts a tiny fraction of a percent while capturing a large share of total conversions, and their dashboard looks excellent because their conversion rate is really the site organic rate.
- Correct behavior: Make the pattern visible rather than degrading the product. Record `referrer_host`, whether the landing was direct, and a click fingerprint (SD-M8-02), and compare each affiliate weekly on clicks-per-conversion and distinct referrer count against the population, routing outliers to the risk queue rather than auto-suspending, because the honest and dishonest cases can look alike in a single week. Declared affiliate properties give the referrer check something to compare against. **Shortening the attribution window is the obvious fix and is the wrong one**: 30 days is the industry norm and cutting it punishes legitimate content affiliates whose readers take weeks to decide, in order to stop a pattern that is directly detectable.
- Golden scenario ref: GS-125.
