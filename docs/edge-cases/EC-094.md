## EC-094: A pass rate is a choice of denominator, and every choice is defensible  (2026-08-14, module: M12, status: specced)
- Scenario: "Pass rate" has no canonical definition. Including accounts purchased but never traded lowers it substantially; including accounts still in evaluation at window close lowers it further and mechanically more so the faster the firm grows; counting a reset as a new attempt rather than a continuation moves it by a large factor on any plan with cheap resets; counting per account rather than per identity diverges sharply once one trader holds up to ten accounts; anchoring the window on purchase date rather than outcome date describes a different cohort entirely. Every choice is defensible individually, so a firm choosing after seeing the numbers can land anywhere across a very wide range and defend each step. TradeDay's published 36 percent is a blog figure with no method attached, so nobody can tell which choices it made.
- Correct behavior: The precommitment is the product, not the accuracy. Definitions are **versioned rows with public method pages** stating numerator, denominator, and every exclusion in words a trader can check against their own experience. **`effective_from` is always in the future**, so no definition can take effect before it was written and therefore none can be chosen with knowledge of its result; a write with a non-future `effective_from` is rejected. Changes require an ADR and apply forward only, historical values are never recomputed under a new definition, and a chart crossing a version boundary renders the discontinuity rather than smoothing it. Numerator and denominator are published alongside the ratio so a reader recomputes rather than trusts. Merit's v1 choices take the unflattering reading of each (OQ-M12-01).
- Golden scenario ref: GS-162, GS-171.
